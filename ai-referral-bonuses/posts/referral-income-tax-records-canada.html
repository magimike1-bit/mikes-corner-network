Referral Income and Taxes in Canada: The Records to Keep All Year

Guides · Published 2026-11-02 · Last updated 2026-11-02

The expensive misconception in referral income is a quiet one: if no tax slip arrives, nothing needs reporting. Canadian rules do not work that way. The CRA’s own tax tips state that self-employment income must be reported, including income earned through gig work, social media platforms, freelancing, or a small business, and that platform economy earners may be considered self-employed and carrying on a business. A referral commission is exactly that kind of income. A bookkeeping guide for affiliate fees, checked on 2026-11-02, puts it plainly: for tax purposes, affiliate income is a commission and business income, and it belongs in its own account rather than mixed into general sales. The work is not the reporting. The work is having records that survive the reporting, and that work is monthly, not annual.

The runbook: a monthly record routine

The CRA requires records of all transactions to support income and expense claims, and asks for a record of daily income and expenses. It does not issue record books or prescribe a system, which leaves the design to you. For referral income, this routine fits the obligation:

  1. Export every program dashboard statement on the same day each month, before balances roll forward. Save the file with the program name and month in the filename.
  2. Log each payout in one ledger with the three fields the CRA names for income records: the date, the amount, and the source of the income. Add the currency the program paid in, exactly as shown.
  3. Log credits on their own line. The CRA instructs taxpayers to record income whether it was received as cash, property, or services. A bonus paid in platform credits gets a line, a value as stated by the program, and a note that it was not cash.
  4. Log reversals when they happen. A clawed back or adjusted commission is a negative entry in the month the dashboard shows it, not a quiet edit to the original entry.
  5. File the supporting documents together: dashboard statements, payout confirmations, and bank deposit records for the same month in one folder. The CRA lists fee statements among the original documents that support income entries.
  6. Total the running year. Add the month to a year to date figure for all taxable income together, not referral income alone. That total drives the threshold watch below.

What success looks like: for any month, the ledger total, the dashboard statements, and the bank deposits agree to the cent, and every difference is explained by a named line, a credit, a fee, or a reversal, rather than by memory.

One wrinkle the bookkeeping guide flags: many programs pay automatically with no invoice, so nobody may be adding or backing out tax, and the program terms decide how a payment should be treated. Keep the terms page for each program with your statements. If a dispute or a review ever asks what a payment was, the terms and the statement answer together.

The two thresholds and dates that catch people

Retention: six years. Business records are generally kept for a minimum of six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to, and the CRA asks that separate records be kept for each business you run. Monthly exports make this painless; reconstructing six years of dashboards does not, because programs do not promise to keep your history that long.

GST/HST: the $30,000 watch. Under the CRA’s definition, a small supplier is a person whose revenue from worldwide taxable supplies was $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration is required when a person is no longer a small supplier and makes taxable supplies in Canada. Referral income rarely crosses that line alone, but it counts alongside your other business revenue, which is why the routine above totals everything together.

The split deadline. For the 2025 tax year, the CRA gave self-employed filers until June 15, 2026 to file, while any balance owing was still due April 30, 2026. Filing late and paying late are different mistakes with different costs; a current ledger is what lets you estimate the April payment before the June filing exists. Check the current year’s dates on the CRA site each spring, because the pattern matters more than the specific year.

When the honest answer is an accountant

Stop and get professional advice when any of these lines is crossed: your combined business revenue approaches the $30,000 GST/HST threshold, programs pay you from outside Canada in foreign currency as a routine matter, credits form a large share of what you earn, or a program issues you a tax slip that disagrees with your ledger. Each of those changes the treatment, not just the total. The kill criterion for the do it yourself approach is simple: the first month your routine cannot reconcile the ledger to the deposits, the system, not the season, is the problem, and it gets fixed that month or handed to someone whose job it is.
