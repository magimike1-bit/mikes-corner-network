When a Referral Commission Goes Missing: The Evidence Pack and the Ask

Guides · Published 2026-11-20 · Last updated 2026-11-20

Four. That is how many different problems hide behind the sentence “my commission did not show up.” The sale may not be reported yet. It may have been denied. It may have been recorded and then reversed. Or it may never have been tracked at all. Each state has a different cause, a different fix, and a different chance of success, and sending the same angry email about all four wastes the one resource a dispute runs on, which is the support team’s willingness to dig. Name the state first. The evidence follows from the name.

State one: not yet reported

Start with the calendar, not the complaint. PartnerStack’s partner documentation, checked in November 2026, gives two ordinary reasons a commission is invisible. The event may not qualify under the company’s offer structure, in which case no commission is generated at all. Or the company may use drip commissions, which delay generation by a set period or pay the commission out over a period, often to cover the customer cancellation window. On top of either, the platform cycle adds its own lag: commissions are invoiced at the beginning of each month and sent to the companies for review, and only after a company has reviewed and paid do the funds become available to withdraw.

The test for this state is patience with a date attached. Find the program’s hold or drip period in its terms, add the monthly review cycle, and mark the day the commission should exist by. A commission inside that window is not missing. It is in transit, and disputing it early teaches the support team to deprioritise your name.

State two: denied

A denial is a decision, and decisions can be reviewed when the facts change. Search Atlas’s affiliate help page lists the causes its team sees: a sign-up method that interfered with tracking at the point of sign-up, a payment processed through a checkout path that is not fully integrated with the tracking system, a referral link visited only after the customer had already created their account, a referred email address that was already associated with an account, and manual denial where the referral did not meet the programme’s eligibility criteria.

Read that list as a set of questions about your referral, not as excuses. Was the account created before the click? Was the email already on file? Two of the five causes end the matter on the facts. The other three are exactly the cases a manual review exists for, because the sale happened and only the recording failed.

State three: reversed

A reversal is not a denial. The commission existed, and then an event the terms name removed it. Descript’s affiliate terms, in this site’s directory, reserve the right to reverse commissions for returns, order cancellations, erroneous order duplications, and erroneous payments, and to defer or refuse commissions for disputed orders. The dispute worth having here is narrow: ask which named event fired, and on which transaction. If the answer is a genuine return, the reversal is the terms working as written. If no event can be named, you have a recording error, and recording errors are correctable.

State four: never tracked

This is the largest state and the hardest, because there is no record to appeal to. PartnerStack’s troubleshooting documentation lists the failure modes from the platform’s side. A click places a cookie in the customer’s browser, and a sign-up or purchase on a different browser or device does not track. Cleared cookies remove the record. Blocked cookies prevent it. A later click on another partner’s link takes the referral under most recent click attribution. A customer who types the address in by hand instead of clicking records nothing at all. The same documentation puts a number on the window: the referral cookie stays active for 90 days, unless another partner’s link replaces it.

None of that sounds recoverable, and yet the platform documents three remedies for the company once it is satisfied the referral was real: manually add the customer and record the sale so existing offers trigger, create a one-off manual commission, or import the missed activity in bulk. Your dispute is therefore not an argument about tracking. It is a request for a manual credit, and it succeeds or fails on proof that the customer was yours.

The evidence pack

Two official sources, one from a program and one from a platform, ask for nearly the same pack. Assemble it before you write a word of the message:

ItemWhy it is asked for
The referred customer’s name and email addressLets the team find the account and the transaction in their own records
The date the customer signed up or purchasedNarrows the search to one day of sign-ups and tests the click against the program window
The exact referral link you sharedShows which link was clicked and whether it carried your partner key
Confirmation emails or screenshots showing the subscription is activeProves a paid, live customer exists on the other side of the missing record
The sign-up method, and the browser and device used for the click and the sign-upTests the known failure modes: method interference and a browser or device switch

One privacy point belongs in the pack, not after it. The customer’s email address is their data. Share it with the program because the program already holds the account, and tell the customer you are doing it. A referral who learns their address was passed around without a word is a referral you will not get twice.

The ask, step by step

  1. Name the state. Check the hold period, the drip terms, and the monthly review cycle first. Dispute only what is genuinely past its date or formally denied.
  2. Run the five denial causes against your facts. Account before click, existing email, ineligible referral: if one fits, stop. The terms decided this one, and the next section explains why.
  3. Assemble the full pack. Every item in the table, in one message. A dispute that arrives in four follow-ups gets investigated in four separate queues.
  4. Send it to the right desk. On PartnerStack programs, offer criteria questions go to the company through the program’s Messages tab, not to the platform. Platform support is for platform failures.
  5. Ask for the specific remedy. Name it: a manual credit, a manually added customer with the sale recorded, or a review of the denial. “Please look into this” invites a form reply. A named remedy invites a decision.
  6. Record the outcome in your own tracking. Program, date, state, evidence sent, result. The next missing commission in the same program is a pattern, and patterns are what change a program’s mind, or yours.

What success looks like: the program replies naming the state and the transaction, and either issues a manual credit or points to the exact terms clause that closes the case. Both are usable outcomes. The unusable outcome is silence, and silence after a complete pack is itself information about the program.

When the terms have already decided

Some disputes are over before they start, and recognising them quickly is worth money. Descript’s terms define a valid purchase as a new customer who clicked the unique URL and purchased within 30 days of the first click, and state plainly that a purchase on day 32 is not a valid purchase. The same terms give the company sole discretion over whether a purchase is valid, and the sole right to decide when tracking records disagree. Against clauses like those, evidence about your promotion effort changes nothing, because the argument is not about what happened. It is about a definition you accepted at sign-up.

The decision framework is short. If the gap is a recording failure inside the program’s own rules, dispute it with the full pack. If the gap is the rules themselves, a window your customer missed, a new customer condition, a sole discretion clause, do not dispute. Reprice the program in your own planning instead, because its effective window and eligibility are narrower than its landing page suggests, and promote it, or drop it, on that basis.

The stop line for dispute effort, stated plainly: one complete pack, one named remedy, one follow-up. After that, the hours cost more than the commission in almost every case this site’s directory covers, and the same hours spent on content for a program whose tracking you have verified will pay more reliably than a third email about one that failed.
