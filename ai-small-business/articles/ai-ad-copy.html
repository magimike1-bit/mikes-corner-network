Testing Ad Copy with AI

One ad rarely wins — testing does. The businesses that get the most from their ad spend try lots of small variations and keep what works. The bottleneck is that writing ten versions of an ad by hand is tedious. AI can generate those variations in minutes: different headlines, different hooks, different angles on the same offer, all ready for you to test.

What it is

Testing ad copy with AI means using a general AI assistant — ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini — to take one ad idea and multiply it. You describe your offer, your customer, and the platform (Facebook, Instagram, Google search, a flyer), and it drafts a set of headlines, primary text versions, and calls to action that approach the offer from different angles: one focused on price, one on convenience, one on quality, one on a seasonal reason to buy.

The point isn't to find a magic sentence — it's to run several variations at once and let the numbers tell you which one pulls. Ad platforms already make this easy: Facebook and Google both let you enter multiple headlines and descriptions and rotate them automatically. AI just supplies the raw material for those slots, which used to be the hardest part.

This also works for non-digital ads. A flyer for the shop window, a postcard mailer, or the text on your website's promo banner can all be drafted and varied the same way, then judged by the same rule: which version brings in business?

Who it's for

Businesses that already spend money on ads — even a modest monthly amount on Facebook or Google — and currently run one version of each ad. If you've ever thought "I wonder if different wording would work better," you now have a way to find out cheaply.

If you don't run ads at all, this guide waits for you when you start. And if your offer itself is the problem (wrong price, wrong audience), no amount of copy testing fixes that — fix the offer first, then polish the words.

What it costs

Most tools offer a free tier that's enough for drafting ad variations; paid plans are usually billed per user per month, often roughly $10–$50/user/month for the AI assistants. Your ad spend itself is separate and entirely up to you. Check the vendor's current pricing — it changes.

Target ROI: the honest math EXAMPLE

The payoff comes from finding ad copy that converts better — the same ad budget bringing in more enquiries — plus the time you save writing variations.

Example: Suppose you spend $200/month on ads and your current ad turns $200 into 10 enquiries. If testing finds copy that brings in 12 enquiries for the same $200, that's 2 extra enquiries a month at no extra spend. If each is worth $50 in profit, that's about $100/month extra — against a $20/month tool, the example net is about $80/month, plus the writing time you saved.

What to actually measure: click-through rate per ad variation; cost per enquiry or per click for each version; which headline style wins and loses; enquiries per week before and after testing; total ad spend vs. revenue from ads. Kill losers fast and put the budget behind winners.

How to set it up

  1. Describe your offer and customer. In one short paragraph: what you're selling, who it's for, the price or offer, and what makes it worth buying. This briefing goes into every AI session so the copy stays grounded.
  2. Generate headline variations. Ask for 10 headlines approaching the offer from different angles — price, convenience, quality, urgency, seasonal. Throw out anything that overpromises or sounds like a stranger's business.
  3. Generate body copy variations. Ask for 3–4 short versions of the main ad text, each under the platform's character limits, each ending in a clear call to action (call, book, visit, message).
  4. Load them into the ad platform. Enter the variations as multiple headlines and descriptions in your Facebook or Google ad. Let the platform rotate them rather than guessing which is best yourself.
  5. Set a testing budget and a review date. Decide up front: a modest daily amount for two weeks, then you'll look at the numbers. A fixed review date stops you from fiddling daily or ignoring it for months.
  6. Review, keep winners, cut losers. At the review date, pause the worst performers and shift budget to the best. Then ask the AI for fresh variations inspired by the winning style — testing is a loop, not a one-time event.
  7. Save what works. Keep a simple document of winning headlines and the angles behind them. Your next campaign starts from your own proven material instead of from scratch.

Watch-outs and honest limitations

AI will happily write claims you can't back up — "best in town," "guaranteed results," fake discounts — and ad platforms can reject or penalize ads with misleading copy, so check every line against what you actually offer. Don't test so many variations that each one gets too few impressions to judge; three to five strong versions beat twenty thin ones. Small businesses with tiny budgets need to be patient: it takes time and spend to get enough data to call a winner, so don't declare victory after a weekend.

What to measure in your first 30 days

  • Click-through rate for each ad variation.
  • Cost per click and cost per enquiry per variation.
  • Total enquiries from ads vs. the previous month.
  • Which angle (price, convenience, quality) won overall.
  • Total ad spend vs. revenue you can trace to the ads.
