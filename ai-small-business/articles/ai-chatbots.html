Customer Service

AI Chatbots for Small Business: Answer Customers Around the Clock

A chatbot is a small chat window on your website that answers common customer questions — hours, prices, booking, directions — any time of day or night, even when you're busy with another customer or the shop is closed.

What it is

An AI chatbot is a text-based assistant that sits on your website, usually as a small "Chat with us" button in the corner of the screen. When a visitor clicks it and asks something like "Are you open Sundays?" or "How much is a haircut?", the chatbot replies in plain sentences, drawing on the information you gave it — your hours, your price list, your services, your FAQ page.

Think of it as a very patient receptionist who never takes a break. It handles the repetitive questions that make up most customer contacts — hours, pricing basics, services, booking, returns — and hands anything too complicated to a human by taking the visitor's name, email, and question for you to reply to later.

You don't build the technology yourself. Established chatbot tools let you sign up, paste in your business details or website pages, and add a small snippet of code to your site. Most let you set the greeting, the tone, and which topics the bot may answer, and they log every conversation so you can see what people ask.

Who it's for

Chatbots help most when you get the same questions over and over, or when customers often contact you outside working hours. Service businesses (salons, plumbers, dentists, repair shops), restaurants taking reservations, and small retailers with an online presence all fit this pattern.

It helps less for complex custom quotes, heavily personalized advice, or sensitive conversations where a wrong answer would be costly — financial planning or medical guidance, say. A chatbot can still handle the simple stuff (hours, directions, booking) but should hand off fast. If nearly all customer contact is by phone and few people visit your website, fix the website first.

What it costs

Most chatbot tools offer a free tier that covers a basic website chatbot with limited monthly conversations — enough to try it out or serve a low-traffic site. Paid plans are usually billed per month, sometimes per user or per number of chats handled, and often land roughly in the $10–$50/month range for small-business plans, with higher tiers for heavy traffic — but check the vendor's current pricing before you commit, because it changes.

Beyond the subscription, budget a few hours of your time to set it up properly: writing your FAQs, reviewing the bot's answers, and testing it yourself. Some business owners hire a freelancer to do the initial setup; that typically runs a few hundred dollars for a basic implementation. Skip the expensive custom-build route until the simple version proves itself.

Target ROI: the honest math EXAMPLE

The payoff from a chatbot is the time you stop spending on repetitive questions, plus the business you stop losing when nobody answers. Here's example math to show how the pieces fit — swap in your own numbers.

Example: Suppose your website gets 200 chat questions a month and staff spend 10 minutes on each — about 33 hours, or $825 of time at $25/hr. If the bot answers half of them well, you free ~16 hours ≈ $400/month, against a $30/month tool — an example net of about $370/month, before counting the enquiries it catches overnight. Your question volume will differ; measure it for a month first.

What to actually measure:

  • How many chats the bot handles per week, and what share get resolved without you stepping in
  • After-hours or weekend chats — conversations that would previously have gone nowhere
  • Time you or your staff used to spend answering the same questions by email or phone
  • Whether those chat conversations turn into bookings, orders, or quote requests

How to set it up

  1. List your 20 most-asked questions. Look at recent emails, voicemails, and messages. Hours, prices, parking, booking, cancellations — write each down with the exact answer you want given.
  2. Pick a chatbot tool with a free trial. Choose one that trains on your own content (your pages or an uploaded document) and offers a human handoff. Ask other business owners for recommendations if unsure.
  3. Feed it your content and set the rules. Upload your FAQ list, pricing, and services pages. Then write an instruction like: "Answer in a friendly, short tone. Never invent prices — if you don't know, say so and offer to take a message." Don't skip this step.
  4. Write the greeting and the handoff. A friendly opening ("Hi! Ask me about hours, prices, or booking.") plus a clear fallback: "I'm not sure — want me to pass your question to the team?"
  5. Add it to your site and test it hard. Paste the code snippet into your website (most builders have a spot for it). Then ask it everything on your FAQ list — including tricky and badly worded questions — and fix wrong answers by improving the source content.
  6. Review the chat logs weekly. For the first month, skim the logs once a week. Add any question it couldn't answer to your source material, and correct anything it got wrong.

Watch-outs and honest limitations

A chatbot can confidently give a wrong answer if its source material is incomplete — for example, quoting an old price from a page you forgot to update. It is only ever as current as the content you feed it, so outdated hours or prices on your website become outdated answers in the chat. Chatbots also struggle with vague or multi-part questions, frustrated customers, and anything needing judgment about a specific situation. That's what the human handoff is for — use it generously. And some customers simply dislike chatting with a bot, so always keep your phone number or email easy to find on the same page.

What to measure in your first 30 days

  • Number of chat sessions per week and the share the bot resolved without a handoff
  • The 10 most common questions asked — and whether your FAQ page already covers them
  • Any wrong or unhelpful answers, with the question text, so you can fix the source content
  • How many chats produced a booking, order, or contact detail for follow-up
  • Rough time saved: estimate minutes per chat × chats that previously came by phone or email
