AI Lead Follow-Up: Reply in Minutes, Not Days

Slow follow-up loses jobs. When a customer reaches out for a quote and hears nothing for two days, they call the next business on the list. AI can't do the job for you, but it can make sure every enquiry gets a fast, professional first reply — even when you're on a ladder, on the road, or up to your elbows in the day's work.

What it is

AI lead follow-up means two things working together: fast first replies and reliable nudges afterwards. The first reply is a short, friendly message that goes out the moment someone contacts you — acknowledging the enquiry, asking the one or two questions you always ask, and saying when you'll get back with details. AI drafts it from a template you approve once, so it sounds like you wrote it at 2am while you were actually asleep.

The second part is the follow-up sequence: polite check-ins at set intervals for quotes that went quiet. Most owners stop after one attempt; a simple "just checking in — still interested?" email or text a few days later costs almost nothing and regularly revives jobs you'd written off.

This isn't about robotic spam. It's about being the business that replies first and follows up reliably — the two habits that consistently separate busy businesses from struggling ones. The AI handles the drafting and the scheduling; you handle the actual conversation and the quote.

Who it's for

Service businesses that win work through enquiries: contractors, cleaners, landscapers, repair shops, real estate agents, consultants — anyone whose phone or inbox brings jobs and whose day job keeps them from answering immediately. If you've ever found an enquiry from three days ago and thought "oh no," this is for you.

If your work comes entirely from walk-ins or scheduled bookings with no quoting involved, the follow-up sequence matters less — but the fast-reply habit is still worth having for the messages you do get.

What it costs

Most tools offer a free tier that's enough for drafting reply templates and follow-up emails; paid plans are usually billed per user per month, often roughly $10–$50/user/month for the AI assistants. If you connect automated replies through a CRM or messaging tool (HubSpot, Calendly, and similar), those have their own pricing tiers. Check the vendor's current pricing — it changes.

Target ROI: the honest math EXAMPLE

The return is recovered jobs — enquiries that would have gone cold but turned into work because you replied fast and followed up. Even one extra job a month can dwarf the cost.

Example: Say you get 20 enquiries a month and win 5 of them. If faster replies and a follow-up sequence convert just 2 more — 7 out of 20 — and each job is worth $200 in profit, that's about $400/month in recovered profit. Against a $20/month tool, the example net is about $380/month — before counting the time you save writing replies.

What to actually measure: your average first-reply time (check your inbox timestamps); enquiry-to-quote and quote-to-job rates before and after; how many quiet quotes revive after a follow-up nudge; the source of each won job so you know which ones came from follow-up.

How to set it up

  1. Write your first-reply template. Draft (or have the AI draft) a short reply: thank them, name the service they asked about, ask your 1–2 standard qualifying questions, and say when they'll hear from you next. Keep it under 100 words.
  2. Add it where enquiries arrive. Save the template as a saved reply or snippet in your email, your Facebook page's instant reply, or your CRM. The goal: the first reply takes ten seconds, not ten minutes.
  3. Draft the follow-up sequence. Have the AI write three short check-ins: a day after the quote, three days later, and a week later — each friendly, each offering an easy way to say yes. Review every one before use.
  4. Set reminders you can't ignore. If you don't have a CRM, put follow-ups on your calendar or phone alarms. A template you forget to send is just a document.
  5. Personalize the key line. Templates are the skeleton; always add one real detail — the job address, the car model, the thing they mentioned. That one line is what makes a template feel human.
  6. Review your reply time weekly. Once a week, glance at your last few enquiries and note how long the first reply took. If it's creeping back up, your template placement needs fixing, not your willpower.
  7. Track what converts. Keep a simple list: enquiry date, when you replied, whether it became a job. After a month you'll see the pattern — and the pattern is the whole point.

Watch-outs and honest limitations

Automated first replies only work if they sound like a person — over-polished corporate wording makes people suspicious, so keep your templates plain and personal. Never let an automated reply promise a price, a timeline, or availability you haven't confirmed; the template acknowledges, it doesn't commit. And don't bombard people: three polite follow-ups is a sequence, ten is harassment. If someone doesn't answer after the sequence ends, leave them alone — they may still come back later on their own.

What to measure in your first 30 days

  • Average time from enquiry to first reply.
  • Enquiries that got a reply within an hour, as a share of total.
  • Quote-to-job conversion rate vs. last month.
  • Jobs revived by a follow-up nudge.
  • Which follow-up message in the sequence gets the most replies.
