AI mistakes to watch for: hallucinations and why you must double-check

Getting started · 6 min read

The most important thing a beginner can learn about AI chatbots is this: they can be wrong while sounding completely right. This has a name — "hallucination" — and understanding it is the difference between using AI safely and getting burned.

What is a hallucination?

A hallucination is when a chatbot generates something that sounds factual but isn't — a made-up quote, a wrong date, a plausible-sounding but nonexistent source, a confident answer to a question it actually doesn't have reliable information about. It's not lying; lying requires intent. It's pattern-completing: producing text that looks like a good answer even when the underlying facts are wrong.

The danger isn't that it happens — it's that the tone is identical whether the answer is right or wrong. There's no hesitation, no warning tone, no visual cue. Confidence is not a signal of accuracy.

Common types of AI errors

  • Made-up details. Names, dates, numbers, and citations can be invented whole-cloth. "According to a 2019 study by…" may be a study that never existed.
  • Outdated information. The chatbot's knowledge has a cutoff. Ask about anything recent — laws, prices, product features, current events — and it may answer with stale information as if it were current.
  • Confident math and logic slips. Multi-step calculations and puzzles get wrong intermediate steps surprisingly often. Always re-check numbers that matter.
  • Misunderstood questions. Ambiguous wording can send the answer in a completely wrong direction, and the confident tone won't reveal the mix-up.
  • Contradictions across answers. Ask the same question two ways and you may get two different "certain" answers. That alone tells you verification is needed.

The double-check checklist

Match your verification effort to the stakes:

  1. Low stakes (brainstorming, drafts, casual curiosity): a quick sanity check is fine. Does it sound plausible? Move on.
  2. Medium stakes (work emails, schoolwork, travel plans): verify key facts with an independent source — an official site, a reputable reference. Re-run any numbers yourself.
  3. High stakes (health, legal, financial, safety): use the chatbot only to prepare — organize your questions, learn the terminology — then confirm everything with a qualified professional or authoritative source. Never act on AI advice alone here.

Practical verification habits

  • Ask for sources — then check them. "What sources are you basing this on?" is useful, but fabricated citations happen, so click through.
  • Cross-check with a second chatbot or a web search. Independent agreement raises confidence; disagreement is a red flag.
  • Ask it to show its work. "Walk me through your reasoning step by step" makes math and logic errors easier to spot.
  • Be suspicious of suspiciously specific details. Precise numbers, exact quotes, and obscure citations are exactly the things most likely to be invented.
  • Watch for "it depends" answers that don't say what it depends on. A good answer names the variables; a hallucinated one waves at them.

It can also correct itself — use that

Here's a useful quirk: if you push back, the chatbot will often catch its own error. "Are you sure about that date?" or "Can you double-check that calculation?" frequently produces a corrected answer. This works because the challenge forces it to re-examine the reasoning instead of repeating the pattern that produced the first answer. It doesn't always work — sometimes it confidently "corrects" a right answer into a wrong one — but as a habit, asking for a second pass on anything important is cheap insurance. When it flips its answer under mild pressure, treat that as information: this is a topic where you definitely need an independent source.

The bottom line

Hallucinations aren't a bug you can wait out — they're a property of how these tools work. The fix isn't a better chatbot; it's a better user. Stay in the editor's chair: let the AI draft, and you verify, decide, and sign off. Do that, and the mistakes become a minor annoyance instead of a real problem.
