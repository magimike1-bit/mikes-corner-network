AI voice chat and dictation for beginners: talk instead of type

Getting started · 8 min read

Typing is not the only way to talk to a chatbot. Modern AI apps offer two different voice features, and beginners often confuse them because both start with a microphone button. One is a spoken conversation: you talk, the chatbot talks back. The other is dictation: you talk, and your words simply become text you can edit. Knowing which is which — and which one a task actually needs — makes voice features go from gimmick to genuinely useful, especially when your hands are busy but your mind is not.

Two different features with similar names

OpenAI's own Academy draws the line clearly: Voice Mode is a two-way conversation where you talk and listen in real time, while dictation means speaking and watching your words appear as text for further refinement. Anthropic makes the same distinction for Claude: dictation converts your speech to text so you can compose prompts by speaking, while voice mode is a full two-way conversation where the chatbot speaks back. Keep that one sentence in mind and every voice button you meet becomes easy to predict.

Voice mode: a conversation you listen to

In voice mode the exchange stays spoken. You ask a question aloud, the chatbot answers aloud, and you can follow up, interrupt, or change direction mid-sentence, much like a phone call. This suits moments when reading a screen is awkward or impossible: cooking with messy hands, walking, practising a language out loud, or rehearsing how a difficult conversation might go. Claude's voice mode offers two listening styles worth knowing about: a hands-free mode, where it listens continuously and replies when you reach a natural pause, and a push-to-talk mode, where you hold a button while speaking and release it when you finish. Anthropic notes hands-free works best in quiet environments, while push-to-talk gives you more control in noisy places like busy streets or crowded rooms. You can also switch between typing and voice inside the same Claude conversation without losing context — handy when you need to type one precise detail, like an address, in the middle of a spoken chat.

One limit surprises beginners: voice time can be capped. OpenAI's help pages state that free ChatGPT accounts get limited voice access, that usage in Chat mode is measured over a rolling 24-hour period, and that the app notifies you when you reach the limit. A voice session that stops is usually a limit, not a fault.

Dictation: your words become text you can edit

Dictation is quieter and, for many beginners, more useful. You speak, the app turns your speech into a written message, and — this is the key detail — you can edit that text before sending it. OpenAI's dictation FAQ confirms the flow: pressing the microphone icon records an audio message, the recording is sent to its models to be transcribed, and the transcription comes back as text you can edit before it is sent as your message. Dictation is the right tool when the end product is writing: a long message you would rather say than type, rough notes from a walk, a first draft of an email, or a detailed prompt that is easier to explain out loud. Because the result is ordinary text, everything else you know about chatbots still applies — you can ask for a summary, a rewrite, or a bullet list of the very words you just dictated.

How to start each one, step by step

Button positions change between app versions, so treat these as the reliable pattern rather than an exact map:

  1. Open the chatbot app on your phone (voice features are richest on mobile) and start a new conversation.
  2. For dictation: tap the microphone icon inside or beside the message box, speak, then stop the recording. Read the transcription that appears, fix any wrong words, and only then press send.
  3. For voice mode: tap the voice or sound-wave control near the message box (on some apps you tap and hold the large microphone button), allow microphone access when the phone asks, choose a voice if offered, and simply start talking. Speak naturally — the tools are built to handle pauses and restarts.
  4. To finish a voice conversation, use the end or stop control on screen. To change the speaking voice later, look in the app's settings, where voice options live.

What success looks like: after dictation, you see an editable text version of what you said sitting in the message box before it is sent. After starting voice mode, the chatbot replies out loud and the conversation continues hands-free until you end it. If you get text when you wanted speech, or speech when you wanted text, you tapped the neighbouring feature — back out and choose the other control.

Getting clearer results when you speak

  • Speak clearly at a natural pace. Rushing blurs words together; slow, steady speech transcribes best.
  • Say punctuation out loud in dictation — "comma," "period," "new paragraph" — when you are dictating something long.
  • Cut background noise. A quiet room improves both dictation accuracy and voice conversations; in noise you cannot avoid, push-to-talk helps.
  • Dictate in short segments for complex topics. Small chunks make fewer errors and are easier to fix.
  • Let the chatbot clean up afterwards. A dictated draft is raw material: follow up with "rewrite this more clearly" or "turn this into bullet points."
  • Always proofread names and numbers before sending a dictated message. Transcription errors cluster exactly there.

What happens to your recordings

Voice features process your audio on the provider's servers, and the details are worth a minute of your attention. For ChatGPT dictation, OpenAI states that the recorded audio is kept for as long as the chat remains in your chat history, and that when you delete the chat the associated audio clip is also deleted within 30 days, with limited exceptions such as security or legal reasons. If you have chosen to share audio to improve the models, consumer audio may be used for training; you can stop sharing in Settings, under data controls, by turning off the "Include your audio recordings" toggle — or the broader "Improve the model for everyone" setting — which OpenAI notes stops sharing across both dictation and voice chat. Business accounts are not trained on by default. For Claude, Anthropic states that text transcripts of voice conversations are saved in your chat history just like typed conversations. The practical rule for beginners: assume anything you say to a voice feature leaves a transcript, and keep passwords, financial details, and other people's private information out of spoken chats.

Good first things to try by voice

  • Dictate a rambling description of your weekend plans, then ask for a tidy itinerary.
  • Use voice mode to practise ordering food or asking directions in another language.
  • Dictate meeting or phone-call notes immediately afterwards, while you still remember them, then ask for a summary.
  • Ask a spoken question while cooking — a substitution, a conversion, a timer check on a step — where typing would mean washing your hands first.

FAQ

Is voice mode free? Partly. OpenAI lists limited voice access on the free ChatGPT plan, with usage measured over a rolling 24-hour window; paid plans allow more. Check your own app's settings for the current allowance, because limits change.

Can I switch between speaking and typing in one chat? Yes — in Claude, Anthropic documents switching between text and voice within the same conversation with context carried over, and the same pattern applies in current ChatGPT apps.

The transcription keeps getting a name wrong. What helps? Spell the name once in the follow-up message, dictate in shorter segments, and reduce background noise. Some words will always need a quick manual fix — that is what the edit-before-sending step is for.
