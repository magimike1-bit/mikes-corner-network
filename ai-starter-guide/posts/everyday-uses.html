Everyday uses: practical things people actually do with AI chatbots

Getting started · 6 min read

Beyond the demos and the hype, what do regular people use AI chatbots for in daily life? A lot of ordinary, useful stuff. Here are practical ideas, each with an example prompt you can try.

Writing and communication

  • Difficult emails. "Help me write a polite complaint to my internet provider about a billing error. Firm but civil, under 150 words."
  • Résumés and cover letters. Paste your job description and your experience; ask it to draft a cover letter, then rewrite every line in your own voice.
  • Speeches and toasts. "Draft a 2-minute wedding toast for my brother. Warm and funny, no inside jokes that need explaining."

Learning anything

  • Explain it like I'm five — or fifty. "Explain how a mortgage amortization schedule works, like I'm a complete beginner."
  • Decode jargon. Paste the confusing paragraph from a contract, doctor's report, or insurance letter and ask for a plain-English version.
  • Practice skills. "Quiz me on French vocabulary for restaurant situations, one question at a time, and correct my answers."

Planning and organizing

  • Trip itineraries. "Plan a 4-day itinerary for Montreal in October for two adults who love food and history. Moderate budget, walking-friendly."
  • Meal planning. "Give me 5 weeknight dinners using chicken, rice, and whatever's cheap. I'm a beginner cook, 30 minutes max each."
  • Event planning. Birthday party checklists, moving-day timelines, holiday gift budgets — describe the constraints and get a plan.

Work and side projects

  • Meeting prep. Paste your agenda and ask for likely questions plus talking points.
  • Summarizing long threads. "Summarize this email chain in 5 bullet points and flag anything that needs a decision."
  • Brainstorming. Names, slogans, gift ideas, blog topics, menu items — quantity first, judgment later.

Home and hobbies

  • Fix-it troubleshooting. "My washing machine makes a loud banging noise on the spin cycle. It's a top-loader, about 8 years old. What should I check first?" (Good for diagnosis; check safety warnings before touching anything.)
  • Gardening plans. "What can I plant in a sunny Toronto balcony container in May that a beginner won't kill?"
  • Gift shopping. "Gift ideas under $50 for a dad who likes fishing and grilling but already owns everything."

Your first week: a gentle ramp-up

If all of this sounds like a lot, try one new use per day for a week:

  1. Day 1: Ask it to explain something you were curious about this week.
  2. Day 2: Draft one real email or message with its help.
  3. Day 3: Ask for a meal plan or a packing list for something coming up.
  4. Day 4: Paste in a long email or article and ask for a summary.
  5. Day 5: Brainstorm ideas for something — gifts, names, weekend plans.
  6. Day 6: Practice something: interview questions, a language, a skill.
  7. Day 7: Plan one bigger thing — a trip, an event, a project.

By the end of the week you'll have a personal sense of where it shines for you — which matters more than any list. Everyone's "killer use" is different: the writer loves drafts, the traveler loves itineraries, the learner loves explanations. Find yours by doing, not by reading about it.

What these all have in common

Notice the pattern: the chatbot does the first draft — of the plan, the explanation, the email, the list — and you apply judgment. None of these uses require you to trust the AI blindly. You're the expert on your own life; the chatbot just clears the blank page.

One caution

For anything with real consequences — medical symptoms, legal documents, financial decisions, anything safety-related — use the chatbot to prepare (organize your questions, understand the vocabulary), not to decide. The final call stays with you and the appropriate professional.
