Early Black Friday Deals: Worth It Now, or Better on the Day?

"Early Black Friday" now starts a month before Black Friday. Some of those early prices will never get better; others are a warm-up act. Four tests separate the two — and none of them is the size of the discount percentage.

Black Friday itself has not moved: in 2026 it falls on Friday, November 27, the day after US Thanksgiving on Thursday, November 26, with Cyber Monday following on Monday, November 30. What has moved is everything around it. Retailers now open the season weeks early, which turns a one-day decision into a month-long series of small ones: every October and early-November offer asks the same question — is this price the deal, or is the real deal still coming? This guide replaces the guess with a test.

The 2026 calendar: one day, three waves

The most recent season is the clearest guide to how the waves break. In 2025, Best Buy opened its Black Friday deals on October 31, with its main event starting November 20. Walmart ran an early round from November 14 to 16, then started its main Black Friday event on November 25. The pattern across big retailers is consistent: a first wave in late October or early November, a bigger wave in the week before Thanksgiving, and then Black Friday itself. As of mid-October 2026, retailers had not yet confirmed their 2026 start dates, so treat any specific "sale starts on" claim for this year with suspicion until the retailer itself publishes it — the calendar dates (November 27 and November 30) are fixed, but the waves are expectations based on 2025, not announcements.

What early deals actually are

Two facts about early deals cut against the instinct to always wait. First, discounts that land from the start of November tend to stick across the sale as a whole: more products join the sale on Black Friday itself, but the savings visible early are generally representative of the prices on the day. Waiting often buys a wider selection rather than a lower price on the same item. Second, early stock is not safe stock: a deal that has been sitting quietly for weeks can sell out quickly once Black Friday attention arrives and every shopper who bookmarked it checks out at once.

The October events also have their own specialty. Amazon's October Prime Big Deal Days are strongest on everyday essentials — categories like diapers, toys, and clothing — while Black Friday remains the better event for big-ticket and premium purchases such as premium car seats, nursery furniture, and luxury strollers. And the earliest deals deserve the same skepticism as any sale price: the first Black Friday deals begin cropping up in October and may not be the best of the season, but a shopper who never looks at them can miss a genuine bargain hiding in the warm-up wave.

The four-test framework: buy early or wait

Run any early deal through four tests. The pattern of answers — not any single one — makes the call.

  1. Price position. Is this price at or near the lowest the item has sold for recently, and does it hold up against the market? A listed sale price still needs checking before it counts as a good deal — compare it with other retailers and with the item's recent history. A price that is already at a recent low has little room to embarrass you later; a first-wave discount on an item that was cheaper last month fails this test outright.
  2. Stock risk. How likely is this exact item — this model, this size, this color — to survive until Black Friday? Popular models can sell out, and gift sets are a known thin-stock category late in the season. High stock risk argues for buying early; a commodity item in endless supply argues for patience.
  3. Protection. Does the retailer offer a price guarantee on early purchases, or a holiday return window long enough to cover a change of mind or a better price later? Protection converts an early buy from a bet into an option — if the downside is refundable, waiting has little left to offer. (More on this below.)
  4. Need-by date. When must the item be in hand? A gift that needs shipping time, or anything required before the holidays, gains real value from arriving early and loses real value in a December delivery queue. An item with no deadline can afford to wait for the best possible price; an item with a hard deadline cannot.

Reading the results: buy early when the price position is strong and any one of stock risk, protection, or need-by date also favors buying. Wait when the price is unremarkable and the item is plentiful, unprotected purchases can be reconsidered, or the category is known to peak later. Split decisions default to the per-item stakes: on a lower-priced item, an extra drop missed by buying early costs only a few dollars, so a strong price today is usually enough. On an expensive gadget — a PC, a laptop, a TV — the gap between an early price and a Black Friday price can be large, nothing at all, or a stock-out, so demand a stronger price position before buying early.

Category calls

The framework generalizes, but categories have known tendencies worth starting from. Laptops, TVs, and headphones: start watching in late October — big retailers began early in 2025 and popular models can sell out. Furniture, decor, and kitchen items: rarely scarce, so waiting for Black Friday week costs little and leaves time to compare. Everyday clothes and shoes: sales usually run across several days of the sale week, so there is no rush. Beauty and gift sets: check early and buy by Cyber Monday, because gift sets are popular presents and stock thins. Toys are the contrarian case: heavily promoted on Black Friday, yet prices frequently drop further as the holidays approach and retailers work through inventory — a toy that is not a must-have gift can reward patience past the big day. Fitness equipment rewards even more patience: its best deals typically arrive in January and February, not in November at all.

The protection play: guarantees and return windows

The least-discussed early-buy tool is price protection. The mechanics were demonstrated at scale in a past season: in Best Buy's 2021 holiday announcement, its October 19 sale was covered by a Black Friday Price Guarantee — the price would not go lower before Black Friday, and if it did, members were automatically refunded the difference, with no email or receipt required; non-members could still be reimbursed by visiting a store or contacting customer care and requesting a price match. Programs change every year, so no retailer is promised to repeat that offer in 2026 — the durable lesson is to check, before buying early, whether the retailer offers any price guarantee on holiday purchases and how long the holiday return window runs. An early price backed by a guarantee is the best of both timelines: the item is secured, and the Black Friday price is still yours if it materializes.

What success looks like

A successful early buy ends one of two ways: the price never drops further and the item was in hand weeks early, or the price does drop and a guarantee or return window hands back the difference. A successful wait ends with the item still in stock at a better price — verified against the market, not against the retailer's discount banner. Any outcome where the decision was made by a countdown timer instead of the four tests is a loss, even if the price happened to cooperate.
