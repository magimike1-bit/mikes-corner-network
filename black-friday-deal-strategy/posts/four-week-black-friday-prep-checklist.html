Preparation

The 4-Week Black Friday Prep Checklist

The shoppers who win Black Friday decided what they wanted weeks ago. Here's how to get there, one calm week at a time.

Black Friday rewards the prepared and punishes the impulsive. Every year, the same thing happens: unprepared shoppers scroll through "deals," feel the urgency, and buy things that were never on their list — at prices that were never that special. Meanwhile, prepared shoppers open a short list, check the price against what they already know, and are done before the coffee cools.

The difference isn't luck or insider knowledge. It's a few hours of setup spread over four weeks. Here's the checklist.

Week 4 out: build the wishlist

This is the most important week, and the most boring one. Sit down and write an actual list of what you want to buy — specific items, not categories. "Noise-cancelling headphones" is a category. The exact model you researched and want is an item. Black Friday discounts apply to items, not vibes.

For each item, note three things:

  • The exact product (model, size, color — whatever identifies it precisely)
  • What it costs right now, at a couple of different retailers
  • Why you want it — one sentence, honest

That last point does real work. When you write down "replacing my cracked winter boots" versus "they look cool," the list starts sorting itself. The honest list is also shorter, which is the point: a short list is one you can actually execute on sale day.

Prep tip

Keep the wishlist somewhere you'll actually look at it — a phone note, a spreadsheet, a sticky note on the fridge. The format doesn't matter; visibility does.

Week 3 out: set the budget and the rules

A wishlist without a budget is a suggestion. Decide now, in the calm before the hype, what the season is worth to you: a total number for all Black Friday spending, and ideally a per-item ceiling.

The per-item ceiling is the quiet hero of this checklist. Before the sales start, write down the maximum you'll pay for each item on your list. On sale day, you just compare two numbers. No feelings, no "but it's such a good deal" — the number is the rule. You can break your own rule, but you have to look at it first.

Also decide your stance on categories now: gifts you still need to buy, things you've been delaying, and things that are simply not this year's problem. Saying "not this year" in advance is far easier than saying it while a countdown timer blinks at you.

Week 2 out: accounts, apps, and payment plumbing

Nothing kills a deal faster than fumbling with a password reset while a cart timer ticks. Week two is housekeeping:

  • Log in to every retailer you expect to buy from — confirm the password works now, not on the day
  • Check saved shipping addresses and make sure they're current
  • Check saved payment methods — expired card on file, and the checkout you don't want to lose, is gone
  • Make sure your email for order confirmations is one you actually check
  • If you use retailer apps, update them and log in once so sale-day notifications actually arrive

This is also the week to set up price tracking on your wishlist items. Bookmark the product pages, and use a price-tracking tool or a manual log to start watching where prices sit before the sale. A price you watched for two weeks is a price you understand; a price you see for the first time on Black Friday is just a number with a red sticker next to it.

The two-week rule: if you start watching an item's price two weeks out, you'll instantly recognize a genuine Black Friday drop versus a price that was raised in October and "cut" in November. That recognition is the single highest-value skill of the whole season.

Week 1 out: finalize alerts and your game plan

The final week is about reducing sale-day decisions to nearly zero. Decide:

  • Which day you'll actually shop. Many "Black Friday" deals go live days early, and some of the best prices appear the week before. Decide your window — and when you'll stop looking.
  • Door crasher or online? For each item, know whether it's an in-store limited-stock situation or an online drop. Don't plan to line up at dawn for something that's been available online all week.
  • Your cutoff time. Decide when you'll be done — Friday afternoon, Friday midnight, whenever. Open-ended shopping is how budgets die.
  • Notification settings. Turn on stock alerts for your short list at the retailers you chose. Turn off everything else. Sale season spams every inbox; only your list deserves a ping.

Then re-read your wishlist, your ceilings, and your budget one more time. Print them, screenshot them, keep them next to you on the day. When the hype is loudest, the list is the quietest voice — and it's the one that's right.

The one-line version

Wishlist in week four. Budget and ceilings in week three. Accounts and price tracking in week two. Game plan in week one. On Black Friday itself, your only job is comparing the price on the screen to the number on your list — and then getting back to your coffee.
