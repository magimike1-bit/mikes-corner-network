How to Track Prices Like a Pro

"40% off" means nothing without the backstory. Learn to read a price's history, spot fake discounts, and let patience do the work.

A Black Friday price is a story, and the sale sticker only shows you the last page. The shoppers who actually save money read the earlier chapters: what did this item cost in September? In October? Was it ever at this "sale" price before?

You don't need special tools or insider data for this. You need a habit and a little patience. Here's how price tracking works in practice.

The one concept that matters: the real price vs. the sticker price

Every product has two prices during sale season: the number on the screen, and the number it has actually been selling for. The gap between them is where the games live.

A genuine deal: the item sold at a steady price for weeks, and the sale price is clearly below that. A fake deal: the price crept up in the weeks before the sale, and the "discounted" price just brings it back to normal — or worse, barely moves it. The sticker says "save," but your wallet says otherwise.

Once you see this pattern a few times, you can't unsee it. That's the point. Price tracking is really just training your eye to ask one question: compared to what?

The low-tech method: the price log

Forget apps for a moment. The simplest price tracker is a note on your phone:

  • Write down the item (exact product, exact retailer)
  • Write down today's price and the date
  • Check once a week and add a new line
  • On Black Friday, compare — the sale price against your own history, not against the crossed-out number

This works because you only need to track the handful of items on your actual wishlist. Five items, four weeks, four lines each. Ten minutes total, and you'll know more about those prices than anyone staring at a banner ad.

Why manual works

Checking by hand forces you to look at the actual product page each week. You'll notice things an app won't tell you: the model changed, the retailer swapped to a refurbished listing, the "same" item has a different item number.

The easy upgrade: price-history tools

If you'd rather not maintain a log by hand, price-tracking tools do the watching for you. They record a product's price over time and show you a chart — you can literally see whether the Black Friday "deal" is a valley or just a bump on a plateau.

Most of these tools also let you set an alert: tell them the price you'd actually pay (your ceiling from the prep checklist), and they notify you when the item hits it — during Black Friday or any other time of year. This is patience, automated. You stop checking and let the number come to you.

One caution: price-history tools track specific listings at specific retailers. Two retailers can sell what looks like the same product at wildly different "regular" prices. Compare within one listing's history, and compare across retailers separately. Don't mix the two.

How to spot a fake discount in 30 seconds

On sale day you won't have time for deep analysis, so train these three quick checks:

  1. The crossed-out price looks unfamiliar. If you've been watching the item and never once saw it at the "was" price, that's not a discount — that's a story the sticker is telling.
  2. The discount is identical everywhere. When every retailer suddenly shows the same percentage off, it's usually the manufacturer setting a promotional price — which can be a fine deal, but it's not the retailer cutting you a special break.
  3. The price history is a hill, not a valley. If the price rose in the last month and the "sale" just returns it to the old normal, you found the oldest trick in the season.

None of this requires you to be cynical about everything. Plenty of Black Friday prices are real — prices genuinely drop on tons of items in November. The tracking just lets you tell the real ones from the theater.

Patience is the actual strategy

Here's the part nobody puts on a banner: the best price on many items doesn't appear on Black Friday at all. It appears weeks later, when unsold stock needs clearing — or months later, when the next model arrives. Price tracking doesn't end in November. It turns you into someone who buys things at their price instead of at the calendar's price.

The shoppers who track prices have a superpower most people don't: the ability to say "not yet" and mean it, because they know the number will come around again. Black Friday is one chapter. The patient buy whenever the real deal shows up.

The 30-second rule for sale day: before you click buy on any "deal," ask — what did this cost two weeks ago? If you can't answer, it's not your deal yet. Check your log, check the history, and then decide.
