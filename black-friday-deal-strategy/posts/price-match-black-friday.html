Black Friday Price Matching: Which Stores Do It in 2026

Target ended competitor matching. Best Buy carves out the whole Thanksgiving week. And the internet's most-shared policy list is about four years old. Here's the current map — and how to actually get a match.

Price matching is the quietest Black Friday tactic there is: you find the lowest advertised price, walk it into the store you prefer, and leave with the item and your store credit intact. The problem is that most of the advice about it is out of date. Policies that were generous in 2022 have been narrowed since, and the guide everyone links to still says otherwise. A match that worked two years ago can get you a polite no at the register this November.

The 2026 landscape: who matches and who does not

Here is where the major retailers stand as of October 2026. The pattern is clear: more stores match their own prices than ever, and fewer match competitors' prices than ever.

Two different policies, one confusing name

Most of the disappointment at the register comes from mixing up two different policies that both get called "price matching":

  1. Competitor matching — the store honors another retailer's advertised price. This is the policy with all the Black Friday exclusions. Best Buy's entire Thanksgiving-week carve-out lives here.
  2. Own-price adjustment — the store you bought from drops its own price and refunds you the difference within the return window. This is still broadly available, and it is the more reliable Black Friday tactic: buy early at a fair price, track the store's own price, and ask for the difference if it falls.

When someone says "the store price-matches," ask which of the two they mean. The answer determines your whole strategy.

The ask procedure: how to request a match

Follow these steps in order. Each one exists because skipping it is how matches die at the register:

  1. Decide which policy you are asking about. If you want a competitor's price honored, confirm that store still does competitor matching — Target no longer does, Walmart suspends it for Black Friday items, and Best Buy excludes Thanksgiving week.
  2. Prove the exact item. Same model number, same configuration, same seller (not a third-party marketplace seller — most policies exclude marketplaces, third-party sellers, and deal sites). Screenshot the competitor's ad or product page showing the price, item number, and seller name.
  3. Call the store ahead of time. Confirm they honor matching for Black Friday-week deals and ask about that store's restrictions. A two-minute call beats a twenty-minute argument at customer service.
  4. Ask at customer service, not the register. Matching decisions usually live at customer service or with a manager. Bring your proof, your receipt if you already bought, and patience.
  5. If the answer is no, ask the second question. "Do you do an own-price adjustment if your price drops within the return period?" This often succeeds where the competitor match failed — and it is the question most shoppers never ask.

What success looks like

A successful run ends one of two ways: you leave with the item at the lower price (with the adjustment noted on your receipt), or you have a clear written answer on the store's policy — an own-price adjustment window you can use later. If you leave with neither, the match attempt failed; re-checking price history and buying at the genuinely lowest seller beats another round at the desk.
