Is this Black Friday gaming deal real? The 5-minute verification

Deal strategy · 6 min read

Some retailers raise prices in the weeks before Black Friday, then "discount" them back to normal — and gaming gear is prime territory for it. One consumer-group study tracked 175 Black Friday deals across a full year and found every single one was the same price or cheaper at some other point. And 36% of Black Friday 2025 sale items offered no savings at all over their prices a few weeks earlier. The fix isn't cynicism — it's a 5-minute verification drill you run before you buy.

The drill, in order

  1. Check the price history. Paste the product into a price tracker — CamelCamelCamel and Slickdeals are the usual ones for Amazon and deal-site listings, and Chrome's Shopping Insights shows price history right in the browser. You're looking for one thing: what did this exact item actually sell for in September and October? If the "was" price barely existed, neither does the discount.
  2. Compare the model number letter by letter. This is the gaming-specific trick. Some electronics sold during big sales are special Black Friday models: nearly identical to the well-known version but with a slightly different model number and fewer features — fewer ports, a lesser panel, less storage. TVs and monitors are the classic targets, but it shows up in gaming peripherals too. If you can't find reviews or a price history for that exact model number, treat the deal with extra caution — you're comparing against a phantom.
  3. Price the real final number at two other stores. Ignore the percentage. Compare the final price — with shipping and taxes — at two other retailers. A "40% off" that lands at the same final price everyone else charges all year is not a deal.
  4. Cart it and walk away. Put the item in your cart, close the tab, come back tomorrow. Genuine deals survive a night; manufactured urgency doesn't. This one test kills more fake deals than any tool.
  5. Check the return window before paying. Several retailers run Black Friday price guarantees with different date ranges — Best Buy's Holiday Price Match Guarantee, Newegg's Black Friday Price Protection, and Target's Price Match Guarantee all exist but cover different windows. Know which one covers your purchase, and note that holiday return periods are extended anyway: Amazon's currently stretches through January 31, 2026.

Gaming-specific red flags

  • The special-edition SKU. That "exclusive" gaming monitor or headset with a model number one letter off from the reviewed version. Compare the full model number, feature list, and panel type against the standard model's reviews.
  • The padded console bundle. A console packed with accessories can look like huge value — until you price out only the pieces you'd actually use. The bundle is a deal only if the main item alone is priced well; the extras are usually cheap add-ons inflating the "total value" figure.
  • The inflated peripheral was-price. Gaming mice, keyboards, and headsets are notorious for was-prices nobody ever paid. This is exactly what step 1 exists for.
  • The same price everywhere, different "regular" prices. If every store sells the item at the same price but each claims a different original price, the "discounts" are fiction.
  • Resetting urgency. A countdown timer or "only 3 left" warning that resets when you reload the page is theater, not inventory.

The verdict framework

After the drill, the deal falls into one of three tiers:

  • Buy: price history confirms a genuine low, the model number matches the reviewed product, and the final price beats two other stores. Buy it — and screenshot the listing in case a price guarantee applies later.
  • Verify: one check failed or was inconclusive — no price history on the exact model number, or the final price only ties other stores. Walk through the drill again on a different day, or ask the retailer directly whether the SKU matches the standard model.
  • Walk away: the was-price was invented, the model number is a phantom, or the urgency is fake. A walk-away isn't a loss — the same item will almost certainly be the same price (or cheaper) in January.

Our take: Shop Black Friday from a list, with the price you'd happily pay already written down. The drill takes five minutes; the fake deal takes your money in seconds. When in doubt, the cart-and-walk-away test is the single most reliable tool you own.

FAQ

Do retailers really raise prices before Black Friday? Some do — prices have been observed rising in the weeks before the event so the "discount" looks larger. That's the entire reason step 1 exists.

What if a deal looks too good to be true? Flashy social-media ads for deep discounts often lead to fake stores or knockoffs. Skip the link, go to the retailer's site yourself, and run the drill there.

Can I get the difference back if the price drops after I buy? Often, yes — the retailer price guarantees listed in step 5 cover Black Friday windows with different date ranges, and many stores will price-match their own lower price within the return period. Keep your receipt and the listing screenshot.
