microSD Cards in Budget Phones: A1 vs A2, U1 vs U3, and the Setup Choice That Matters

Buying criteria · 6 min read · Published 2026-11-14 · Last updated 2026-11-14

Can a five dollar difference in a memory card decide whether a budget phone feels fast or broken? Yes, and the reason is printed on the card in a code most buyers never decode. A microSD card carries two different speed ratings because a phone asks it to do two different jobs: bulk storage, where big files move in long streams, and app storage, where the system makes thousands of tiny reads and writes. The letter and number pairs on the package, A1, A2, U1, U3, certify different jobs. Buying the wrong one is invisible in the camera and painfully visible everywhere else.

The two rating families, decoded

The SD Association publishes both families, and Kingston's naming guide lays them out in one place.

Application Performance Class, the A rating, governs random access. A1 guarantees 1,500 random read IOPS, 500 random write IOPS and 10 MB/s sustained sequential speed. A2 doubles down: 4,000 random read IOPS, 2,000 random write IOPS and the same 10 MB/s sustained floor. Random access is the workload of an operating system and its apps: small files, constant lookups, scattered writes. A card with a strong sequential figure and no A rating is a card designed to be written by a camera, not run by a phone.

Speed Class and UHS Speed Class govern sequential transfer. The older Speed Class marks its floor directly, Class 10 meaning 10 MB/s. The UHS classes compress the same idea: U1 certifies a 10 MB/s minimum sequential write, U3 certifies 30 MB/s. Those floors decide video behaviour. The 30 MB/s floor of U3 is the one 4K recording asks for; U1's floor suits HD video and bulk photo storage.

One interface fact bounds both families in phones. The UHS I bus tops out at roughly 104 MB/s, and nearly every phone card slot is UHS I. A faster rated UHS II card still works, but it works at the slot's speed. Paying for interface headroom the slot cannot use is the card equivalent of buying a 100W charger for a 25W phone.

Portable or adoptable: the format choice that decides what the card is for

Android offers two ways to use a card, and the choice is made in settings at setup. Portable storage keeps the card as removable bulk storage: photos, video, music and documents live there, the card can be pulled and read in another device, and apps stay on the phone's internal storage. Adoptable storage formats the card as an extension of internal storage, so apps and their data can live on it; the card is then bound to that phone and is not readable elsewhere.

The decision framework is direct. Choose portable by default: it survives a phone change, it keeps the failure of a cheap card from taking apps with it, and it covers the use most buyers actually have, which is photo and video room. Choose adoptable only when the phone's internal storage is genuinely too small to hold the buyer's apps, and then only with an A2 rated card, because the apps will live or die by the card's random access figures. On either path, format a new card in the phone before loading it, and treat any card that arrives preloaded with files as untrusted.

Matching the card to the job, and setting it up

  1. Confirm the slot exists and its type. Dedicated on the Moto G Power (2025), hybrid on the A17 5G, absent on the Pixel 9a.
  2. Buy the rating the job needs, from a reputable seller. Counterfeit cards misreport their capacity, and a card that lies about its size corrupts data silently; price gaps far below market are the tell.
  3. Insert the card and format it in the phone. Choose portable or adoptable at this point, with the framework above.
  4. Point the camera at the card in the camera app's storage setting, so the internal storage stops filling with the bulkiest files.
  5. Keep the only copy of nothing on the card. Cards fail without asking permission; the photos worth keeping belong in a backup as well.

What success looks like: the camera saves to the card without stutter, video records at the phone's full resolution without dropped recording errors, and apps, if any were moved, open at close to their internal storage speed. A card that delivers all three was bought by rating, not by the biggest capacity number in the bin.
