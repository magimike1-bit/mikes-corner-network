Carrier deals vs. unlocked phones

Buying strategy · 5 min read

"Get this phone FREE!" is the most expensive sentence in wireless. Carrier deals can be genuinely good value — or cleverly disguised lock-in. The difference is always in the fine print, and learning to read it takes about ten minutes. Here's how to think about it.

How carrier phone deals actually work

Most carrier deals today aren't discounts on the phone so much as financing arrangements dressed up as promotions. The common structures:

  • Installment plans. The phone's price is split across monthly payments added to your bill, typically over one to three years. You own nothing extra — you're just paying over time.
  • Bill credits. The carrier advertises a discount, then applies it as small monthly credits spread across the agreement term. Leave early and the remaining credits vanish — you pay the difference.
  • Trade-in offers. Your old phone's value is applied as credit, sometimes inflated well beyond its actual worth — but usually also spread over the term as bill credits.
  • "Free" with a new line or plan. The phone costs nothing upfront but requires a specific, usually pricier, plan for the full term.

None of these are scams. They're contracts. The question is always whether the total cost and the commitment are worth it to you.

Reading the fine print: what to look for

  • The agreement length. How many months are you committing to? Longer terms mean longer lock-in for the same phone.
  • What happens if you leave early. This is the big one. Do you owe the remaining device balance? Do unapplied bill credits disappear? Get the early-exit math in writing before you sign.
  • Which plan is required. Many deals require a plan tier above the cheapest. A "free" phone on a plan that costs more per month than you'd otherwise pay isn't free — the premium is the price.
  • Whether the phone is locked. Carrier-sold phones may be locked to that carrier for a period. Ask when and how it can be unlocked.
  • Who owns the phone if it's lost or broken. You're still on the hook for the remaining balance even if the phone is gone — consider whether insurance or a case is part of your real cost.

Total-cost thinking

The only honest way to compare is total cost over the same period. Add up everything you'd pay with the carrier deal — monthly plan charges plus any device payments, over the full agreement term — and compare it against buying an unlocked phone outright plus a cheaper plan or prepaid service over the same months. Don't compare monthly payments to the phone's sticker price; compare total dollars out of your pocket over the whole term. Often the carrier deal wins on convenience and sometimes on price. Often it doesn't — especially if the required plan is more than you need.

Carrier deals: when they make sense

  • You're staying with that carrier anyway and the required plan fits your usage
  • Bill credits genuinely reduce what you'd already be paying
  • You prefer spreading the cost monthly with no upfront hit
  • Trade-in values are genuinely generous for your old device

Unlocked: when it wins

  • You want freedom to switch carriers for a better rate anytime
  • You're happy with a cheaper plan or prepaid service
  • You travel and need to use local SIMs or eSIMs abroad
  • You keep phones for years — no contract clock ticking

Questions to ask before signing anything

Deal checklist

  • What is my total out-of-pocket cost over the full term, plan plus device?
  • What exactly do I owe if I leave or switch carriers early?
  • Is the discount a lump sum or monthly bill credits I'd lose by leaving?
  • Could I buy this phone unlocked and pair it with a cheaper plan for less total?
  • When and how can the phone be unlocked?
  • Am I buying the phone, or buying the feeling of a deal?

Our take: Treat every carrier phone offer as a financing contract first and a deal second. Run the total-cost math over the full term against the unlocked-plus-cheap-plan alternative — it takes ten minutes and it's the only comparison that matters. If the carrier deal still wins and you're happy staying put, take it with confidence. If the math is murky, that's your answer: buy unlocked and keep your freedom.
