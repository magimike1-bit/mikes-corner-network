Warranty vs. protection plan: what the first year already covers

The two products sound alike and cover different things. A manufacturer's warranty covers defects caused by faulty materials or workmanship. A device protection plan expands coverage to accidental damage and, in many cases, theft and loss. TELUS states the boundary plainly on its own page: Device Care Products do not cover defects covered by the one-year manufacturer warranty.

So in year one, a monthly plan buys you exactly one thing the free warranty does not: accidents (and, where included, loss or theft). The defect protection the salesperson gestures at is already in the box, free, for twelve months. From month thirteen the plan's extended-warranty side starts doing work — on a phone whose replacement price is falling the whole time.

The published guidance agrees with the band this site covers. WhistleOut's Canadian guide frames a protection plan as generally worth considering when a phone costs more than $1,000 and will be kept more than two years, and notes that for a cheaper model it may be more advantageous over time to purchase a new device instead. A sub-$300 phone is the case that framing was written about — from the other side.

A worked example, with the assumptions on the table

Take the shelf anchor: a Galaxy A17 5G at $339.99, kept two years, on TELUS Device Care Complete at $16.50 a month. Three futures:

No claim. Premiums paid: $396. Value received: $0. The plan cost $56.01 more than the phone did.

One cracked screen at month 12. Premiums to that point: $198. Plus the $39 damaged screen repair fee: $237 total for a covered repair. That is a real saving only if the uncovered repair would have cost more than $237 — a figure worth asking for an actual quote on before assuming, because it is the one number nobody at the till volunteers.

The one-time alternative. Samsung Care+ in Canada is priced by device tier at $119, $199 or $249 one-time, plus tax. Even the top tier undercuts two years of the TELUS monthly plan by $147, and the $119 tier costs less than eight months of it. If a household has decided it wants coverage on a Samsung, the one-time structure is the cheaper shape — the comparison is the plan's own published pricing, not a negotiation.

None of these futures involves invented repair quotes or assumed accident rates. They are the published prices, multiplied out. That is deliberate: at this phone price, the published prices alone carry the argument.

How to decide at the till, in five steps

  1. Multiply the monthly price by the months you will keep the phone. Write the total down. If it exceeds the phone's replacement price — $339.99 for the anchor phone here — the default answer is no, and the burden of proof moves to the plan.
  2. Add one claim fee. Premiums are not the cost of a claim; the service or processing fee rides on top ($39 for a TELUS screen repair; $39 to $400 across Rogers tiers). Premiums-plus-one-fee is the honest price of the protection actually being used once.
  3. Ask what the uncovered repair costs. Get a real quote for a screen replacement on your exact model. The plan only wins the one-claim future when that quote beats your step-2 total.
  4. Price the one-time version. Manufacturer plans such as Samsung Care+ publish one-time tier prices. Compare the one-time total against the monthly plan's total over the same window before choosing a shape.
  5. Check the clock before you leave. Enrolment windows are short — 60 days from purchase at TELUS, and up to 60 days in the Rogers program — so a decision deferred is usually a decision made. Know which day your window closes.

What success looks like: you can state, in dollars, the plan's total cost over your keeping window, the fee on one claim, and the uncovered repair quote — before anyone asks for your card.

Traps and fine print

The stale comparison price. A widely read Canadian carrier comparison lists TELUS Device Care Complete at $15 a month. TELUS's own page currently prices it at $16.50. Over 24 months that gap is $36 — small, and exactly the kind of small that monthly billing is designed to hide. Always price a plan from the carrier's own current page.

The already-damaged exclusion. Both TELUS and Rogers require the device to be undamaged and fully operational at enrolment; problems that existed before enrolment are not covered. A plan cannot be bought after the drop that motivated it.

The window that varies. Many Canadian plans require sign-up within 30 days of purchase, while TELUS and Rogers allow 60. A buyer who assumes the longer window on a 30-day plan discovers the difference after it closes.

Fees before taxes. Rogers states its fees are subject to applicable taxes, and Samsung's tier prices are plus tax. The real totals are a tax line higher than the headline figures in the table.

When it is NOT worth it — and the self-insure alternative

It is not worth it when the two-year premium total meets or beats the replacement price, which is the normal case in this band. It is also a poor fit for a careful owner whose realistic claim is a cracked screen: premiums plus the claim fee approach the cost of simply paying for the repair, with the quote check in step 3 as the arbiter. The honest exception is loss and theft anxiety on a phone a household genuinely cannot replace in one payment this month — that is a cash-flow argument, not a value argument, and it should be made with the $396 figure in view, not the $16.50 one.

The alternative is unglamorous and effective: self-insure. Set aside the same $16.50 a month in a separate account when the phone is bought. After 12 months the fund holds $198 — enough to cover most single repairs on a budget phone or a large share of a replacement; after 24 months it holds the full $396, more than the anchor phone costs new. If nothing breaks, the money is still yours, which is the one outcome no plan on this page offers. Pair it with a case — the cheapest claim is the one that never happens — and a budget phone carries its own insurance at a profit.

Our take: On a phone that costs $339.99 to replace, a $16.50-a-month plan is a $396 answer to a $339.99 problem. The free warranty already covers defects for year one; what a plan adds is accidents and loss, priced here at more than the device. Run the five steps, price the one-time Samsung Care+ tiers if you want coverage on a Galaxy, and otherwise put the premium in your own account — after two years of nothing breaking, it is still there.
