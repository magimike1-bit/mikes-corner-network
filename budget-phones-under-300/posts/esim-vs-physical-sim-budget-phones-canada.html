eSIM vs. physical SIM on budget phones in Canada: which models actually have eSIM?

Connectivity · 7 min read

Ask a search engine whether a budget phone supports eSIM and the answers contradict each other — sometimes on two listings for the same phone, sold by the same retailer. That is not a search problem. It is a variant problem: manufacturers ship the same model name in several SIM configurations, and which one reaches a Canadian shelf depends on the exact model number. This guide verifies what the listings, carrier pages, and manufacturer documentation actually say for the current budget shortlist, then gives the one check that settles the question for the specific phone in front of you.

The short answer: check the model number, not the brand

There is no brand-level rule. Samsung's own setup documentation for the Galaxy A17 5G states that the device supports dual SIM functionality, including a physical SIM and an eSIM, and describes the eSIM as embedded directly into the device so two separate SIMs can be used at the same time. Yet a Best Buy Canada Marketplace listing for a Galaxy A17 5G with model number SM-A176B states, in plain text, "Not Compatible with eSIM." Both statements can be true at once, because they describe different variants of the same phone. The same pattern shows up one model down: global specification listings for the Galaxy A16 5G include Nano-SIM + eSIM variants, while Canada Computers lists the Canadian model SM-A166W with a SIM type of Nano-SIM only. If a listing does not name the model number, it has not answered the eSIM question.

One Canadian rule does work in the buyer's favour. The CRTC announced that as of December 1, 2017, all newly purchased devices in Canada must be provided unlocked. An unlocked phone does not guarantee eSIM hardware, but it does mean that if the hardware is present, no carrier lock stands between the buyer and activating a second line on it.

Carrier support adds a second filter. Public Mobile's eSIM compatibility guidance lists iPhone XS and newer, Google Pixel 4a and newer, Samsung Galaxy S20 and newer flagships, and the Motorola Razr 5G as compatible examples — Galaxy A-series models do not appear in that list. That does not prove a given A-series phone fails; it proves the carrier's published examples were written around flagships. Samsung itself adds the matching caveat that eSIM activation varies by carrier. Hardware support and carrier support are separate questions, and a budget buyer needs a yes to both.

The 30-second check that settles it: the EID test

Spec sheets argue. The phone itself does not. Before ordering any eSIM plan, or before paying for a phone bought partly for its eSIM support, run this check on the actual handset:

  1. Open the phone dialler and type *#06#. It runs immediately, with no call placed.
  2. Look at the identifiers shown. If an EID appears alongside the IMEI, the handset has eSIM hardware. If only an IMEI appears, it does not — and no carrier, plan, or software update can add it later.
  3. Cross-check in Settings. On Android, look under Network & Internet > SIMs (on Samsung, Connections > SIM Manager) for an "Add eSIM" option. Its presence confirms the hardware path; its absence confirms the *#06# result.
  4. Only then order the plan. eSIM activation needs a Wi-Fi connection to download the profile and the carrier's QR code or app flow, so set it up somewhere with reliable Wi-Fi rather than at an airport or a trailhead.

Success looks like this: an EID on the *#06# screen, an "Add eSIM" entry in Settings, and a carrier whose published compatibility covers the model. Two out of three is a reason to call the carrier before money changes hands, not after.

eSIM vs. physical SIM: the practical trade-offs in Canada

Where eSIM wins. A second line without a second phone: one physical SIM plus one eSIM means a personal number and a work number, or a home plan and a travel data plan, in a single handset. Activation is a download rather than a store visit or a mailed card, which is genuinely useful when a plan needs to be live the same day. And nothing physical can be lost, bent, or left in the old phone at trade-in time.

Where a physical SIM still wins. Swapping is instant and carrier-free: move the card, and calls, texts, and data follow in seconds — including into a borrowed or backup phone when the main one breaks. That matters more than it sounds like in the budget segment, where a cracked screen or a dead battery is a real possibility and a spare handset is a common backup plan. A physical SIM also never depends on a carrier's app, account login, or QR-code process working correctly on a stressful day.

The honest middle. For a single-line user who rarely travels and keeps a phone for years, eSIM support is a nice-to-have, not a reason to pay more or to reject a phone that lacks it. The Galaxy A16 5G's Canadian variant being physical-only is a limitation for two-line users and frequent travellers, and a non-issue for everyone else. Features should be weighted by the life they will actually be used in.

Moving to a new phone: where eSIM gets annoying

The physical SIM's best day is upgrade day, and the eSIM's worst day is the same one. Carrier practices differ sharply. Bell, Rogers, and TELUS and their flanker brands support transferring an eSIM or issuing a replacement QR code through their account tools. Public Mobile, by contrast, is listed as not yet supporting eSIM transfer: moving to a new device requires a new eSIM activation for that device. None of this is difficult, exactly — it is a download and a QR scan — but it adds steps, an account login, and a working Wi-Fi connection to a day that used to involve moving one small card. Budget buyers who upgrade through discount carriers should expect the new-activation route and keep account credentials reachable during the switch. Anyone who hands a phone down to a family member should also remember that an eSIM profile stays with the account, not the handset: remove the profile before the phone leaves, the same way a physical card would be taken out.

Which setup fits you: a decision framework

Match the SIM setup to the use case

  • One line, light travel, long ownership: physical SIM only is fine. Spend the money on storage or battery instead of chasing eSIM support.
  • Work and personal numbers on one phone: require a verified physical + eSIM model — and verify the exact model number, because the same model name ships both ways.
  • Frequent cross-border travel: eSIM support earns its keep for travel data plans, provided the carrier at home publishes compatibility for the model.
  • Phone doubles as the family backup: favour a physical tray. A card anyone can move beats a profile only the account holder can re-issue.
  • Buying imported or Marketplace stock: run the EID check on delivery, inside the return window. Listings for grey-market variants are where the contradictions live.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Samsung Galaxy A17 5G support eSIM in Canada?

It depends on the variant. Best Buy Canada lists its unlocked A17 5G as Single SIM + eSIM (Hybrid) and Samsung's setup documentation describes dual SIM with a physical SIM and an eSIM, while a Marketplace listing for model SM-A176B states it is not compatible with eSIM. Check the model number, then confirm with the *#06# EID test.

Can a carrier add eSIM to a phone that lacks it?

No. eSIM is hardware — an embedded chip identified by the EID. If the *#06# screen shows no EID, there is nothing for a carrier to activate. Carrier support is the second requirement, not a substitute for the first.

Is a travel eSIM a good reason to upgrade a budget phone?

Only if travel is frequent. For one trip, a physical local SIM or a roaming add-on on an existing plan usually costs less than replacing a working phone. For several trips a year, verified eSIM support starts paying for itself in convenience.

Our take: Under $300, eSIM is a variant lottery, not a brand feature. The verified Canadian listings show the Galaxy A17 5G sold both with and without eSIM depending on model number, the Canadian Galaxy A16 5G listed as Nano-SIM only, and the Moto G (2025) listed with one physical SIM plus one eSIM. Run the EID check before ordering any eSIM plan, weight the feature by how many lines and trips it will actually serve, and never let it outrank battery, storage, or update support in the budget.
