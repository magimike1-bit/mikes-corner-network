Wireless charging on a budget phone: the short Canadian list, and what the pad adds

General information only, not financial advice. Prices checked October 31, 2026 and change often; confirm the current price and the exact model variant before you buy.

CA$454.99. That is the honest Canadian total for wireless charging on a current budget-class phone today: CA$410 for the one phone in reach of this site's band that actually has the feature, plus $44.99 for a pad to charge it on — because the pad is never in the box. US listicles quote the same phone at US$199.99 on sale and call wireless charging a sub-$300 feature. In Canadian dollars, at Canadian listings, it is not. The rest of this guide is the working behind that number, so you can redo it at whatever prices you find.

Which phones actually have it, verified

Motorola Moto G Power (2025) — yes, 15W. Motorola's launch specifications list a 5,000mAh battery with 30W wired charging and 15W wireless charging, and the model carries IP68 and IP69 ratings. It launched in the US at US$299.99 unlocked, and Best Buy US lists it at US$199.99 on sale. The Canadian outright listing found for this comparison is CA$410 through Freedom Mobile — the exchange rate and the Canadian shelf, not a typo, are what move this phone out of the under-$300 band its US coverage puts it in.

Google Pixel 9a — yes, 7.5W, and above the band. Videotron's published technical specifications for the Pixel 9a list wireless charging at 7.5W alongside 23W wired charging — and no charger in the box. It is the reference point, not the recommendation: a Canadian buyer pays step-up money for the slowest wireless rate in this guide.

Samsung Galaxy A17 5G — no. Best Buy Canada's listing for the phone that anchors this site's shelf documents wired super-fast charging over Type-C, and its box contents are a data cable, an ejection pin and a quick start guide. There is no wireless charging in the listing, because the phone does not offer it. A buyer who assumes the feature is standard at this price will discover otherwise at the first pad they borrow.

That is the whole verified list for phones in or near the band. Listicles pad their roundups with models credited with wireless charging on no manufacturer source — the Moto G Stylus line is the recurring example — and with refurbished flagships, which are a different purchase with a different warranty story. If a model is not on Motorola's, Google's or a carrier's own spec page with the feature named, treat the claim as unproven.

What the pad adds, and the Qi2 trap

The phone price is only the first half of the feature. Motorola's box for the Moto G Power (2025) contains a USB cable and a SIM tool — no charger of any kind. The Pixel 9a likewise ships without one. So the pad is a mandatory second purchase: Belkin's Canadian storefront prices wireless charging stands and stations from C$34, with a 15W stand at $44.99, and a household that wants a pad at the bedside and the desk buys it twice.

Then there is the standards trap. The Pixel 9a is Qi-certified but does not support Qi2 — the Wireless Power Consortium standard built on Apple's MagSafe approach, which adds a ring of alignment magnets and speeds up to 15W. A Qi2 pad still charges a Qi phone; the standards are backward compatible. It just charges it at the phone's own rate — 7.5W for the 9a — so the magnetic alignment and speed on the pad's box deliver nothing extra to a phone without magnets of its own. Paying a Qi2 premium for a Qi-only phone is buying a feature twice and receiving it once. The Moto G Power's 15W Qi rate, by contrast, can actually use a 15W pad's full output.

One more cost hides in the speed column. Wireless at 7.5W or 15W is slower than the wired rates these same phones carry — 23W and 30W respectively. The pad is a convenience for overnight and desk charging, not a faster way to fill a battery before heading out.

When it is worth it — and when it is not

Wireless charging earns its $115 Canadian premium in a narrow, honest set of cases: a worn or damaged USB-C port on a phone you are keeping, a bedside routine where the phone goes down in the dark every night, or a shared household pad that several Qi devices already use. In those cases the Moto G Power route is the only current one that stays anywhere near this site's territory, and its 15W rate is the real thing.

Skip it if the appeal is the listicle headline. A buyer whose charging is a cable at the desk and a cable in the car gains a slower charge, a $44.99 stand, and a phone that cost CA$70 more than the shelf anchor to begin with. The Galaxy A17 5G at $339.99 with the cable in its box is the better household arithmetic for most people — and the $115 saved covers a case, a spare cable for every room, and money left over.

Our take: In Canada on October 31, 2026, wireless charging on a budget phone is a CA$454.99 proposition — CA$410 for the Moto G Power (2025) plus $44.99 for the pad its box leaves out. The Galaxy A17 5G does not have the feature at any price, and the Pixel 9a charges wirelessly at half the Motorola's speed from above the band. Buy the feature for a routine that uses it nightly, not for a spec-sheet line.
