A November Christmas budget countdown for Canada: what to buy, book, and decide each week

Seasonal guide · Published 3 November 2026 · Last updated 3 November 2026

$900 is the example total used throughout this countdown, and none of it is meant to land in one week. Spread across the eight blocks from Tuesday, November 3 to Thursday, December 24, the biggest single block is $180 and the last one is $70. That staging is the whole idea. A Christmas budget usually fails on timing, not on the total: everything gets bought in the same three December weeks, on the same paycheques that also carry December bills. A countdown fixes the timing by giving each block one job, one decision deadline, and a cap.

The countdown at a glance

The caps below are a labelled example built on a $900 total. Keep the proportions and swap in your own number. The running total column is the check that matters: at the end of any block, money spent so far should be at or under it.

Set the total the way the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada sets any budget: list income, savings and expenses from pay stubs, bills and statements, then review what is left. Its free Budget Planner holds the numbers and adds tips as you go. The Christmas total is whatever survives that listing honestly, not a number borrowed from last year.

Weeks one to three: decide, order long lead items, stock the supplies

November 3 to 8 is decisions, not shopping. Write the total, the gift list with a per person cap beside each name, and the hosting answer: who cooks, who travels, who sleeps where. The $60 in this block covers cards and stamps ordered early and nothing else. A decision made this week costs paper. The same decision made at a checkout in December costs whatever the store asks.

November 9 to 15 is for anything with a lead time. Personalized and handmade gifts, anything shipping from far away, and the gifts most likely to sell out go now, inside a $120 cap. Book travel in this block too, while the booking is a price comparison and not a rescue. If a long lead item is already out of stock, this is the week with time to pick the substitute calmly.

November 16 to 22 is one supplies trip. Wrapping paper, tape, tags, batteries, and the pantry staples the December menu needs, bought in a single pass for $90 or less. Buying supplies before the gifts arrive means wrapping happens as parcels land, and the true spent figure stays visible instead of hiding in a pile of unwrapped boxes.

Week four: the Black Friday decision rule

November 23 to 29 holds the biggest cap, $180, and the only sale day in the countdown: Black Friday falls on Friday, November 27, 2026. The rule for the whole week is short. A sale price only counts on an item that was already on the written list, at or under its per person cap, bought with money from this block. A discount on an unlisted item is not a saving, it is a new expense wearing a percentage sign.

Two practical notes. First, track the regular price of listed items during the November 16 block, so a claimed sale price can be checked against a real earlier price instead of a store banner. Second, anything this week can be bought online at the same price usually stays online: the cap is for gifts, not for parking, food court lunches, and the extras a mall trip attaches.

Weeks five to seven: ship, finish gifts, buy food in stages

November 30 to December 6, cap $110: finish every gift that has to travel, and mail the cards. Canada Post publishes its holiday send by dates each year, and the card dates for 2026 are December 22 local, December 21 regional, and December 18 national. Cards written this week beat all three without paying for speed.

December 7 to 13, cap $140: this is shipping week, and it is not early paranoia. For a parcel crossing the country, Regular Parcel's send by date is Thursday, December 10, and the Expedited Parcel and flat rate box date is Monday, December 14. A national parcel posted inside this block travels at ground prices. The same parcel posted a week later travels at express prices, and the countdown budget never planned to pay them. Canada Post notes its dates apply between major urban centres and depend on origin and destination, so rural senders should treat this block as the real deadline, not the printed one.

December 14 to 20, cap $130: the first food stage, non-perishables, baking supplies, drinks, and any ticketed events still on the plan. Gifts should be finished by now. A gift still missing on December 15 gets the substitute chosen back in November, not a panic upgrade.

The final four days and the one rule that protects them

December 21 to 24 has a $70 cap and a single job: fresh food. Turkey timing, produce, cream, the one ingredient somebody forgot. Nothing new joins the list in this block, no extra guest gift, no just in case bottle, no decoration that caught an eye. The first seven blocks already made every decision those purchases would need, which is what makes the rule holdable.

If a week overspends: where the next week gives it back

It will happen in at least one block. The fix is mechanical, and it never comes out of the final food block. Take the overage from the nearest later block that holds the same kind of spending: a gift block overage comes out of the next gift block, a supplies overage comes out of food stage extras before it touches staples. Write the moved amount into the table the same day. FCAC's stick to it advice is the same habit in general form: keep the receipts, limit spending to what is in the budget, and compare the budget with actual spending regularly, adjusting figures that keep missing. A countdown is that comparison, scheduled eight times before it can become a January surprise.

What success looks like on December 24: spent so far equals the running total, the last block bought only fresh food, and January opens with a card balance that was planned in November.
