Christmas Gifts for Movie and Music Lovers in Canada

Gift ideas · Entertainment · Canada · ~6 min read

What can they actually play? Ask that before you spend a dollar. A record is a brilliant gift for someone with a turntable and a flat, unusable disc for someone without one. A film on a disc they cannot play fails the same way. The setup comes first, the gift second — and once the setup is clear, the Canadian listings below sort themselves into a short, honest sequence.

Step one in the sequence: name the setup

Three setups cover almost every movie and music lover, and each one points at a different gift family. This is a choosing framework, not a quiz — one honest answer from the recipient, a partner, or a quick look at their shelves settles it.

Notice what is missing from the first row's gift family: themed decor. A clock shaped like a record and a magnet shaped like a record depict music. They do not deliver any. That distinction — depicts versus delivers — is the whole guide in four words.

Step two: for the music lover with a turntable, records — priced

The strongest priced finds in the listings checked are vintage, and local. In the Ottawa/Gatineau listings on Kijiji, a lot of 7 vintage Christmas vinyl records was listed at $20, and individual vintage Christmas LPs in the same results ran from $5 to $25. All figures checked 2026-10-25.

Do the small maths on the lot: $20 for 7 records is under $3 a record before any negotiation, against $5 to $25 for a single LP bought one at a time. A lot suits a recipient building a Christmas shelf. A single, chosen LP suits a recipient who wants one specific album. Same format, different intention — pick by intention, not by the headline price.

Two cautions ride with secondhand listings, and both are process, not fine print. Listings like these are one-off: when the lot sells, that exact gift is gone, so a screenshot and a fast decision beat a saved link and a slow one. And condition on a vintage record is a question to ask the seller, not a fact a listing headline can settle for you.

Step three: for the movie lover, start from the same question

The movie side runs on the identical logic, which is why it shares a guide with the music side. A film gift only works in a format the recipient can watch. So the sequence repeats: name the viewing setup first, then choose.

Where the setup is a disc player, a physical film is the direct equivalent of the vinyl record above — an object in a playable format, chosen for one person. Where the setup is streaming only, the honest equivalent is an upgrade to the viewing they already do, not a disc that becomes a coaster with a cover.

No verified Canadian prices for specific films or viewing upgrades were captured in the listings checked for this guide, so none are quoted here. That gap is stated rather than filled: a price invented to complete a table would be worse than an empty row. Use the music-side prices as the budget shape — a thoughtful main gift in this territory does not need to clear $50 — and price the specific film or upgrade at the retailer, on the day.

Neither item is a con. Both become bad gifts only when they are bought under a false description — as a serious listening upgrade, or as a present for someone who wanted real vinyl for a real turntable.

Bought as what they are — a playful, displayable, Christmas-specific object for a fan who will enjoy the object itself — they are the right tier for the unknown-setup problem in step one. Note the practical detail on the second row: in-store only, per the listing, which rules it out as a ship-to-them gift.

FAQ

What if I do not know whether they own a turntable?
Then the setup question is unanswered, and format-locked gifts are a gamble. Ask someone who shares their home, or stay in the novelty tier, where the gift does not depend on equipment they may not have.

Is the $20 lot of 7 vintage Christmas records still available?
It was listed in the Ottawa/Gatineau results checked on 2026-10-25. Secondhand lots are single listings — check the live listing, and expect it to be gone once sold.

Why are there no prices for films in this guide?
No verified price for a specific film was captured in the listings checked, so none is quoted. Prices here come only from listings actually retrieved and dated — the same rule that produced the $5–$25 LP range and the two player prices above.
