Superhero dad jokes with capes, powers, and zero supervillain energy

Joke collections · 7 min read

Clean is a promise, and on superhero joke pages it is a broken one. One widely ranked Marvel page is labelled clean and suitable for kids and friends, yet its list includes gender-identity punchlines and a joke built on a hero’s religion, material no parent who trusted the label would choose for a seven-year-old. So this collection starts with an audit instead of a joke: every line below was checked against a simple test. Could it be read aloud, in full, to a young kid, with no edits and no awkward skip? If not, it stayed out. What survived is sorted the way the search results refuse to sort it. Marvel, Spider-Man on his own, DC, and capes that belong to no universe at all.

The cleanliness check: what clean actually means here

Three rules did the checking. First, no punches down: a joke whose laugh depends on who a hero is, rather than what a hero does, fails. Second, no double meanings a kid repeats innocently and an adult hears differently, that is the exact failure hiding inside several clean-labelled lists. Third, the hero test: the joke should still work if the hero were standing in the room being mildly embarrassed by it. Costumes, powers, gadgets, and terrible secret-identity maintenance are fair game. Identity is not.

The second finding of the audit is about balance. The top of the search results is a Marvel monopoly: the best-known kids’ list is Marvel-only, a 65-joke rival list is Marvel-only, and the one dedicated collection for younger kids covers a single hero, Spider-Man. DC, home of the two heroes most young kids meet first, has no dedicated clean list in the top results at all. The split below corrects that on purpose.

Marvel jokes that pass

Two documented favourites pass the audit cleanly and open the set, credited to the lists that carry them. Why was Thor avoiding Loki? He was Odin money. Who is Thor’s favourite singer? MC Hammer. And a kitchen one: what is always missing from Tony Stark’s kitchen? The Pepper Potts. Those three come from a kids’ Marvel list first published on June 12, 2019 and updated on December 2, 2022, old enough to be canon, clean enough to keep. The rest of this section is new material written to the same standard.

  1. Captain’s shield miss the team meeting? It was busy covering for everyone else.
  2. Banner’s shirt ask for a raise? It kept getting stretched beyond the job description.
  3. Hawkeye’s arrows apply for overtime? Every target was a bullseye emergency.
  4. Ant-Man’s lunchbox feel full? One grape, cut very, very small.
  5. Black Panther’s calendar stay organised? Every day was a royal engagement.
  6. Doctor’s cape get a promotion? It had a real flare for dramatic entrances.
  7. Wakanda’s doorbell ring twice? Visitors kept announcing themselves as friends of the crown.
  8. Quill’s mixtape skip track three? Even heroes have one song too many.

Spider-Man’s own section

Spider-Man earns a solo section because the search results give him one: a dedicated collection aimed at kids, parents, and teachers. Its two best-known lines pass the audit untouched. What does Peter Parker do for extra money? He works as a web developer. Why did Spider-Man do so well on his first driving test? He is a great parallel Parker. New lines in the same spirit follow, the jokes stay on the webs, the wall-crawling, and the neighbourhood, which is where this hero’s humour has always lived.

  1. Aunt’s porch light stay on all night? Somebody kept swinging past and waving.
  2. Web fluid run out at the worst moment? Even the friendly neighbourhood has stairs.
  3. Peter’s camera demand a raise? It did all the hanging around for the action shots.
  4. Daily commute feel short? Rooftops rarely have traffic lights.
  5. Homework land on the ceiling? Study habits changed after the field trip.
  6. Umbrella feel unemployed? Webs make a decent canopy in a light drizzle.

The DC set the lists forgot

Here is the section the top results skip. DC jokes for young kids have to be built, because no ranked list builds them: the DC material in the results is a list about adult jokes hidden in an animated series, which is precisely the wrong shelf for this page. So these are original lines, written against the same three audit rules, for the heroes a kid is most likely to meet first, the bat, the cape, the lasso, and the fastest feet in any school corridor.

  1. Bat-Signal get sent to voicemail? The commissioner was in a meeting about meetings.
  2. Superman’s glasses pass every inspection? Nobody has ever looked past them.
  3. Wonder’s lasso tell the truth about itself? It said the job was a real bind.
  4. Flash arrive early to his own surprise party? He had already eaten a slice and left.
  5. Aquaman’s phone bill stay low? Every call was a quick splash and gone.
  6. Gotham’s gargoyles unionise? They wanted better perches and fewer pigeons.
  7. Robin’s map stay unfolded? Shortcuts are hard when every route is a rooftop.
  8. Cyborg’s charger feel important? The whole team’s plans ran through it.

Generic capes and powers

Some kids love the idea of heroes more than any franchise, and some jokes are better with no logo attached. Generic cape jokes also travel: they work at a party where half the room knows one universe and half knows the other, and nobody has to explain a backstory before the punchline. Keep these for mixed rooms, classrooms, and any audience whose fandom has not been polled in advance.

  1. Cape get tangled in the revolving door? Heroic entrances take practice.
  2. Sidekick carry a spare mask? One sneeze can end a secret identity.
  3. Super-strength hero open the pickle jar last? The lid had already surrendered twice.
  4. X-ray vision fail the eye test? The chart was behind a lead wall.
  5. Invisible hero win hide-and-seek? The trophy presentation took a while to find.
  6. Flight suit need ironing? Wrinkles at altitude are still wrinkles.
  7. Hero’s boots squeak on patrol? Stealth is a skill, not a superpower.
  8. Alarm clock fear the speedster? It never stood a chance of being beaten.

Pick the universe first: a choosing framework

  1. Ask which heroes the kid actually knows. A perfect Batman joke dies in a room that only watches one franchise. Universe first, joke second.
  2. Default to generic in mixed rooms. Cape-and-power jokes need no backstory, which makes them the safe opener for parties and classrooms.
  3. Give Spider-Man his own moment. For younger kids he is often the one hero they know by name; the solo set exists for exactly that audience.
  4. Run the read-aloud test on any joke from elsewhere. If a line needs an edit, a skip, or a whispered explanation, it does not belong in a clean set.

Played out: retire these five

Every superhero list leans on the same furniture. Named here so they can be rested, not repeated as fresh material: the Brie cheese line for Captain Marvel and the wild Goose chase about her cat, both carried by the 65-joke list; the Fe-Male chemistry gag built on the iron symbol; the flerken explanation offered as a punchline; and the Stan Lee cameo line, how will you know you are in a Marvel movie? You are bound to bump into Stan Lee. None of these fail the cleanliness audit. They fail the freshness one: each has been told so often that a kid can finish it from the setup. A clean set that is also a new set is the whole point of this page, the audit keeps the first half honest, and this drawer keeps the second half honest too.
