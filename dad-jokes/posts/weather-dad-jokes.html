Weather dad jokes for every forecast, fair or foul

Weather · 7 min read

Weather is the one topic every listener has already checked today, which makes it perfect dad-joke material. Merriam-Webster defines a dad joke as a wholesome joke of the type said to be told by fathers, with a punchline that is often an obvious or predictable pun or play on words. The forecast is full of those double meanings: a degree is a temperature and a qualification, pressure is air and stress, a front is weather and a facade, and being under the weather is somehow both a forecast and a feeling. This collection is organized by what is happening outside the window, so the joke can match the day instead of fighting it.

The sincere part: a joke from this page is a small, clean reset for the day it lands in. Tell one, let the groan happen, and leave the rest of the set for another day — scarcity is what keeps the groan affectionate.

Why forecast words pun so well

A pun, in the standard definition, exploits multiple meanings of a term, or similar-sounding words, for humorous effect. Weather language does double duty all day long. People borrow it for moods — a sunny disposition, a frosty reception, a stormy meeting, feeling under the weather — so every forecast word arrives with its second meaning already installed. The joke does not have to build the double meaning; it only has to point at it.

Two of the most-punned words deserve a plain definition, because the jokes below depend on the difference. The National Weather Service glossary defines mist as minute water particles suspended in the atmosphere, reducing visibility to less than seven statute miles but not below five-eighths of a mile — and notes that mist does not reduce visibility as much as fog, and is often confused with drizzle. That single fact sorts a whole family of jokes: mist is the light, almost-see-through version; fog is the thick one. A joke about trying to catch fog and missing works precisely because the real thing is thin enough to slip through your hands.

The sections below follow the forecast in the order a family actually talks about it: sun first, then rain, then the noisy weather, then the cold. Each section keeps its jokes to words a listener has heard on the news or at the window, and the groan-rating table after the jokes is honest about which ones need a listener who already knows the word.

Sun and heat jokes

  1. Sun apologize for arriving late in winter? It said the days were getting shorter on its side too.
  2. Sunbeam apply for a promotion? It had been shining in its role for years.
  3. Thermometer get nervous before the heat wave? It felt the degrees climbing and could not keep its cool.
  4. Shade tree become the most popular seat in the park? It kept its cool no matter how heated things got.
  5. Sunburn lecture the sunscreen? It warned that some lessons really sink in.
  6. Ice cube stay calm in the lemonade? It knew melting down would only water things down.
  7. Fan club for summer hold its meetings outdoors? Its members were big fans of a good breeze.
  8. Sunglasses feel confident at noon? Nothing could outshine their outlook.
  9. Heat wave say goodbye so slowly? It liked to linger and make an impression.
  10. Sunny day compliment the garden? It said everything out there was really coming up bright.

Rain and cloud jokes

  1. Cloud bring a notebook to the storm? It wanted to jot down every striking idea.
  2. Raindrop feel brave on the window? It knew the slide down was the whole point.
  3. Puddle apply to be a mirror? It already reflected well on everyone who passed.
  4. Umbrella feel underappreciated in April? It only got noticed when things turned grey.
  5. Mist feel misunderstood at the party? People kept confusing it with its thicker cousin, fog.
  6. Raincoat retire after one season? It was tired of being hung out to dry.
  7. Thunderstorm's cloud keep a diary? Every entry started with a little precipitation of feelings.
  8. Drizzle feel left out of the forecast? It fell between a mist and a moment.
  9. Rainbow refuse to brag about its colours? It preferred to arc quietly over the whole argument.
  10. Storm drain throw the best parties? Everything in the neighbourhood eventually flowed its way.

Wind and storm jokes

  1. Wind win every race it entered? The competition was blown away before the finish.
  2. Lightning take up photography? It had a flash for it from the very first strike.
  3. Thunder feel jealous of lightning? Everyone saw the other one first, every single time.
  4. Breeze apologize for the mess? It said things had just gotten carried away.
  5. Hurricane's eye stay so calm? It had learned to stay centred no matter what spun around it.
  6. Weather vane feel dizzy at work? The whole job was turning with every change of direction.
  7. Kite give the wind a good review? It said the lift was uplifting from start to finish.
  8. Storm cloud hold a grudge? It kept its feelings bottled up until they had to burst.
  9. Gust of wind join the choir? It wanted to be a breath of fresh air in every verse.
  10. Lightning bolt retire from racing? It was still fast, but tired of always finishing in a flash.

Snow and cold jokes

  1. Snowflake feel unique at the reunion? No two stories there were ever exactly alike.
  2. Icicle apply for a job in spring? It knew the position would melt away, but applied anyway.
  3. Snowman's scarf feel important? The whole look hung on it staying in place.
  4. Freezer give the leftovers a pep talk? It told them to chill — their moment would come.
  5. Winter coat feel optimistic in the closet? It knew its season always came back around.
  6. Hot chocolate get promoted at the ski lodge? It warmed up every conversation it joined.
  7. Ice rink compliment the skaters? It said they handled every slippery situation with grace.
  8. Frost introduce itself to the window? It said the meeting would be a pane, but a pretty one.
  9. Snowball feel nervous rolling downhill? Things were picking up speed and size at once.
  10. Mitten feel lonely in the drawer? Its partner had been missing since last January.

The table carries one safety rule inside it: storm jokes are for after the storm. A joke about lightning told while thunder is still close is not brave timing, it is bad timing — the framework below makes that an explicit step, because the forecast is a real thing and not only a punchline supply.

Matching the joke to the forecast

Use this decision framework before deploying a joke. Three questions, in order:

  1. What is actually happening outside? Tell the section that matches the sky. A snow joke in a heat wave reads as a wish, not a joke, and a sun joke in a downpour reads as denial. The match between the window and the punchline is half the laugh.
  2. Has the weather finished being serious? If the storm, flood, or deep cold is still a problem for anyone in the room, wait. Weather jokes are for the all-clear: the shovelling break, the power coming back, the drive after the rain stops. When in doubt, pick sun.
  3. What groan level is wanted? Use the rating table. An opener for a mixed-age table is a sun or snow joke. Save the mist-versus-fog material for listeners who will enjoy catching a real definition hiding inside a pun.

Applied together: on the first snow morning, the snowflake and the mitten do the work at breakfast. On a grey, drizzly school run, the mist joke lands because the window agrees with it. After a summer storm clears, thunder's jealousy gets the biggest groan of the week — told from a dry porch, with the sky already blue.

How to deliver a weather joke

  1. Glance outside, or at the forecast, and name the real weather in one plain sentence first.
  2. Ask the setup slowly, then pause a full beat before the punchline.
  3. Say the punchline flatly, without laughing first — the deadpan is what makes it a dad joke.
  4. If it lands silently, unpack the double meaning in one line — or, for mist and fog, the real difference between them.
  5. Stop at one or two per forecast. A joke for every hour of the weather report is a flood, and floods are where this page draws the line.

What success looks like: a groan, an eye-roll, or — the highest honour — the listener retelling the same joke later as if it were their own discovery.

FAQ

What is the difference between mist and fog in these jokes? A real one. The National Weather Service defines mist as suspended water particles that reduce visibility to less than seven statute miles but not below five-eighths of a mile, and notes mist does not reduce visibility as much as fog. So mist jokes are about the thin, almost-clear kind of grey; fog jokes are about the thick kind.

Are storm jokes ever in bad taste? They can be, if the timing is wrong. The framework above is the rule used here: joke about a storm after it has passed and nobody in the room is still dealing with its damage. The jokes themselves are clean and kind; timing is the teller's responsibility.

Where does the term dad joke come from? Merriam-Webster records the first known use of the term in 1987, and defines it as a wholesome joke with an obvious or predictable pun, judged endearingly corny.

Can a listener make up new ones? Yes — weather is the easiest category to extend. Pick a forecast word with a second life — front, pressure, degree, fair, current — state a tiny scene, and let the second meaning be the punchline.
