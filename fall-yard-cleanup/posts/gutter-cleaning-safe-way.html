The safe way to clean gutters

Safety first · 7 min read

⚠ Read this first. Falls from ladders are one of the most common serious household injuries. This guide covers how to do the job as safely as possible — but if anything about your gutters, roof, or ladder situation feels wrong, hiring a professional is the right call, not a failure. A gutter cleaning service typically costs a fraction of an emergency room visit.

What clean gutters actually prevent

Gutters do one job: move roof water away from the house. When they're clogged with leaves, water spills over the edge and soaks the siding, or pools at the foundation. Over time that means:

  • Fascia and soffit rot — standing water eats the wood behind the gutters.
  • Foundation moisture problems — water dumped next to the house instead of away from it finds its way into basements and crawl spaces.
  • Ice dams in cold climates — debris plus meltwater refreezing at the eave edge can push water under shingles.
  • Pest habitat — soggy leaf sludge in gutters is a nursery for mosquitoes and a buffet for rodents.

Two cleanings a year — mid-fall and late fall, or spring and fall — are enough for most houses.

When to hire it out (no shame)

DIY this job only if all of these are true. Otherwise, hire it out:

  • Your gutters are on a single-story roof you can comfortably reach from a standard ladder.
  • The ground below is firm and level (not a slope, not soft mulch, not ice).
  • You have a stable extension or tall stepladder in good condition — no wobbles, no bent rungs, no frayed rope.
  • You feel steady on it. Nervous and shaky is your body telling you to call someone.

Multi-story houses, steep roofs, gutters over concrete patios or sloped beds, power lines near the roofline, or any health issue affecting balance — these are all reasons to hire a pro or an insured handyman service. Ask for proof of insurance; a legitimate outfit will have it.

If you're doing it: ladder safety essentials

  1. Set the ladder right. Extension ladder: base one foot out for every four feet of height, extending at least three feet above the gutter line. Lock the spreaders on a stepladder. Never lean an extension ladder against the gutter itself if it's plastic or flimsy — rest it on the fascia or the wall.
  2. Stabilize the feet. Level ground only. If the ground is soft, set the feet on boards or ladder levelers. Tie off the top if you can.
  3. Have a spotter. A second person holding the ladder and handing tools is the single biggest safety upgrade available. Don't climb alone if you can avoid it.
  4. Keep three points of contact. Two hands and a foot, or two feet and a hand, on the ladder at all times. Clip the trash bucket to the ladder or have the spotter hold it — don't carry things in your hands while climbing.
  5. Don't overreach. Keep your belt buckle between the rails. Move the ladder often instead of leaning sideways — most falls happen during a reach.
  6. Watch the weather. No wind, no rain, no ice, no wet roof. Morning dew on a ladder rung is enough to matter.

The cleaning itself

Wear thick gloves (gutter sludge hides screws, sharp metal edges, and the odd wasp nest — late fall nests are usually inactive, but check before you reach in). Scoop debris by hand or with a gutter scoop into a bucket, then flush the gutter with a garden hose toward the downspout. If the downspout is clogged, a hose nozzle up the bottom of the spout usually clears it — if not, you may need to disassemble the elbow. Watch for where water goes when you test the flow; add or extend downspout extensions so it lands several feet from the foundation.

Keeping them clearer longer

  • Gutter guards reduce how often you climb, but they don't eliminate maintenance — fine debris still gets through, and guards can be difficult to remove for cleaning. They're a trade-off, not a miracle.
  • Trim back overhanging branches if you can do it safely from the ground (pole pruner). Fewer overhanging branches, fewer leaves in the gutter.
  • Check the slope: gutters should drop slightly toward the downspout. Standing water between cleanings means the pitch is off — that's a repair, not a cleaning job.

The honest math. A professional gutter cleaning for a typical two-story house is a routine service call. Compare that to the cost — and the recovery time — of a fall. If you're unsure at any point, the right move is the same every time: come down and call someone.
