Selling bulky furniture flips locally without owning a truck

Reselling · 7 min read

The usual advice assumes the hard part is over once you own the dresser. For a flipper it is not. A bulky piece has to travel twice: from the auction hall or the original seller to you, and then from you to the buyer. Owning no truck does not make either leg impossible. It makes both legs a cost — and a cost that is not written into the bid is a cost that comes straight out of the margin.

So run the transport plan before the auction, not after the win. The steps below are the order of operations.

Step 1: Decide the second leg before you bid on the first

  1. Write down how the piece gets to you: a borrowed vehicle, a short rental run, a paid local mover, or a seller who can hold it until a buyer is lined up.
  2. Write down how it leaves: buyer pickup, your delivery in a rented vehicle, or a third-party local delivery arranged by you or the buyer.
  3. Put a dollar figure beside each leg, even a rough one. If either leg has no workable option at any price, pass on the lot — however cheap the hammer looks.

That last point is the discipline. Bulky, slow stock occupies finite space while it waits, and cash tied up in a piece that cannot move loses to smaller, faster wins. A $15–$25 profit on a small item collected on a run you were already making can be the better business than a large headline margin on a wardrobe with no route out of the room.

Step 2: Price the rental run honestly

US-sourced rental figures, stated as US context rather than Canadian prices: one US moving guide lists U-Haul rentals starting at US$19.95 plus mileage and gas, and Home Depot flat-rate rentals starting at US$19 for 75 minutes. Read those as a floor, not a quote. The same source names the costs sellers forget: gas, insurance, mileage fees, and loading labour. Your Canadian rental will be priced locally — get the actual rate, the per-kilometre charge, and the fuel terms before the run, and add them together.

A rental run earns its fee when it moves more than one piece. Batch the auction pickup, a second purchase, and a delivery into a single hire and the cost per item falls sharply. A rental booked for one mid-priced item usually fails the margin test in Step 4.

Step 3: Set the listing up for the leg you chose

Buyer pickup only. This is the default for a reason: furniture and large items sell locally for cash, and the heavier the item, the more local selling beats shipping. Facebook Marketplace is the strongest local channel in the sources for this piece — free to list, with no fee on local sales. Price for the friction you are asking the buyer to absorb. Large used furniture often starts at 30% to 50% of its original retail price for well-maintained pieces, and buyers expect to negotiate 10% to 20% off the list price, so build that room in rather than listing at your walk-away number.

Delivery offered. Offer it as a priced option, not a favour. If you sell through a dedicated furniture resale app instead, note that those services may charge commission from 5% to 20% — a fee that belongs in the same calculation as the rental.

Third-party local delivery. When the buyer arranges and pays a mover directly, your transport cost drops to zero and the risk moves with it. Make that path explicit in the listing: the piece is available for a buyer's own mover on named days, with measurements listed so the buyer can book the right vehicle the first time.

Step 4: Run the margin test

Delivery only wins this table if it does something the table cannot show: sell the piece weeks sooner, or sell it at all. If pickup-only stock is sitting while the space is needed, the faster route can be the right one — price that speed honestly instead of pretending the rental was free.

Success looks like this: no bulky lot is bid on without two transport legs and a dollar figure beside each, and no piece enters storage without a named exit route.

Two questions sellers ask

Can money be made reselling without shipping anything? The local-only model is a documented one: heavy items are exactly where local cash sales outperform shipping, because the carrier's price is the cost you never pay.

What is the best app to sell locally? The sources for this piece name Facebook Marketplace as the strongest local channel, free to list with no fee on local sales; furniture-specific apps exist, at the commissions noted above.

The walk-away line

Skip the lot when the only workable transport plan costs more than the margin the piece can carry, or when neither leg has a confirmed option before the auction closes. A cheap wardrobe with no way home is not a deal. It is rent you pay in space, starting the day it arrives.
