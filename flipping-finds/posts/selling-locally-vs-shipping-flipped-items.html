Selling locally vs shipping flipped items

Reselling · 8 min read

Every auction lot you win eventually asks the same question: does this sell to someone who drives to you, or to someone a parcel carrier drives to? Sellers tend to pick a side once — "local only" or "I ship everything" — and then force every item through that one door. That is where margin leaks. The right answer changes item by item, and it is mostly arithmetic: what each channel costs, how big the buyer pool is, and how much of your own time each route eats.

The real question is per item, not per business

A useful way to frame it comes from resellers who sort every item into one of several destinations before listing anything: list it online, lot it with similar pieces, sell it locally, hold it for a better venue, donate it, or discard it. The local option in that framework is specific — the item has real value, but shipping cost, size, fragility, or packing time makes a local sale the smarter route. Notice that none of those four factors is about the item's price. A $30 item can be a shipping item and a $300 item can be a local one. What decides is the relationship between the item, the channel's costs, and the buyers within reach.

There is also a clock running. Holding everything because somebody might buy it someday is how inventory starts controlling the business instead of serving it. A channel decision is partly a speed decision: which route turns this specific item into cash soonest, at an acceptable margin?

Two details in that table surprise people. First, eBay's percentage is charged on the total amount of the sale — including the shipping you charge the buyer and the sales tax eBay collects — not just the item price. Second, there is no single Canada Post price to memorize: Canada Post sets base prices from several factors, including the service you pick, the weight and size of the parcel, and where it starts and ends. A light item going one province over and the same item crossing the country are different math. That is why flat "shipping is about fifteen bucks" thinking quietly eats profits on heavy or far-away orders — get a real quote per item, per destination, before you commit to a shipped listing.

The same item, three ways: a worked comparison

Take a typical auction find: a small appliance with an all-in cost of $18 (hammer, premium, and tax already in), which sells for $60. Illustrative math using the fees above; your postage quote will differ by destination.

  • Local pickup at $60: no selling fee. Gross gain before your time: $42. Your costs are a meetup drive and the messages to arrange it.
  • Marketplace shipped at $60: 5% selling fee is $3. If the buyer pays postage, your gain is about $39; if you folded postage into the price, subtract the parcel cost as well.
  • eBay shipped at $60 + $15 shipping charged: the fee base is the $75 total, so 13.6% is $10.20, plus a $0.40 per-order fee — $10.60 in fees. If the parcel actually costs you $15, your gain lands around $31.40.

Local wins this round on pure math — when a local buyer exists. That last clause is the whole game. A niche collectible, an odd-size part, or anything with a thin local market can sit for months at $60 locally while a national listing sells it in a week at the same price. The fee you avoid is worth nothing on an item that never sells.

When local wins

  • Bulky, heavy, or awkward items. Furniture, large appliances, exercise gear: parcel pricing punishes size and weight, and a local buyer with a truck solves the problem for free. Bulky stock is also the stock that pays the most "rent" in your storage space, so a fast local sale at a slightly lower price often beats a slow shipped sale at full price.
  • Fragile items. If packing it safely takes twenty minutes and a prayer, a local handoff deletes the breakage risk, the packaging cost, and the return argument in one move.
  • Common items with deep local demand. Brand-name tools, popular small appliances, kids' gear — the local pool is big enough that the wider audience adds little.
  • Low-value items. On a $15 sale, a per-order fee plus postage friction can take a third of the price. Locally, the full $15 is yours.

When shipping wins

  • Niche and collectible items. The buyer for a specific part, out-of-print item, or hobby piece may not exist within driving distance. A national pool turns "unsellable" into "sold this week."
  • Small, light, sturdy items. Cheap to pack, cheap to post, low breakage risk — the fee buys you reach at very little handling cost.
  • Items priced by a national market. If sold listings cluster tightly around one price across the country, local buyers anchored to that same number gain you nothing; ship and take the bigger pool.
  • When your time is the bottleneck. Three meetups can eat an evening. One trip to drop off five parcels can be the better trade — count your own hours as a cost, because they are one.

Local selling has one more hidden line item: the no-show. Every local seller collects stories of buyers who confirmed and never appeared. It is not a reason to avoid local sales, but it belongs in the math — and it is why the meetup habits matter: Facebook recommends meeting in a public, well-lit place such as a police station, and Marketplace lets you share a meeting plan, with location and time, with a trusted friend or family member through Messenger.

A five-question routing check before you list

  1. Would shipping this cost more than about a quarter of its expected price? If yes, lean local.
  2. Is it bulky, fragile, or miserable to pack? Lean local.
  3. Is the likely buyer a specialist who may not live near you? Lean shipped.
  4. How fast does it need to move — is it taking storage space you need? The channel with the deeper matching demand wins, whichever it is.
  5. Run the fee math with today's rates: total-amount fee on eBay, per-shipment fee on Marketplace checkout, zero on local pickup. If the shipped gain still beats local after fees and postage, ship it.

Success looks like this: no item sits listed in the wrong channel for weeks. Each piece is routed once, on purpose, with the numbers written down — and if an item stalls, you change the channel, not just the price.

FAQ

Does Facebook Marketplace charge for local pickup sales? No. Listing and selling locally carries no selling fee; the fee appears only on shipped orders processed through Facebook's checkout — 5% per shipment, or a flat $0.40 on sales of $8 or less (checked October 13, 2026).

Is eBay's fee really charged on shipping too? Yes. On eBay Canada the final value fee is a percentage of the total amount of the sale, which includes the shipping charged to the buyer and taxes eBay collects — one reason "free shipping" listings need the fee run on the full price.

Can I list the same item locally and shipped? Many sellers do, but the discipline is non-negotiable: the moment it sells in one place, it comes down everywhere else, immediately. A double sale costs you a refund, a disappointed buyer, and a mark on the account — track which channel each item is live on, and pull listings the same day.
