Games to finish before launch season

Backlog · 6 min read

Here's a prediction that requires no insider knowledge: once GTA VI is installed, everything else in your backlog is going on ice for months. That's not a problem — it's the plan. The problem is arriving at November 19, 2026 mid-way through three open-world epics, feeling guilty every time the map icon glows. Fix that now, while it's still fun.

The principle: finish or park, never linger

Every game on your shelf should be in one of three states by early fall 2026: finished, deliberately parked (you've decided it can wait until 2027 and you mean it), or not started. The enemy is the fourth state: half-played, guilt-inducing, silently judging you from the dashboard. Audit your backlog and force everything into one of the three buckets.

Priority one: Rockstar's own back catalog

If you haven't played them — or never finished them — Rockstar's recent games are the most relevant prep there is, and they're often discounted heavily by now:

  • Grand Theft Auto V (2013). The direct predecessor, and still the best way to calibrate your expectations for what a Rockstar open world feels like. If you've only ever played GTA Online, the single-player story is a genuinely different experience and worth your time before VI arrives.
  • Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018). Rockstar's most recent major open-world release, and the game GTA VI's development followed. It's long — budget 60+ hours for the story alone — which is exactly why you start it now, not in September. It also sets the bar for the level of detail Rockstar is capable of, which makes the wait more bearable, not less.

Both are the kind of games that reward unhurried play. Starting either one in October is a scheduling error; starting now is a plan.

Priority two: your open-world half-finishes

Open-world games compete directly with GTA VI for the same mental slot: the "big map I live in after work" game. You will not play two of those at once. Look at your in-progress list and be ruthless:

  • Anything over 70% done? Finish it. The end is closer than it feels.
  • Anything under 30% done that you weren't loving? Park it officially. Uninstall, no guilt.
  • Anything in the middle that you genuinely enjoy? That's your summer game. Give it a real push and an end date.

What to play in the final stretch

September and October are for short games: 8–15 hour experiences with real endings. Roguelikes, tight action games, indie darlings you've been meaning to try, the story mode of a sports or racing game. The criteria: it ends, it ends soon, and it doesn't install a new 100-hour habit. This is also a great window for genres GTA doesn't touch — a horror game in October is practically seasonal.

The fall 2026 trap. Do not start a massive new RPG, live-service grind, or 200-hour epic in September or October 2026. You will abandon it mid-way in November and it will sit in your backlog radiating guilt until 2028. If a big game launches that fall and tempts you, add it to a 2027 list. Future you says thanks.

Multiplayer and live-service games

These don't need "finishing," but they do need a decision. If you're deep in a competitive season or a battle pass, check when it ends relative to November 19 — and decide whether you're playing GTA VI's launch week or your ranked climb, because "both" is how sleep schedules die. A clean break (finish the season, take a month off) beats a guilty half-presence in both.

The week before: go in clean

Seven days out, your queue should be empty: no in-progress epics, no just-one-more-season multiplayer, nothing installed "for later." Install the games you're done with guilt-free, keep one short palate-cleanser if you like, and let the anticipation build. The pre-launch checklist covers the technical side of that final week; this is the mental side. Arrive at Leonida with nothing hanging over you.
