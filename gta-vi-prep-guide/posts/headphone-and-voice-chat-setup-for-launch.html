Headphone and voice chat setup for launch: prove your mic works before November 19

Hardware · 7 min read

GTA VI launches on November 19, 2026 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, per Rockstar's official page. Rockstar publishes no audio or voice-chat specification for the game — and does not need to: voice chat on launch night is a console feature, and it will run on whatever settings your PS5 or Xbox has today. That is exactly the problem. Headset failures rarely announce themselves in advance. The classic launch-night version goes like this: the game sounds great in your headphones, your friends can barely hear you, and twenty minutes of menu-diving later someone works out that the console has been listening to a microphone across the room the whole time. Every part of that failure is findable now, in about fifteen minutes per console, using only the settings each platform documents. This guide is a verification pass for the headset you already own — not a buying guide, and not a pairing walkthrough.

First, identify what you actually own: connection type decides everything

Before any setting matters, name how your headset connects, because the connection type determines which microphone the console can even use:

  • Wired into the controller's 3.5mm jack. The simplest path on both consoles: the headset's own microphone is the input, and the console's audio screens control volume, chat balance, and monitoring.
  • USB, wired or with a wireless dongle. The console sees a named audio device. Both output and input normally travel the same path, but the device still has to be selected as the input — never assume it selected itself.
  • Generic Bluetooth headphones. The trap. On PS5, the console treats ordinary Bluetooth headphones as a stereo output device with no input channel: they cannot carry your voice into party chat at all, even if the headphones themselves have a microphone. The console falls back to another mic — usually the one built into the controller — while the game audio in your ears sounds perfect. Nothing appears broken. That is why this failure survives until launch night.
  • The controller's built-in microphone. PlayStation documents the DualSense's built-in mic as a real voice-chat input, and it is a legitimate backup. It is also the mic your friends will describe as "quiet and echoey" if it ends up as your main mic by accident rather than by choice.

PS5: the audio and voice pass, step by step

  1. Set the output on purpose. Go to Settings > Sound > Audio Output and select your headset as the Output Device. Then check Output to Headphones: this decides whether the headset carries chat audio only or all audio. Pick deliberately — "all audio" is what most players want for a story launch; "chat only" leaves game sound on the TV speakers.
  2. Set the input on purpose. Go to Settings > Sound > Microphone and read the Input Device line. It must name your headset (or "Controller" only if you have consciously chosen the built-in mic). If your Bluetooth headphones do not appear in this list at all, the mic path is not supported — that absence is the diagnosis, not a glitch.
  3. Calibrate the level. In the same menu, open Adjust Microphone Level and speak at your normal playing volume and distance. PlayStation's guidance is to set the level so your input volume sits in the Good range. Shouting at the calibration screen calibrates for shouting; speak like you will actually play.
  4. Set the mute defaults. Under the Microphone settings, review two switches most players never see. Microphone Status When Logged In can be set to Mute, so your mic is only live when you deliberately turn it on. Microphone Status When Starting Voice Chat or Broadcast offers Switch to Mute, which turns the mic off automatically when a chat starts — PlayStation documents it specifically to prevent unintentional hot-mic situations. Know also that the controller's MUTE button does more than mute the mic: pressed, it blinks, mutes the microphone, and turns off sound output to all speakers until pressed again. A "my audio died" panic on launch night is often just this button.
  5. Set headphone volume in the right place. Settings > Sound > Volume holds the Headphones level. Set it here first, then use the headset's own controls for fine adjustments, so the two volume controls are not fighting each other.

Xbox Series X|S: the audio and voice pass, step by step

  1. Connect, then open the one screen that matters. With the headset connected (a wired headset plugs straight into the controller's 3.5mm jack), press the Xbox button to open the guide and select Audio & music at the bottom. Microsoft's own support guidance points to this screen for headset setup.
  2. Turn on Headset mic. It is a toggle on that screen, separate from the headset's physical mute. A headset can be plugged in, unmuted at the earcup, and still silent because this toggle is off.
  3. Centre the Headset chat mixer to start. The mixer balances game audio against chat audio. Start in the middle; push it toward chat only after the launch-night test below shows you actually need it. A mixer parked at either extreme silently deletes one half of the sound.
  4. Use Mic monitoring as your first test. Raise Mic monitoring on the same screen and speak. Hearing your own voice in the headset proves the mic is live and gives a rough sense of its level before anyone else is involved.
  5. Check the privacy gate if anything refuses to work. Xbox account privacy settings can block communication regardless of hardware — if a correctly set-up headset cannot join or speak in chat, the Communication settings under Xbox privacy are the next place to look, before buying anything.

The two-way test: prove it before launch week

Settings screens show intent; only a live test shows truth. Run this on each console you plan to play on, ideally with one friend, at least a week before launch:

  1. Start a party. On PS5: PS button > Game Base > your party. On Xbox: Parties & chats > Start a party. (Xbox players can run the first pass solo: start a party alone, speak, and watch the indicator around your gamerpic respond.)
  2. Speak at playing distance, at playing volume. Read a sentence the way you would actually talk mid-game — not leaned into the mic.
  3. Get a rated answer, not a polite one. Ask your friend to judge three things separately: loud enough? clear, or muffled/clipping? any echo or room noise? "Sounds fine" is not data.
  4. Test the mute both ways. Mute, confirm they hear nothing; unmute, confirm you are back. Then deliberately test the failure mode: on PS5, if you used Bluetooth audio, have your friend confirm which mic they are hearing by tapping the headset mic and then the controller — the one that thumps is the one that is live.
  5. Switch between party chat and game chat once. On PS5 the voice chat card's drop-down does this; doing it once now means not learning it mid-session on launch night.

What success looks like: your friend rates your voice loud and clear at normal speaking volume, the mute test passes in both directions, the input device named in settings is the microphone your friend actually heard, and the game/chat balance lets you hear both the game and the party without touching a menu.

If something fails the test

  • Friends hear the room, not you (PS5, Bluetooth audio): the console is on the controller's built-in mic. Fix by using the controller mic deliberately, switching chat to a wired headset in the controller jack, or using a two-way USB adapter with mic passthrough — PlayStation's own first-party wireless headsets exist precisely because they carry both directions.
  • Friends hear nothing (Xbox): work the list in order — physical mute, Headset mic toggle on, Auto-mute not set to High (turn it off and retest), restart the console, check for a headset firmware update, then the privacy settings.
  • Echo: PlayStation's documented fix for headset echo is the microphone level: open Adjust Microphone Level and slide the fader a few notches down. Echo is usually level, not hardware.
  • Audio vanished entirely (PS5): check the controller MUTE button before anything else — it silences output to all speakers as well as the mic.

Questions players commonly ask

Can I just use the DualSense microphone? Yes — PlayStation documents it as a voice-chat input, and as a deliberate choice it is fine. The failure this guide prevents is using it by accident while assuming a headset mic is live.

My headphones sound great but the Input Device list does not show them. Why? On PS5, generic Bluetooth headphones present as output-only: no input channel exists for the console to select. The list is telling you the truth about the connection, and the fix is a different chat path, not a setting.

Does GTA VI need any special audio settings? Nothing Rockstar has published says so. Everything in this guide is the consoles' standard, documented voice-chat setup — which is the point: it can all be verified today, against games you already own.

When should this be done? At least a week before November 19, 2026. A failed test today costs a cable or a setting; the same failure at 8 p.m. on launch night costs the session.
