The pre-launch checklist

Checklist · 6 min read

Launch day is Thursday, November 19, 2026. The difference between a great day one and a frustrating one is about two hours of boring admin — done in advance. Work through this list over the coming months and you'll spend November 19 playing, not resetting passwords and watching a progress bar.

1. Clear your storage — now, not in November

Rockstar's open-world games are among the largest downloads in gaming. GTA VI will be a huge install; the exact size hasn't been announced, but planning for a very large download is the only sane assumption. Go to your console's storage settings today and look at the real number. Then:

  • Delete games you finished and can re-download later (your saves are separate).
  • Move rarely-played games to external or expanded storage if your console supports it.
  • If you're consistently near full, price out expanded storage now — it won't be cheaper in November.

2. Audit your platform account

Sign into your PlayStation or Xbox account this week and confirm: the password works, two-step verification is enabled, your email is one you can still access, and your payment method is current (expired cards are the #1 launch-night purchase killer). This is a fifteen-minute job that saves a miserable support-chat session later.

3. Test your internet's real speed

Run a speed test on the console itself, not your phone — consoles on Wi-Fi often get far less than the household headline speed. A huge game on a slow connection can mean an overnight download. Options if it's slow: a wired Ethernet connection (the cheapest meaningful upgrade in gaming), scheduling the download overnight, or buying the physical disc if you prefer — disc installs still pull patches, but the bulk of the data comes off the disc.

4. Set your backlog end date

Pick a date — say, early October 2026 — after which you don't start anything new. Finish what's in progress or deliberately park it. A half-finished 80-hour RPG will still be there in 2027; launch week is not the time to feel guilty about it. (The full strategy is in games to finish before launch season.)

5. Decide your edition and budget cap

Rockstar hasn't announced editions or prices yet — but your budget doesn't need their permission. Set a hard number now for game + extras, and decide your policy on deluxe editions before the marketing starts. The budgeting guide breaks down what editions have historically included so you can judge the offers coolly when they appear.

6. Sort your controllers and headset

Charge, test, and if needed replace: controllers (stick drift is real and miserable), charging cables or docks, and your headset. Comfort gear — a decent chair setup, a headset you're happy wearing for hours — is worth sorting while there's no urgency. This is also the moment to update controller firmware, which both consoles occasionally require.

7. Plan your download strategy

Big launches often offer pre-loading — downloading the game files days early so they're unlocked at release. Rockstar hasn't confirmed this for GTA VI, but if it's offered, take it: it turns launch day into minutes instead of hours. Digital buyers should also decide where they're buying from (platform store vs. retailer codes) since prices and pre-load timing can differ.

8. Block the time — deliberately

November 19 is a Thursday. If you're taking time off, request it early — launch week is popular. Equally important: protect the weekend after. And set expectations with the people you live with. A planned gaming weekend beats a negotiated one.

9. Mute the spoilers in advance

Decide now how spoiler-averse you are, and set up muted words ("GTA VI", "GTA 6", "Leonida", "Lucia") on social platforms before launch week. The first 48 hours are when the biggest story moments leak. If you care about experiencing the story fresh, the mute button is your best pre-launch purchase and it's free.

10. Do a dry run the week before

The weekend before launch: update the console firmware, update the controller, confirm your storage is clear, confirm your account and payment method, and make sure your internet is behaving. Twenty minutes. Then November 19 is just: download (or insert disc), install the day-one patch, and play.

The one-line version: storage, account, internet, backlog, budget, gear, download plan, calendar, spoiler mutes, dry run. Do one item a month and you'll walk into launch week completely calm — which, for a game people have waited over a decade for, is a genuine achievement.
