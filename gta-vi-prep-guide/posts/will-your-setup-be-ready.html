Will your setup be ready?

Hardware · 5 min read

Here's the honest starting point: Rockstar has published zero technical details about GTA VI — no minimum specs, no recommended specs, no resolution or frame-rate targets, no install size. Anyone quoting numbers is guessing. So this guide isn't about specs. It's about how to think about readiness with the one solid fact we have: the game launches on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and nothing else is confirmed.

If you play on PlayStation 5

Good news: readiness is mostly about space. The game is confirmed for PS5, and it will be built to run on a stock PS5 — Rockstar doesn't ship games that require hardware that doesn't exist yet. Your prep list:

  • Storage. This is the one real bottleneck. Rockstar's recent open-world games are enormous downloads, and a game of this scale will almost certainly demand a large chunk of your SSD — think big three-digit gigabytes, though Rockstar hasn't confirmed a number. Start clearing space now, or plan on adding compatible expanded storage. It's the single most useful hardware move you can make this year.
  • System software. Keep the console updated; launch-week patches for big games sometimes require a recent firmware.
  • Your PSN account. Confirm you can sign in, your payment method is current, and two-step verification is on. Do it now, not on launch night.

The PS5 Pro exists as an option if you want the best possible version, but nothing confirms what it will add — don't buy hardware for benefits Rockstar hasn't announced.

If you play on Xbox Series X|S

Same logic, one wrinkle: the Series S has significantly less internal storage than the Series X, and this game will be big. Series S owners should take storage prep the most seriously of anyone — the expansion card or compatible USB archive drive conversation should happen now, not in November.

Beyond storage: same story as PS5. The game is confirmed for the platform, it will run on stock hardware, and your job is to have your Xbox account healthy, your console updated, and room for the download.

If you play on PC

Don't buy PC hardware for GTA VI yet. There is no announced PC version and no system requirements. A GPU or SSD bought today for a game with no confirmed specs and no confirmed release date is a guess with a receipt. If your PC needs upgrades for the games you play now, upgrade for those — a faster drive and a healthy GPU benefit everything. But "future-proofing for GTA VI" is marketing, not a plan.

Past GTA games have come to PC eventually, but timelines have varied wildly, and Rockstar has said nothing. If GTA VI matters to you enough to buy a console for it, that's a legitimate call — just make it on the confirmed platforms (PS5, Series X|S), not on rumors of a PC date.

What about your TV and audio?

Nothing about GTA VI requires a new display. A 4K HDR TV will show the game at its best, but a good 1080p screen will play it fine — games are designed for the range of TVs people actually own. The one upgrade that genuinely changes big open-world games is audio: a decent headset or soundbar makes a bigger difference to immersion than most people expect, and it's useful for everything you already play.

The readiness test that actually matters

Forget specs. Ask these five questions:

  1. Do you own a PS5 or Xbox Series X|S (or plan to before November)?
  2. Will you have enough free storage for a very large download?
  3. Can you sign into your platform account right now without a password reset?
  4. Is your payment method on that account current?
  5. Is your internet connection fast enough to download a huge game in a reasonable time — and do you have a data cap to think about?

Five yeses and you're ready. Everything else — new controllers, fancy headsets, display upgrades — is optional quality-of-life. The pre-launch checklist walks through the logistics behind each of these, one by one.
