Last-minute Halloween checklist: the final week before Saturday, October 31, 2026

Planning · 8 min read

Halloween 2026 falls on a Saturday, October 31 — which sounds like a gift and quietly is not. A Saturday Halloween means trick-or-treaters start earlier, parties run later, and the whole neighbourhood has had all day to build expectations. It also means the final week, Sunday October 25 through Saturday October 31, is the only planning time left. Most checklists fail this week in one of two ways: they cover a party and forget the trick-or-treaters, or they list forty undated tips with no order and no permission to skip anything. This one is built differently: a triage table first, then a day-by-day procedure, then the safety checks that have real deadlines.

The triage table: must-do, should-do, safe-to-skip

When time runs short, the question is not "what is left?" but "what actually matters?" Sort every remaining job into one of three lanes:

LaneJobsRule
Must-doCandy and non-food treats bought and counted; costumes tried on and fitting; flashlights, reflective tape, and battery checks done; walkway cleared and lit; treats inspection plan agreedSafety or the night simply does not happen. Do these first, in this order.
Should-doPumpkins carved (adults only); party headcount confirmed with a simple yes/no message; route planned; emergency costume kit packed; costume photos taken before leavingImproves the night noticeably. Do after the must-dos, in daylight where possible.
Safe-to-skipNew decorations bought this week; elaborate homemade treats; a second costume option; themed playlists longer than the partyNobody remembers these by November 2. Drop without guilt if the must-dos are not done.

The pattern behind the table: anything involving fire, batteries, traffic visibility, or a child’s airway is a must-do; anything purely decorative is skippable. Final-week advice from organizing guides agrees on the direction — the last few days are for finishing plans already made, not adding new ones.

The day-by-day final-week procedure

  1. Sunday, Oct 25 — the count and the shop. Count expected trick-or-treaters from last year’s turnout, decide treats per child, and buy candy and non-food treats in one trip. Buy batteries, reflective tape, and a flashlight per child in the same run. Success check: treats are in the house, sealed, and stored where nobody grazes on them all week.
  2. Monday, Oct 26 — costume fitting. Full dress rehearsal, including shoes and the layer that goes underneath. Health Canada’s fit rules: costumes should fit well, because long or loose costumes lead to trips and falls, and bright colours or added reflective tape make children visible to motorists. Hem, pin, or swap anything that drags. Success check: every child can walk downstairs, sit, and climb back up in costume without holding fabric.
  3. Tuesday, Oct 27 — patch tests and battery checks. Do the face-paint patch test today, not Saturday: Health Canada notes even products labelled hypoallergenic can cause reactions, and a reaction found Tuesday leaves time to switch to a mask that fits and allows easy breathing and seeing — or to plain makeup. Check that battery compartments in light-up costumes, accessories, and flameless candles are child-resistant, requiring a tool or multiple steps to open; button batteries are very dangerous if swallowed. Success check: no skin reaction by Wednesday morning, and no battery door a small child can open.
  4. Wednesday, Oct 28 — lights and walkway. Check decorative lights for damaged wires and loose connections, and use only lights carrying a certification mark from a recognized organization such as CSA, cUL, or cETL. Clear the walkway of obstacles, turn on outdoor lighting, and decide the candle question now: battery-powered candles or flashlights inside jack-o’-lanterns instead of open flames remove the biggest fire risk in one decision. Success check: a walk from the sidewalk to the door in the dark finds no cords, hoses, or dark steps.
  5. Thursday, Oct 29 — party confirmations and route. For parties, send one simple yes/no headcount message — last-minute RSVPs need questions that are easy to answer — and share the address. For trick-or-treating, plan the route through populated, well-lit areas, agree the return time, and check the forecast: Health Canada advises paying attention to the weather and bringing warmer clothes if the evening will cool down. Success check: a headcount number, a route, and a home-by time all exist in writing.
  6. Friday, Oct 30 — carve, chill, stage. Adults carve the pumpkins (a Health Canada rule, not a preference). Chill party drinks, stage serving items, stock the bathroom, and set out trash bags and paper towels — the unglamorous items on every final-24-hour party checklist. Pack the emergency costume kit: safety pins, bandages, hair ties, wipes, and a small roll of tape. Label each child’s treat bucket or bag with their name. Success check: Saturday needs cooking and costumes only — no shopping, no carving, no hunting for tape.
  7. Saturday, Oct 31 — the day itself. Stop adding ideas. Costumes on early enough to take photos before leaving, while everyone is still clean and fully dressed. Young children stay supervised by an adult; older children go in groups on the planned route with the agreed return time. Success check: the group leaves on time, lit and reflective, with the porch light on behind them.

The safety checks that have deadlines

Three checks cannot be done at the last minute, which is why they sit on specific days above. The patch test needs at least a day of skin-watching before the makeup is trusted. The battery-compartment check needs a store run if a light-up accessory fails it, since loose button batteries and young children are a genuinely dangerous combination. The decorative-light inspection needs daylight to spot damaged wires — and a replacement string cannot be bought at 6 p.m. on Saturday if the old one is frayed. Fire safety ties them together: choose fabrics less likely to catch fire, such as nylon or heavyweight polyester, remember that "flame-resistant" does not mean fire-proof, and keep children away from open flames, including the candles inside jack-o’-lanterns. If real candles are used anywhere, they stay out of reach of children and pets, away from curtains and decorations, and are never left unattended.

After the door closes: the candy check

The final must-do happens at home. Inspect all treats before children eat them, and throw away anything opened or unwrapped, damaged or unsafe-looking, or homemade from an unfamiliar house. Keep small, hard, or chewy candies away from children under 3 — they are a choking risk. Agree the candy plan before anyone gets home (a few pieces tonight, the rest saved) so the inspection is not also a negotiation. Then the week is genuinely done: costumes in a labelled bin, leftover treats sorted, and one note for next year about how many trick-or-treaters actually came.

FAQ

What day of the week is Halloween 2026? Saturday, October 31, 2026 — expect earlier starts, later finishes, and more party overlap than a weekday Halloween.

What if it is already Friday and nothing is done? Run the must-do lane only: treats, costume fitting, flashlight and reflective tape, walkway light, candy-check agreement. That is roughly two hours of work and it covers everything safety-related on this page.

When should pumpkins be carved? Friday October 30 or Saturday morning — late enough to stay fresh, early enough that an adult does the carving without rushing. Battery candles inside remove the fire deadline entirely.

What is the one purchase people forget? Batteries — for flashlights, flameless candles, and light-up accessories — followed closely by reflective tape. Both are on the Sunday shop for a reason.
