What you risk without it

Drives fail. Backblaze's 2025 Drive Stats — 344,196 hard drives tracked — recorded a 1.36% annualized failure rate (down from 1.57% in 2024; lifetime AFR 1.30%). That's roughly 1 in 74 drives failing each year.

Over five years, a single drive's cumulative chance of failing is roughly 6–7% — about 1 in 15 (illustrative arithmetic from the published 1.36% AFR, not a separate study).

A backup plan doesn't change the odds. It changes what a failure costs you.

Source: Backblaze 2025 Year-End Drive Stats report, published Feb 12, 2026 — Business Wire release. The stat describes data-center drives; your odds may differ, which is exactly why redundancy exists.

5 · Make it real

Free steps that matter more than any purchase:
  1. Automate it. Use your OS's built-in backup (File History / Time Machine) or your cloud app's scheduler — backups you have to remember don't happen.
  2. Test a restore. Pick a random file, restore it, confirm it opens. Repeat every few months.
  3. Unplug the local drive between scheduled backups — an offline copy can't be hit by ransomware or a power surge.
  4. Keep recovery info somewhere safe. Cloud login, encryption key, and the drive itself should all be findable by you (or family) in an emergency.
