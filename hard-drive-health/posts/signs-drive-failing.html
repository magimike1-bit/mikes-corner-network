Signs your drive is failing

Drive failure · 5 min read

Drives rarely die without any warning — but the warnings are easy to miss if you don't know what to listen and look for. Here are the signs that your hard drive (HDD) or solid-state drive (SSD) may be heading toward failure, in plain language. None of these is a guarantee of doom on its own, but any one of them is a reason to check your backups today, not next month.

1. Strange noises (HDDs)

This one applies to traditional spinning hard drives — SSDs have no moving parts and are silent. A healthy HDD makes a soft, steady hum or the occasional quiet click of normal head movement. Warning sounds include:

  • Repeated clicking or ticking — often called the "click of death." It can mean the read/write heads can't find the data they're looking for and keep retrying.
  • Grinding or scraping — a serious sign of mechanical trouble. Power down if you can do so safely and don't keep using the drive.
  • Loud or irregular buzzing that wasn't there before.

Important: don't confuse normal drive sounds with failing ones. Drives are noisier during heavy work (big file copies, game loading). The red flag is a new sound that repeats or gets worse over days or weeks.

2. Everything suddenly gets slow

A computer that used to boot in seconds now takes minutes. Files take forever to open or copy. Programs freeze when saving. Yes, slowdowns have many causes (full drive, background updates, malware), but a drive that's struggling to read its own data is a classic one. The giveaway is slowness paired with other symptoms on this list — especially freezing during file access or disk-heavy operations like backups, which suddenly take far longer than usual.

3. Files disappear, corrupt, or won't open

You open a photo and it's half grey. A document that worked yesterday now won't open, or the file is simply gone. One corrupt file can be a fluke; a pattern of corrupt or missing files across different folders is one of the strongest signs of media problems on the drive. Treat it seriously the first time you notice a pattern, not the fifth.

4. Frequent crashes and freezes

Blue screens, sudden reboots, or the system locking up — particularly crashes that mention storage or file-system errors, or freezes that happen when opening, saving, or searching for files. Again, many things cause crashes, but crashes tied to disk activity point at the drive.

5. The computer won't boot, or the drive isn't detected

Messages like "no boot device found," "operating system not found," or the drive vanishing from the BIOS/UEFI or from your file manager are late-stage warnings. Sometimes reseating cables fixes it (loose SATA cables are genuinely common on desktops), but if the drive keeps disappearing, assume the drive — not the cable — and back up anything you can still reach before doing more troubleshooting.

6. Your system warns you

Sometimes you don't have to play detective: Windows may warn that a hard disk problem was detected, or a SMART monitoring tool reports the drive's health as "caution" or "bad." Take these warnings at face value. They can occasionally be wrong, but they're right far more often than people hope. (See our guide to SMART basics for what these warnings mean.)

What to do first — in this order

  1. Back up your important files now. Copy documents, photos, and anything irreplaceable to a different drive or cloud storage before you run any diagnostics. A dying drive can get worse during heavy scanning.
  2. Stop writing to the drive if you suspect failure. Every write is more stress on failing hardware. Read-only use while you copy data off is the safest posture.
  3. Then diagnose. Run a health check (see our free tools guide) to confirm what you're dealing with.
  4. Replace, don't nurse. A drive showing real failure signs isn't worth trusting again, even if it "gets better" for a while. Demote it to scratch duty at most.

The honest truth

Some drives die with zero warning — an electronics failure can be instant and silent, and SSDs in particular tend to give less notice than HDDs. That's why backups matter more than vigilance: you can watch for every sign on this list and still lose a drive overnight. Read the signs, act on them, and keep backups current so that no single failure — warned or not — costs you anything irreplaceable.
