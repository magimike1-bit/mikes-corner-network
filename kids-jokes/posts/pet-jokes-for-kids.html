Pet Jokes for Kids Ages 9–12

🐾 Pets · 7 min read · Updated October 2026

Quick question: where are the pet jokes hiding in most animal joke collections? At the back. One big collection of 73 animal jokes files its animals under dog, cat, farm, zoo, bird, ocean and wild — so the pet who sleeps in your house shares a page with sharks. Another collects over 100 animal jokes and puts the cats-and-dogs section at the very end. This page flips the order. Pets only, sorted by the pet actually in the house, with every joke written fresh for readers aged 9–12. One house rule before the first punchline: jokes are told about pets, never at them. No teasing a real animal, no scaring it for a laugh, and if a pet wanders off mid-joke, the joke waits. The pet is the audience’s honoured guest, not the prop.

Dogs: loyal, muddy, joke-ready

Dog jokes run on devotion and disaster: a dog will learn any trick, chase any ball, and still sit in the exact spot where the clean laundry landed. That combination — total loyalty, zero dignity — is the engine under every joke in this set.

  1. Why did the dog bring a notebook to the park? It was taking notes on advanced stick studies.
  2. What did the dog say after its first swimming lesson? I came, I splashed, I shook it everywhere.
  3. Why do dogs make terrible secret agents? Every mission ends with them reporting to the nearest biscuit.
  4. What is a puppy’s favourite school subject? Recess, with a minor in lunch.
  5. Why did the dog sit beside the washing machine? It was waiting for the spin cycle — the closest thing to chasing its tail by machine.
  6. What did the old dog teach the puppy about doors? Every door is an announcement. Bark first, investigate second.

Cats: the joke is that they are in charge

Cat jokes work in the opposite direction. The dog in a joke is trying hard; the cat in a joke is barely trying at all, and winning anyway. Tell these slowly, with a straight face — a cat joke delivered too eagerly has already lost to its subject.

  1. Why did the cat refuse to tidy its toys? Tidying implied the mess was not a deliberate design choice.
  2. What did the cat say about the new sofa? Finally, a scratching post with cushions.
  3. Why do cats make excellent critics? They can dismiss a whole performance with one slow blink.
  4. What is a kitten’s first rule of the house? If a box exists, the box is the destination. Everything else is travel.
  5. Why did the cat sit on the homework? It was the warmest page in the house, and seniority has privileges.
  6. What did the cat name its autobiography? Nine Drafts, All Perfect.

Small pets, big punchlines

Hamsters, rabbits and guinea pigs get a headed pet section in bigger collections — one list of 50 animal jokes runs farm, pet and sea sections side by side — but the small pets usually get the fewest jokes in it, which is unfair to animals with this much comic timing. A hamster stores snacks in its face. The material writes itself.

  1. Why did the hamster start a bakery? It already had the cheeks for storing the ingredients.
  2. What is a rabbit’s favourite meeting? Any meeting with snacks and an early finish — a hop-in, hop-out.
  3. Why did the guinea pig get the lead role in the play? Nobody else could deliver the squeak on cue.
  4. What did the hamster wheel say at retirement? It had been going in circles for the company for years.
  5. Why do rabbits make good detectives? They follow every lead right down the burrow.

Birds and fish: quiet pets, loud wordplay

The pets behind glass and bars are easy to overlook, and the pun collections agree — one list of more than 100 pet puns organises most of its material around dog puns and cat puns, with printable joke cards on the side. But a parrot that repeats punchlines and a goldfish with a three-room castle are wordplay waiting to happen.

  1. Why did the parrot ace the spelling test? It had been revising out loud all week — the whole house heard it.
  2. What did the goldfish say about its castle? Small, but the location is unbeatable and the rent is bubbles.
  3. Why did the budgie join the choir? It wanted its high notes officially recognised.
  4. What is a pet fish’s favourite subject? Current events. It keeps up with all of them, daily.
  5. Why did the parrot get a promotion? Management valued an employee who repeated instructions exactly.

Pick the joke by the pet

  • Telling jokes with a dog in the room → a dog joke, told while the dog is awake and unbothered. Dogs read tone, not words, so a cheerful telling is the whole show.
  • A cat is present and ignoring everyone → a cat joke, deadpan. The cat’s indifference is not a problem; it is the second punchline.
  • A friend has a small pet you have just met → a small-pet joke about the species, never about their specific animal’s looks or habits. Species jokes welcome; personal jokes wait for an invitation.
  • Quiet house, tank or cage nearby → a bird or fish joke, at normal speaking volume. Quiet pets get quiet tellings.
  • No pet in the house at all → the workshop below. Borrow a friend’s pet’s habit — with permission — or invent a dream pet and joke about the pet you would choose.

Make a joke from your own pet’s habit

Every joke on this page was built from one real pet habit plus one word doing two jobs — and that formula is yours now. Watch your pet for one ordinary day and write down the strangest true thing it does: sleeping in the laundry, shouting at dawn, inspecting every bag that enters the house. Then hunt the habit for a double-duty word — draft, current, check, press — and build the smallest scene where the word is literal. A cat that inspects bags becomes a security officer; a fish that follows the tank’s filter flow becomes a follower of current events. Test the result on the household first. If the people who know the pet laugh before the punchline is finished, because they have seen that habit a hundred times, the joke is done — it belongs to your house now, and no collection anywhere has it.
