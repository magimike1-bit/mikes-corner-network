Pirate Jokes for Kids Ages 9–12

🏴‍☠️ Pirates · 8 min read · Updated October 2026

Pirate jokes online come in treasure-chest-sized piles, and almost all of them are sorted the wrong way. One big list of 80 pirate jokes for kids is divided by format — one-liners, dad jokes, knock-knocks, puns and riddles — and a general-audience list of 76 pirate jokes runs in one flat line from sail puns to blizzard puns, while another famous collection hides its pirate section inside 260 jokes about everything. Format is how adults file jokes. Theme is how kids remember them. So this page sorts the pirate world the way a pirate would: by the parts of the job. Ships first, because everything happens on one. Treasure next, because treasure is the whole point. Parrots after that, because the parrot has opinions. And pirate talk last, with the real glossary built in — because a pirate joke told in proper pirate talk is twice the joke, and the talk itself is learnable in one page. Every joke here is original, clean, and built for ages 9–12: old enough for wordplay with moving parts, young enough that the golden rule still runs the ship. Kind first, clever second, arrr third.

Ship and sailing jokes

A pirate ship is a workplace, a home and a vehicle all at once, which makes it a perfect joke setting: everything on board has a job, and every job can go comically wrong. Ship jokes are the steadiest jokes in the collection because the props do the work — sails, anchors, decks and storms are funny before the punchline even arrives. Tell these with a hand shading the eyes, scanning an invisible horizon. It helps. Nobody knows why. It just does.

  1. Why did the pirate ship refuse to race? It had already won by a nautical mile in practice, and it did not want to make the rowboats feel bad.
  2. What did the anchor say to the ship? "I am the only reason you ever stay in one place. You are welcome, and also, sorry."
  3. Why did the pirate captain bring a ladder on board? The crew kept saying the standards were high, and somebody had to check.
  4. What do you call a ship that tells jokes at dinner? A real port entertainer — booked solid at every harbour on the coast.
  5. Why did the sail get promoted? It always rose to the occasion, caught every opportunity on the wind, and never once folded under pressure. Well. Once.
  6. What did the storm say to the pirate ship? "Nothing personal. The forecast made me do it. Blame the forecast."
  7. Why do pirates keep a map of the ship in the kitchen? So the cook can finally find out where the mess is supposed to be.
  8. What is a pirate ship's favourite subject at school? Sail-ence — mostly the study of wind, plus one very loud experiment per term.

Treasure jokes

Treasure is the pirate world's favourite word because it can mean gold, a map, a secret, or the good biscuits hidden from the crew — and jokes love a word with four meanings. Treasure jokes run on hiding, digging and guarding: three activities every 9–12-year-old already understands deeply from experience with siblings. The rule of the section is that the treasure is never the punchline by itself. The guarding is.

  1. Why did the pirate bury the treasure in the garden? The map said to plant it somewhere safe, and the pirate follows instructions literally. It is a whole personality.
  2. What did the treasure chest say to the lock? "We make a great team. You keep the secrets, and the crew keeps guessing the combination is 1-2-3."
  3. Why did the pirate draw a map to the kitchen? The biscuits were the real treasure all along, and some secrets are too important for memory alone.
  4. What do you call a pirate who shares the treasure map? Generous, lost, and somehow still the most popular pirate on the ship.
  5. Why did the gold coin go to school? It heard it could get ahead there — and every coin in the chest wanted a head start.
  6. What did one treasure map say to the other? "X-cellent work. Truly spot-on. The spot, in fact, is the whole point of you."
  7. Why did the pirate count the treasure three times? Once for the captain, once for the crew, and once because counting treasure is the best part and nobody wanted it to end.
  8. What is a pirate's favourite gemstone joke material? Anything with a good facet — a treasure joke should shine from at least two angles.

Parrot jokes

The parrot earned its place on the pirate's shoulder honestly, at least in the stories: in the 18th century, the picture of a pirate got a famous makeover, and pirates were drawn with eye patches, bandanas and a pet — usually a parrot or a monkey — at their side. Parrot jokes run on one glorious engine: the parrot repeats everything. A creature that says the last thing it heard, at the worst possible moment, in front of the whole crew, is not a pet. It is a comedy department with feathers.

  1. Why did the captain stop telling secrets near the parrot? The parrot attended every meeting twice — once in person, and once again at full volume in front of the crew.
  2. What did the parrot say to the mirror? "Finally, a conversation partner who agrees with everything. Excellent debating technique. Terrible new ideas."
  3. Why is the parrot the ship's worst referee? Every decision gets repeated back as a question, and both teams leave convinced the parrot agreed with them.
  4. What do you call a parrot who learns a joke properly? A stand-up comic with a built-in echo — the punchline lands twice, and the second landing is the funny one.
  5. Why did the parrot bring a notebook to dinner? Research. Everything said at that table would be quoted later, word for word, and accuracy matters.
  6. What did the parrot say when the ship changed course? "New plan, new plan!" — two minutes before the captain announced it, which raised some questions below deck.
  7. Why did the crew elect the parrot as lookout? It was the only crew member who could announce a sail on the horizon loudly enough to wake the entire ship. Twice.
  8. What is a parrot's favourite school subject? Echo-nomics — the study of how every sentence comes back, usually at a family dinner.

Pirate talk jokes (with the real glossary)

Now the section that teaches the accent. Real pirate-talk glossaries agree on the core words: ahoy is a greeting — it means hello. Avast means stop, or pay attention. Shiver me timbers shows shock or surprise, and batten down the hatches means tie everything down before a storm hits. Hearties means friends, a landlubber is someone who is uncomfortable at sea, and the Jolly Roger is the famous pirate flag with a skull and crossbones. Learn those seven and every joke below doubles in value, because the audience can hear the translation and the pirate version at the same time. There is even a whole day for it: Talk Like a Pirate Day is observed every year on September 19. It was dreamed up in 1995 by two friends, John Baur and Mark Summers, stayed a private joke for years, and spread in 2002 after humor columnist Dave Barry wrote about it. Mark the date. Practise the glossary. Then deploy these:

  1. What did the pirate teacher say on the first day of school? "Ahoy, class. Avast — that means eyes up here. There will be a quiz, and the quiz is also a treasure hunt."
  2. Why did the pirate say "shiver me timbers" at the library? The book about storms was overdue, the fine was enormous, and some shocks are too big for indoor words.
  3. What do pirate friends call each other at roll call? Hearties — every single morning, at full volume, whether the roll needs it or not.
  4. Why did the landlubber win the ship's dance contest? A career spent being uncomfortable at sea turns out to be excellent training for staying upright on a moving floor.
  5. What did the captain shout when the snack cupboard ran out? "Batten down the hatches — a storm is coming, and it is mostly the crew asking about snacks."
  6. Why does the Jolly Roger never win at hide-and-seek? A skull and crossbones on a black flag is an excellent flag and a terrible hiding strategy. It can be spotted from the next ocean.
  7. What did the pirate say when the joke landed perfectly? "Arrr-mazing." Then the parrot said it eleven more times, and by the eleventh it was somehow funnier.
  8. Why did the pirate practise saying ahoy in the mirror? A greeting is the first line of every adventure, and this pirate intended to absolutely nail the opening scene.

Which pirate joke for which moment

  • Boat trip, ferry ride, or beach day → a ship joke. The props are right there: point at a real sail or a real anchor and the joke tells itself.
  • Birthday party with a treasure hunt → a treasure joke, delivered at the exact clue where the hunt gets stuck. Comedy plus a hint is a party legend move.
  • A friend with a pet bird → a parrot joke, with the friend warned first. The golden rule applies: kind first, and a joke about a pet lands best when the owner is laughing hardest.
  • September 19, Talk Like a Pirate Day → a pirate talk joke, in the full voice, glossary and all. This is the one day a year the accent is basically homework.
  • Class presentation about the sea → a pirate talk joke with the glossary line included. Teachers reward a joke that smuggles in seven real definitions.

How to deliver a pirate joke like a captain

  1. Plant the feet and own the deck. Stand still, shoulders back, one hand on an imaginary wheel. A pirate joke told while shuffling loses half its authority before the first word.
  2. Do the voice at 70 percent. A light growl on the arrr words and full volume only on the punchline. One hundred percent pirate voice for eight jokes in a row is a sore throat with a theme.
  3. Pause before the punchline like it is a horizon. Ask the question, scan the room slowly, then deliver. The pause is where the audience builds the wrong answer — which is what makes the right one funny.
  4. Let the parrot have the encore. Repeat the punchline once, in a squawky voice, as if a parrot just said it again. One repeat is a callback. Two is a negotiation. Stop at one.
  5. End with the glossary joke. Save a pirate talk joke for last and translate it as it lands. The set finishes, the audience leaves knowing seven real pirate words, and the performance counts as education.

What success looks like: somebody in the audience says "avast" correctly in a sentence within the hour — and the parrot encore gets requested by name at the next gathering.

Make your own pirate pun (workshop)

Pirate vocabulary is a pun workshop that stocks itself, because pirate words keep colliding with ordinary words: sail sounds like sale, deck is both a ship's floor and a pack of cards, and arrr hides inside ordinary words like garden, harbour and argument. The method is three steps. First, pick a pirate word from the glossary or the ship — sail, deck, anchor, hearty, ahoy. Second, write the most ordinary sentence possible using its everyday twin: the shop is having a sale, the cards are on the table, the garden needs watering. Third, move the sentence on board: why did the pirate love the shop's big event? Because everything was on sail. If the joke needs a map and a legend to explain, the two meanings are too far apart — pick a closer word and rebuild. The finest pirate puns feel like the sea was named by a comedian in the first place, and with seven glossary words and a whole ship of props, the raw material never runs out. That, me hearties, is the real treasure.

FAQ

When is Talk Like a Pirate Day? Every year on September 19. Two friends, John Baur and Mark Summers, invented it in 1995, and it spread widely after humor columnist Dave Barry wrote about it in 2002.

What does "shiver me timbers" actually mean? It is an expression of shock or surprise — the pirate way of saying "well, that is astonishing." Timbers are the wooden frames of a ship, so the phrase pictures the whole ship shuddering. Use it for excellent surprises only.

What is the easiest pirate joke to invent first? A parrot echo joke. Give any ordinary announcement — dinner is ready, the bus is here — to a parrot, and let the repeat arrive at the worst moment. The engine is built in; the writer just chooses the announcement.
