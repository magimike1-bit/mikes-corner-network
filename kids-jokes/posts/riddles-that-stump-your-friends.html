Riddles That'll Stump Your Friends

Riddles · 6 min read

Riddles are jokes with a puzzle attached: your friends do the thinking, and you get to watch them squirm. Here are 30 tricky-but-fair riddles. Try each one before peeking at the answer — and give your friends the same chance.

Warm-ups

  1. What has a face and two hands but no arms or legs? A clock.
  2. What goes up but never comes down? Your age.
  3. What has keys but can't open a single door? A piano.
  4. What can you catch but never throw? A cold.
  5. What has a head and a tail but no body? A coin.

Getting trickier

  1. What runs all around the backyard but never moves? A fence.
  2. What gets wetter the more it dries? A towel.
  3. What has many teeth but can't bite? A comb.
  4. What belongs to you but other people use it more than you do? Your name.
  5. The more of me there is, the less you see. What am I? Darkness.
  6. What has one eye but can't see? A needle.
  7. What can travel around the world without leaving its corner? A stamp.
  8. What is full of holes but still holds water? A sponge.
  9. What goes through towns and over hills but never moves? A road.
  10. What has a neck but no head? A bottle.

Brain benders

  1. I'm tall when I'm young and short when I'm old. What am I? A candle.
  2. What can fill a room but takes up no space? Light.
  3. What question can you never answer yes to? "Are you asleep?"
  4. What gets bigger the more you take away? A hole.
  5. You see a boat filled with people. It hasn't sunk, but when you look again, there isn't a single person on the boat. Why? Everyone on board was married — not single!
  6. What has thirteen hearts but no other organs? A deck of cards.
  7. What comes once in a minute, twice in a moment, but never in a thousand years? The letter M.
  8. David's father has three sons: Snap, Crackle, and...? David!
  9. What can you hold in your right hand but never in your left? Your left elbow.
  10. Forward I'm heavy, but backward I'm not. What am I? The word "ton."
  11. What has a thumb and four fingers but isn't alive? A glove.
  12. What kind of room has no doors or windows? A mushroom.
  13. What starts with T, ends with T, and has T in it? A teapot.
  14. What goes up and down but doesn't move? A staircase.
  15. What word is spelled incorrectly in every dictionary? Incorrectly.

How to make your own riddles

The best riddles describe something ordinary in a strange way. Pick an everyday object — a chair, a shoe, a toothbrush — and write three true things about it that sound mysterious. "It has legs but never walks" is a chair pretending to be a riddle. Test yours on a friend: if they guess it after a few tries, you did it right.

A great riddle is like a locked box with the key hidden in plain sight. The answer is always something the solver already knows — your job is to make them see it sideways. Try mixing categories: compare an object to an animal, or describe a sound as a color. The weirder the description, the more satisfying the "aha!" moment.

And if you want a riddle that really earns its reputation, hide the answer in the question itself, like the teapot riddle above. Solvers will groan when they hear it — and that's the sound of a riddle done right.
