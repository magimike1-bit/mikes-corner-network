Superhero Jokes for Kids Ages 9 to 12

🦸 Superheroes · 7 min read · Updated November 2026

What makes a superhero joke work for a 9 to 12 reader, and not just a younger one? The wordplay has to carry it. One large collection rounds up over 120 superhero jokes and sorts them by franchise, into Marvel jokes, X-Men jokes, DC jokes and knock-knocks. Another offers over 167 jokes for all ages, with the kids section tucked inside. Franchise sorting helps only if you already know every hero, and all ages sorting helps nobody check cleanliness: one list aimed at families still slips in a punchline about a hero caught without his suit. The set below takes a different route. Every hero here is a made up, generic hero, every joke is clean from start to finish, and the jokes are grouped by pun family, so you can raid the family you like best and learn how it is built.

Capes, masks and costumes

Costume jokes are the gentlest family in the set, because everybody understands getting dressed, even if the outfit in question can fly. A cape is a blanket with ambitions. A mask is a secret you wear on your face. The humour comes from treating these dramatic items like ordinary clothing: they tear, they wrinkle, they get left by the door, and they still have to be ready before breakfast. Start here with younger listeners in the group, then move on to the powers.

  1. What did the cape say to the superhero? “I have got your back, literally, on every single flight.”
  2. Why did the superhero bring a spare mask to school? In case the first one had to keep a secret during show and tell.
  3. Why are superhero boots so confident? They land on their feet every time, even on rainy pavement.
  4. What do you call a superhero who is brilliant at sewing? Captain Stitch, first to arrive whenever a cape tears.
  5. Why did the superhero iron her costume before breakfast? A hero with a wrinkled cape cannot take off smoothly.
  6. What did the gloves say to the mask? “High five, partner. We cover for each other.”
  7. Why did the young hero practise tying his cape in front of a mirror? He wanted his knot skills super tight before his first big breeze.

Flying, strength and super speed

Power jokes run on exaggeration with a seatbelt. A hero who can lift anything still has to carry the shopping. A hero who can fly still has to land somewhere sensible. A hero who is faster than everyone still has to wait for the slowest part of the day, which is usually a queue. The laugh lands when the amazing power meets the most ordinary problem in the room, so read the setup plainly and let the clash do the work.

  1. Why did the flying hero carry a map? Even a sky expert likes to know which cloud is which on a foggy morning.
  2. What do you call a hero who can lift anything at all? Captain Carry, whose motto is no suitcase left behind.
  3. Why did the fast hero win the spelling bee? He raced through the alphabet and still paused to spell each word properly.
  4. What does a super strong hero put in a smoothie? Extra grip, so the glass never slips during a rescue.
  5. Why did the hero with super hearing wear earmuffs at lunch? The cafeteria echo had given away the surprise dessert three days running.
  6. What is a flying hero’s favourite board game? Anything with a landing strip, with bonus points when the runway is clear.
  7. Why did the speedy hero keep a slow, careful notebook? Even the fastest rescuer writes thank you notes by hand.

Everyday hero life

Between rescues, a hero still has homework, lunch and a garden. This family is built for family tables, because the settings need no explaining at all. A hero sandwich does double duty as a lunch and a pun at once. A hideout is just a den with better lighting. Keep these jokes warm: the hero is capable and kind, never the punchline for being clumsy or slow, and the cat in the apple tree is a rescue, not a teasing target.

  1. What does a superhero pack for lunch? A hero sandwich, extra pickles, and a note that says be kind out there.
  2. Why did the superhero finish her homework before patrol? Saving the day is easier once the fractions are already defeated.
  3. What did the hideout say to the hero? “Welcome home. The snacks are stocked and the cape hook is empty.”
  4. Why did the superhero bring a ladder to the garden? The rescue cat was in the apple tree again, and flying in would have scared the apples.
  5. What is a superhero’s favourite after school club? The kindness club. Meetings are short and everybody leaves stronger.
  6. Why did the young hero set two alarms? One for waking up, and one for remembering that capes and bicycles do not mix.

Knock-knocks and one-liners

Knock-knock jokes are built for doorways, queues and the walk between classrooms. Hero words hide neatly inside the answer line: hero, cape and zoom all sound like the start of a longer sentence, which is exactly the trick. Say the answer out loud before the punchline, commit to both voices, and keep it moving. A knock-knock that pauses to explain itself has already landed, just without the laugh.

  1. Knock, knock. Who’s there? Hero. Hero who? Hero we go again, another door, another daring knock.
  2. Knock, knock. Who’s there? Cape. Cape who? Cape calm, the rescue is nearly here and the kettle is on.
  3. Knock, knock. Who’s there? Zoom. Zoom who? Zoom in, the tiny hero on the poster is the bravest one of all.
  4. Why do superheroes make good librarians? They rescue overdue books and return them without a single bent page.
  5. What did the city say to the superhero at bedtime? “Thanks for today. Same time tomorrow, and please land quietly.”

Which superhero joke for which moment

  • A mixed-age family table → an everyday hero life joke. Homework, lunch and gardens need no franchise knowledge at all.
  • A friend who loves comics → a power joke. Flying, strength and speed are that reader’s first language.
  • A card for a younger cousin → a knock-knock. It fits in two lines and invites the reader to say half of it aloud.
  • Dressing up or costume day → a cape or mask joke. The outfit in front of you does half the setup work.
  • Only ten seconds → a one-liner. Skip the setup-heavy jokes; a short line survives a noisy hallway.

Write your own superhero joke in three steps

  1. Pick a family. Costume, power, or everyday life, one family per joke. Two families in one punchline tangle the cape.
  2. List three double-meaning words. Cover (a blanket, and to protect), carry (to lift, and to help a team), land (to touch down, and a place). Pick the pair that surprises you most.
  3. Put a hero in an everyday scene. A library, a lunch queue, a rainy garden. Write the plain sentence first, then let the second meaning deliver the punchline last.

What success looks like: a listener asks for the joke again so they can retell it at home. A superhero joke that gets retold has done its rescue for the day.
