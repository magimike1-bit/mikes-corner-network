Thanksgiving Jokes for Kids Ages 9–12

🦃 Thanksgiving · 8 min read · Updated October 2026

Thanksgiving has a timing problem that jokes are perfectly built to solve: the food takes hours, the guests arrive early, and the gap between "when is dinner?" and dinner is the longest afternoon of the year. Online, the joke supply does not match the assignment. One collection offers 111 clean Thanksgiving jokes in a single long list, another packs in 151 jokes for all ages at once, and a third is turkey jokes only, start to finish — funny, but filed like a grocery bag, everything in together. This page sorts the holiday into the four themes a 9–12-year-old actually needs: turkey jokes, because the turkey is the star; pie and feast jokes, because dessert is the second star and it knows it; parade jokes, with the real parade story underneath them; and grateful jokes, the section almost every other list skips, even though gratitude is the entire point of the day. Then comes the practical half other lists leave out entirely: which joke belongs in a place card, which one opens the table, and how to run a joke round while the gravy is still warm. Every joke is original, clean, and kind — the golden rule holds even when the turkey cannot hear the jokes. Especially then.

Turkey jokes

The turkey is the hardest-working character in holiday comedy: it has drumsticks, it gobbles, it is stuffed, and it has a completely reasonable suspicion about the month of November. Turkey jokes run on those four facts, recombined. The craft rule of the section is sympathy — the turkey in these jokes is clever, organised and slightly ahead of everyone else at the table. A hero turkey gets bigger laughs than a helpless one, and it keeps the jokes kind, which is the whole brand.

  1. Why did the turkey start a calendar club? November kept arriving without any warning at all, and this turkey intended to see it coming next year.
  2. What did the turkey say to the drum set? "Impressive collection. The original models come with feathers and a built-in escape plan."
  3. Why did the turkey bring a suitcase to the farm gate? It had read the guest list for Thursday, checked the menu twice, and booked itself a lovely weekend elsewhere.
  4. What is a turkey's favourite school subject? His-story — specifically the chapter where the turkey is described as a magnificent, intelligent bird and everyone agrees to have sandwiches.
  5. Why did the turkey win the talent show? It performed its famous disappearing act in November, and the judges are still applauding an empty stage.
  6. What did the turkey say when the cranberry sauce arrived? "Bold move, showing up in red. Respect. This table respects confidence."
  7. Why do turkeys make excellent librarians? They are world champions of the quiet gobble — every recommendation delivered at exactly library volume.
  8. What is a turkey's favourite dance? The gobble-stop: two steps forward, one dramatic pause, and a head bob that brings the whole barn down.

Pie and feast jokes

Feast jokes cover the supporting cast: pumpkin pie, cranberry sauce, stuffing, mashed potatoes and the gravy that holds the whole production together. These are the best jokes for the waiting hours, because every target is sitting on the counter in plain view — a joke with the actual pie in the room needs no setup at all. Pie jokes get their own energy because pie is dessert, and dessert jokes are told by an audience that has already decided to love the punchline.

  1. Why did the pumpkin pie get a standing ovation? It arrived sliced, spiced and completely un-squash-able — a performance the whole table had been waiting for since October.
  2. What did the cranberry sauce say to the turkey? "We meet once a year, we never write, and somehow this is still the best tradition on the table."
  3. Why did the mashed potatoes apply for a promotion? Years of being called a side dish, and frankly, the gravy had been taking all the credit.
  4. What do you call stuffing that tells jokes? A seasoned performer — literally seasoned, twice, with sage.
  5. Why did the gravy boat get invited everywhere? It pours itself into every conversation smoothly and makes everything at the table a little better.
  6. What did the sweet potato say to the marshmallows? "Thanks for the support up there. This casserole is a team effort and the team is delicious."
  7. Why did the pie refuse to be cut into eight slices? It had done the maths, counted the guests twice, and insisted on a fairer crust-ribution for the smallest cousins.
  8. What is a pumpkin's favourite maths joke? Pumpkin pi, of course — a dessert and a number at the same time, which makes it the smartest course of the entire meal.

Parade jokes (with the real parade story)

Parade jokes deserve their real history, because the history is funnier than most fiction. The Library of Congress records that the first Macy's parade was held on Thanksgiving Day, November 27, 1924 — and it was originally promoted as the Macy's Christmas Parade. Store employees organised it, and the stars of the first march were live animals from the Central Park Zoo, not balloons. The giant balloons the parade is famous for only arrived in 1927, and the parade was first televised nationally in 1947. One more record for the joke-teller's pocket: Snoopy is the character with the most balloons in parade history. So the parade began with real zoo animals, upgraded to floating cartoons three years later, and eventually put a beagle in the sky more often than anyone else. Tell the jokes with that in mind — they are barely exaggerating.

  1. Why did the parade balloon apply for a height permit? Its career goals were ambitious, its role model was a flying beagle, and the sky had an opening.
  2. What did the marching band say to the turkey balloon? "Stay in formation, stay floating, and if the wind picks up, you are officially the parade's first flying drumstick."
  3. Why did the zoo animals retire from the parade after 1924? Excellent reviews, enormous crowds, and one shared conclusion: the balloons could have the sky. The ground animals had done their parade.
  4. What do you call a parade that starts a day early? A rehearsal with an audience problem — ten thousand people showed up anyway, because a Thanksgiving parade cannot be kept secret.
  5. Why did the television camera love the parade? It first broadcast the balloons nationally in 1947 and has had the best seat in the country ever since: indoors, warm, with snacks.
  6. What did the float say to the balloon? "You get the skyline photos. The ground route gets the marching bands. This parade has room for both kinds of famous."
  7. Why is the parade route the best classroom in November? History you can watch: a 1924 idea, 1927 balloons, and a beagle with the all-time appearance record, all marching past in one morning.

Grateful jokes

The section the big lists skip — and the one this holiday was invented for. Grateful jokes are thank-you notes wearing a punchline: they work in cards, across the table, and in the quiet minute when somebody asks what everyone is thankful for and the table needs a laugh before it needs a speech. They also travel between both Thanksgivings on the calendar. The Old Farmer's Almanac notes that Canadian Thanksgiving falls on the second Monday in October — October 12 in 2026 — while American Thanksgiving lands on the fourth Thursday in November, November 26 in 2026, and that both countries serve the same classic line-up of turkey, stuffing, potatoes, cranberry sauce and pumpkin pie. Two dates, one menu, and gratitude that works in both months:

  1. What did the thank-you card say to the envelope? "Thanks for holding this together. Literally. The gratitude is sealed because of you."
  2. Why did the gratitude list win the school award? It was outstanding in its field — a field full of pumpkins, which the scarecrow also had opinions about.
  3. What do you call a turkey who writes thank-you notes? Grateful and organised: the only guest whose card arrives before the leftovers run out.
  4. Why did the Canadian turkey send its thanks in October? Its Thanksgiving comes six weeks earlier, and gratitude, like pie, is best served fresh rather than saved.
  5. What did one place setting say to the whole table? "Thankful for every seat here — especially the one that tells the jokes between courses."
  6. Why is a grateful joke the best dessert? Zero dishes, unlimited servings, and every guest can take a second helping home in their pocket.
  7. What did the pumpkin say at the gratitude round? "Thankful for good soil, good rain, and a promotion to pie. It has been a very full season."
  8. Why did the leftovers feel appreciated? They got thanked twice: once at dinner, and once at lunch the next day, which is the highest honour in the fridge.

Which Thanksgiving joke for which card or table moment

  • Place card at each seat → a grateful joke, short and warm. Guests find it before dinner, and the table starts the meal already smiling.
  • Thank-you card after the holiday → the thank-you card joke itself. A joke about gratitude, inside a card about gratitude: the recipient will explain it to somebody, guaranteed.
  • Lunchbox note in Thanksgiving week → a turkey joke. Short, heroic-turkey energy is exactly right next to a sandwich.
  • Parade on television in the morning → a parade joke, timed to a balloon actually on screen. A joke about a floating beagle lands hardest while a floating beagle is floating.
  • The long wait before dinner → a pie joke, told within sight of the actual pie. Props beat delivery, every single time.
  • The gratitude round at the table → a grateful joke offered as the answer itself. "Thankful for jokes that feed everybody" has closed more rounds than any speech.

How to run a Thanksgiving joke round at the table

  1. Claim the slot between courses. The natural pause after the main course, before dessert, is the joke round's home: nobody is carving, nobody is serving, and every seat is still full.
  2. Set the order by seat, not by volunteering. Going around the table means the quiet cousins get a turn without having to fight the loud uncle for the floor. One joke each, no auditions.
  3. Offer the menu to anyone who freezes. Keep this page's shortest jokes ready as loaners — the gravy boat, the thank-you card, the parade balloon. A borrowed joke told bravely beats a perfect joke never told.
  4. Protect the kind rule out loud. Announce it at the start: jokes about the food, the turkey and the parade; never jokes about a person at the table. Said once, cheerfully, it runs the whole round by itself.
  5. Close with a grateful joke and mean it. The last joke of the round is the thank-you: to the cooks, the hosts and the person who drove the farthest. The laugh and the thanks land together, which is the entire holiday in one move.

What success looks like: dessert arrives to a table still retelling the gravy boat joke — and at least one guest asks for a joke in writing, for a card, before the coats come out.

Make your own Thanksgiving pun (workshop)

Thanksgiving vocabulary is a pun workshop with the ingredients already chopped: gobble doubles as eating fast, stuffed means both full and filling, grateful hides a grater in it, and pi turns a pumpkin dessert into a maths lesson. The method is three steps. First, pick a feast word with a double life — stuffed, gobble, grate, slice, or roll. Second, write the plainest possible sentence with the everyday meaning: the library book was full, the baker grated the cheese, the wheel began to roll. Third, serve the sentence at the table: why did the dinner guest thank the cookbook? It was completely stuffed with good ideas. If the pun needs the whole recipe explained, the two meanings are too far apart — pick a closer word and rebuild. The test of a finished Thanksgiving pun is the card test: written on a place card, in one line, it should still be funny to somebody who has not eaten yet. Hunger is the strictest editor there is.

FAQ

Why do Canada and the United States celebrate Thanksgiving on different dates? They simply keep different calendar rules: Canada's Thanksgiving falls on the second Monday in October and the American holiday on the fourth Thursday in November — in 2026, October 12 and November 26. Both tables, helpfully for joke writers, serve turkey, stuffing, potatoes, cranberry sauce and pumpkin pie.

Did the first Thanksgiving parade really have zoo animals instead of balloons? Yes — the Library of Congress records that the first Macy's parade, on November 27, 1924, featured live animals from the Central Park Zoo, organised by store employees. The giant balloons arrived in 1927, national television in 1947, and Snoopy went on to collect the most balloons of any character in parade history.

What is the easiest Thanksgiving joke to invent first? A feast prop joke. Look at the counter, pick the dish with the best double-meaning name — gravy boat, sweet potato, dinner roll — and give it one ordinary human errand. The prop does half the writing; the errand does the rest.
