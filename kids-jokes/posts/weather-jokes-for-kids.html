Weather Jokes for Kids Ages 9–12

🌦️ Weather · 9 min read · Updated October 2026

Weather jokes are the only jokes you can verify by looking out the window. The facts behind them are better than the jokes: thunder is the sound of air exploding outward around a lightning bolt. Hail is a raindrop that got stuck in an elevator between freezing and falling. Every snowflake has six sides, and no two in history have been exactly alike. This collection is written fresh for ages 9–12, sorted by forecast, with each section opening on a real weather fact — because a joke with a fact inside is two presents in one box. After the jokes: a storm-chaser delivery guide, quiz games, and a cloud-pun workshop. Every joke below is original writing for this page — no scraped lists, ever. And one real rule rides along: when thunder roars, go indoors.

Thunder and lightning jokes (the storm section)

The fact first: lightning is a giant spark of electricity, and thunder is the boom the air makes when that spark superheats it — the air expands so fast it shatters into sound. Count the seconds between the flash and the boom and you can tell how far away the storm is: about three seconds per kilometre. Now the jokes.

  1. Why did the lightning get detention? It had a shocking attitude and kept striking without raising its hand.
  2. What did the thunder say to the lightning? "You always have to make an entrance — I will handle the applause."
  3. Why are thunderstorms terrible secret-keepers? They always let out a little thunder.
  4. What is a cloud's favourite instrument? The thunder drums.
  5. Why did the kid bring a ladder to the thunderstorm? He heard the lightning was striking and wanted a front-row seat. (Do NOT do this. Lightning and ladders are a terrible team.)
  6. What do you call a polite thunderstorm? One that knocks — thunder — before it comes in.
  7. Why did the lightning break up with the rain? Too many sparks, not enough substance.

Rain and rainbow jokes

The fact: weather starts with the sun heating the air unevenly — warm air rises, cool air rushes in, and that rush is wind. Water evaporates, rises, cools, and gathers into clouds; when a cloud is holding more water than it can carry, it rains. And when the sun shines while it rains, the light bends through the falling droplets and paints a rainbow. Jokes:

  1. What does a cloud wear under its raincoat? Thunder-wear.
  2. Why did the raindrop go to therapy? It was feeling a little down — falling will do that.
  3. What is a rainbow's favourite subject? Prism-ary colours.
  4. Why did the umbrella apply for a job? It was tired of just hanging around waiting for rain.
  5. What do you call rain that tells jokes? Drizzle with a punchline.
  6. Why did the rainbow blush? It saw the sun changing.
  7. What did one raindrop say to the other? "Two's company, three's a cloud."

Snow, hail, and wind jokes

The facts: snow crystals grow when temperatures drop below freezing, and while no two snowflakes have ever matched, every single one grows six sides. Hail is tougher: a raindrop gets blown high into a freezing cloud, ices over, starts to fall, gets hurled back up, and ices again — layer after layer until it is too heavy to stay up. Wind is simply air on the move. Jokes:

  1. Why are snowflakes so good at keeping secrets? Each one is one of a kind — and they never repeat themselves.
  2. What do you call a snowman with a six-pack? An abdominal snowman.
  3. Why did the hailstone go to the gym? It wanted to bulk up layer by layer.
  4. What is the wind's favourite game? Hide and gust-seek.
  5. Why did the snowman call in sick? He was feeling a little flaky.
  6. What do you call a grumpy snowstorm? A bliz-zard of complaints.
  7. Why did the kite break up with the wind? It needed some space — the wind was too clingy.
  8. What did the snow say to the road? "I am just passing through — try to keep your cool."

Storm-chaser delivery guide

  1. Sound effects sell storms. Rumble the thunder joke, crack the lightning one. The audience laughs at the performance as much as the punchline.
  2. Fact first, joke second. "Thunder is exploding air" then the thunder joke — the fact earns the laugh twice.
  3. Seasonal timing. Thunder jokes in summer, snow jokes at the first flake, rainbow jokes after any sun-shower. A joke told under the right sky is twice as funny.
  4. Trade, don't stack. One joke buys one joke — the cafeteria exchange rate applies to weather too.

What success looks like: someone counts "one-Mississippi, two-Mississippi" at the next storm — the thunder fact landed — and repeats the hailstone gym joke at dinner.

Two quiz games

Flash-to-boom: at the next real storm, everyone counts seconds between lightning and thunder; closest guess to the storm's distance wins. Fact or fib: "Every snowflake has six sides," "Hail forms in summer clouds," "Rainbows appear when it rains at night" — vote fact or fib, then check this page.

Make-your-own cloud pun workshop

Cloud puns follow a formula: weather word + everyday word that sounds like it. Thunder → "thunder-wear" (underwear). Drizzle → "drizzle with a punchline." Hail → "hail yeah." Step one: list weather words (cloud, rain, storm, fog, frost, mist). Step two: say each slowly and listen for sound-alikes. Step three: build the joke so the weather fact from this page is the setup. Step four: test on one friend — if they groan, it worked.

Weather words worth stealing (a tiny glossary that makes jokes better)

Comics steal vocabulary the way chefs steal recipes, so here is a pantry of real weather words, each one a joke waiting for a setup. A meteorologist is a scientist who studies weather — not meteors, despite the name, which makes the word itself the oldest weather joke in the book. Precipitation is the official word for any water falling from the sky: rain, snow, hail, and sleet all count, and saying "precipitation" in a joke setup instantly makes a nine-year-old sound like a news anchor. A cumulonimbus is the tall, dark cloud that builds thunderstorms — the skyscraper of clouds, and the longest word on this page, worth two bonus points in any spelling game. Updrafts are the rising air currents inside storm clouds that carry raindrops back up to freeze into hail, layer after layer. And refraction is the bending of light — the trick that splits sunlight into a rainbow when it passes through falling rain. Try the upgrade move: take any joke from this page and rebuild it with the official word swapped in. "Why did the cumulonimbus get detention?" is the same joke wearing a lab coat, and lab coats, scientifically, make punchlines 40 percent funnier. (That last statistic is a joke. The rest of the glossary is real.)

FAQ

What should I actually do in a thunderstorm? The real rule, no joke attached: when thunder roars, go indoors. Stay away from tall lonely trees, metal fences, and open water until 30 minutes after the last thunder.

Are these okay for a weather-themed birthday party? Perfect — print the quiz games, run fact-or-fib between cake and presents, and award the silliest pun.
