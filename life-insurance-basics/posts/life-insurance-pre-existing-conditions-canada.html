Life insurance with a pre-existing condition in Canada: ratings, simplified issue, and the two year rule

Under the hood · By Penny Hughes · Published November 21, 2026

A pre-existing condition does not usually make life insurance impossible in Canada. It changes which of three routes you travel: a fully underwritten policy that prices your health in detail, a simplified issue policy that asks a short list of health questions and skips the exam, or a guaranteed issue policy that asks nothing at all and pays for that silence with lower coverage and a two year rule. The routes differ most in one place readers rarely compare directly: when full coverage actually begins.

Three routes, compared before anything else

Simplified issue is the middle route most applicants should understand first. It asks a limited set of health questions, varies them by insurer, and can provide coverage from day one. Guaranteed issue asks nothing, qualifies you on age alone, and typically offers $25,000 to $100,000 of coverage, at a higher price for less protection. Between them sits one more route worth knowing: accelerated issue, which waives the medical exam for applicants whose profile and questionnaire answers qualify, typically offers up to $1 million of coverage, and screens precisely for pre-existing conditions in its questions. Skipping the exam is not the same as skipping the questions.

How a rating changes the price

On the underwritten route, a condition that raises risk often produces a rating rather than a refusal. A rating means the policy is issued at a higher premium than the standard rate for the same coverage. Either way, the coverage itself is real coverage from the start date, which is the underwritten route's quiet advantage over guaranteed issue. Two further possibilities sit beside a rating: a modified policy, or an exclusion for a specific ailment, where the policy pays for other causes of death but not the named one. Read an exclusion literally before accepting it; an exclusion that names the very condition you are worried about changes what you are actually buying.

One hopeful detail is worth stating plainly: a rating may be reconsidered if your health improves. A condition that comes under control, sustained over time, can support a request to revisit the extra premium. It is not automatic and it is not quick, but it means the price set in a bad health year is not necessarily the price for life.

What underwriters actually weigh

Underwriters looking at a pre-existing condition ask a consistent set of questions, and knowing them turns a vague worry into a checklist you can prepare for. When did the condition start? Is it under control? When did treatment last occur? Have you fully recovered? Are any medical tests still pending? Current health carries the most weight in the assessment, ahead of the bare fact that a diagnosis exists. Pending tests are the item applicants most often underestimate: an unresolved investigation can stall an application that a completed one, even with an unwelcome result, would let an underwriter price. Where the answers are complicated, an advisor who places cases with several insurers can matter, because one insurer's rating is another insurer's standard rate often enough that a single application is a sample of one.

Guaranteed issue and the two year rule, in the insurer's own terms

Guaranteed issue is the route of last resort, and its central term deserves exact language rather than the usual phrase waiting period. Manulife's CoverMe guaranteed issue policy, open to Canadian residents aged 40 to 75 for $5,000 to $25,000 of coverage with no health questions and no medical exam, states the limitation this way: benefits will not be paid if death results, directly or indirectly, from a pre-existing condition within two years of the policy's effective date. In that case the premiums paid are returned, plus 10 percent interest compounded annually. Death by suicide in that window returns the premiums without interest. The same document defines a pre-existing condition broadly: any injury, illness or physical condition, diagnosed or not, for which medical treatment was sought, recommended, required or obtained, or for which drugs were prescribed or taken, within the two years before the policy took effect.

Read that definition twice. It reaches back two years before the policy and forward two years into it, and it captures conditions that were treated without ever being formally diagnosed. A guaranteed issue policy bought today is therefore best understood as full coverage from the third year, plus a premium refund arrangement for the first two. For a household that needs a small amount for final expenses and can get nothing else, that can still be the right buy, entered with open eyes. The policy also carries a 30 day money back guarantee from issue, which is the window to re-read the limitation against your own medical history while a refund is still simple.

A decision order that protects your options

Sequence matters more than product choice here. Start with the underwritten route if your condition is stable and documented, because it offers the most coverage per dollar and full coverage from approval. Keep simplified issue as the genuine second step, not a shortcut taken out of dread of the exam; its day one coverage is a real advantage over guaranteed issue. Take guaranteed issue when the other routes have actually closed, or when the amount needed is small and final expense shaped. And whatever the route, answer every question asked completely and accurately. A condition disclosed and priced is a policy that pays. A condition left off an application that asked about it is a claim dispute waiting for the worst possible moment to happen.
