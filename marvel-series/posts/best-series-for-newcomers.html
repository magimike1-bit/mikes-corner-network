The best Marvel series for newcomers

Recommendations · 5 min read

Some Marvel shows assume you've done your homework. These don't — or at least, they don't assume much of it. Each pick below works as a standalone experience, with a note on what (if anything) helps going in. No spoilers beyond basic premises.

WandaVision

Homework required: almost none, though knowing who Wanda and Vision are from the films adds a lot. The show deliberately starts in mystery-box mode, and new viewers are actually in the best position — everyone was confused at first, including the movie fans. It's a strange, funny, eventually heartbreaking story about grief wearing a sitcom costume. If you only watch one show to see what Marvel TV is capable of, make it this one.

Daredevil

Homework required: zero. A blind lawyer fights crime in Hell's Kitchen, and the show barely acknowledges the wider universe. It's the strongest of the street-level corner — a crime drama that happens to have superheroes in it. The fight choreography is legendary, and the characters are written like actual people. If your taste runs more prestige TV than blockbuster, start here.

Ms. Marvel

Homework required: very little. A teenage fan of superheroes in Jersey City discovers she has powers of her own. It's warm, funny, and culturally specific in the best way — part coming-of-age story, part origin story. Knowing who Captain Marvel is helps, but the show explains what you need. Easily the most joyful series on this list.

What If...?

Homework required: some, but it's the fun kind. Each animated episode asks "what if this one thing in the movies went differently?" The more films you've seen, the more you get — but every episode is self-contained, so you can dip in and out. It's the perfect palate cleanser between longer series, and a great way to see which corners of the universe interest you.

Hawkeye

Homework required: minimal. A retired Avenger stuck in New York at Christmas, reluctantly mentoring a new hero. It's light, funny, and small-scale — a holiday action-comedy that doesn't ask you to follow five years of plot threads. Think of it as the Marvel equivalent of a cozy crime novel.

Jessica Jones

Homework required: zero. A private investigator with super-strength takes cases in a seedy New York. Fair warning: this one is dark and deals with heavy themes — it's closer to a noir thriller than a superhero show. But it's also one of the best-written things Marvel has ever put on screen, and it stands entirely on its own.

The honorable-mention rule

You might notice Loki isn't on this list. That's not a quality judgment — it's a sequencing judgment. Loki is excellent, but it pays off more if you've watched a few films first, and it sets up concepts that echo through later shows. Watch it second or third, not first.

Same goes for The Falcon and the Winter Soldier — solid, but it works best when you already care about its two leads from the movies. Starting with a show you have to "earn" is the fastest way to bounce off.

Give it two episodes

One more piece of advice before you start: Marvel series often open slowly. Mystery-driven shows like WandaVision can feel confusing by design in the early going, and character dramas like Daredevil spend their first episode setting the board. That's normal — and it's why the two-episode rule is the fairest test. If the premise interests you, watch two episodes before deciding. Almost every series on this list gets significantly better in its second hour, and plenty of viewers who bounced off episode one later call the same show a favorite. The corollary: if two episodes in you still aren't feeling it, that's a legitimate verdict, not a failure of attention. Move on to another pick on this list — there's always another door in.

How to pick your first

Match the show to your taste, not the continuity chart:

  • Prestige crime drama → Daredevil or Jessica Jones
  • Warm and funny → Ms. Marvel or Hawkeye
  • Weird and ambitious → WandaVision
  • Bite-sized and experimental → What If...?

Once you've finished one, pick your next show by mood — or dig into how the shows connect if you're ready for the bigger picture.
