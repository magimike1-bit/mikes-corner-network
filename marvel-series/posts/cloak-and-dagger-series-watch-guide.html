Cloak & Dagger series watch guide: 20 episodes in New Orleans, and the Runaways crossover explained

Viewing guide · 6 min read

What happens when a Marvel series gets a proper city, a slow burn romance, and almost no connection to the films, then quietly disappears after two seasons? You get Marvel's Cloak & Dagger, the Freeform series that critics rated well, audiences largely missed, and new viewers now keep tripping over in the Disney+ catalogue with no idea whether it is a teen drama, a superhero show, or both. It is both, and the episode count makes the decision easy to test.

The facts in one box

Cloak & Dagger was created by Joe Pokaski for Freeform, based on the Marvel Comics characters created by Bill Mantlo and Ed Hannigan, and produced with Marvel Television. Olivia Holt stars as Tandy Bowen, called Dagger, and Aubrey Joseph stars as Tyrone Johnson, called Cloak. The series runs two seasons of ten episodes each, 20 in total, first airing June 7, 2018 and ending May 30, 2019. Episodes run 42 to 49 minutes, filming took place in New Orleans, and the series is rated TV-14. Freeform cancelled it on October 24, 2019, months after the second season finished airing.

Two teenagers, one shared disaster

The premise is a pairing. As children, Tandy and Tyrone were both caught in the collapse of the Roxxon Gulf Platform on the same night each lost someone: Tandy's father Nathan died in a car accident that night, and Tyrone's older brother Billy was shot by a police detective. Years later the two meet again as teenagers and discover linked powers shaped by that night. Tandy emits daggers of light, and when she touches people she can see their hopes. Tyrone can engulf others in darkness, move them through the Darkforce Dimension, and witness their fears. The showrunner's own framing, recorded in the reference coverage, is that a cynical Tandy who believes in nobody keeps seeing the best in people, while Tyrone, living in fear since his brother's death, learns that everyone around him is afraid too.

The powers work better when the pair are together, which gives the series its engine: two leads who need each other practically before they trust each other at all. New Orleans is not a backdrop pasted behind that story. The production filmed in the city, Tyrone's family is tied to a Mardi Gras Indian tribe, and a supporting character, Evita, is played by a New Orleans actor who drew on the city's voodoo culture for the role. Few Marvel series are this rooted in one place.

How the two seasons divide

Season 1 is the origin, told slowly: the powers emerging, the Roxxon thread, and the detective whose secret connects both families' losses. It aired to positive reviews and was renewed during its first summer. Season 2 turns the pair outward. Tandy and Tyrone investigate abductions of women run by Andre Deschaine, a community figure with despair feeding powers, while Detective Brigid O'Reilly, played by Emma Lahana, splits into a second self, the vigilante Mayhem, who pursues her own goals rather than mirroring O'Reilly as a simple dark side. The season is darker in subject matter than the TV-14 rating might suggest, and parents sampling it as a teen show should know the trafficking storyline is the central case, not a subplot.

The ending question, answered honestly

Cancellation came after the second season aired, so the finale, titled Level Up, was written as a season ending rather than a series ending. Viewers who need every thread tied off should set expectations accordingly. What the finale does close is the season's central case and the pair's working partnership, so the 20 episodes play as a complete chapter with doors left open, not as a story cut off mid sentence. That distinction decides the recommendation for many viewers, so it belongs up front rather than buried in fine print.

The one crossover, graded

After this series ended, Tyrone and Tandy crossed into the third season of Runaways, in an appearance set after the Level Up finale. That is the pair's only crossover anywhere in the franchise, and this series itself features none. Grade it as flavour, not homework. Nothing in Cloak & Dagger requires Runaways first, and the crossover works as a short coda for viewers who finish these 20 episodes wanting one more outing with the characters. Watching order inside this series is simply season 1, then season 2, in episode order.

Where to watch in Canada

Canadian viewers can stream both seasons on Disney+ (availability checked November 2, 2026, listing updated October 31, 2026). At 42 to 49 minutes an episode, the full run totals roughly 14 to 16 hours, which makes this one of the shortest complete live action Marvel series of its era and a realistic one week watch at two or three episodes an evening.

Who should watch, sample, or skip

Watch it in full if: grounded, place specific superhero drama is your preference, or a two lead partnership story sounds better than another ensemble. Twenty episodes with a documented 89 percent first season is a low risk commitment. Sample first if: the teen drama label puts you off. Try the first two episodes, which aired together on June 7, 2018 and cover the shared childhood disaster, the reunion, and the first power displays. The tone they set is the tone the series keeps. Skip it if: you are watching strictly for MCU plot connections, or an open ended cancellation will sour the whole run for you. The films neither need this series nor reference it, and the open doors at the end are real.
