Daredevil, the original series explained: three seasons and what carries forward

Viewing guide · 7 min read

Daredevil arrived in 2015 as the first of the street-level Marvel series, and it remains the one most viewers come back for — especially now that its lead character has a continuing series again. The questions around it are consistent. How big is it? What else do I need? And the debate that followed it for years: does it count? This guide answers all three with the numbers on the table, and keeps the focus where the interest is — on Daredevil's own story, with the wider saga treated as what it is for most viewers: optional depth around a complete core.

The core first. Daredevil ran three seasons and 39 episodes between 2015 and 2018 (s1). Around it grew the Defenders Saga — six series, thirteen seasons, 161 episodes in total (s1). Nobody needs all 161 to follow Matt Murdock. But one piece of the wider saga is genuinely part of his story, and the canon question now has a cleaner answer than the internet's older arguments suggest.

The series itself: 39 episodes, three complete arcs

Daredevil's three seasons were released on April 10, 2015, March 18, 2016, and October 19, 2018 (s2). Each season is a self-contained arc of 13 episodes — an origin and a first great antagonist; an expansion that introduces two major figures from the wider saga; and a stripped-down final season that adapts the character's most famous comics storyline. Watched alone, the 39 episodes form a complete work: beginning, middle, and an ending that was written as an ending, because the streaming landscape that produced the show was already changing around it.

The tone is the other fact a new viewer needs. The saga as a whole is a grittier, more mature corner of the Marvel screen world than the films, and Daredevil set that register: street-level, morally serious, and built around long character work rather than spectacle. That is also why the series rewards order. Its seasons build directly on each other, and the second season in particular assumes the first completely.

Within the saga's table, Daredevil is the joint-largest component — 39 episodes, tied with Jessica Jones, ahead of Luke Cage and The Punisher at 26 each, Iron Fist at 23, and The Defenders at 8 (s1, s4). It is also the foundation in a literal sense: the first released, the tone-setter, and the series whose antagonist crosses into the wider MCU most prominently.

The one required crossover: The Defenders

Between Daredevil's second and third seasons sits The Defenders, the 8-episode team-up released August 18, 2017 (s2, s1). For a Daredevil-only viewer, this is the one piece of homework that is not optional. Matt Murdock's personal story runs straight through it — his circumstances at the start of Daredevil season 3 are the direct consequence of The Defenders' ending, and season 3 does not stop to re-explain them. Skipping it produces the specific confusion of a protagonist whose situation has changed off-screen for reasons everyone on screen treats as known.

The Defenders does ask for a little orientation: it unites the leads of Daredevil, Jessica Jones, Luke Cage, and Iron Fist, and one of its threads grows out of Daredevil season 2. A minimal path exists — Daredevil seasons 1 and 2, then The Defenders, then Daredevil season 3 — and it works, at the price of meeting two of the four leads for the first time inside the team-up. The fuller path interleaves the other series' first seasons before The Defenders, in the order the saga was released (s3). The middle path, and the one most returning viewers take: Daredevil 1–2, Jessica Jones season 1 (the saga's other most-regarded season, and the one that introduces Luke Cage), then The Defenders, then Daredevil 3.

Everything after that is enrichment, ranked by relevance to Daredevil himself. The Punisher's first season continues a character Daredevil season 2 introduces. Jessica Jones, Luke Cage, and Iron Fist seasons 2, and the saga's later seasons generally, run parallel to Daredevil's story rather than inside it — the full release order interleaves all thirteen seasons for viewers who want the whole tapestry (s3).

The canon question, as it now stands

For years the honest answer was 'ambiguous.' The shows were produced by a different arm of Marvel, referenced the films without being referenced back, and their status was debated without an official word. Some watch guides still carry that older framing, stating that Marvel has neither confirmed nor denied the shows' canonicity. That is no longer the operative fact. In March 2024 the Defenders Saga was added to the official MCU timeline on Disney+, and the shows are now presented as part of MCU canon (s1).

The bridge had been built in plain sight beforehand: Charlie Cox's Matt Murdock appeared in Spider-Man: No Way Home and She-Hulk, and Vincent D'Onofrio's Kingpin appeared in Hawkeye, carrying both central performances of Daredevil into unambiguous MCU productions (s4). The timeline placement then formalised what the casting had already told viewers. And the story continues on that basis: Daredevil: Born Again premiered its first season on March 4, 2025, with the saga as its foundation (s2).

For viewing purposes the practical translation is simple. The 2015 series is not an alternate take to be treated as apocrypha; it is the first three chapters of the screen Daredevil's ongoing story, catalogued by Disney+ as part of the official timeline — all 161 saga episodes, roughly 161 hours, of it (s4, s2). Release order and chronological order being identical for the saga removes the last practical obstacle: there is one order, and it is the order they came out (s2).

Choosing your commitment

Size the decision before starting. Daredevil alone: 39 episodes. Daredevil with its required crossover: 47. The middle path with Jessica Jones season 1: 60. The complete saga: 161 episodes across six series (s1). Each step up buys context, not comprehension — nothing in Daredevil season 3 requires Luke Cage, and nothing in The Defenders requires Iron Fist's full season for a viewer willing to meet its lead in the team-up itself.

The recommendation this site gives newcomers is the middle path, for a reason beyond completeness: Jessica Jones season 1 is the saga's other peak, it shares Daredevil's register and quality, and it makes The Defenders a meeting of characters you know rather than a roll call. Viewers who fall for the saga there have the remaining hundred episodes waiting in a confirmed order. Viewers who do not have still watched the essential Daredevil — all of it, in order, with nothing essential missing.

The Daredevil-focused watch path

  1. Daredevil season 1 (13 episodes) — the foundation; everything assumes it.
  2. Daredevil season 2 (13 episodes) — introduces figures the wider saga and Born Again build on.
  3. Optional but recommended: Jessica Jones season 1 (13 episodes) — the saga's other peak, and Luke Cage's introduction.
  4. The Defenders (8 episodes) — required before season 3; Matt's story runs through it.
  5. Daredevil season 3 (13 episodes) — the core story's conclusion, now fully legible.
  6. Then, by appetite: The Punisher S1, the remaining saga in release order, and Born Again.

What success looks like: Success looks like this: season 3's opening situation needs no explanation, every character in The Defenders is either known or clearly introduced, and your total commitment was a choice you made with the episode counts in front of you — not a surprise at episode 100.

FAQ

Can I skip The Defenders and go straight to Daredevil season 3?

You can, but season 3 opens in the aftermath of The Defenders' ending and does not recap it. Eight episodes is the cheapest confusion-avoidance in the saga (s1).

Is the 2015 Daredevil really canon now?

For viewing purposes, yes: the saga was added to the official Disney+ MCU timeline in March 2024, after Cox and D'Onofrio had already reprised their roles in MCU productions (s1, s4). Older guides calling it undecided predate that placement.

Do the other series matter for Born Again?

Born Again builds on Daredevil itself first. The saga's other series add context for returning characters, but Daredevil's 39 episodes plus The Defenders are the foundation it assumes (s2).
