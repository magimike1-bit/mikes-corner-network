How the shows connect (spoiler-light)

Explainer · 5 min read

People ask this constantly, and the honest answer is: it depends which shows you mean. Marvel TV was made in different eras by different teams with different plans. Here's the map, kept free of major plot reveals.

The one rule that explains almost everything

Think of Marvel's series in two layers. Layer one: shows that are actual chapters of the ongoing movie saga, made by Marvel Studios itself. These are the Disney+ series, and they share characters, actors, and storylines directly with the films. Layer two: everything else — shows that borrow the universe's flavor but mostly tell their own stories. Once you know which layer a show is in, the connection question gets much simpler.

Layer one: the Disney+ chapters

From WandaVision onward, the series are woven into the film saga. Characters debut in a show and show up in a movie later, or a film sets up a storyline that a series continues. You don't need a flowchart to enjoy any individual show — each one introduces its own premise — but the connections are real and cumulative. Watch these in release order if you want the threads to land the way they were designed to.

The multiverse concept introduced across several of these series is worth knowing about in advance, not because it's complicated, but because it explains why you'll sometimes see alternate versions of characters. The shows themselves do a decent job of explaining it — you don't need to study.

Layer two: the street-level corner

The Netflix-era shows — Daredevil, Jessica Jones, Luke Cage, Iron Fist, The Punisher — connect to each other tightly and to the films barely at all. Characters cross between these shows constantly: supporting players show up in each other's series, and the whole corner converges in The Defenders team-up. It's its own ecosystem. You can watch the entire corner as one self-contained saga.

One thing to know: some characters from this corner have since migrated into the main Marvel Studios productions, played by the same actors. This is a treat for longtime viewers, not a requirement — the new appearances reintroduce everyone.

The loose ends: the early network shows

Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D. started closely tied to the films and drifted into its own continuity over time. Agent Carter is a period piece with light connections. The Inhumans... exists. These shows are referenced by almost nothing that came later, so they're entirely optional. Fun if you want more, invisible if you don't.

What about the animated shows?

What If...? is mostly standalone "alternate timeline" stories — they're connected to the films by premise, not by plot. You enjoy them in proportion to how many films you've seen, but nothing in them is required reading for anything else. Think of animation as the bonus round.

Viewing-order tips that actually help

Release order is the default recommendation for the Disney+ chapters, and it's a good default: the threads were built to land in the order they came out, and you'll never meet a spoiler that way. But you can also slow down without fear. Every one of these series re-establishes its own premise in its opening episodes — the creators assume some viewers arrive fresh. If a show name-checks a film you haven't seen, don't panic: the series almost always re-explains the relevant bit in plain language. And if you take a break of weeks or months between series, a quick plot-summary refresher (of the previous show, not the one you're about to watch) is all the catching up you'll need.

The practical takeaway

If you're trying to "keep up" with Marvel, you really only need to track layer one — the Disney+ chapters and the films. Everything else is bonus. And even within layer one, the shows are designed so a newcomer can follow any single series cold. The connections are dessert, not the meal.
