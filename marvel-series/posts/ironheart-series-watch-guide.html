Ironheart series watch guide: six episodes in two drops, tech versus magic, and the Wakanda Forever homework

Viewing guide · 8 min read

Ironheart arrived quietly and left loudly. The six-episode miniseries is the 14th MCU television series, the final entry of Phase Five, and the solo debut of Riri Williams — the MIT inventor Dominique Thorne introduced in Black Panther: Wakanda Forever, a teenager who builds an armoured suit rivalling Tony Stark's on a student's budget (s1). It was also review bombed before a single episode had aired, which has made its actual reception unusually hard to read from the outside (s1). This guide assembles the version a deciding viewer needs: the two-drop release mapped episode by episode, the homework graded, the critics' verdict separated from the bombed audience scores, and the one finale choice that spoiler-free coverage never prepares you for — kept in a single flagged section at the end.

The shape of the series: six episodes, two Tuesdays, the end of Phase Five

Ironheart was created by Chinaka Hodge, who served as head writer, and produced by Marvel Television with Ryan Coogler's Proximity Media; Sam Bailey directed the first three episodes and Angela Barnes the last three (s1, s4). Disney+ released it in two batches: episodes 1–3 on June 24, 2025, and episodes 4–6 on July 1, 2025 (s1). That shape is the viewing plan — two sittings, a week apart in release and perfectly watchable back-to-back now, with a genuine midpoint turn at the end of episode 3. Episodes run 42 to 60 minutes (s1), with episode listings putting the first three at 44, 50 and 56 minutes (s3), so the whole series is roughly five to six hours: a weekend, not a saga. The premise picks up after Wakanda Forever: expelled from MIT, Riri returns home to Chicago still grieving her best friend Natalie and her stepfather Gary, and takes work with Parker Robbins — the Hood — whose thieving crew funds her suit while his cloak introduces the thing her engineering cannot explain: magic (s1, s2).

Two engines drive the six episodes. The first is money: Riri's genius is Stark-level and her resources are not, a contrast the reviews kept returning to (s4). Executive producer Sev Ohanian described her arc bluntly as a "breaking bad" path, in the vein of Walter White and Tony Soprano — a good person making progressively worse bargains (s1). The second engine is grief. Riri maps her own brain to build her suit's AI and produces N.A.T.A.L.I.E. — Neuro Autonomous Technical Assistant and Laboratory Intelligence Entity — a digital recreation of Natalie herself, played, as Natalie is in flashbacks, by Lyric Ross (s1). Around them: Anthony Ramos as Parker Robbins, Alden Ehrenreich as a black-market tech dealer living under the name Joe McGillicuddy, Anji White as Riri's mother Ronnie, and — arriving late and mattering enormously — Sacha Baron Cohen (s1). The Chicago setting is not decoration; the crew's heists target companies presented as extracting wealth from local communities, and the series was filmed in Atlanta and on location in Chicago, Riri's hometown (s1, s5).

The structural read: episodes 1–3 are the descent — recruitment, competence, catastrophe — and episodes 4–6 are the consequences, which is why the two-drop release worked and why a single weekend binge blurs the turn. The DiscussingFilm review's sharpest structural complaint lands here too: at six episodes, the Hood's crew — hacker Slug, pyrotechnics specialist Clown, the Blood siblings, tech specialist Rampage — are a fun ensemble the format never gives room to become more than background, and an episode or two more would have served both them and the friends-to-enemies arc at the centre (s4).

That is the shortest homework list of any Phase Five series: one film required, one optional, done. It is also why the series works as an entry point for the younger end of the audience The Guardian identified — its reviewer compared the aim to Ms. Marvel's, a fast, furious pace pitched at younger fans (s1) — provided the rating guidance in the FAQ below is respected.

What the critics converged on — and the bombing caveat

Start with the caveat, because it distorts everything downstream: before the first episodes released, Ironheart was review bombed on Rotten Tomatoes and IMDb with a wave of negative audience reviews, a pattern TheWrap's Casey Loving noted had become common for MCU projects led by women, people of color, or LGBTQ+ individuals (s1). The critics' numbers are a different story: a 76% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes from 131 reviews, with a consensus that Thorne ignites the MCU skies with charisma and confidence, making the series a worthy watch despite conventional genre armour, and a 57 out of 100 on Metacritic — mixed or average (s1). IndieWire's Ben Travers graded it a B, judging that its accessible approach lets its lessons land even where it cannot fully wrestle with its own ethical arguments (s1); The Guardian's Lucy Mangan gave three of five (s1). The fullest single review, DiscussingFilm's four-star take, argued Thorne's casting ranks among the MCU's perfect ones and praised the production and costume design's blend of science and magic — while faulting Ramos as an underwhelming, underplayed Hood and the six-episode squeeze described above (s4). The honest summary: critics liked the lead and the world, split on the villain and the length, and none of that resembles the audience-score picture the bombing painted.

Spoiler-flagged paragraph — skip to the verdict if you have not watched. The finale's withheld name is Mephisto, played by Sacha Baron Cohen: the demon who gave Parker his cloak, revealed in flashback, who then offers Riri the same kind of deal (s1). She accepts, on the condition her loved ones are left alone — and Natalie appears restored to life as his marks spread across Riri's arm (s1). It is the "breaking bad" arc completing itself: the hero ends the series having made the villain's bargain, which is precisely why the absence of a confirmed continuation stings.

FAQ

How long is Ironheart in total? Six episodes at 42–60 minutes (s1) — roughly five to six hours across two sittings, matching its two-drop release (s1, s3).

Will there be a season 2? None has been announced. The series was commissioned as a six-episode limited series, and any continuation of Riri's story is expected in future films or crossover events rather than a second season (s5).

Is Ironheart okay for kids? It is rated TV-14, aimed at viewers 14 and older, with action, violence, crime and dark-magic themes; it is not made for very young children (s6). For younger Marvel fans, the site's family guide is the safer shelf.

Why do the audience scores look so much worse than the reviews? Because of the documented pre-release review bombing described above (s1). Judge the series by the critics' consensus — 76% on Rotten Tomatoes — and by the specific split the reviews actually report.
