Loki series watch guide: both seasons, the TVA explained, and where to watch it in Canada

Viewing guide · 8 min read

The misconception that stops people starting Loki is the homework fear, the idea that a time travel series about the multiverse must need a shelf of films first. It needs one premise, and it is a premise the first episode shows on screen: during the time heist in Avengers: Endgame, the 2012 version of Loki grabbed the Tesseract and vanished, and a bureaucracy called the Time Variance Authority arrested him for it (s1). That Loki is not the reformed Loki of the later Thor films. He has not lived Thor: The Dark World or Thor: Ragnarok, the films that softened the character before his death in Avengers: Infinity War, so the series starts with the vain, scheming 2012 original and rebuilds him from there (s1). Twelve episodes, two seasons, one continuous story. What follows is the machinery explained plainly, both seasons mapped, and the homework cut down to size.

The TVA, explained in plain words

The Time Variance Authority, always shortened to the TVA, is a bureaucratic organization that exists outside time and space and monitors the timeline (s1). Four terms carry the whole series, and each one is simpler than it sounds.

The Sacred Timeline is the single approved flow of events the TVA protects. A variant is any person who steps off that flow and starts a branch, which is the charge Loki faces: the TVA gives him the choice of being erased from existence as a time variant or helping fix the timeline and stop a greater threat (s1). Pruning is the TVA erasing a branch before it grows. The Temporal Loom, which matters most in season 2, is the machine the timeline runs through, and Victor Timely, an inventor in 1893 on a branched timeline, built an early version of it (s1).

Behind the office sits one man. He Who Remains is a scientist from the 31st century who ended the first multiversal war by destroying evil variants of himself, then created the TVA to stop a new multiverse forming and to keep his other variants from returning (s1). Season 1 ends with the timeline breaking and a multiverse created, and season 2 follows Loki, Mobius, Hunter B-15, and the other TVA agents in a battle for the soul of the TVA itself (s1). Hold those four terms and the plot is followable from episode 1. Nothing else is required.

The time math is friendly. Twelve episodes at 41 to 56 minutes each puts the full run between about 8 hours and about 11 hours and 15 minutes, so roughly 10 hours in practice, two seasons that play as one story with no recap episode and no reset between them (s1, s3). Critics scored season 1 higher, at 92% on Rotten Tomatoes with a Metacritic score of 74, against 81% and 65 for season 2 (s1). Read that gap with care. The season 2 notices praised its conclusion, its musical score, and Loki's character arc specifically (s1), which is another way of saying the second season is the harder, denser half that pays off the first. Skipping to season 2 for the famous finale is the one viewing order that does not work.

Homework: one premise, the rest is colour

Essential: the Endgame premise above, which the premiere itself restages. A viewer who has never seen Endgame still gets the arrest, the charge, and the choice from the episode (s1). Helpful, not required: a general memory of the film Loki, the Thor's adopted brother and god of mischief of the early Marvel films, because the series is a character study of that 2012 version (s1). Not homework: Thor: The Dark World and Thor: Ragnarok. This Loki never lived them (s1). Watching them first is harmless, but it teaches the reformed Loki, and the series is about the unreformed one learning the same lessons the hard way.

Where to watch it in Canada (checked November 20, 2026)

The Canadian listing checked for this guide, with streaming availability last updated November 17, 2026, places Canada on Disney+ for the full series, all 12 episodes across both seasons, and records the series as no longer running (s2). American answers that route through Hulu bundles do not apply here. In Canada, Disney+ is the streaming home to check first, and because the series has ended, the whole story is available now with no weekly wait (s1, s2).

Who should watch which way

ViewerVerdict
New viewer worried about homeworkStart at season 1, episode 1. One premise, shown on screen, and four TVA terms carry the rest (s1).
Film Loki fanWatch both seasons in order. This is the 2012 variant, not the reformed film Loki, and the arc is the point (s1).
Multiverse followerWatch, and do not skip season 1. The TVA, He Who Remains, and the breaking timeline are set up there, and season 2 builds directly on them (s1).
Viewer short on timeWatch whole or wait. At roughly 10 hours total the run is short, but it is one continuous story, so a finale only shortcut misses the setup it pays off (s1, s3).
Viewer who dislikes time travel puzzlesSkip. The TVA machinery is the show, not a backdrop, and season 2 leans further into it (s1).
