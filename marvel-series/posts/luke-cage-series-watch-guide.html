Luke Cage series watch guide: 26 episodes, the Harlem of it all, and where it sits in the Defenders Saga

Viewing guide · 7 min read

The usual warning about this corner of Marvel is that you must watch four series in a strict order before anything makes sense. Luke Cage is where that warning is weakest. The show has a seat in a shared saga, and it rewards that context, but its story is a Harlem story first: one neighbourhood, one reluctant hero, and a soundtrack doing as much world building as the scripts. Here is the map, the honest costs, and the one production fact worth knowing before the season 1 midpoint.

Where this show sits

Marvel's Luke Cage was the third of the Marvel Netflix series leading to the crossover miniseries The Defenders in 2017, following Daredevil and Jessica Jones. Mike Colter was cast in December 2014 to appear as Cage in Jessica Jones before headlining his own series, so the character arrives here with a history some viewers will have seen. None of it is required. The solo series restates what it needs: a sabotaged experiment gave former convict Carl Lucas superhuman strength and unbreakable skin, and he is trying to rebuild a life in Harlem when the fight finds him anyway.

The facts in one box

The series was created by Cheo Hodari Coker, who served as showrunner, for Netflix, based on the character created by Archie Goodwin, George Tuska, Roy Thomas, and John Romita Sr. Both seasons run 13 episodes: season 1 was released in full on September 30, 2016, and season 2 in full on June 22, 2018. Episodes run 44 to 69 minutes. Alongside Colter, the cast includes Simone Missick as Detective Misty Knight, Alfre Woodard as councilwoman Mariah Dillard, Rosario Dawson as nurse Claire Temple, Mahershala Ali as nightclub owner Cornell "Cottonmouth" Stokes in season 1, and Mustafa Shakir as Bushmaster in season 2. Marvel's official series page lists the rating as TV-MA. The score is by Adrian Younge and Ali Shaheed Muhammad, built on a 1990s hip-hop flavour with many musical guests, and production filmed in New York City to capture Harlem itself.

The spec check on commitment: at the stated runtime range, 26 episodes come to somewhere between roughly 19 hours (at 44 minutes each) and roughly 30 hours (at 69 minutes each). Most episodes sit inside that window rather than at its edges, so budget for a few weeks of evening viewing, not a single weekend. The documented scores say the first season is the stronger bet, 90 percent from 72 reviews against season 2's 86 percent from 63, with the Metacritic gap wider at 79 to 64. Season 2's critical consensus still credits a complex narrative and singles out Woodard's performance as its archvillain, so the drop reads as good following great rather than a collapse.

One warning before the spoiler line

Season 1 changes villains partway through, and knowing why makes the season fairer to sample. Ali took the Cottonmouth role knowing the character would die early in the series, and described the job as being like shooting a film, giving everything to a part with a planned exit. The handover to Mariah Dillard's longer game is the design, not an accident, and viewers who quit at the change often quit one episode before the season's real subject, political power in Harlem, takes the foreground.

What the seasons are about

Season 1 is a fight for the heart of Harlem among Cage, Cottonmouth's nightclub empire, and Dillard's political machine, with Cage's prison past and the man who framed him closing in behind it. Season 2 opens with Cage as a Harlem celebrity, famous and broke at once, facing Bushmaster, a Jamaican gang leader whose folk magic grants him powers close to Cage's own and whose vendetta targets the Stokes family rather than Cage himself. The Defenders sits between the seasons in release terms, and a viewer following the saga order can slot it there. A viewer watching only this series can skip it and lose nothing the second season depends on.

Where to watch in Canada

The Netflix era is over for this title. All of the Marvel Netflix series were removed from Netflix on March 1, 2022, and this one began streaming on Disney+ on March 16, 2022, where Canadian listings still show both seasons (availability checked November 2, 2026, listing updated October 23, 2026). Disney+ has since placed the series inside its official MCU timeline order, season 1 after Ant-Man and season 2 between Doctor Strange and Thor: Ragnarok, which is a tidy confirmation of where the saga now officially sits. Because the rating is TV-MA, a profile restricted to lower ratings will not show the series.

Watch, sample, or skip, plus quick questions

Watch it in full if: neighbourhood scale superhero drama, a documented 90 percent first season, and music as a storytelling tool all appeal. Sample first if: 13 episode seasons sound long. The first three episodes establish Harlem, Cottonmouth, and Cage's reluctance in full; the origin episode, Step in the Arena, is episode 4 for viewers who want the backstory early. Skip it if: TV-MA violence and language are a deal breaker, or you only want stories the current films build on directly.

Do I need Jessica Jones first? No. It adds context for Cage's past, nothing more. Was there a season 3? No. Netflix cancelled the series on October 19, 2018, after creative differences during third season development, so season 2's ending is the ending the series has. Is it still on Netflix? No, it moved to Disney+ in March 2022.
