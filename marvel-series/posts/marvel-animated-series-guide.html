Marvel animated series guide

Viewing guide · 9 min read

Marvel animation is not one catalogue; it is at least three different projects sharing a logo. One line extends the live-action Marvel Cinematic Universe in animated form. A second line revives and continues beloved older cartoons, most prominently the 1990s X-Men series. A third line tells standalone stories for younger or newer viewers that deliberately sit outside the main continuity. Guides that merge all three into a single ranked list, or that cover only the X-Men corner or only Disney+ titles, leave readers with the wrong homework, the wrong order, and occasionally the wrong series entirely.

This guide separates the three lines, grades the homework each one actually requires, and uses attributed published review context only where it helps a reader choose. Episode-order traps are flagged where sources document them. The aim is a decision, not a marathon: by the end, a reader should know which animated line matches their viewing history and taste, and exactly where to start inside it.

The table is the guide's main original tool, and it corrects the most common animated-series mistake: assuming that because two shows are both animated and both on Disney+, they relate to each other the same way. They do not. Your Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man, for example, was described by Disney's own 2025 animation preview as a story set outside the MCU, following Peter Parker, voiced by Hudson Thames, in a style celebrating the character's comic-book origins. Treating it as required MCU viewing misreads its design; treating What If...? as fully standalone misreads its design in the opposite direction.

The MCU-linked line: What If and friends

What If...? is the anchor of this line. Each episode reimagines a known film storyline with one change, which makes the series unusually dependent on live-action familiarity for an animated show: the premise is the homework. That dependence is also its appeal for film fans between live-action releases, and published review context reflects a strong but softening reception across its run. MovieWeb's comparison table of Rotten Tomatoes scores listed What If...? season 1 at 89 percent from 123 reviews, season 2 at 88 percent from 32 reviews, and season 3 at 80 percent from 30 reviews. Those figures are Rotten Tomatoes aggregates as reported by MovieWeb, not a verdict from this site, and they are best used as a mild signal: start with season 1, and continue while the premise still entertains.

Around that anchor sit shorter and stranger pieces. I Am Groot offers brief, largely dialogue-free shorts that demand almost no continuity knowledge despite their MCU setting. Eyes of Wakanda and Marvel Zombies arrived in the 2025 wave discussed below, at opposite ends of the homework scale: a historical Wakanda anthology versus a horror spin-off growing out of a What If...? episode. MovieWeb's same table listed Eyes of Wakanda at 92 percent from 37 reviews and Marvel Zombies at 67 percent from 15 reviews, again as reported aggregates. The practical rule for this line is to watch by connection, not by release date: shorts anytime, What If...? after the relevant films, and its spin-offs after What If...? itself.

The revival line: from the 1992 X-Men series to X-Men '97

The X-Men animated lineage, in release order as listed by Popverse, runs from the 1989 pilot Pryde of the X-Men, through X-Men: The Animated Series from 1992 to 1997, X-Men: Evolution in 2000, Wolverine and the X-Men in 2009, the Marvel Anime Wolverine and X-Men series in 2011, and finally X-Men '97 from 2024. Only one link in that chain is a direct continuation: Popverse and Collider both describe X-Men '97 as a sequel to, and direct continuation of, the 1992 series. The others are reimaginings that can be watched in any order relative to each other.

That continuation creates the line's one genuine order trap, and it is about episodes, not series. Radio Times documented that X-Men: The Animated Series was frequently broadcast out of its intended story order, that some physical releases repeated the shuffle, and that Disney+ initially listed the incorrect order before correcting it in July 2022 to what the service called the original story order. A reader starting the 1992 series on Disney+ today can simply press play, which is exactly why the history matters: advice written before that correction, and any non-Disney+ source, may still present the shuffled order as if it were correct.

Reception context helps set expectations without turning this guide into a review. Collider reported X-Men '97 at 99 percent critics and 95 percent audience scores on Rotten Tomatoes at the time of its article, and MovieWeb's later table also listed it at 99 percent, from 81 reviews, the highest figure in that table. Attribution matters here: those are published aggregates reported by Collider and MovieWeb. The useful takeaway is narrower than a superlative. A revival that continues a thirty-year-old cartoon was received, by those measures, as the standout of modern Marvel animation, which makes the 1992-plus-'97 pairing the safest recommendation in this entire guide for viewers who enjoy serialised, character-driven storytelling.

The 2025 wave and where each show sits

Disney's Marvel Animation 2025 preview announced three new series for that year, following the series finale of What If...? and with X-Men '97 season 2 in production. Your Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man premiered first, on January 29, 2025, followed by Eyes of Wakanda and Marvel Zombies later that year; the preview dated the latter two to August 6 and October 3 respectively. Placing those three premieres on the three-line map is more useful than their shared release year suggests. The Spider-Man series belongs to the standalone line, a freshman-year Peter Parker story told outside MCU continuity, with Norman Osborn voiced by Colman Domingo in the preview's casting notes. Eyes of Wakanda belongs to the MCU-linked line as a Wakanda-centred anthology. Marvel Zombies belongs there too, but downstream of What If...?, and its horror tone makes it the least suitable starting point in the wave for younger viewers.

Review context, again strictly as reported by MovieWeb's Rotten Tomatoes table, put the wave in a clear spread: Your Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man at 97 percent from 38 reviews, Eyes of Wakanda at 92 percent from 37 reviews, and Marvel Zombies at 67 percent from 15 reviews. Sample sizes differ, especially for the last title, and this guide draws no ranking from them beyond a starting-point suggestion: a new viewer testing the 2025 wave should sample the Spider-Man or Wakanda series first and treat Zombies as a for-fans extension of a series they already like.

Choosing an animated starting point: a decision framework

Start with the 1992 X-Men series, then '97, for serialised drama. No live-action homework, one documented episode-order fix to be aware of only off Disney+, and the strongest reported reception in this guide. Start with What If...? season 1 for film-fan variety. The requirement is film familiarity, not series familiarity, and episodes can be sampled rather than marathonned. Start with Your Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man for a clean, low-homework origin story. Its alternate-timeline design is a feature for newcomers, not a gap. Start with I Am Groot for the youngest viewers or the shortest sessions. It is the lightest commitment in the MCU-linked line. Avoid starting with Marvel Zombies or X-Men '97 cold: one assumes What If...?, the other assumes, ideally, a 1990s cartoon, and both introductions work better as second steps.

That framework also explains what not to do. Do not watch animated Marvel in global release order from 1989 forward; the legacy reimaginings do not build on each other, and the effort buys no continuity. Do not skip the 1992 series straight into '97 expecting a recap to carry a full continuation; sampling key 1992 arcs first is the minimum that published continuation descriptions imply. And do not treat any single aggregate score quoted here as a promise about personal taste: the scores, attributed to their publishers above, describe critics' aggregates at a moment in time, while the lines, homework loads, and order traps in this guide are the durable part.

Frequently asked questions

Is X-Men '97 part of the MCU? Published descriptions treat it as a continuation of the 1992 animated series rather than a chapter of live-action MCU continuity, which is why this guide places it in the revival line. Does What If...? need the films? Substantially, yes; its episodes rework film storylines, so film familiarity is the intended context. What is the fastest animated watch? I Am Groot shorts, followed by single sampled episodes of What If...?, require the least time and the least preparation.
