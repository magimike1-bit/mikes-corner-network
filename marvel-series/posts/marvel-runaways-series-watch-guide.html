Marvel's Runaways series watch guide: three seasons, one crossover, and where to watch it now

Viewing guide · 8 min read

Six teenagers from different backgrounds — a nerd who longs to reunite with his childhood friends, a Wiccan isolating herself behind a gothic look, a lacrosse player everyone underestimates, an activist with a telepathic dinosaur — discover that their parents are not just embarrassing. They are the Pride, a criminal organization, and the six unite against the common enemy they all go home to (s1). That is the whole pitch of Marvel's Runaways, and it powered three seasons on Hulu between 2017 and 2019 (s1). The series then did something almost no other Marvel show has done: it vanished from its own streaming home. This guide maps the three seasons, grades the one crossover, answers the cliffhanger question honestly, and untangles where the series can actually be watched now.

The facts box

Created by: Josh Schwartz and Stephanie Savage, from the comics by Brian K. Vaughan and Adrian Alphona (s1). Starring: Rhenzy Feliz (Alex Wilder), Lyrica Okano (Nico Minoru), Virginia Gardner (Karolina Dean), Ariela Barer (Gert Yorkes), Gregg Sulkin (Chase Stein), and Allegra Acosta (Molly Hernandez), with the parents of the Pride played by an ensemble including James Marsters, Annie Wersching, and Kip Pardue (s1). Run: 3 seasons, 33 episodes, 43–54 minutes each; rated TV-14 (s1, s2). Original release: Hulu — season 1 weekly from November 21, 2017 to January 9, 2018; season 2 in full on December 21, 2018; season 3 on December 13, 2019 (s1). One production detail earns its place here: Old Lace, Gert's genetically engineered dinosaur, is a puppet operated by six people, breathing apparatus included (s1).

The reception story is consistency. Where its Netflix-era cousins swung wildly season to season, Runaways holds a narrow band — 85%, 84%, 83% — with the first season's consensus calling it "earnest, fun, and more balanced than its source material" and the third season's crediting it with ending "on an exciting — and surprisingly introspective — high note" (s1). Season 2's consensus is the only mixed note: deeper connections between the cast, held back by "formulaic stories and an over-dependence on plot devices" (s1). Metacritic scored season 1 at 68, "generally favourable" (s1). Episode titles track the tone — season 1 runs from "Reunion" and "Rewind" to "Doomsday" and "Hostile"; season 3 closes with "The Broken Circle" and the finale, "Cheat the Gallows" (s3).

The crossover question: Cloak & Dagger

Season 3 includes a crossover with Cloak & Dagger, the fellow Marvel Television young-adult series — announced August 1, 2019, after an earlier period in which the producers said there were no plans to cross the two shows over (s1). The grading is gentle: the crossover is one episode's worth of visitors inside a season whose main business is Morgan le Fay and the team's own fractures, and Schwartz had said from early on that the Runaways team was "very capable of telling the story that we wanted to tell independent of any of the other Marvel stories that are out there" (s1). Treat Cloak & Dagger as optional enrichment for viewers who end up liking the corner, not homework. Nothing else in the MCU is required at all — that independence was the design.

Where to watch it now (checked October 29, 2026)

Here is the part most guides get wrong, because the answer changed completely. Runaways was a Hulu original, and its first season also aired on Showcase in Canada (s1). Then, on May 26, 2023, the series was removed from Hulu and Disney+ globally as part of Disney's cost-cutting measures — while remaining available to purchase or rent on demand (s1). It resurfaced on the CW's streaming platform in May 2024, and became available on Tubi in February 2026 (s1). The current US listing checked for this guide shows all three seasons streaming free with ads on Tubi, or for purchase as downloads from the Apple TV Store, Fandango At Home, and Amazon Video, from $14.99 per season (s2). Canadian availability has historically differed from the US picture, so check the services above directly — what can be stated flatly is that "it's on Hulu" has been wrong since 2023.

Who'll love it, who should skip

ViewerVerdict
Teen-drama viewer new to MarvelWatch first. TV-14, no homework, and the parents-as-villains engine works without any franchise knowledge (s1, s2).
Comics readerWatch. The consensus credits the adaptation as more balanced than its source material, and early reviewers praised its faithfulness alongside its changes (s1).
MCU completistWatch, but calibrate. It acknowledges the franchise's world while telling a deliberately independent story — expect references, not plot dependencies (s1).
Viewer who needs an endingWatch with one warning. Season 3 was announced as the final season before it aired, yet the finale still ends on a cliffhanger the showrunners discussed publicly afterward (s1). The arcs resolve; the last beat dangles.
Hard-action viewerSkip. This is an ensemble teen drama with powers, paced like one — the dinosaur is a puppet with feelings, not a set piece (s1).
