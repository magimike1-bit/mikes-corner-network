Marvel Zombies watch guide: four episodes, one sitting, and the one episode of homework

Viewing guide · 6 min read

Five years after a zombie virus devastates the Earth of an alternate timeline, the survivors are not the Avengers. The Avengers are the problem — turned, most of them, into superpowered zombies — and the people left to cross a ruined landscape looking for a way to end the plague are a younger, stranger crew, led by Kamala Khan. That is the whole proposition of Marvel Zombies, and it takes about two hours to watch all of it. The questions worth answering before pressing play are smaller than the premise: how much homework it really needs, whether the adults-only rating is a marketing sticker or a genuine warning, and who this is actually for.

The facts in one box

Marvel Zombies is an animated series created by Bryan Andrews and Zeb Wells for Disney+, produced by Marvel Studios Animation and counted as the 16th television series in the MCU. Andrews served as showrunner and director, with Wells as head writer, and the series uses the same animation style as What If...? — because it is a spin-off of that show, continuing the alternate timeline introduced in its first-season episode “What If... Zombies?!” (2021). All four episodes were released on Disney+ on September 24, 2025, as part of Phase Six, and the series received generally positive reviews from critics. Kamala Khan is central to the story; the creators described her role as “the Frodo of the story.” The ensemble voice cast includes Iman Vellani, Dominique Thorne, Hailee Steinfeld, Florence Pugh, David Harbour, Simu Liu, Awkwafina, Randall Park, Elizabeth Olsen, Hudson Thames, Paul Rudd and Tessa Thompson, many of them returning to characters they play elsewhere in the franchise.

The one episode of homework

The homework answer is unusually clean. Watch, in order:

  1. What If...? season 1, “What If... Zombies?!” (2021). This is the episode whose alternate timeline the series continues: it establishes the virus, the fallen Avengers, and the world the survivors inherit. It is the one piece of viewing that genuinely changes how the opening lands.
  2. Then Marvel Zombies, episodes 1–4, in order. All four arrived in a single drop, so there is no release-order puzzle to solve.

Everything else is optional. The rest of What If...? adds context about the anthology's framing, not about this timeline, and the live-action appearances of the voice cast's characters are background flavour rather than prerequisites. Success looks like this: after the single What If...? episode, the series' first scenes need no pausing to work out who anyone is or what happened to the world. If a viewer skips even that episode, the series still explains its premise — a group of survivors who discover the key to ending the plague and risk their lives travelling across a dystopian landscape to use it — but the fallen-heroes reveals land as information instead of as a gut punch.

What the TV-MA rating actually means here

This is Marvel Studios Animation's first series to be rated TV-MA, and that fact deserves more weight than ratings usually get on this site. The premise is zombie horror played straight: the undead are former heroes, the survivors' journey is a fight against superpowered zombies, and the rating exists because the show does not soften that premise for a family audience. The practical consequence is simple — this is the one animated Marvel series that should not be handed to younger viewers on the assumption that animation means all-ages. Viewers in Canada can stream it on Disney+ (availability checked October 28, 2026), where the TV-MA rating is listed as 18+.

Quick questions

Do the episodes need to be watched weekly? No — all four were released on September 24, 2025, in a single drop. Is it connected to the main MCU timeline? No — it is set in the alternate timeline from the What If...? zombie episode, so nothing in it is homework for the films. How long is the whole thing? 124 minutes by the per-episode runtimes — one long film's worth.

Who should watch, sample, or skip

Watch it in full if: horror is the draw, or the alternate-timeline experiment is — four episodes at roughly two hours total is the smallest full-series commitment in the recent catalogue, and the generally positive critical reception says the experiment mostly works. Sample first if: the interest is really Kamala Khan and the younger ensemble. The fair test is the What If...? zombie episode plus episode 1: together they state the premise, the tone, and the gore level, and a viewer who bounces off that pairing has spent about an hour learning so. Skip it if: zombie horror is a hard no, or younger viewers are the audience — the TV-MA rating is the honest headline here, and the series' alternate-timeline setting means skipping it costs nothing in continuity for anything else.
