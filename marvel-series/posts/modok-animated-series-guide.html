Marvel's M.O.D.O.K. animated series guide: ten episodes, stop motion, and a villain midlife crisis

Viewing guide · 7 min read

88% is the number that sells this series, and the sentence attached to it is the number's honest shadow. Rotten Tomatoes reports an 88% approval rating for Marvel's M.O.D.O.K., with an average rating of 7.77 out of 10 based on 34 critic reviews, and its consensus praises slick stop motion, a killer voice cast, and a seemingly endless well of jokes, while warning that a tendency toward too muchness may test some viewers' patience (s1). Metacritic lands cooler at 71, based on 8 reviews and labelled generally favorable (s1). Both readings are true at once, and which one describes your evening depends on a simple taste test. If ten short episodes of a giant headed villain losing his company, his marriage, and his composure in stop motion sounds like a good time, the critics who loved it are your people. If relentless is a warning word for you, the consensus already told you so.

The facts box

Created by: Jordan Blum and Patton Oswalt for Hulu, based on the Marvel Comics character created by Stan Lee and Jack Kirby, produced by Marvel Television with Blum and Oswalt as showrunners and stop motion animation by Stoopid Buddy Stoodios (s1). Voices: Patton Oswalt as M.O.D.O.K., with Aimee Garcia (Jodie), Ben Schwartz (Lou), Melissa Fumero (Melissa), Wendi McLendon-Covey (Monica Rappaccini), Beck Bennett (Austin Van Der Sleet), Jon Daly (Super-Adaptoid), and Sam Richardson (Gary), plus Jon Hamm as Iron Man and Nathan Fillion as Wonder Man in guest roles (s1). Run: 1 season, 10 episodes, 23 to 25 minutes each, rated TV-MA, all released on May 21, 2021 (s1, s3). The name is the joke and the biography in one line: Mental, Mobile, Mechanized Organism Designed Only for Killing, now a suburban father in New Jersey facing a midlife crisis (s1). A tie in comic by Blum and Oswalt, M.O.D.O.K.: Head Games, arrived ahead of the series (s1).

The two stories explain the split in the reviews. NPR compared the series to Robot Chicken favourably, describing a tiny, weird backwater corner of the Marvel Universe cordoned off so the show could go nuts (s1). The Hollywood Reporter placed it in Robot Chicken country, short of Harley Quinn territory, and credited the voices and the rapid fire references for making a ten episode binge easy, quickly digested entertainment (s1). IGN scored it 8 out of 10 and heard a surprisingly moving family sitcom inside the farce (s1). That family thread is the deciding factor. Viewers who find the Tarletons worth caring about get a complete little tragedy with jokes. Viewers who do not are left with the jokes alone, at full volume, for ten episodes.

The bigger plan that fell away

The series was never meant to stand alone at first. It was announced in February 2019 as part of a Hulu animated slate alongside series built around Hit-Monkey, Tigra and Dazzler, and Howard the Duck, all intended to lead into a crossover special titled The Offenders (s1). Marvel Television was then folded into Marvel Studios in December 2019, and in January 2020 Marvel dropped Howard the Duck, Tigra and Dazzler, and The Offenders themselves, while M.O.D.O.K. and Hit-Monkey continued (s1). Blum gave this series its own multiverse designation, Earth-1226, which settles the homework question completely: nothing in the Marvel Cinematic Universe is required, referenced in passing only, and a second season was planned before Hulu cancelled the show after one season in May 2022 (s1). The cancellation still leaves a watchable object behind, because the family story was built to close inside the season even while the door was left open for more.

Where to watch it in Canada (checked November 10, 2026)

The launch pattern explains the confusion in older guides. All ten episodes dropped at once on Hulu in the United States on May 21, 2021, while the first episode arrived on the Disney+ Star hub the same day internationally, with the rest following on a weekly window (s1). Canada followed the international pattern, on Disney+ through Star (s1). The Canadian listing checked for this guide, last updated November 9, 2026, still places Canada on Disney+ for the series, while the United States is listed on Hulu, and it also shows purchase options in Canada through services including Prime Video and the Apple TV Store (s2). So the 2021 launch advice still points the right way for Canadians, with one update: the full ten episodes are the set to expect now, not a weekly rollout (s1, s2).

Watch it or skip it: a short checklist

Watch it if stop motion is a draw in itself. Stoopid Buddy Stoodios packed every frame with detail and shot the puppets with handheld style cinematography, and the craft is a documented part of the praise, not a footnote (s1).

Watch it if a villain workplace sitcom is your format, and a TV-MA rating is a feature rather than a problem. This is an adult animated comedy, closer in address to its late night peers than to any family Marvel title (s1, s3).

Try one episode if the premise charms you but volume worries you. "If Saturday Be... For the Boys!" works as a low commitment sample, and the premiere sets up both storylines in under half an hour (s1).

Skip it if you want MCU story. Earth-1226 is a designation that means freedom, not foreshadowing, and no film or series homework connects to this corner (s1).

Skip it if relentless is your stop word. The critics who scored it 88% named the too muchness in their own consensus, so a viewer who bounces off density in episode 1 has been given fair notice by the fans, not just the skeptics (s1).
