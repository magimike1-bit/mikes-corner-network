Moon Knight series watch guide: six episodes, standalone, and where to watch it in Canada

Viewing guide · 7 min read

Six episodes is the whole commitment, and the documented reception explains why those six episodes keep getting recommended to viewers who feel finished with homework television. Rotten Tomatoes reports an 86% approval rating for Moon Knight, with an average rating of 7.65 out of 10 based on 240 reviews, and a critics consensus that calls the series refreshingly weird (s1). Reviewers praised the performances, the darker tone compared with earlier Marvel series, and the portrayal of dissociative identity disorder at the centre of the story (s1). A Marvel show with no required viewing before it, a beginning and an end inside one short season, and a lead performance built on two, then three, distinct identities sharing one body. That combination is rare enough to grade carefully.

The facts box

Created by: Jeremy Slater for Disney+, produced by Marvel Studios as the sixth Marvel Cinematic Universe television series, with Slater as head writer, Mohamed Diab leading the directing team on four episodes, and Justin Benson and Aaron Moorhead directing the other two (s1). Starring: Oscar Isaac as Marc Spector and Steven Grant, with May Calamawy (Layla El-Faouly), Ethan Hawke (Arthur Harrow), and F. Murray Abraham as the voice of Khonshu (s1). Run: 1 season, 6 episodes, 45 to 53 minutes each, rated TV-14 (s1, s3). Original release: weekly on Disney+ from March 30 to May 4, 2022, as part of Phase Four (s1, s3).

The arithmetic is the selling point for a busy viewer. Six episodes at 45 to 53 minutes each totals between 4 hours and 30 minutes and 5 hours and 18 minutes, a single weekend at an unhurried pace (s1). Isaac plays the alters as distinct characters and separates them with different accents, Spector in his own American accent and Grant in a London English one (s1). Spector is the avatar of Khonshu, the Egyptian moon god, and Grant becomes Mr. Knight when he serves as the avatar instead (s1). Harrow, the calm centre of the opposition, was Khonshu's previous avatar before Spector and now serves Ammit, a goddess who judges people for crimes they have not yet committed (s1).

Spoiler line: the standalone discussion below names a third identity revealed late in the run. Nothing past this point is hinted at above it.

The standalone question, answered properly

Standalone is a claim, so it should be checked rather than repeated. The check comes back clean. The series has no explicit connections to other parts of the Marvel Cinematic Universe (s4). No Avenger appears to recruit anyone, no earlier film scene needs replaying, and the conflict, Egyptian gods using human avatars to fight over how justice should work, is introduced and resolved inside these six episodes (s1). Timeline binge orders that slot Moon Knight between other series are filing systems, not viewing requirements.

Two honest limits belong beside that verdict. First, standalone does not mean unfinished by accident. The finale's mid credits scene confirms a third alter, Jake Lockley, a more ruthless identity who speaks Spanish and had been teased through the run (s1). That is a door left open, not a plot hole. Second, the door has stayed open. Brad Winderbaum said in February 2025 that a second season was not planned (s4). A new viewer should start the series as a complete six episode story with one deliberate loose thread, because that is what the record supports.

Content notes: darker than the rating suggests

The series is rated TV-14 (s3), and the rating is accurate as far as it goes. What it does not convey is tone. Kevin Feige described Spector as a brutal action hero and said the series would not pull back from portraying the violence of the character (s1). Reviewers singled out the darker tone compared with previous Marvel series as part of the praise, alongside the portrayal of dissociative identity disorder (s1). For some viewers that portrayal is the reason to watch. For a viewer sensitive to depictions of mental illness, childhood trauma, or close quarters violence, episode 5 in particular goes to difficult places by design, since it tells the origin of the disorder itself (s1). Treat TV-14 as the floor of the guidance, not the whole of it, and preview first before watching with younger teenagers.

Where to watch it in Canada (checked November 20, 2026)

The Canadian listing checked for this guide, with streaming availability last updated November 12, 2026, places Canada on Disney+ for the full series, all six episodes, and records the series as no longer running (s2). Premiere era guides quoting 2022 subscription prices and weekly release times are describing a launch that finished in May 2022. Now the whole run sits in one place, ready for the weekend the time math above describes (s1, s2).

Who'll love it, who should skip

Watch first if the appeal is a Marvel story with zero homework: no explicit connections to other titles, one season, and a conflict that resolves inside it (s1, s4). Watch if a dual performance is the draw, because Isaac's two accents and two temperaments carry scenes where the same actor argues with himself across a mirror (s1). Watch with the content notes attached if the darker tone or the disorder storyline is a sensitivity rather than an attraction, since both are central, not incidental (s1). Skip if a loose thread at the end spoils a series for you: the third alter reveal is real, and no second season was planned as of February 2025 (s1, s4). And skip if Egyptian mythology plus psychological drama sounds like work, because the series asks for attention in every episode and rewards it late.
