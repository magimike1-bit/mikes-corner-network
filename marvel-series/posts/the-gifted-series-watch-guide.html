The Gifted series watch guide: two seasons, no X-Men, and where to watch it in Canada

Viewing guide · 7 min read

The first misconception to clear away is the billing. The Gifted sounds like an X-Men series, and it is set in the X-Men world, but the X-Men themselves have disappeared before the story starts, and that absence is one of the central mysteries of the show (s1). What fills the space is smaller and, for many viewers, better: two ordinary parents discover their children have mutant abilities, take the family on the run from the government, and fall in with an underground community of mutants fighting to survive (s1). Across two seasons and 29 episodes on Fox between 2017 and 2019, the series built the most grounded version of the mutant story television has tried, and then got cancelled before its biggest question could be answered. This guide explains the timeline, maps the seasons, and weighs that trade honestly.

The facts box

Created by: Matt Nix for Fox, based on the Marvel Comics X-Men properties, produced by 20th Century Fox Television in association with Marvel Television, with Nix as showrunner (s1). Starring: Stephen Moyer (Reed Strucker) and Amy Acker (Caitlin Strucker), with Sean Teale (Eclipse), Natalie Alyn Lind (Lauren Strucker), Percy Hynes White (Andy Strucker), Coby Bell (Jace Turner of Sentinel Services), Jamie Chung (Blink), Blair Redford (Thunderbird), and Emma Dumont (Lorna Dane, also called Polaris) (s1). Skyler Samuels, as the telepathic Frost sisters, and Grace Byers, as Inner Circle leader Reeva Payge, joined as regulars in season 2 (s1). Run: 2 seasons, 29 episodes, 45 to 47 minutes each, rated TV-14 (s1, s3). Original release: Fox, season 1 from October 2, 2017 to January 15, 2018, and season 2 from September 25, 2018 to February 26, 2019 (s1). Episode titles carry the signature in plain sight, opening with "eXposed" and "rX" and closing season 2 with "Monsters" and "oMens" (s1).

No X-Men, no homework: the timeline question

The series sits in an alternate timeline to the X-Men film series (s1). Creator Matt Nix used the multiple timelines established by the film X-Men: Days of Future Past to place the show in its own stream, free to do its own thing rather than slot neatly beside any single film (s1). In this stream, a catastrophic event caused enormous social upheaval and a wave of hatred toward mutants, and the X-Men vanished in its aftermath (s1). The practical result for a new viewer is liberating. There is no required film list, no post credits scene to chase, and no Marvel Cinematic Universe connection at all. Knowing who Magneto is adds one layer, because Lorna Dane controls magnetism and the series openly asks whether she accepts the mantle of her birthright, but the question works as family drama even without the reference (s1). Nix pitched the approach as coming at the mutant world from the side, through people who are not already X-Men, and the mutant underground itself is modelled on the Underground Railroad (s1).

The reception split is the interesting part. Critics warmed to the show as it went, with season 1 landing at 76% on Rotten Tomatoes and a Metacritic score of 63, and season 2 rising to 83% (s1). The first season consensus praised a superhero drama that powers past origin story doldrums by focusing on grounded, topical stories over mindless action (s1). Broadcast audiences moved the other way, with average viewers falling from 4.97 million in season 1 to 3.32 million in season 2 (s1). Fox cancelled the series on April 17, 2019, weeks after the season 2 finale (s1). One design choice softens the blow. Nix said he wanted each season to end in a satisfying way that does not feel close ended, so season arcs land even though the long mystery, where the X-Men went, was built to run longer (s1). Go in expecting two complete movements and one open door, and the ending plays fairly. Go in expecting the door to close, and it will not.

Where to watch it in Canada (checked November 10, 2026)

Canadians met this show early. CTV held the Canadian broadcast rights during the original Fox run, and the series launched in more than 183 countries in a day and date format (s1). Both seasons were added to Disney+ on February 23, 2021 (s1). The Canadian listing checked for this guide, last updated November 8, 2026, still places Canada on Disney+ for the full series, while the United States is listed on Hulu, and it also shows purchase options in Canada through services including Prime Video, the Apple TV Store, and Google Play (s2). The often repeated answer that the show lives on Hulu is the American answer. In Canada, Disney+ is the streaming home to check first (s1, s2).

Who'll love it, who should skip

ViewerVerdict
X-Men fan tired of film continuityWatch first. An alternate timeline, the X-Men gone, and familiar figures such as Blink, Thunderbird, and Polaris rebuilt for a street level story (s1).
Family drama viewerWatch. The engine is two parents protecting two teenagers, with the powers arriving unlabeled and evolving with the children (s1).
MCU completistWatch only on its own terms. This is a Fox and Marvel Television production in the X-Men world, with no MCU plot to collect (s1).
Viewer who needs the central mystery solvedWatch with the warning attached. Season arcs resolve by design, but cancellation on April 17, 2019 left the disappearance of the X-Men unexplained (s1).
Viewer who wants big team spectacleSkip. The stated aim was a more intimate story than a two hour film can hold, built on persecution and survival rather than set pieces (s1).
