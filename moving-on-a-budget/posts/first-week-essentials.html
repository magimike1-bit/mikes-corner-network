The First-Week Essentials Box + Address-Change Checklist

One clearly labeled box stands between you and a week of buying duplicates, eating takeout, and losing mail.

Moving on a Budget · Guide 5 of 5

The first week in a new home is where budget moves quietly bleed money. You can't find the can opener, so you order takeout. You can't find the phone charger, so you buy another. A bill goes to the old address, and the late fee arrives before the bill does. Almost all of it is preventable with one box and one checklist.

The essentials box: pack it last, open it first

Use a distinctive box or bag — a different color, a big label on all sides reading OPEN FIRST. It rides with you in the car, not in the truck, and it gets unpacked before anything else on moving day.

  • Toilet paper, paper towels, hand soap, and a shower curtain with rings
  • Toothbrushes, toothpaste, deodorant, and any daily medications (plus a few days' spare)
  • Phone chargers, laptop charger, and a power strip
  • A change of clothes and sleepwear for everyone, plus one towel per person
  • Bedding for the first night: sheets, pillows, blankets
  • Basic kitchen kit: kettle or coffee maker, mugs, a few plates and utensils, dish soap, a sponge
  • Snacks, bottled water, and any baby or pet food for the first 48 hours
  • Basic tools: box cutter, screwdriver, hammer, tape measure, flashlight
  • Cleaning basics: all-purpose spray, trash bags, a broom or vacuum if it fits
  • Important documents folder: lease, IDs, insurance papers, moving receipts (see below)
  • Cash in small bills — for tips, parking meters, and the pizza that saves moving night
  • Comfort item for kids (and honestly, for you too): the first night in a new place is strange for everyone

Tip: Pack the essentials box the morning of moving day, after your last shower and breakfast in the old place. Packing it a week early guarantees you'll unpack it "just for one thing" and never repack it.

The address-change checklist

Do these in roughly this order. The postal service change catches stragglers, but many senders need to hear it from you directly — and some, like voter registration and vehicle documents, have legal deadlines that vary by region, so check yours.

Week before the move

  • File a change of address with the postal service (and set up mail forwarding if available in your country)
  • Update your address with your bank, credit cards, and any loan or investment accounts
  • Notify your employer / payroll for tax documents
  • Update insurance: renter's/homeowner's, auto, health, life — an un-updated auto policy can complicate a claim
  • Schedule utility transfers: electricity, gas, water, internet — arrange overlap of a day or two so you're never dark

Moving week

  • Driver's license and vehicle registration (check your region's deadline — some are as short as 10–30 days)
  • Voter registration
  • Doctor, dentist, pharmacy — transfer prescriptions before you run out
  • Schools and childcare: enrollment paperwork and records transfers
  • Subscriptions and memberships: streaming, gyms, meal kits, magazines

First week in the new place

  • Confirm mail is actually arriving — send yourself a letter the first week as a test
  • Update your address on delivery apps, ride-share apps, and online shopping accounts
  • Give the new address to family, your emergency contacts, and anyone who sends holiday cards
  • Check in with the old landlord or building manager about any final mail
  • Photograph and file all moving receipts — some moving expenses are tax-deductible in certain situations and regions; keep the paperwork and ask a tax professional

The first-week unpacking order

Unpack in this order and the place becomes livable fast: bedrooms → bathroom → kitchen → everything else. Set a daily box quota (say, five boxes a day) rather than marathon sessions — decision fatigue is real, and a calm pace means things actually find their right homes instead of their first available surfaces.

The frugal first week: resist furnishing the new place in week one. Live in it for two weeks first — you'll discover which "urgent" purchases weren't urgent at all, and the money you don't spend is the cheapest savings in this whole guide.
