Moving Awkward Items on a Budget: Pianos, Aquariums, and Oversized Furniture

Would you pay a few hundred dollars to move a couch you could replace for less? Awkward items punish households that price the truck instead of the item — so price the item.

Moving on a Budget · Budget move guide

What does it cost to move a piano? Search that question from Canada and the answers arrive in US dollars, from US cost guides, for US moves. That is the trap under this whole topic: the numbers are easy to find and quietly belong to another country. Research checked October 25, 2026 retrieved no published Canadian price list for specialty-item moves at all. What follows does two things honestly: it shows the US figures labelled as US figures, so the shape of the pricing is visible, and it gives you the per-item decision that matters more than any borrowed number — move it, sell it, or let it go.

The published prices are American. Treat them that way.

US cost guides published these local-move ranges: a piano at US$260–$580, an aquarium at US$100–$300, and a pool table at US$375–$1,500. Single-item and specialty movers in the US market commonly charge US$30–$100 per hour per mover. Aquarium pricing in the same guides scales with tank size: small tanks at US$50–$100, large tanks at US$300 and up.

Use those rows for one purpose only: understanding what a quote is made of. A Canadian quote that lands far outside the US shape is a prompt to ask why — more crew, more stairs, more distance — not proof that it is wrong. Your actual price comes from Canadian movers quoting your actual item, and specialty items are typically priced either as add-ons to a household move or as standalone single-item jobs, so ask which basis a quote uses before comparing two of them.

The currency rule: a US figure converted in your head is not a Canadian price. Labour markets, distances, and fees differ. Budget from the Canadian quotes you collect; keep the US table as a sanity check on the structure, never as the number in your spreadsheet.

Pianos: the quote follows the obstacles

A piano move is priced on access more than on the instrument. US guides put stairs, disassembly needs, and distance among the factors that push oversized-item costs upward, and a piano concentrates all three: it is heavy, it does not come apart casually, and the route from its room to the truck decides the crew size.

Before requesting quotes, do the measuring the US guides recommend for oversized items — the item itself and every doorway, hallway, and stair turn on its way out, and the same route in at the new home. Give every mover the same measurements and the same obstacle list. Quotes gathered on identical information can be compared; quotes gathered on guesses cannot.

Then run the value test from the decision section below. A piano the household plays daily and a piano nobody has opened in years can sit in the same room and deserve opposite answers.

Aquariums: the price is the easy part

An aquarium quote pays for moving glass, equipment, and water weight. The fish are a separate logistics problem, and no price in the research covers their survival — the US guides price the tank by size and stop there. Plan the livestock side yourself, as its own small project:

  1. Decide who carries the fish. Assume they travel with the household, in a vehicle whose temperature and timing you control, unless a mover states in writing that livestock is included.
  2. Never plan to move a filled tank. The tank travels empty; water, fish, and the contents each get their own arrangement.
  3. Keep the move short for the animals. Sequence the aquarium last out and first set up where the layout allows, so the gap between teardown and a running tank stays as small as your move permits.
  4. Ask the mover precise questions: who drains and packs the tank, what is excluded, and what happens if the tank is damaged in transit. Get the answers on the quote, not in a phone call.

Short tanks and short distances forgive sloppy planning. A large tank — US$300 and up in the US guides — across a long day does not. The bigger the system, the more the livestock plan matters and the less the moving quote does.

Pool tables and oversized furniture

The pool table's US$375–$1,500 range is the widest in the table, and the width itself is information: disassembly and reassembly dominate. A table that must be taken apart, carried in pieces, and rebuilt level at the other end is a different job from one that rolls out of a ground-floor games room. When quoting, state plainly whether taking apart and rebuilding are inside the price.

Oversized furniture follows the same drivers the US guides name — stairs, disassembly, and distance raise the cost. Sectionals, wall units, and large wardrobes share one budget question: does the piece come apart, and will it fit the new home's rooms and doorways? Measure the destination before paying to move anything bulky. An item that cannot enter the new home has a moving cost and a disposal cost, and both were avoidable with a tape measure.

Move, sell, or donate: the per-item decision

Price each awkward item on its own line, with three numbers:

  1. The move cost: the Canadian quote for that item, as an add-on or a standalone job.
  2. The replacement cost: what an equivalent item costs you at the destination, from current local listings you check yourself.
  3. The exit value: what selling it returns, or zero for a donation or disposal — against the cost and effort of arranging pickup or removal.

The rule that follows is plain arithmetic. When the move cost approaches or exceeds replacement minus exit value, moving is the expensive option wearing a sentimental disguise. When the item is hard to replace, holds real value, or the household genuinely uses it, the move cost is simply a line in the moving budget — pay it knowingly, with the obstacle list priced in.

Sentiment is a legitimate input. It is not a blank cheque. Naming the number first and choosing to pay it anyway is a decision; never seeing the number is how households pay a few hundred dollars to transport furniture worth less than the trip.

Booking a single-item mover without overpaying

  • Send measurements and photos with the request. Item dimensions, doorway widths, stair counts at both ends. Vague requests get padded quotes.
  • Ask the basis: add-on to a household move or standalone single-item job, hourly or flat, and how many movers the crew includes. US guides price specialty labour per mover per hour, so crew size is a price lever — confirm it is set by the item, not by habit.
  • Get the exclusions in writing: disassembly, reassembly, stairs beyond a stated count, and damage terms.
  • Compare at least two quotes on identical information. A cheaper quote built on fewer facts is not cheaper yet.

You have handled an awkward item well when its line in the budget was decided on its own three numbers — move cost, replacement cost, exit value — and the crew that arrived knew the measurements, the stairs, and the plan before they lifted anything. That outcome is available at every price level. It only requires deciding per item instead of per truck.
