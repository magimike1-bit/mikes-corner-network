The 8-Week Moving Countdown: A Week-by-Week Checklist to Moving Day

Eight weeks sounds generous until the bookings, the packing, and the paperwork all land in the same fortnight. This countdown gives each week one finishable job — and names the cost that job prevents.

Moving on a Budget · Budget move guide

Most moving stress is not about the weight of the boxes. It is about timing: the mover you wanted is booked, the free boxes are gone, the utility transfer window has closed, and the essentials you need tonight are taped inside a box labelled “misc.” An eight-week countdown fixes that by sequencing the work. Early weeks are for decisions and bookings, when choice is widest and prices are calmest. Middle weeks are for shrinking the load and filing paperwork. The final week is protected — packing and verification only, with no new projects started. Follow the weeks in order and moving day becomes an execution day, not a negotiation with chaos.

Eight weeks is not early — it is on time

Canadian moving guides converge on the same starting line. Great Canadian Van Lines advises that eight weeks before the move date is a good starting point for most moves, and ten to twelve weeks is even better for a larger home or a long-distance move. Royal LePage’s Canadian guide gives the same window — begin the timeline at least six to eight weeks out — and adds the reason: in peak season, typically late spring through early fall in Canada, moving companies book up quickly. Starting at eight weeks is therefore not keen behaviour. It is the last point at which you still get to choose your mover, your date, and your price, rather than accepting whoever has a gap.

Weeks 8–7: decide, measure, and book — the tested procedure

  1. Lock the date first. Confirm lease end, closing, or possession dates before anything else. Every other task hangs off this date, and an assumed date that shifts by a week invalidates bookings made around it.
  2. Decide DIY, movers, or hybrid. Use your inventory, distance, stairs and elevators, and available help to choose. Do not decide on price alone at this stage — decide on feasibility, then price the feasible options.
  3. Get written estimates. Centennial Moving’s 8-weeks-out checklist starts with requesting written estimates from at least three providers and comparing services, coverage, and cancellation policies. Verbal quotes cannot be compared and cannot be enforced.
  4. Create the budget and the binder. One folder — paper or digital — for contracts, receipts, inventory, quotes, and contact numbers. The budget should include a contingency line; moves reliably produce one surprise expense, and a planned contingency is cheaper than a credit-card one.
  5. Measure the new place. Door widths, stair turns, and room sizes decide which furniture travels. Discovering a sofa does not fit after you have paid to move it is the most avoidable cost in the entire countdown.
  6. Book, in writing. Great Canadian Van Lines calls booking the mover as early as possible, once the date is confirmed, the most important first step — good movers and good dates are reserved weeks in advance, especially in summer. The same applies to a rental truck, a container, an elevator reservation, and time off work.

Weeks 6–5: shrink the load and gather records

Every item you discard in week six is an item you do not pack, carry, insure, or pay to transport. Work room by room, starting with storage areas — attic, basement, garage, and lockers — exactly as Allied Van Lines’ timeline directs at eight weeks: clear the storage spaces first and start using up food and supplies that cannot be moved. Sort into keep, donate, sell, and discard, and give each exit a deadline inside these two weeks. A donation pile with no pickup date is not decluttering; it is relocation of clutter from one room to another.

In parallel, gather the records that are slow to replace. Allied’s six-week list includes obtaining personal records from doctors, dentists, schools, and accountants, and building the list of everyone who must be notified of the move — creditors, subscriptions, family, and professional contacts. Add vet records if you have pets, and vehicle and insurance documents. Keep personal copies even when an institution says it will forward records directly; copies in your binder close gaps when a transfer stalls.

Weeks 4–3: pack the non-essentials and file the paperwork

Packing begins with what you can live without for a month: off-season clothing, books, décor, spare linens, and the kitchen items used twice a year. Label every box by room and contents, and keep a simple inventory list in the binder. Pack one room at a time to completion rather than skimming all rooms at once — finished rooms give you an honest picture of progress and keep daily life functional in the rooms that remain open.

This is also the paperwork window. File change-of-address notices, arrange mail forwarding to start on moving day, schedule utility disconnections at the old address and connections at the new one, and notify insurers, banks, and subscriptions from the notification list you built in week six. Paperwork done at week four can be corrected at week three. Paperwork discovered on moving day cannot. If children or pets are part of the move, school registration, childcare arrangements, and a vet visit belong in this window too — not because they are urgent yet, but because appointments and school offices keep business hours that moving week will not respect.

Week 2: confirm everything in writing

Week two has a single discipline: nothing is confirmed until it is confirmed in writing. Reconfirm the mover or truck reservation with date, arrival window, addresses, and contact numbers. Reconfirm elevator and loading-dock bookings at both buildings. Reconfirm helpers, childcare, and pet care for moving day. Confirm utility transfer dates and record the confirmation numbers in the binder. Then pack the essentials kit that will travel with you — documents, medications, chargers, toiletries, a change of clothes, basic tools, snacks, and the first-night items for the new home. As the moving guides consistently warn, important documents, medications, jewellery, cash, and anything irreplaceable travel with you personally, never on the truck.

Week 1: finish packing, protect the first night

The final week is for finishing, not starting. Complete all packing except the daily-use items for the remaining days. Disassemble furniture that needs it, bagging and labelling hardware and taping it to the piece it belongs to. Defrost the freezer, use up perishables, photograph meter readings and the condition of both homes, and clean as rooms empty. Stage boxes by room near the exit, heaviest closest to the door. Charge phones, lay out the day bag, and do a final walk-through list for moving morning: every closet, cabinet, drawer, balcony, and storage locker. If a task appears this week that is not packing, cleaning, or verification, it is a task that belonged in an earlier week — note it for next time rather than letting it consume the protected week.

Moving day and the first 48 hours

On the day, one person directs and everyone else carries. Keep the essentials kit and day bag in your own vehicle before loading begins. At the new home, set up beds, bathroom, and kitchen basics first, and stop there — full unpacking is a first-week project. In the first 48 hours, verify utilities are live, check every delivered item against the inventory before signing any delivery receipt, note damage in writing at the time, and keep all receipts in the binder until the final bills have cleared.

What success looks like

You have run the countdown well when moving week contains no bookings, no purchasing decisions, and no paperwork — only packing, cleaning, and confirmations you can complete in minutes. Your mover or truck was booked at a price you chose from at least three written estimates, nothing you paid to move was discarded within the first month at the new home, and the first night required no emergency shopping because the essentials kit travelled with you. If something did slip, it slipped into the contingency line of a budget that had one — which is also a success, of the quieter kind.

FAQ

How far in advance should I start a moving checklist?

Eight weeks out for most moves, and ten to twelve weeks for a larger home or a long-distance move. In Canadian peak season — late spring through early fall — book the mover as soon as the date is confirmed, because availability fills weeks ahead.

What is the most important thing to do first?

Lock the moving date, then book the mover or truck. Every other task is scheduled backwards from the date, and the booking is the one item whose price and availability both worsen with delay.

What should never go on the moving truck?

Important documents, medications, jewellery, cash, and anything irreplaceable. Keep them in the essentials kit in your own vehicle on moving day.
