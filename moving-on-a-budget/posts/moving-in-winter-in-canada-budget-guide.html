Cold weather, lower quotes

Moving in Winter in Canada: Keep the Off Season Discount and Lose the Weather Surprises

Off season moves run about 10 to 30 percent below peak pricing. The saving is real, and so are the three costs that can eat it: a storm delay, a cold damaged screen, and an unheated house at the other end.

Moving on a Budget · Budget move guide

10 to 30 percent. That is the discount range one Canadian long distance mover, citing industry data, puts on off season moves from roughly October through April compared with peak season pricing, checked November 3, 2026. On a labelled example quote of $2,000 in summer, the same move priced 10 percent lower costs $1,800, and at 30 percent lower it costs $1,400. The arithmetic is simple. Keeping the $200 to $600 difference is the actual job, because winter charges its own fees in a different currency: time, breakage, and one cold night in a house with no heat.

This guide is general information, not financial advice. Prices and policies vary by company and region, so confirm every quote and date in writing before you book.

The three traps that spend the discount

Trap one is a fixed date with no flex. A single storm can close a mountain pass or slow a highway for a day or more, and a crew cannot load safely in a whiteout. Environment and Climate Change Canada defines a blizzard in its alerts as blowing snow giving widespread reduced visibility of 400 metres or less. If your lease ends, your truck is due back, and your crew is booked for one exact day, a storm turns a discount move into hotel nights and late fees.

Trap two is temperature shock. Cold on its own rarely breaks furniture. Moisture, condensation, and a fast warm up do the damage. Screens are the clearest case: a cold television or monitor powered up too soon can suffer condensation on cold components. The winter procedure used by the mover cited above is to let electronics reach room temperature for several hours before plugging anything in.

Trap three is arriving to a cold, dark house. Heat and power need to be live before delivery day, not after. Arriving to an unheated house in a deep freeze turns an unload into an emergency, and pipes, people, and plants all dislike it equally.

Two free tools do most of the forecasting work. Environment and Climate Change Canada issues colour coded weather alerts through Canada.ca/weather and the WeatherCAN app, covering warnings, advisories, and watches. Ontario 511 publishes forecasted driving conditions in three hour blocks up to 12 hours ahead, rated good, fair, caution, or poor, alongside reported road conditions, cameras, and a Track My Plow feature showing where winter maintenance vehicles are working. Other provinces run their own 511 services. None of these tools costs anything, and checking them is the cheapest insurance in this entire guide.

The winter move procedure

  1. Book a window, not a day. Ask every mover and truck rental what happens if weather closes your route: who calls whom, how the date shifts, and what it costs. Get the answer in writing before you pay a deposit. Success looks like a booking that can slide by a day without a new quote.
  2. Confirm heat and power at the new home first. Before load day, verify the furnace runs and the electricity is on at the destination. If you are renting, get that confirmation from the landlord in writing.
  3. Clear and salt both ends. Shovel a wide lane from door to truck at the old home and the new home, and salt or sand steps and ramps before the crew arrives. Keep a shovel and salt at the destination too, because snow that falls during the drive still needs clearing before the first carry.
  4. Protect floors before the first box crosses the door. Slush, salt, and grit travel indoors on boots. Lay runners, old rugs, or plastic sheeting along the carry path, tape the edges so the protection itself does not become a slip hazard, and keep towels and a boot tray at the door.
  5. Pack for cold, not just for distance. Wrap furniture in blankets first, then plastic over the blanket, so condensation is absorbed by the blanket layer instead of sitting against a finish. Keep boxes off wet or snowy surfaces with a barrier underneath, because softened cardboard crushes in transit. Liquids, candles, instruments, and electronics deserve the warmest spot in the load or a ride in your own heated vehicle where rules allow.
  6. Carry a winter go bag in your own vehicle. Warm layers, blankets, medications, phone chargers, and a day of non perishable food travel with you, never in the truck. If travel stalls overnight, you already have what you need.
  7. Check conditions the morning of travel. Open your provincial 511 service and the Environment Canada alerts for both ends of the route. If conditions are rated poor or a warning is in effect, use the flex you booked in step one instead of arguing with the weather.
  8. Let cold electronics warm up before power. At the new home, set screens and computers aside for several hours until they reach room temperature, then plug in and test. A screen that powers on normally after warming is the success check.

When winter is not worth it

Skip the off season discount if any of these are true. Your dates cannot move by even one day, because a storm delay then costs more than the saving. Your load is heavy with items that cannot tolerate sustained sub zero cold and you have no heated transport or storage option for them. Your route crosses mountain passes and your own vehicle is not legal for the corridor, since winter tire and chain rules in British Columbia run from October 1 to April 30 on most highways. Or the quote you were given shows no real discount at all: a winter price that matches the summer price buys you all of the weather and none of the saving, so ask for the off season number explicitly and compare it in writing.

Handled with a flexible window, a cleared lane, and heat waiting at the other end, a winter move is not a gamble. It is the same move, at a lower price, with a shorter list of surprises. Pair it with the timing advice in the cheapest time to move guide and the day itself runs off the moving day timeline.
