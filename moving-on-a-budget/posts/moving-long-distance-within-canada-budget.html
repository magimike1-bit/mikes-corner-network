Moving Long-Distance Within Canada on a Budget: Ship, Drive, or Container?

For a one-bedroom from Toronto to Vancouver, published Canadian ranges run from $1,500 for freight to $8,000 for full-service movers — before the car is counted at all. The honest comparison puts every option, and the car, on one ledger.

Moving on a Budget · Budget move guide

$1,800 to $8,000. That is the spread for moving the same one-bedroom household from Toronto to Vancouver, depending on which method carries it — and none of those figures includes getting your car across the country. Long-distance quotes frustrate people because each source prices a different basket: one quotes a one-bedroom, the next a two-bedroom, and the do-it-yourself figure quietly leaves out the fuel and hotel nights of a cross-country drive. Fix the route, fix the home size, and add the car back in, and the ranking gets clearer. The ranges below come from Canadian moving-cost sources checked October 25, 2026. They are market ranges to verify with dated quotes, not tariffs — no mover in the research publishes a fixed price list, and your addresses will move the number.

One route, five options, one home size

Start with the household alone. For a Toronto-to-Vancouver one-bedroom, one Canadian cost source published these ranges: freight at $1,500–$3,000, consolidated moving at $1,800–$2,800, a DIY rental truck at $2,500–$4,500, a portable container at $3,000–$5,000, and full-service movers at $4,500–$8,000.

Home size bends every one of those numbers. A second source pricing two-bedroom cross-Canada moves put full-service at $4,200–$9,000, containers at $2,500–$6,000, and a DIY truck at $2,500–$4,500. The overlap is the lesson: a small household moved the expensive way can cost less than a large household moved the cheap way, which is why comparing a neighbour's quote to your own rarely tells you anything.

Timing bends them too. The Toronto–Vancouver source notes that peak season adds roughly 15–20% to long-distance prices. On a $4,500 full-service quote, that range is worth roughly $675–$900 — arithmetic on the published figures, and a reason to ask any summer quote what the same move costs in a quieter month.

The car belongs on the same ledger

Most comparisons stop at the furniture and leave the car as a separate problem. It is not separate; it comes out of the same bank account. Vehicle shipping within Canada was priced at $1,700–$3,400 depending on route and vehicle in the research for this guide, and rail shipping between Vancouver and Toronto at $2,200–$3,200, with transit times of two to five weeks.

The DIY truck row is missing from that table on purpose. Driving the truck usually means the car problem solves itself one of two ways — a second driver brings the car, or the car is towed or shipped anyway — and the truck figure itself is incomplete, which is the next problem.

The two-to-five-week rail transit time deserves a line of its own. Ship the car by rail and the household may arrive well before the vehicle does. Price that gap honestly: rental days, transit passes, or simply doing without — whichever applies to your household — belong in the comparison beside the shipping quote.

What the DIY sticker leaves out

The DIY range of $2,500–$4,500 looks like the budget answer until its exclusions are named. The two-bedroom source states plainly that its DIY figures exclude fuel, accommodation, and drop-off costs. A one-way rental also carries a one-way structure: the truck is dropped in a different city from where it was collected, and that fee sits outside the headline rate.

None of that makes DIY the wrong choice. It makes the sticker the wrong comparison. Build the DIY total the way the other options are already built:

  1. Start with the rental quote for your exact dates and drop-off city, in writing.
  2. Add the road costs as their own lines: fuel for the full distance, hotel nights for every night the drive realistically takes, and food for the travel days. Use your own vehicle's consumption and your own stopping pace — a range borrowed from a guide would be a guess wearing a number's clothes.
  3. Add the car line: a second driver's travel back, towing, or the shipping range above.
  4. Compare totals to totals. Only now does the DIY figure sit honestly beside the container and consolidated ranges.

Short version: DIY still wins for many households. It just wins by less than the sticker suggests, and occasionally — once hotels and a shipped car are added — it does not win at all.

The middle options, and what full-service buys

Freight ($1,500–$3,000) and consolidated moving ($1,800–$2,800) sit at the low end of the one-bedroom ranges. Their trade is flexibility: consolidated loads share truck space with other households, which is where the saving comes from, and shared space means the schedule is partly the carrier's, not yours. If your dates can bend, that bend is worth real money — the gap between consolidated and full-service at the top of the ranges is more than $5,000.

The container ($3,000–$5,000) prices differently again: you load it, it travels without you, and nobody in the household drives a large vehicle across four time zones. Against full-service ($4,500–$8,000), the premium buys the labour and the driving being someone else's job from door to door. Whether that is worth $1,500–$3,000 over the container range is a question about your household's time, backs, and holiday days — not a question a price table can settle.

Quote discipline: every figure here is a published range, checked October 25, 2026, from mover- and marketplace-authored sources that disagree with each other. That disagreement is normal for this market. Get two or three dated quotes for your actual addresses, in the same week, and let those — not any guide's range — make the final call.

Choosing by distance, load, and labour

  • Load first. A one-bedroom and a two-bedroom do not share a price list. Shrink the load before pricing it; every option gets cheaper when there is less to carry.
  • Dates second. Flexible dates favour freight and consolidated loads. Fixed dates push you toward the options you control: the truck, the container, or full-service.
  • Labour third, honestly assessed. If nobody can load, drive, or unload, the DIY range is fiction for your household no matter how low it sits. Price the options that match the labour you actually have.
  • The car last, but never skipped. Decide ship, drive, tow, or sell before signing the household contract — a $1,700–$3,400 surprise after the fact undoes careful work everywhere else.

Success looks like this: one page, every option totalled the same way, the car included, the road costs written down for the driving options, and a dated quote confirming the range you budgeted from. If the final invoice lands inside the combined range you built, the plan worked — even if the option you picked was not the cheapest row in the table.

FAQ

What is the cheapest way to move long-distance within Canada?

On the published one-bedroom Toronto–Vancouver ranges, freight ($1,500–$3,000) and consolidated moving ($1,800–$2,800) sit lowest — before the car is added and before DIY road costs are counted. The cheapest option for a given household is the one with the lowest complete total, not the lowest sticker.

Is it cheaper to ship a car or drive it across Canada?

Shipping was priced at $1,700–$3,400 in the research, with rail between Vancouver and Toronto at $2,200–$3,200 and a two-to-five-week transit. Driving costs fuel, nights, and days that vary too much by household for a guide to price honestly — total your own road costs and compare them against a shipping quote for your route.

How much does peak season add?

One Canadian source puts the peak-season increase at roughly 15–20% on long-distance prices. Treat it as a prompt to ask for an off-peak quote for the same move, not as a promised discount.
