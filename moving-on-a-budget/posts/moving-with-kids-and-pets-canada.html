Moving With Kids and Pets in Canada: School, Daycare, and Pet Records Without the Scramble

Adults move boxes. Kids and pets move routines, records, and relationships — and each of those has a handoff that can stall if you leave it to moving week. Here is how to run the three handoffs in parallel with the packing.

Moving on a Budget · Budget move guide

A move with children or pets fails in predictable places, and none of them is the truck. It fails when the new school cannot enrol your child because a document is in a packed box, when childcare has a waitlist you joined too late, or when a pet’s vaccination record is needed and nobody knows which vet holds it. The fix is to stop treating “the family move” as one job. It is three record handoffs — school or daycare, health, and pet — running alongside the household move, each with its own documents, its own office hours, and its own deadline. Start them in the same weeks you start packing and they finish quietly. Start them in moving week and they compete with the truck for your attention, and the truck always wins.

Keep a family folder — separate from the household moving binder — that travels with you, never in the truck. Every document in the table above goes in it as a paper copy plus a phone photo. If a handoff stalls, the folder is what lets you fix it from a parking lot instead of from inside a sealed box.

School and daycare: registration is not the same as records

The most useful distinction in the Canadian school-move guidance is between two jobs that share a name. Registration establishes that your child may enrol. Academic records tell the new school where your child is and what support is already in place. They involve different documents, often different offices, and confusing them is how enrolment stalls.

Registration requirements vary by province, school board, district, and your family’s circumstances — there is no single Canadian list, so check the receiving board’s current requirements rather than assuming the old board’s list transfers. Depending on the system and situation, registration may require proof of age or identity, proof of the new home address, immigration or status documents where applicable, and custody or guardianship records where applicable. Toronto’s board, for example, lists proof of age and address among its required documentation, while Vancouver’s board guidance also covers status documents and parental-responsibility records in applicable situations. Daycare and preschool moves are simpler on paper but harder on timing: licensed spaces are allocated from waitlists, so joining the new waitlist belongs in week eight, not week two, and you should confirm in writing what happens to your deposit and notice period at the current provider.

Academic records are the second handoff. Keep accessible copies of recent report cards, current coursework or major assignments, and — where applicable — individual education plans or other learning-support documentation and a note of accommodations already in use. Vancouver’s board specifically asks families in relevant situations to provide report cards and available IEP or learning-support records. Ask the current school, explicitly, what it will transfer directly to the new school and what the family is expected to provide. Schools handle this differently — some forward records school-to-school, others hand parents sealed copies to deliver. Agree the method before you leave, and keep your own copies regardless: personal copies are the backup that keeps a delayed transfer from becoming a delayed start.

Health records: the step that can delay a start

Health paperwork is the handoff families most often discover last. Many schools require up-to-date health and vaccination records, and depending on the province the immunization record may be reported to the local public health unit rather than handed to the school — the systems differ, which is exactly why this belongs at four to six weeks out, while there is still time to follow whichever process applies where you are going. Before the move, confirm each child’s immunization record is complete and obtain a copy for the family folder. Refill prescriptions with enough margin to cover the move plus the time it takes to register with a new doctor or clinic, and carry medications with you — never in the truck, where a delayed delivery becomes a missed dose. If your move crosses a provincial border, ask both the current provider and the new province’s health registration office what applies for coverage and registration; do not assume the card in your wallet simply continues.

Pets: records, transport, and the new vet — the tested procedure

  1. Book the vet visit at four to six weeks out. Confirm vaccinations are current, discuss the journey honestly (distance, mode, your pet’s temperament), and ask whether any health certificate or documentation is advisable for your specific route and mode of travel.
  2. Get the records into the family folder. For a long-distance move in Canada, pet medical records — including vaccination records and health certificates — are essential documents to keep at hand, in case an airline or a new landlord asks for them. Add the current vet’s contact details and a list of medications, foods, and routines.
  3. Check the transport rules for your actual carrier and route. Rules for pets in cabin, in cargo, or in a vehicle vary by airline, ferry, and carrier, and they change — read the carrier’s current pet policy directly and confirm crate, size, and booking requirements with the carrier before moving week. Do not rely on a summary in any guide, including this one, for a rule that decides whether your pet boards.
  4. Plan the moving-day separation. The safest place for a pet on loading day is a closed, quiet room away from open doors — clearly signed — or with a trusted person off-site. The same logic applies to young children: if childcare or a family helper can cover loading hours, that is a safety decision, not a luxury.
  5. Register with the new vet early. In the first week, transfer records to a vet near the new home, update microchip and licence contact details to the new address and a phone that will not change, and note the nearest emergency or after-hours clinic before you need it at midnight.

For the journey itself, keep the routine boring: familiar bedding in the carrier, water offered at stops, feeding kept light and on schedule where possible, and never leave a pet in a parked vehicle in warm or cold weather. A pet that arrives calm is usually the product of a short, predictable trip — which is a planning outcome, not luck.

The first-night kit for kids and pets

The first night sets the emotional temperature of the whole move. Build one kit per child and one for pets, packed last, loaded into your own vehicle, and opened first. For each child: pyjamas and a change of clothes, toothbrush and toiletries, medications, a charger, snacks and a water bottle, and the specific comfort items that make a strange room sleepable — a familiar blanket, a stuffed toy, a night-light. Add one low-effort activity for the morning while boxes are still closed. For pets: two days of food measured out separately from the main supply, water and a portable bowl, leash and waste bags or litter and a disposable tray, medications, familiar bedding, and cleaning supplies for the accident that a stressful day makes likely. Set up one room at the new home completely — beds made, bathroom functional, pet area contained — before any general unpacking. A child or a pet who can eat, wash, and sleep in a finished room experiences the move as an adventure with an odd first chapter. Everyone else experiences it as camping in a warehouse.

What success looks like

You have run the family handoffs well when the new school enrols your child on the first visit because every document was in the folder you carried, childcare is confirmed in writing with a start date, and no prescription runs out during the gap between providers. Your pet travels with records in hand, arrives to a contained and familiar-smelling space, and is registered with a new vet before any problem forces the introduction. And the first night ends with everyone — adults included — fed, washed, and asleep in a real bed, with the rest of the boxes still closed and no longer frightening. That is the standard. The boxes can wait until morning; the routines cannot.

FAQ

What documents do I need to enrol my child in a new school in Canada?

It varies by province, board, and circumstances. Commonly: proof of age or identity and proof of the new home address, plus status or custody documents where applicable. Separately, bring academic records — recent report cards and any IEP or learning-support documentation. Check the receiving board’s current list; do not assume your old board’s requirements are the same.

What pet records should I carry when moving?

Vaccination records and, where issued, a health certificate, plus your vet’s contact details and a medication and feeding list. Keep them with you, not in the truck — an airline, a landlord, or a new vet may ask for them, and “they are in box fourteen” is not an answer that boards a pet.

Do schools transfer records themselves?

Sometimes school-to-school, sometimes by sealed copies carried by parents. Ask the current school which method it uses and what the family must provide — and keep your own copies of report cards and support plans either way.
