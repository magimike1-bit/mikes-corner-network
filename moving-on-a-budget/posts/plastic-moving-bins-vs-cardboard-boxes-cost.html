Plastic Moving Bins vs Cardboard Boxes: The Real Cost Per Move

Rented bins arrive assembled, stack tight, and leave when you are done. Cardboard can be free or a few dollars a box. Which one wins depends on one number most comparisons hide: how many weeks you actually keep them.

Moving on a Budget · Budget move guide

Which is cheaper for a one bedroom move in Toronto: renting plastic bins, or buying cardboard?

For one week, renting wins on the published numbers checked November 3, 2026. Cargo Cabbie, a Toronto mover, rents 25 bins for a studio or one bedroom at $162.50 for the first week, and publishes $190.00 as its cardboard comparison for the same move. Keep those bins a second week and the rental rises by $50.00 to $212.50, which is now more than the cardboard figure. The answer flips on a single line, the extension week, and almost no comparison table shows it. This guide is general information, not financial advice.

What each option actually involves

Rented plastic bins. Cargo Cabbie bins measure 27 by 17 by 12 inches and are rated at 40 to 50 pounds per bin. They arrive pre assembled with hinged lids, so there is no tape and no box building, they stack square, and the lids must close completely for safe stacking. The provider delivers, you pack, and the provider picks the bins up when you are done. The costs hiding in this option are the extension weeks above and your unpacking speed: bins are billed by time, so a slow unpack is a meter running.

Bought cardboard. You pay per box, plus tape, and you own the result: boxes you can keep as long as a slow unpack takes, give away, resell, or recycle. Cargo Cabbie publishes $190.00 as the cardboard cost for its 25 bin one bedroom comparison, $240.00 against its 35 bin two bedroom bundle, and $650.00 or more against the 100 bin home bundle. Those are a bin rental company comparing its own product, so treat them as the vendor case for cardboard, not a neutral audit. Your own cardboard total could be lower, especially with the free sources in our free packing supplies guide, or higher if you buy specialty boxes.

Free and reused cardboard. The price is zero and the costs are time and quality control: hunting, collecting, inspecting, and taping boxes of mixed sizes that stack less evenly. For a small load with time to spare, it remains the cheapest option in this entire comparison.

The decision, in four questions

  1. How many weeks will the containers be in your home? One week favours rental on the Toronto numbers above. Two weeks or more, run the extension math before you decide: the 25 bin bundle at $162.50 plus $50.00 beats $190.00 cardboard in week one and loses to it in week two.
  2. How wet is your move? Rain, snow, and a slushy carry path soften cardboard and leave plastic bins unaffected. A winter or rainy week move shifts the value toward bins even at the same price.
  3. How much do you value your packing time? Bins skip assembly and taping entirely and stack faster in the truck. If you are paying movers by the hour, faster stacking is a real offset, not a comfort feature.
  4. What happens after the move? Rentals leave with the provider. Bought cardboard becomes storage, a resale listing, or a recycling trip. If you have no storage space and no use for the boxes, the disposal chore belongs in the cardboard column.

Traps and fine print

Overfilling bins. Cargo Cabbie warns against filling a bin with books or papers: divide heavy items across several bins, because a single overloaded bin can crack and is dangerous to carry. The 40 to 50 pound rating is a ceiling, not a target.

Unclosable lids. A bin that cannot close completely cannot stack safely. Pack to the lid line, not above it.

The quiet extension. Rental periods are easy to extend and the charge follows automatically at pickup with some providers. Put the pickup date in your calendar the day the bins arrive, and book the pickup before moving day fatigue sets in.

When bins are not worth it. A long, slow unpack, a move with a storage gap of several weeks, or a tiny load you can cover with free boxes all point to cardboard. The rental saving is a one week saving; outside that window, owning the box is usually the cheaper and calmer choice.
