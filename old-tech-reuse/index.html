Second jobs for retired gadgets

That drawer of old gadgets is a toolbox.

Before you recycle or donate that retired phone, tablet, router, or PC, check what it can still do. These short, plain-language guides walk you through giving old tech a second job — camera, kitchen display, Wi-Fi extender, or media server — and how to wipe and recycle devices properly when reuse isn't an option.

Guides

Turn an old hard drive into external storage

A bare drive from a retired PC becomes useful storage with the right enclosure or adapter. Check its health first, mind the 2.5 vs 3.5-inch power rule, and wipe it properly.

Use an old desktop PC as a home backup server

An old tower makes a capable backup target for every computer in the house — with honest limits: redundancy is not backup, 24/7 power costs money, and the test restore is the whole point.

Reuse old game console controllers on a PC

Wired Xbox 360 pads work instantly, wireless ones only with the right receiver, a DUALSHOCK 4 pairs over Bluetooth, and even a Wii Remote has a route in — generation by generation, traps included.

Turn an old laptop into a Chromebook — or a lightweight Linux machine

A slow laptop may need a lighter system, not replacing. Triage the hardware first, choose ChromeOS Flex or Linux Mint Xfce with a clear decision framework, and test before anything is erased.

Use a retired game console as a media player

The streaming apps on the PS3, Xbox 360, and Wii have shut down on dated schedules — but discs and your own files over the home network still play. Here is what still works in 2026, console by console.

Put an old monitor back to work as a second display

Run a quick pass-or-fail check on the panel and its connectors, then extend your Windows or Mac desktop onto it — and learn why an all-in-one screen is not a spare monitor.

Turn a retired phone into a home camera

A phone you're not using still has a camera, Wi-Fi, and a charger port. Set it up as a simple home monitoring camera for the front door, the nursery, or the garage.

Turn a retired tablet into a kitchen display

Recipes, timers, a shopping list, and music — mount or stand an old tablet in the kitchen and give it a permanent, useful job.

Give an old router a second job extending Wi-Fi

That router you replaced may still have life in it. Reconfigure it as a wired access point or extender to reach dead spots in the house.

Turn an old computer into a home media server

An old desktop or laptop can host your movies, music, and photos on your home network — streaming to your TV and phones without new hardware.

When reuse isn't an option: donate and recycle responsibly

Wipe your data properly first, then donate working devices where they're wanted — and recycle the rest through real e-waste channels, not the trash.
