25 Uses That Have Nothing to Do With Reading

The big list · 7 min read

You know the books. The organic chemistry textbook, still in its shrink-wrap of good intentions. The statistics doorstopper that taught you exactly one thing: the probability you would finish it was zero. They have sat on your shelf for years, too expensive to throw away, too boring to open. Good news: reading was never their only job. Here are twenty-five others.

Around the house

  1. Doorstop. The single greatest application of a 1,400-page textbook. It will hold any door, including doors you never want open.
  2. Monitor riser. Two large textbooks, one on each side, laptop in the middle. Ergonomics is just stacking things until your neck stops hurting.
  3. Paperweight. The obvious one. A textbook holds down anything short of a balloon.
  4. Wobbly table stabilizer. Shove one under the short leg. Cafés have been doing this with menus for decades; you're just doing it with higher education.
  5. Laptop cooling stand. Open a hardcover at a right angle and rest the laptop on it. Air flows underneath. Thermodynamics, sort of.
  6. Ironing board substitute. Folded towel on top of the thickest book you own. Fine for a shirt collar emergency. Fine.
  7. Wall art. Frame the cover. "Calculus: Early Transcendentals" in a gold frame says "I am sophisticated" in a way a diploma never could.
  8. Booster seat. A toddler on two textbooks at the dinner table is living the dream.
  9. Mouse pad. For a trackball mouse, technically. For a regular mouse, the spine is an excellent wrist hazard. Use the flat cover side.
  10. Speaker stand. Two matching textbooks, one per speaker. Audiophiles pay hundreds for isolation stands. Yours are pre-amortized.

Kitchen and pantry

  1. Cutting board stand-in. A sacrificial thin textbook under a cutting board keeps everything steady. (Not the cutting surface itself. We have standards. Barely.)
  2. Countertop height for rolling dough. One thick book under the board brings the work up to your arms instead of your back.
  3. Flour and sugar bag holders. Books leaned against bags keep them upright in the cabinet.

Garage and outdoors

  1. Wheel chock. On a truly flat driveway, behind a wheel. (On a hill: use a real chock. We're comedians, not engineers.)
  2. Kneeling pad. Garden weeding on a thin textbook is luxury compared to bare dirt.
  3. Plant riser. Short books lift pots to window light. Bonus: the plants finally read something, vicariously.
  4. Kindling of last resort. In a genuine emergency, paper burns. This one hurts to type. Do it only if you are truly cold and have truly forgiven the book.

Office and pranks

  1. Noise barrier. A wall of textbooks between you and a talkative coworker absorbs some sound and sends a clear message.
  2. Secret storage. Hollow out the middle pages and store... more bookmarks you'll never use.
  3. Present-wrapping decoy. Wrap an actual gift in a textbook-sized box and watch a child's face fall. Educational.
  4. Halloween costume prop. Carry one visibly and be "a person carrying an unreadable burden." Very meta.

The classics

  1. Proof you went to college. Shelves of unopened textbooks are the fastest way to win an argument about your education.
  2. Gift to a younger sibling. "It's a classic. Treasure it." Watch the cycle begin anew.
  3. Souvenir. That one chapter you actually read deserves a monument.
  4. A very square pillow. Road trip naps wait for no one.

The verdict

Notice that none of these required opening the book. These volumes have spent their lives being potential — potential knowledge, potential regret, potential shelf support. Finally, they can be something real: furniture, ballast, a punchline. The textbook's highest calling was never the syllabus. It was always gravity.
