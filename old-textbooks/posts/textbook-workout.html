The Textbook Workout

Comedic fitness · 6 min read

Your gym membership is gathering dust next to your textbooks. Clearly, the solution is to combine the two sources of guilt into one routine. The average textbook weighs between 3 and 8 pounds of concentrated unread potential, which — and I cannot stress this enough — is not a real fitness credential. This workout is a joke. Do not get injured. If something feels wrong, stop, put the book down, and open an actual window instead.

The warm-up: the textbook shelf-reach

Stand before your bookshelf. Reach for the highest textbook with both hands, slowly. Extend. Hold. This stretches your shoulders and builds the specific muscle group responsible for reshelving books you just moved out of the way. Repeat with three different books. Rest by reading the back cover, which is more than you've read all year.

The textbook curl

Grip a medium textbook by the spine, elbow pinned to your side, and curl it toward your shoulder. The pages fan open as you lift, which is either terrible form or a reading plan, depending on your optimism. Do three sets of ten per arm. Switch to a heavier book when "Intro to Psychology" starts feeling light — that happens right around the moment you give up on self-improvement.

The overhead knowledge press

Hold a textbook flat in both hands at chest level, then press it straight overhead. This is called "putting your knowledge over your head," and it is exactly as productive as it sounds. Eight to ten reps. Bonus difficulty: press "Organic Chemistry" and contemplate every decision that led you to own it. The emotional weight adds roughly 15% to the perceived load.

The farmer's carry (textbook edition)

Grab the two heaviest textbooks you own, one in each hand, and walk the length of your hallway. This is what moving apartments felt like, except voluntary and with better posture. Keep your back straight, your shoulders back, and your eyes forward — never down at the titles, lest you remember the resale value and stop mid-hall to grieve.

The textbook balance challenge

Place a thin textbook on your head and walk across the room. This exercise builds core stability and also reenacts a debutante class you never attended. Graduate to a thicker book for advanced training. Fall over at least once: the sound of a textbook hitting the floor is the sound of knowledge settling.

The cool-down: the reshelve

Return every book to its shelf slowly, with control. This eccentric movement — putting things back where they were before you started — is the hardest part of any routine and the least practiced. Congratulations: you have exercised, sort of, and the books are exactly where they started. Nothing has changed. And yet, somehow, everything has.

The heavy-lift bonus round

Ready to graduate from joke-gym to slightly-less-joke-gym? Try the textbook squat: hug the biggest book to your chest, keep your heels down, and squat until your thighs object. Ten reps, then sit down and think about what you're doing with your life. The answer is: comedy. The correct answer is always comedy. If you enjoyed this, consider actual dumbbells — they're designed for this, have no chapters, and won't guilt you about your GPA.

The light safety note

Genuinely, for one paragraph: books are awkward, unbalanced weights with sharp corners and pages that shift mid-lift. If you actually try any of this, use the lighter books, clear some floor space, and don't lift anything heavy over your head while standing on furniture. The joke ends where the sprained wrist begins. Stretch, hydrate, and remember that walking around the block is free and has no spine injuries associated with it.
