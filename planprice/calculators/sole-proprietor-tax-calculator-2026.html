Sole-Proprietor Tax Estimate Calculator 2026 (Canada)

Free simplified Canadian sole-proprietor tax estimator: net business income into estimated federal tax plus self-employed CPP contributions, using 2026 federal rates. Estimate only — not tax advice.

How it works

Two things hit a Canadian sole proprietor that don't hit an employee the same way. First, CPP: you pay both halves — 11.9% of pensionable earnings between $3,500 and the $74,600 maximum (2026), plus 8% CPP2 on the band up to $85,000. On $80,000 of net income that's $8,460.90 + $432.00 = $8,892.90. Second, federal income tax at 2026 brackets (14% up to $58,523, then 20.5%), less the basic personal amount credit of $16,452 × 14%.

Worked example at $80,000: CPP $8,892.90; federal tax ≈ $9,381.20 after the basic personal credit and an approximate deduction for half the CPP; total ≈ $18,274.10 — an effective federal rate of about 22.8%, before provincial tax.

What changed for 2026

Two changes pull in opposite directions: the lowest federal rate drops to 14% for the full year, which saves every filer money, while the CPP ceilings rise, which costs higher earners more. Bracket thresholds and the basic personal amount also rise with indexation.
