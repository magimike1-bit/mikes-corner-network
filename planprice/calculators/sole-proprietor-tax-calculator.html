How it works

Two things hit a Canadian sole proprietor that don't hit an employee the same way. First, CPP: you pay both halves — 11.9% of pensionable earnings between $3,500 and the $71,300 maximum (2025), plus 8% CPP2 on the band up to $81,200. On $80,000 of net income that's $8,068.20 + $696.00 = $8,764.20. Second, federal income tax at 2025 brackets (15% up to $57,375, then 20.5%), less the basic personal amount credit of $16,129 × 15%.

Worked example at $80,000: CPP $8,764.20; federal tax ≈ $9,926.69 after the basic personal credit and an approximate deduction for half the CPP; total ≈ $18,690.89 — an effective federal rate of about 23.4%, before provincial tax.

Your real liability will differ. Set money aside monthly and confirm the final numbers with an accountant or the CRA before filing.
