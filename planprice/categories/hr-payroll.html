How to compare HR & Payroll pricing

HR and payroll tools are priced per employee per month, or, as with Humi on this page, by custom quote. "Custom" means there's no published list price: you request a quote at your headcount, and the price scales with employee count plus which modules you turn on (payroll, benefits, time-off, onboarding). The only honest way to compare is at the same headcount with the same modules: a quote for 10 employees tells you nothing about the cost at 50, and a payroll-only quote tells you nothing about the full platform.

This is the category where a Canadian vendor earns its keep. Canadian payroll compliance. CRA remittances, records of employment, T4s, and provincial rules, is the whole reason to pick a Canadian-built system, and the page flags Humi as exactly that. Ask where employee data is hosted, because payroll records are the most sensitive data a small business holds. And get the quote in writing with the per-employee rate spelled out, so renewal increases don't surprise you.

How to choose

HR software sits in an odd spot: you need it most at the moments you think about it least, the new hire's first day, the parental leave request, the payroll run that has to be right. The right platform makes those moments boring, which is exactly the point.

Humi, now part of Employment Hero, is a Canadian HR platform covering onboarding, time off, documents, and payroll in one system. The core HR platform is quote-based, you talk to sales for pricing, while payroll is the published piece. This guide covers how to evaluate that model and what Canadian businesses should check first.

Separate HR from payroll

HR and payroll are sold together but fail separately. HR is onboarding, time-off tracking, documents, and org charts; payroll is the money movement with tax filings attached. Decide which pain is sharper: if it is onboarding chaos, weight the HR features; if it is payroll errors, weight the payroll engine.

Integration between the two still matters, time-off balances should flow into payroll without retyping. But do not let a slick HR demo distract from a weak payroll engine, or vice versa. Evaluate each half on its own.

Put Canadian compliance first

Payroll is where jurisdiction bites. Canadian payroll means the right tax tables, records of employment, and year-end slips handled correctly, not as an afterthought to a US-first product. A Canadian-built platform like Humi starts from Canadian compliance; with anything else, verify it explicitly.

Ask who is responsible when the rules change. Tax tables update yearly; the vendor should handle that silently. If compliance updates are your job, you do not have an HR platform, you have a spreadsheet with a login.

Understand quote-based pricing

Some HR platforms publish every tier; others, like Humi's core platform, price on a quote after a sales conversation. Quote-based pricing is not automatically a bad deal, it often reflects that the product is configured to your headcount and modules, but it changes how you compare.

Get the quote in writing with the exact headcount, modules, and term it assumes, then re-quote if any of those change. And always ask what the renewal looks like: the first-year quote is a sales tool; the renewal quote is the real price.

Trial the moments that matter

Do not trial the dashboard, trial the stressful moments. Run a mock onboarding for a fictional hire, request time off as an employee, and process a test payroll. The platform should make each of these feel routine.

Involve the people who will actually click the buttons: the office manager running payroll, the new hire onboarding themselves. Their verdict matters more than the buyer's, because they live with the choice.

Quick checklist

  • Decide whether HR chaos or payroll pain is the sharper problem, and weight accordingly.
  • Verify Canadian payroll compliance: tax tables, records of employment, year-end slips.
  • Get quote-based pricing in writing with headcount, modules, and term specified.
  • Ask what the renewal price looks like, not just the first-year quote.
  • Trial onboarding, time-off requests, and a test payroll, not just the dashboard.
  • Confirm time-off balances flow into payroll without manual re-entry.
