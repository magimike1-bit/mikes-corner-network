BigCommerce vs Shopify: Platform Pricing for Canadian Stores (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: Shopify is the ecosystem buy: CAD list pricing (Basic C$49, Grow C$132, Advanced C$517 monthly), the largest app and theme market, and a standing new-merchant promotion (3 days free, then $1/month for 3 months). BigCommerce is the built-ins buy: lower USD entry (Core $29/month billed annually) with more native features — discounts, ratings, faceted search — that Shopify often rents back as apps. Price the app stack, not just the plan: that's where this comparison is actually decided.

Where they actually differ

Currency: Shopify publishes a CAD price list; BigCommerce prices in USD. At small volumes that FX line can erase BigCommerce's headline advantage.

Fee angle: both platforms steer you toward their own payments; using third-party gateways can add platform fees on some tiers — confirm the current terms on the tier you are buying, in the vendor's own pricing/terms pages, before projecting margins.

Fit: BigCommerce's native B2B and multi-storefront features suit catalogue-heavy sellers; Shopify's checkout, POS tie-in and app depth suit most Canadian small retailers — see the retail teardown for the POS side.

Who should pick which

Pick Shopify if you want CAD pricing, the deepest app market, and the current $1/month on-ramp. Pick BigCommerce if its built-ins replace two or three paid Shopify apps at your volume — do that math app by app, not by headline plan price.

The app-stack audit

Do this comparison with a spreadsheet, not a vibe. List the functions your store needs beyond the platform — reviews, subscriptions, loyalty, advanced search, email — then price each as a Shopify app and check which are native on BigCommerce. Stores commonly find $50–$150 a month of app subscriptions on the Shopify side, against a shorter list on BigCommerce. But run it honestly in both directions: Shopify's app market also contains the niche tool BigCommerce simply doesn't offer, and a missing integration can cost more than a subscription. The platform fee is the floor of the comparison; the app stack is the building.

Growth breakpoints

Both platforms re-price you as you grow — BigCommerce's tiers historically key off annual sales volume, Shopify's off features. A store forecasting a breakout year should model next year's tier, not this year's: the cheapest platform at $100k of sales is not always the cheapest at $400k. And both platforms' payment steering (preferred rates on their own rails, added fees possible on third-party gateways at some tiers) means the payments decision and the platform decision are the same decision — model them together with your real card mix.

Frequently asked questions

Which platform is cheaper in year one? For a new merchant, Shopify's $1/month-for-3-months promotion makes its platform line nearly free in quarter one; BigCommerce's $29 USD entry can still win if its built-ins replace paid apps. Model both with your app list.

Do I need a separate POS with either? Shopify has its own POS line (relevant if you also sell in person — see the retail teardown). BigCommerce connects to third-party POS systems. Stores that are online-only can ignore the question.

What happens if I outgrow the entry tier? Both re-price by tier as features or volume demand — model next year's tier before choosing, especially on BigCommerce where tiers historically key off annual sales volume.
