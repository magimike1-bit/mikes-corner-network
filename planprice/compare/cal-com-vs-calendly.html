Cal.com vs Calendly: Scheduling Pricing Compared (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: Calendly is the incumbent: Standard at $10/seat/month billed annually (checked October 25, 2026), Teams at $16, and the polish of a decade of booking pages. Cal.com is the open challenger: a real free tier, Teams at $16/user/month, Organizations at $37 — and an open-source core you can self-host if data residency drives the decision. Most teams should buy on fit, not the $6 seat gap: Calendly for frictionless external booking today, Cal.com for teams that want control, branding depth and a free start.

Where they actually differ

Free-tier reality: Cal.com's free plan covers solo scheduling properly; Calendly's free tier is a single event type — a trial in disguise. Per-seat math only starts when the team does.

Where Calendly earns it: routing forms, round-robin at scale, and an integration catalogue that rarely says no. Where Cal.com earns it: workflows, white-labelling, and the self-host option no closed rival offers.

Who should pick which

Pick Calendly if booking volume is high, the team is sales-shaped, and $10/seat is trivial next to meeting value. Pick Cal.com if you want to start free with room to grow, or if hosting the scheduler yourself is a compliance answer.

Team math

A five-person team on Calendly Standard pays $50/month billed annually. The same team on Cal.com Teams pays $80 — but starts free until the team features bind, which for many small teams is months later than they assume. The crossover isn't really price; it's shape. Calendly charges from the first serious use and delivers the mature product. Cal.com charges later and delivers control (branding depth, workflows, self-hosting). Organizations ($37/user) is Cal.com's governance tier — SSO and admin machinery — comparable to Calendly's higher tiers rather than its Standard.

The residency question

Cal.com's open-source core makes it the only mainstream scheduler a Canadian business can run on its own infrastructure — relevant if client contracts or sector rules restrict where booking data (names, emails, meeting patterns) may live. That's a niche requirement, but when it applies it's absolute, and no discount on a closed platform answers it. Everyone else should treat self-hosting as what it is: a capability that costs your own maintenance time, worth pricing at your hourly rate before calling it "free."

Frequently asked questions

Is Cal.com really free? Yes for solo scheduling with a functional feature set. Team features (round-robin across members, shared workflows) are where the paid tiers start at $16/user.

Which handles round-robin better? Calendly's routing and round-robin are the mature benchmark; Cal.com covers the pattern for most small teams. High-volume sales teams should trial both with real routing rules.

Can either take payment at booking? Both support paid bookings through payment integrations — relevant for consultants and clinics charging for appointments; the processing runs at standard card rates on top of the subscription.

The bottom line for Canadian teams

Canadian buyers should note both tools bill in USD as published, so the seat gap moves with the exchange rate — and both integrate the calendars (Google, Outlook) a Canadian office already runs. The practical recommendation: solo and duo practices start on Cal.com's free tier and revisit at the first team-booking pain; established teams with routing complexity trial Calendly for a month with real booking volume before committing seats. Either way, the subscription is small next to the value of the meetings themselves — choose the tool your clients complete bookings on without emailing you, and measure exactly that: booking completion rate, not feature lists.
