Capsule CRM vs Pipedrive: Small-Team CRM Pricing Compared (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: Pipedrive starts cheaper per seat (from $14/user/month) and is built by salespeople for pipeline discipline. Capsule starts free for two users, prices its ladder evenly ($18/$36/$54/$72 annual billing), and covers contacts, projects and a shared mailbox in one calmer package. Solo operators and tiny teams → Capsule's free and Starter tiers are hard to beat; pipeline-driven sales teams → Pipedrive earns its seats.

Where they actually differ

The pipeline gate: Capsule allows one sales pipeline until Growth ($36) — a structural limit that decides the tier for any firm with two sales processes. Pipedrive is pipelines all the way down; its gates are automation and reporting depth instead.

Annual vs monthly: Capsule's monthly ladder runs ~4–14% above annual depending on tier (unusually uneven — see our Capsule pricing guide). Pipedrive's headline is its entry per-user price; confirm the annual/monthly split on its page before budgeting.

Currency: both publish in USD; budget exchange on top.

Who should pick which

Pick Capsule if two or fewer people sell, contact management matters as much as pipeline, and free-to-$18 covers you. Pick Pipedrive if the pipeline is the business's operating picture and activity-based selling is the discipline you're buying.

Five-seat math

Five users make the ladders comparable. Capsule Starter: 5 × $18 = $90/month billed annually (one pipeline). Capsule Growth: 5 × $36 = $180 (five pipelines, automation). Pipedrive at its $14 entry: 5 × $14 = $70/month at the entry tier, rising by tier as automation and reporting deepen. The interesting comparison isn't Starter against Pipedrive's entry — it's Growth against Pipedrive's mid-tiers, because both products gate their serious selling machinery there. A team choosing today should trial at the tier it would actually run in year two; both products are cheap at the tier that doesn't fit.

What neither price includes

Email marketing lives outside both CRMs at these tiers (Capsule's is the separate Transpond product; Pipedrive's marketing tools are add-on territory), and telephony/integrations can carry their own costs. If your buying reason includes campaigns, price the companion products into the comparison before deciding — the CRM seat is the visible part of a stack that both vendors sell in pieces.

Frequently asked questions

Which CRM is better for a solo consultant? Capsule, on price and shape: free for 2 users covers a solo practice plus an assistant, and Starter at $18 is a gentle first paid step.

Do both integrate with Xero and QuickBooks? Both connect to major accounting tools on paid tiers — confirm the integration sits on the tier you're buying, not just somewhere in the product.

Why do both bill in USD? Both are non-Canadian vendors publishing USD price lists. Budget the exchange on top of every seat, and note the total moves with the dollar — a quiet argument for the free tiers while you prove fit.

The bottom line

These are the two friendliest first paid CRMs in the dataset, and the choice is mostly temperament. Capsule feels like a well-kept contact book that grew pipeline powers; Pipedrive feels like a pipeline that grew contact powers. A firm whose work arrives by relationship and referral will live happily in Capsule for years at $18–36 a seat. A firm whose work arrives by prospecting will feel Pipedrive's activity engine paying rent within a quarter. Both verdicts assume the discipline from our break-even guide: whichever you buy, the follow-up habit is the product — the software is just where it lives.
