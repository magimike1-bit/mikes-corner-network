Deel vs Wagepoint: Global Payroll vs Canadian Payroll Pricing (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: These two rarely compete for the same hire — and pricing them side by side shows why. Wagepoint runs Canadian payroll for a base fee plus a few dollars per employee (Lite $20 + $4, Core $40 + $6, Plus $80 + $10, per its October 22, 2026 fee schedule). Deel sells cross-border employment itself: contractor management at $49/contractor/month and full Employer-of-Record at $599/employee/month, where Deel is the legal employer in the worker's country. Canadian staff → Wagepoint, by an order of magnitude. Hiring where you have no entity → Deel, and the $599 is the price of the entity, not of payroll software.

Where they actually differ

What the money buys: Wagepoint calculates, deducts, remits and files for Canadian employees (T4s, ROEs, CRA remittances). Deel's EOR price bundles foreign employment law, local contracts, benefits administration and payroll in-country. Comparing the two on 'payroll cost' alone compares a tool with a legal structure.

The middle lane: Deel's own Global Payroll ($29/employee/month) is for paying employees of your own foreign entities — closer to software pricing, and the tier to compare if you already have entities abroad.

Who should pick which

Pick Wagepoint for Canadian employees, full stop — it's Canadian-built for exactly this. Pick Deel when the hire is outside Canada and you have no local entity, or for managing international contractors cleanly. Mixed teams routinely run both: Wagepoint at home, Deel abroad.

The mixed-team pattern

The common real-world answer is both. A Toronto firm with eight Canadian staff and two engineers in countries where it has no entity runs Wagepoint for the eight (Core: $40 + 8 × $6 = $88/month) and Deel EOR for the two (2 × $599 = $1,198/month). The ratio looks absurd until you price the alternative to EOR — incorporating abroad, local payroll registration, employment counsel — which makes $599 the cheap version of legal foreign employment. Meanwhile the domestic side at $88 is what Canadian payroll software costs when it's a tool rather than a legal structure. Budget them in different categories, because they are different purchases.

Contractors are the middle ground

Between "employee at home" and "EOR abroad" sits the contractor lane: Deel's Contractor Management at $49/contractor/month handles contracts, compliance and payments across borders. For a Canadian firm engaging two foreign contractors, that's $98/month against the DIY alternative (wire fees, FX spread, misclassification risk). The classification point is the serious one: a "contractor" who works like an employee creates liability in their country, not just yours — the $49 buys the paperwork discipline as much as the payment rail.

Frequently asked questions

Can Wagepoint pay employees outside Canada? Wagepoint is a Canadian payroll product — CRA remittances, T4s, ROEs. Cross-border pay is Deel's territory (or a local provider in the other country).

What does Deel's EOR price not include? The $599/employee/month is the service price; the employee's salary, statutory costs and benefits in their country bill through on top, itemized by Deel.

Can a Canadian firm hire a US contractor directly instead? Yes, with proper contracts, tax forms (W-8BEN-E/W-9 handling) and payment rails — Deel's $49 contractor tier sells the paperwork discipline and payment plumbing for exactly that.
