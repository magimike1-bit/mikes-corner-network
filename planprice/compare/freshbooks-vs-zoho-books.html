FreshBooks vs Zoho Books: Canadian Pricing Compared (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: FreshBooks is the simpler buy for a Canadian freelancer or small firm that invoices: CAD pricing (Lite C$26, Plus C$42, Premium C$72 list), expense and time tracking built around billing, and a current 80%-off-for-4-months promotion. Zoho Books is the cheaper full accounting system once you outgrow invoicing-first tools: flat per-organization pricing (Standard $15 USD/month billed annually) instead of per-user math, with a free tier under its revenue threshold. Invoicing-first → FreshBooks; ledger-first on a budget → Zoho Books.

Where they actually differ

Pricing shape is the whole story. FreshBooks charges one flat business price in CAD and includes unlimited invoicing on paid plans; client counts are the tier gate (5 billable clients on Lite). Zoho Books charges one flat organization price in USD — Standard $15, Professional $40, Premium $60 billed annually — and its Free plan covers very small businesses. Neither charges per seat the way per-user tools do, which keeps both predictable; the exchange rate is Zoho's hidden line for Canadians.

Depth: Zoho Books is the more complete accounting package (inventory, multi-currency, client portal). FreshBooks is faster to live in day-to-day for service businesses that bill time and expenses.

Canadian fit: FreshBooks is a Canadian company (Toronto) with CAD price lists and Canadian tax handling front and centre. Zoho Books supports Canadian taxes inside a global product.

Who should pick which

Pick FreshBooks if invoicing, time tracking and expenses are your accounting life and you want CAD billing from a Canadian vendor. Pick Zoho Books if you need a real ledger with inventory or multi-currency at the lowest flat price in this dataset, and you are comfortable with USD billing. Both undercut per-seat rivals at small team sizes.

A year of each, priced

A solo consultant on FreshBooks Lite pays C$312 a year at list (or about C$62 across the first four months on the current 80%-off promotion, then full price — roughly C$229 in year one). The same consultant on Zoho Books Standard pays $180 USD a year flat. Zoho wins the invoice; FreshBooks wins the currency (no FX line, no USD card conversion spread, which typically adds 1.5–2.5% through a Canadian card) and the client-facing polish most freelancers actually use. A five-client firm on FreshBooks Plus (C$504/year) against Zoho Professional ($480 USD/year) narrows further once exchange is applied — the two are closer in true cost than their stickers suggest, which returns the decision to depth versus invoicing craft, where it belongs.

The migration question

Switching between these two is a known path: both import clients, invoices and chart-of-accounts basics, and both export cleanly enough that an accountant can rebuild a year. The real switching cost is historical attachments and recurring-invoice templates — budget half a day, not a weekend. That low switching cost cuts both ways: start on the one that fits today, because neither locks you in hard enough to justify over-buying for a hypothetical future.

Frequently asked questions

Do both handle Canadian GST/HST properly? Yes — both support Canadian tax rates and place-of-supply handling; FreshBooks, as a Canadian product, surfaces it more prominently. Our province pages explain what your invoices must apply.

Can I migrate mid-year? Both import opening balances and client lists; the standard practice is switching at a quarter or year boundary with your accountant reconciling the cut-over.

Which is better for a corporation vs a sole proprietor? Sole proprietors billing time lean FreshBooks; corporations with inventory, departments or multi-currency lean Zoho Books. Legal form matters less than workflow shape.
