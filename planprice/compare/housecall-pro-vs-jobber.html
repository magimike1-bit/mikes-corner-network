Housecall Pro vs Jobber: Field-Service Pricing Compared (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: Jobber is the Canadian company buy: USD pricing (Core USD 49, Connect USD 139, Grow USD 199, Plus USD 499, extra users USD 29) from an Edmonton company built for home-service businesses. Housecall Pro is the US-scale buy: USD pricing (Basic $59, Essentials $149, MAX $299 billed annually) with a large feature machine behind it. For most Canadian trades firms Jobber's price ladder and local fit win the spec sheet; Housecall Pro earns consideration when its specific automation depth matches how your office actually runs.

Where they actually differ

Currency is a first-order difference: both vendors publish in USD, so at par-nobody numbers, Housecall's USD 149 Essentials is roughly CAD 200 territory once exchanged, near Jobber Grow money. Convert both ladders to CAD before comparing features.

Seat math: Jobber prices extra users explicitly (USD 29/user). Housecall Pro's per-tier seat caps have changed historically, confirm the current cap on the vendor page before costing a growing crew.

Payments: both push their own payment rails; the processing rate on job invoices will outweigh the subscription gap at volume, model it with your real invoice sizes (see the trades teardown).

Who should pick which

Pick Jobber if you want Canadian support hours and a ladder that starts at USD 49. Pick Housecall Pro if a specific feature in its higher tiers (marketing automation, larger-team dispatch) is the bottleneck you're actually buying your way out of.

Five-person crew math

On the official USD price lists, the five-person crew math is an exact tie. Jobber Connect for a five-user crew is USD 149 per month billed annually (entity jobber, checked 2026-10-27), and Housecall Pro Essentials, which covers up to 5 users, is also USD 149 per month billed annually (entity housecall-pro, checked 2026-10-23). At typical exchange both are a C$200-class line for a Canadian firm, and the translation should be re-done at renewal because the exchange rate, not the vendor, may have moved your price. A tie at list means this comparison is decided by workflow fit and by what sits outside the plan: Jobber prices extra users beyond a bundle at USD 29 per month each in the same readings, while Housecall Pro's next step is MAX at USD 299 per month billed annually for up to 8 users.

What the office feels

Owners compare features; offices feel workflows. Jobber's quote → job → invoice flow is deliberately linear and trains fast, a real advantage when seasonal hires rotate. Housecall Pro's depth shows in marketing automation and larger-team dispatch. The deciding exercise: have the person who runs your schedule work a week in each trial with real jobs. The $30 to $60 monthly difference is smaller than one mis-dispatched day per quarter, and no spec table prices that.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for a solo operator? Jobber's Core at USD 49 is the gentler start; Housecall Pro's Basic at USD 59 is close on the same currency. The solo decider is usually quoting workflow fit, not the small gap.

Do both take payments in the field? Both offer integrated payments; the processing rate at your invoice sizes will outweigh the subscription gap, model it, as the trades teardown does.

Is Jobber really Canadian? Yes. Jobber is an Edmonton company, bills in USD, and builds for Canadian home-service businesses. Housecall Pro is US-based with USD pricing.
