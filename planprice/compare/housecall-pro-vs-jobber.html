Where they actually differ

Currency is a first-order difference: at par-nobody numbers, Housecall's $149 Essentials is roughly C$200 territory once exchanged — near Jobber Grow money. Compare tiers in CAD before comparing features.

Seat math: Jobber prices extra users explicitly (C$29/user). Housecall Pro's per-tier seat caps have changed historically — confirm the current cap on the vendor page before costing a growing crew.

Payments: both push their own payment rails; the processing rate on job invoices will outweigh the subscription gap at volume — model it with your real invoice sizes (see the trades teardown).

Who should pick which

Pick Jobber if you want CAD billing, Canadian support hours, and a ladder that starts at C$49. Pick Housecall Pro if a specific feature in its higher tiers (marketing automation, larger-team dispatch) is the bottleneck you're actually buying your way out of.

Five-person crew math

Jobber Connect is C$139/month; adding users beyond the plan's included seats runs C$29 each in the published data. Housecall Pro Essentials is $149 USD/month billed annually — at typical exchange, a five-crew Canadian firm should model it as a C$200-class line before comparing features. That currency translation, done honestly, moves Housecall from "similar price" to "premium tier" in most comparisons, and it should be re-done at renewal time because the exchange rate, not the vendor, may have moved your price.

What the office feels

Owners compare features; offices feel workflows. Jobber's quote → job → invoice flow is deliberately linear and trains fast — a real advantage when seasonal hires rotate. Housecall Pro's depth shows in marketing automation and larger-team dispatch. The deciding exercise: have the person who runs your schedule work a week in each trial with real jobs. The $30–$60 monthly difference is smaller than one mis-dispatched day per quarter, and no spec table prices that.

Frequently asked questions
