Notion vs ClickUp: Workspace Pricing Compared (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: Notion is the document brain: pages, wikis and databases at $10/user/month (Plus) and $20 (Business) billed annually, with tasks living inside the docs. ClickUp is the task machine: everything-tracker pricing at $7 (Unlimited) and $12 (Business) per user billed annually, with docs living inside the tasks. Writing-and-knowledge teams → Notion; delivery-and-operations teams → ClickUp. The $3–8 seat gap is smaller than the cost of forcing the wrong centre of gravity on a team.

Where they actually differ

AI pricing: both vendors now gate AI features by tier or add-on; Notion's AI sits in its Business tier story, ClickUp sells AI as an add-on line — confirm the current packaging on each vendor page if AI features are the buying reason, because this is the fastest-moving line in both price lists.

Free tiers: Notion's free plan is a real personal/small-team workspace; ClickUp's free plan is generous but capped in ways teams hit sooner.

Who should pick which

Pick Notion if the work product is documents, wikis and light tracking. Pick ClickUp if the work product is tasks with dependencies, time and goals attached — our wider field is in the comparison tool.

Ten-seat math and the consolidation claim

Ten seats: Notion Plus $100/month, Business $200; ClickUp Unlimited $70, Business $120 (all billed annually). ClickUp's pitch is consolidation — replace the task tool, the doc tool and the goal tracker with one $70–$120 bill. The claim is real only if the replaced tools actually get cancelled; teams that keep paying for the old doc tool "just in case" run both bills and get neither benefit. Before migrating, list the subscriptions ClickUp would retire and their renewal dates — the consolidation saving lives on that list, not in ClickUp's price table.

The AI line, again

Both vendors repackage AI frequently enough that any printed claim ages in months — which is itself the guidance: do not sign an annual term justified mainly by an AI feature's current packaging. Trial the AI on your real documents/tasks for a month (both tools let you), measure whether it's in your weekly workflow, and only then let it influence the tier. Seat price is stable; AI packaging is weather.

Frequently asked questions

Can Notion replace a task manager entirely? For doc-centric teams with light task needs, often yes. The moment dependencies, workloads and time tracking become operational, purpose-built task machinery (ClickUp) earns its seat price.

Which is cheaper for a writing team? A writing team's real tool is the doc — Notion Plus ($10) usually retires more other tools. An operations team usually consolidates better into ClickUp ($7 Unlimited).

Do either offer Canadian-dollar billing? Both publish USD pricing as checked; budget exchange on top, and remember the seat total moves with the dollar — another reason to audit seats quarterly.

The bottom line

Strip the comparison to its centre of gravity: Notion organises what a team knows; ClickUp organises what a team owes. Knowledge businesses — agencies, consultancies, content and research teams — feel Notion's shape as relief. Delivery businesses — operations, product, services with deadlines — feel ClickUp's task machinery as control. Price follows that choice more than it leads it: over three years, the wrong-centre tool costs more in workarounds and shadow tools than the $30-a-month seat gap ever will. Trial both against one real project each, with the whole team's hands on it, and let the project's friction — not the pricing page — cast the vote.
