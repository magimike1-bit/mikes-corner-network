Square vs Lightspeed: Which POS Costs Less for a Canadian Store? (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: Square wins on published price for most single-location businesses — free POS software and a 2.5% in-person credit rate with a genuinely cheap Interac debit rate (0.75% + 7¢). Lightspeed wins when inventory is the business: multi-location stock, purchase orders and vendor management are its home turf, and its $89/month-plus entry price buys depth Square approximates. The full math for a $42,000/month store is in our retail teardown.

Where they actually differ

The rate cards differ in shape, not just size. Square charges no monthly POS fee and 2.5% on credit, with debit priced separately and cheaply — a mix that rewards small-ticket, debit-heavy Canadian tills. Lightspeed charges a real subscription (Retail Basic $89/month billed annually) and Lightspeed Payments at 2.6% + 10¢ on every transaction, so its processing line grows with transaction count, not just volume: 2,600 transactions a month add $260 in per-transaction fees alone.

Software depth runs the other way. Lightspeed is built for retail inventory — matrices, purchase orders, multi-store transfers. Square's retail features exist but are shallower; its strength is that payments, POS, online ordering and invoicing live in one free-to-start system.

Hardware: Square readers start at $69 with no contract. Lightspeed hardware is quoted per setup.

Who should pick which

Pick Square if you run one location, your inventory fits in a spreadsheet's worth of complexity, or your average ticket is small and debit-heavy. Pick Lightspeed if you manage serious inventory across locations, reorder from vendors inside the POS, and can amortize the subscription across real retail volume. Skip both if you are restaurant-first — that comparison is Toast vs Square.

24-month total cost, modelled

Using the retail teardown's model store ($42,000/month card volume, 2,600 transactions): Lightspeed's stack runs about $1,529/month before tax (subscription $89 + processing ~$1,352 + shared lines) against Square's ~$1,089 — a gap of roughly $440 a month, or about $10,500 over 24 months. That is the honest size of the inventory-depth premium at this volume. Run the same model at $15,000/month and 900 transactions and the gap compresses to the low hundreds per month, because the per-transaction dime and the subscription weigh more than the rate gap at small scale. Neither direction is a rounding error — which is why this decision deserves the arithmetic, not a demo.

Switching costs, honestly

Moving POS systems costs a weekend of catalogue rebuilding, staff retraining measured in hours, and — the real item — hardware: Square's readers and stands don't run Lightspeed and vice versa, so a switch writes off most of the register hardware. Data export (products, customers, gift-card balances) is the other trap; gift cards and store credits issued on one system need a manual bridge to the other. Price a switch at its true one-time cost before treating the monthly gap as pure saving.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Square hardware with Lightspeed or vice versa? No — the readers, stands and terminals are proprietary to each platform, which is why hardware is a switching cost, not a transferable asset.

Does Lightspeed negotiate its processing rate? Published pricing is 2.6% + 10¢ in the data checked; larger-volume merchants can ask — a quote conversation costs nothing, and at $40k+ monthly volume even 0.1 of a point matters.

Which is better for a café inside a retail store? Run the tills separately if you can: the café side is small-ticket and debit-heavy (Square's shape), the retail side may justify Lightspeed's inventory depth. One system forced onto both usually over-serves one side.
