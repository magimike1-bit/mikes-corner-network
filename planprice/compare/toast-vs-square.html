Toast vs Square for Canadian Restaurants: Pricing & the Rate GAP (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: Square is the transparent buy: free POS software and published Canadian rates (2.5% credit, 0.75% + 7¢ Interac debit). Toast is the restaurant-native buy: purpose-built hardware and workflows at C$120/month (Starter) or C$220/month (Essentials) in Canada — but Toast does not publish its Canadian processing rate, so its true monthly cost cannot be totalled from public data. That GAP is the deciding fact for many buyers: never sign a Toast quote without the processing rate in writing, because at restaurant volumes the rate is worth more than the subscription difference.

Where they actually differ

Subscriptions: Toast charges real money monthly (Canada-specific pricing: Starter C$120, Essentials C$220, checked October 20, 2026). Square's POS is free; Square for Restaurants adds paid tiers on top.

Processing: Square publishes 2.5% / 0.75% + 7¢ for Canada (checked October 25, 2026). Toast's Canadian rate is quote-only in the sources checked — modelled honestly as a GAP, not estimated. On $40,000 of monthly card volume, every 0.1 point of processing rate is $40 a month, so a quote 0.3 points over Square's published rate erases Toast's software-price story entirely.

Fit: Toast's handhelds, kitchen displays and menu management are built only for restaurants. Square is a generalist that happens to serve food well at the simple end.

Who should pick which

Pick Square for cafés, food trucks, counters and any concept where published pricing and zero subscription matter more than restaurant-specific hardware. Pick Toast for full-service rooms where the kitchen workflow is the bottleneck — after getting the processing rate in the quote, in writing, and comparing it against 2.5% at your real volume. The questions to ask a Toast quote

Since Toast's Canadian processing rate is quote-only, the quote meeting is the pricing page. Four questions decide it. What is the exact processing rate — percentage and per-transaction — for in-person cards, and is Interac debit priced separately? What hardware is included, leased, or purchased, and what happens to it on cancellation? What is the contract term and the early-termination cost? Which features in the demo are in Starter (C$120) versus Essentials (C$220) versus add-ons? A quote that answers all four in writing can be compared against Square's published 2.5% / 0.75% + 7¢ in one sitting; a quote that waves at any of them is telling you where the margin hides.

Volume breakpoints

At café scale ($20–30k/month card volume), Toast's subscriptions alone (C$120–220) exceed Square's entire software cost ($0) by enough that Toast must win on operations, not price. At full-service scale with high average tickets, processing differences dominate and the negotiation moves to the rate — a restaurant doing $80,000 a month feels every 0.1 point as $80 monthly. The tier of restaurant where Toast's kitchen system plausibly pays for itself in ticket times and error reduction is exactly the tier that should also be negotiating its rate hardest.

Frequently asked questions

Why doesn't Toast publish its Canadian processing rate? Toast prices processing by quote in Canada based on volume and business type. That's common for full-service POS — and exactly why the rate must be pinned down in writing before signing.

Is Toast available across Canada? Toast sells into Canada with Canada-specific plan pricing (Starter C$120, Essentials C$220 in the data checked October 2026); confirm service coverage for your province at quote time.

Can a food truck use Toast? It can, but the subscription-and-hardware model fits fixed locations; most trucks land on Square's free POS — see our food-truck teardown.
