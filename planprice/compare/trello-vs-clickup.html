Trello vs ClickUp: Work-Management Pricing Compared (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: Trello is the board, perfected: the fastest tool to put a team's work on cards, with paid tiers that mostly add automation, views and admin control. ClickUp is the suite: docs, goals, time and boards in one per-user price (Unlimited $7/user/month, Business $12, billed annually) that undercuts most single-purpose tools. Teams that think in boards → Trello; teams that want one workspace for everything → ClickUp, accepting its steeper learning curve as the real price.

Where they actually differ

Free tiers: both are genuinely usable free — Trello's free boards for small teams, ClickUp's free plan with task limits to watch. The upgrade triggers differ: Trello charges when you need power-ups at scale, advanced views and admin features; ClickUp charges when storage, guests and advanced features bind.

Cost shape: both are per-user, so headcount discipline matters more than tier choice — five unused seats on either tool cost more than the tier gap between them.

Who should pick which

Pick Trello if the work is visual, the team is non-technical, and boards are the whole requirement. Pick ClickUp if you are consolidating several tools into one workspace and someone will own its configuration. Compare the wider field in the comparison tool.

The seat-count audit

Both tools bill per user/member in ways that punish untidiness. Trello bills workspace members on paid boards; guests can quietly become billable when they join multiple boards. ClickUp bills members, with guests cheaper but not free of limits. The shared lesson, worth more than the tier difference: a quarterly seat audit — who is in the workspace, on how many boards, doing what — recovers 10–20% of the bill in most small teams. At ten seats, that's $200–$400 a year for an hour of housekeeping, on whichever tool you pick.

Migration reality

Boards export from Trello cleanly (JSON/CSV), and ClickUp imports them — the mechanics of switching are an afternoon. The cost is behavioural: automations, power-ups and habits don't migrate, and a team that has built its rituals inside Trello's simplicity will experience ClickUp's density as friction for a month. Conversely, a team outgrowing Trello usually knows exactly which missing feature (dependencies, docs, workload views) is forcing the move — name it before migrating, because "more powerful" is not a requirement, and ClickUp seats bought speculatively are just a bigger bill.

Frequently asked questions

Which free plan is better for a 4-person team? Trello's free tier is simpler to run; ClickUp's free tier does more but caps usage in ways active teams hit sooner. For a first team tool, simple usually wins.

Does ClickUp really replace docs and goals tools? For many small teams, yes — that's its consolidation pitch. It works when the team actually retires the other tools; keeping both bills is the common failure.

What does Trello charge for beyond boards? Paid tiers add views (calendar/timeline), advanced automation, larger power-up allowances and admin controls — the price of running Trello as an operating system rather than a board.
