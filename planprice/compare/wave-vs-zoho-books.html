Wave vs Zoho Books: Free Accounting Compared (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Verdict up top: Wave is the free Canadian default: accounting and invoicing at $0, a paid Pro plan at C$25/month, and payments priced at 2.9% + 60¢ in Canada. Zoho Books is the flat-price upgrade: a free tier for very small businesses, then per-organization pricing (Standard $15 USD/month billed annually) that buys inventory, multi-currency and a client portal. Start on Wave if free covers the books; move to Zoho Books when the books need structure Wave doesn't sell at any price.

Where they actually differ

The real Wave price is the payment rate. 2.9% + 60¢ is at the higher end of the online range in our dataset (Stripe is 2.9% + 30¢) — on $10,000 of monthly card-paid invoices the 30¢-per-invoice gap plus rate parity adds up slowly; on many small invoices it adds up fast. Zoho routes payments through gateways you connect, so its payment cost is your gateway's rate.

Depth: Zoho Books is a fuller accounting system (inventory, projects, portal). Wave is deliberately simple — double-entry basics done cleanly, with payroll as a separate paid add-on in supported regions.

Who should pick which

Pick Wave if you invoice modestly, want Canadian-dollar simplicity, and free is a strategy, not a phase. Pick Zoho Books if inventory, multi-currency or client-portal needs are already visible — its flat $15–$60 ladder is the cheapest structured step up in this dataset.

The two-year view

Year one on Wave costs $0 for the books. Year one on Zoho Books Standard costs $180 USD. The interesting year is the second: businesses that add inventory, a second currency, or a bookkeeper who wants a client portal discover that Wave's ceiling isn't a price — it's absence. Zoho's ladder (Standard $15 → Professional $40 → Premium $60) prices that growth in gentle steps, per organization, so a growing team doesn't multiply the bill by headcount the way per-seat tools do. Wave's own growth path is Wave Pro (C$25/month) plus paid add-ons like payroll in supported regions — a different shape, still cheap, but not a deeper accounting system.

Payments decide more than plans

For invoice-heavy businesses, the payment rail out-earns the subscription decision. Wave Payments at 2.9% + 60¢ on $8,000 of monthly card-paid invoices costs about $264 in fees; routing the same invoices through a 2.9% + 30¢ gateway from Zoho costs about $255 — the 30¢-per-invoice gap is worth $9 per 30 invoices, not a platform decision by itself. But Wave's convenience (payments inside the free books) versus Zoho's flexibility (your choice of gateway, including cheaper rails) compounds with volume. At $50,000 a month of card-paid invoices, gateway choice is a four-figure annual decision wearing a thirty-cent disguise.

Frequently asked questions

Is Wave really free? Accounting and invoicing, yes — Wave monetizes payments (2.9% + 60¢ in Canada) and paid add-ons like the Pro plan (C$25) and payroll in supported regions.

Which handles GST/HST filing better? Both track Canadian taxes; Zoho Books offers deeper reporting structure. Either way your accountant reviews the return — neither files your taxes for you.

When should I leave Wave? When you need inventory, multi-currency, a client portal, or project accounting — the features Zoho sells from $15/month — or when payment-fee shape at your volume justifies choosing your own gateway.
