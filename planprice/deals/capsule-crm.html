Think of it as: the CRM for people who found Salesforce exhausting.

Capsule is the deliberately simple CRM: contacts, pipelines and tasks without the enterprise sprawl. Editions scale mainly on contact counts and pipelines. AI features start at the Growth tier, which is also where workflow automation arrives.

Best for: Solo operators and small teams that want a CRM they'll actually keep updated.

Who It Fits, and Who It Does Not

Fits: micro teams and professional services firms that want contacts, pipelines, and tasks without CRM sprawl.

Does not fit: teams that need marketing automation or deep reporting. Capsule deliberately stops before both, and outgrowing it is a good problem with a known next step.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Capsule really free to start?

Yes. The free tier covers 2 users and 250 contacts at USD 0, which is a real trial on your own data rather than a timed demo.

Which tier do most small teams land on?

Growth, because multiple pipelines, workflow automation, and advanced reporting arrive there. Starter is the right first paid step while the process is still forming.

Is billing in USD?

Yes, the figures here are USD as billed. Canadian teams should budget the conversion on every seat.

What integrations matter?

The accounting and email connections decide daily usefulness: if Capsule talks to your invoicing tool and your mailbox, the contact record stays current without double entry.

When should I move to Pipedrive?

When the pipeline becomes the business: multiple reps, activity targets, and forecasting. At five seats the two are close in dollars (see the Capsule versus Pipedrive comparison), so the move is about selling machinery, not price.

Before You Sign: The Renewal Checklist

  • Trial on the free tier with real contacts. The free tier holds 250 contacts and 2 users, which is enough to learn whether the team will actually update it. A CRM habit formed free is worth more than any feature comparison, because the failure mode of every CRM is disuse, not missing features.
  • Count contacts before they count you. Tier caps run on contacts (30,000 on Starter, 60,000 on Growth). Importing a decade of stale marketing contacts on day one can push you a tier up for data nobody will ever call. Clean the list first.
  • Keep the exit simple. Capsule's virtue is that contacts, pipelines, and tasks export cleanly. Verify the export once, early, while it is a curiosity instead of a crisis. Simple tools should be simple to leave; hold this one to that standard.
  • Revisit the tier when reporting starts to matter. Advanced reporting and AI features arrive at Growth and above. When the weekly pipeline meeting starts asking questions Starter cannot answer, that is the upgrade trigger, and it is a healthy one.

Switching and Exit Notes

Capsule's exit is the friendliest in this category, and it should be. Contacts, companies, pipelines, and tasks are simple objects, they export simply, and a team moving up to Pipedrive or HubSpot later will find the import on the other side equally simple. That portability is part of the product's value for a small firm: the risk of trying it is close to zero, which cannot be said of the platforms it sits beneath. The one habit to keep is list hygiene. A CRM that stays current, with duplicates merged and dead contacts archived, exports cleanly on the day you move. The same database neglected for three years exports as an argument. Ten minutes a month of tidying is the whole maintenance burden of this tool, and it buys you a permanent option: the freedom to graduate the day the pipeline outgrows it, taking three years of clean customer history with you.

Verdict

The CRM for people who got burned by a big CRM. It does the unglamorous 90 percent. I would rather see a team actually using Capsule than paying Enterprise money for a system they log into twice a month.
