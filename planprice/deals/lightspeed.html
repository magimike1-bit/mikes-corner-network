Think of it as: the Montreal POS for serious retail.

Lightspeed, headquartered in Montreal, is the POS for retailers and restaurants that have outgrown a basic till: deep inventory, multi-location and ecommerce sync. Software is priced per location register plan; Lightspeed Payments charges 2.6% + 10¢ in person. Model software plus processing together, either alone misleads.

Best for: Retailers and restaurants with real inventory complexity or multiple locations.

Canadian note: Lightspeed is a Canadian company (Montreal). Published plan prices are in USD, confirm the billing currency for your account, and budget processing on top of software.

Who It Fits, and Who It Does Not

Fits: inventory heavy Canadian retail, bike shops, apparel, and specialty stores that need serious stock control at the till.

Does not fit: cafes and simple tills, which belong on Square, and online first sellers, who belong on Shopify.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Lightspeed cost in Canada?

Core is USD 179 per month billed monthly or USD 149 billed annually, and Basic starts at USD 109 per month. Canadian stores are billed in USD on these readings, so budget the conversion.

Is pricing in USD?

Yes, on the price list our research reads. Your actual CAD cost moves with the exchange rate at each invoice, which is worth a line in the budget.

What does Lightspeed Payments charge?

In person, 2.6% plus USD 0.10 per transaction. Model it on your own volume and average ticket beside the subscription.

When does Core beat Basic?

When inventory depth, multi store handling, or reporting saves more staff time than the USD 40 to 70 per month tier gap costs. For a single simple till, it does not.

What hardware is required?

Lightspeed runs on standard till hardware and its own payment terminals. Get the hardware list priced in the quote, in CAD, before you sign, because terminals are the second invoice nobody budgets.

Before You Sign: The Renewal Checklist

  • Take annual billing when the store format is settled. The USD 360 a year Core saving is free money for a stable store. A store still changing locations or concepts should keep monthly flexibility and revisit at the format freeze.
  • Model payments beside the subscription. At 2.6% plus USD 0.10 in person, a store doing USD 40,000 a month pays roughly USD 1,300 a month in processing against a USD 149 to 179 subscription. The negotiation that matters is the rate, and it happens before signature.
  • Count the stock problem honestly. Lightspeed's case is inventory accuracy. Do a manual count first: if the variance is small, you are buying horsepower for its own sake, and Square's simpler till is the honest buy.
  • Get the hardware list in CAD, in the quote. Terminals and peripherals are the second invoice. A quote that prices software to the cent and waves at hardware is hiding the number you will actually argue about later.

Switching and Exit Notes

A POS accumulates the business's memory: every SKU, supplier, price, and stock count lives in it. That is why Lightspeed's inventory depth, its main selling point, is also its main switching cost. A store that has run its stockroom in Lightspeed for three years owns data quality that took three years to build, and any move means re-proving that data in a new system during the busiest possible week. Choose the timing accordingly: migrations belong in your quietest trading month, with a full physical count at cutover, because the count is the only moment the new system's opening stock is provably true. None of this argues against buying Lightspeed. It argues for buying it as the multi year commitment it actually is, priced over the term you expect to keep it, rather than as a subscription you can casually cancel. Retailers who treat the till as infrastructure make better till decisions than retailers who treat it as software.

Verdict

Lightspeed earns its money in the stockroom, not at the cash wrap. If your inventory count is a guess, this is the fix. If you sell coffee and muffins, you are paying for horsepower you will never use.
