Think of it as: the enterprise CRM with a small-business front door.

Salesforce is the CRM most growing businesses eventually evaluate, and the one whose real cost is hardest to see. Seat prices are only the start: implementation for a small business runs into five figures, and training is a separate line. The Starter Suite is genuinely priced for small teams; everything above it assumes an admin.

Best for: Businesses with a dedicated admin or a growth path that justifies enterprise CRM depth.

Canadian note: Salesforce bills in USD; implementation and training costs are typically quoted separately from licences.

Total Cost: A Worked Example

Price ten users on Pro Suite. At USD 100 per user per month, licences alone are USD 12,000 a year. Starter Suite at USD 25 per user per month is USD 3,000 a year for the same ten people. Now the honesty block most pages skip: our research puts a small business single cloud implementation at USD 10,000 to 25,000 at the low end, and training and change management at a mid estimate of USD 9,000. Year one is licences plus implementation. A ten user Pro Suite year one is therefore a USD 22,000 to 37,000 conversation before training, not a USD 12,000 one, and any quote that hides that is selling you the cover charge as the bill.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Salesforce really cost a small business in year one?

Licences plus implementation plus training. Ten users on Pro Suite: USD 12,000 in licences, plus USD 10,000 to 25,000 of implementation and about USD 9,000 of training and change management in our research estimates.

Is Starter Suite enough?

For a small team that needs core sales tooling, often yes, at USD 25 per user per month. It is also the honest way to learn whether your process needs Salesforce at all before you fund Pro Suite.

What implementation costs should I budget?

Budget the low end of the range in our research, USD 10,000 to 25,000 for a single cloud small business implementation, and treat anything quoted below it as a scope question, not a bargain.

Do I need an admin?

On Pro Suite and above, yes, a named person whose job includes the CRM. Without one, permissions, reports, and data quality decay within two quarters.

When is Salesforce the wrong answer?

When the honest requirement is a shared pipeline and follow up reminders. Pipedrive or Capsule does that job for about a tenth of the year one money.

Before You Sign: The Renewal Checklist

  • Refuse a licence only quote. Any proposal that prices licences without implementation, training, and admin time is a partial quote. Send it back and ask for the year one total; the comparison in the worked example shows why the gap is not a rounding error.
  • Run the Starter Suite pilot honestly. A quarter on Starter Suite, at USD 25 per user per month, teaches you whether your process needs Salesforce depth or merely wanted it. That lesson costs USD 750 for ten users and can save a five figure implementation aimed at the wrong problem.
  • Count the ecosystem tax. AppExchange products, premium support, and added storage each bill separately. List the add-ons your design assumes and price them in year one, because they renew at the same time as the licences.
  • Protect the exit. Data export rights and formats belong in the contract conversation, not in a support ticket two years later. Salesforce data models are deep; confirm what leaves with you, in what shape, before the first object is customized.

Switching and Exit Notes

No page about Salesforce is honest without the exit. The platform's depth means your processes, custom objects, automations, and reports become the business's operating memory, and unwinding that is measured in quarters, not weekends. Enterprises accept this because the alternative, outgrowing a smaller CRM mid scale, has its own brutal migration. Small businesses should accept it only with the same reasoning applied deliberately. Two habits keep the exit survivable. First, document every customization and the business reason for it, in a register a successor admin can read. Second, keep a scheduled export of the core data, accounts, contacts, opportunities, in standard formats, and test restoring it once. A firm that does both can leave Salesforce in a planned project. A firm that does neither discovers that its sales history is effectively rented. Buy the depth when you need the depth; keep the paperwork as if you will leave, because eventually, everyone does.

Verdict

I have managed Salesforce vendors. The licence is the cover charge, not the bill. If nobody on your team has CRM admin in their actual job, buy Pipedrive and spend the difference on selling.
