Think of it as: the Shopify of Main Street.

Square's point-of-sale software is free, the business model is the processing fee on each sale (2.5% for in-person credit payments, with no fixed per-transaction fee; Interac debit is 0.75% + 7¢). Hardware readers are cheap and the ecosystem grows into online stores, appointments, and payroll. Square Appointments adds paid tiers for salons, clinics, and services that book online.

Best for: Retailers, cafés, and service businesses that need a free POS and transparent card rates.

Who It Fits, and Who It Does Not

Fits: single and small multi location retail and cafes, and in-person first sellers who want no monthly software fee to start.

Does not fit: online first sellers, where Stripe's math wins at volume online, and restaurants that need full Toast workflows rather than a general purpose till.

Total Cost: A Worked Example

Take a cafe doing CAD 30,000 a month in person across 1,000 transactions. At the in-person credit rate of 2.5%, processing is CAD 750 a month, CAD 9,000 a year, with no per transaction fixed fee on that in-person rate. Hardware entry is the Reader at CAD 69, one time. Now run the same volume online at 2.8% plus CAD 0.30: CAD 840 in percentage fees plus CAD 300 in fixed fees is CAD 1,140 a month. The channel mix, not the headline rate, decides whether Square is cheap for your business.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Square charge in Canada, exactly?

In person credit is 2.5%. Online sales and invoices paid online are 2.8% plus CAD 0.30 per transaction. There is no monthly fee for the core POS software.

How is Interac priced?

Interac debit is priced separately from credit. Our research reading records in-person debit at 0.75% plus CAD 0.07 per transaction (entity square, checked 2026-10-20). Confirm the current debit line on Square's Canada pricing page before you model a debit heavy till.

What hardware do I actually need?

Most small stores start with the Reader at CAD 69 and a phone or tablet. Stands, terminals, and registers are upgrades for fixed counters and higher volume, not requirements.

Is there a monthly fee?

Not for the core POS. Paid plans exist for appointments, invoices, and team features, and each is optional until the workflow needs it.

When does Square stop being the cheap option?

When monthly volume grows to the point where a half point of processing is a real salary line, or when most sales move online, where the fixed fee on every transaction adds up.

Before You Sign: The Renewal Checklist

  • Read your channel mix monthly. The worked example shows a CAD 390 per month gap between the same volume taken in person and online. A cafe that adds delivery or online ordering changes its own rate; re-run the mix math whenever a new channel opens.
  • Keep hardware boring. The CAD 69 Reader covers most counters. Stands, terminals, and registers earn their price at fixed, high volume stations. Buy the second reader as a spare before you buy the prettier hardware.
  • Watch the paid plan creep. Appointments, invoices, and team features each carry optional paid tiers. They are cheap individually and meaningful together; review the stack twice a year and cancel what the workflow outgrew.
  • Re-price the till at volume milestones. Square's honesty is that the rate does not improve with volume. At CAD 30,000 a month that is fine. At CAD 100,000, a half point is CAD 500 a month, and interchange plus quotes from rivals belong on the table.

Switching and Exit Notes

Leaving Square is easier than leaving most tills, and that is worth something at signing time. There is no monthly software contract on the core POS to unwind, your sales history exports, and the hardware investment is small enough (a CAD 69 reader at entry) that walking away does not strand real capital. The genuine lock in is habit and hardware at scale: a multi location estate with stands, terminals, and trained staff on Square workflows faces a retraining project, not a data problem. If you are choosing a first till today, that asymmetry is the point in Square's favour. The cost of being wrong is one afternoon and a new reader, which is exactly what a starter system should cost to leave. Revisit the decision when volume, not frustration, says so: the rate milestones in the checklist above are the honest triggers, because they are the moments the flat rate quietly stops being the cheap one.

Verdict

Square is the honest starter till. I have put it in stores in an afternoon. The mistake is staying on starter math at grown up volume, where a half point is a staff member's wages.
