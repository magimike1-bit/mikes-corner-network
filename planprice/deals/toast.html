Frequently Asked Questions

What does Toast cost in Canada, in CAD?

Starter is CAD 120 per month and Essentials is CAD 220 per month on the Canada list. Hardware, contract, and processing sit on top and belong in the same quote.

What contract length should I expect?

Expect a term contract rather than month to month, and read the early exit terms before you sign. The exact length is a quote item; get it in writing.

What hardware is included or extra?

Treat hardware as extra until the quote says otherwise. The Canadian bundle and its price are quote items, and this page prints no Canadian hardware figure because our research does not hold one.

How do Toast payments rates work?

Toast's Canadian pricing page does not publish the flat processing rate. That makes the rate a negotiation item: ask for it in writing and model it on your Friday night volume.

When is Square enough for a restaurant?

For cafes, counters, and food trucks where the menu is short and the kitchen flow is simple. When ticket times, coursing, and kitchen displays run the room, Toast's shape earns its subscription.

Before You Sign: The Renewal Checklist

  • Refuse the demo order of operations. Demo first, numbers later is how restaurants end up with a contract they never priced. Ask for the full written quote (subscription, hardware, processing rate, contract length, exit terms) before the second demo, and model it on a real Friday night.
  • Price the processing rate at your volume. The Canadian rate is not published, which makes it the negotiation. At CAD 80,000 a month of card volume, every tenth of a point is CAD 80 a month, every month of the contract.
  • Write the exit terms into your calendar. Contract length and early exit cost decide what switching would mean in year two. If the quote will not state them plainly, that silence is itself an answer about the vendor.
  • Stage the hardware. Buy the terminals the dining room needs on day one, and make expansion hardware a priced option in the contract rather than a fresh negotiation at your busiest moment.

Switching and Exit Notes

Toast's contract structure makes the exit a clause, not a decision, so the time to think about leaving is before you sign. Ask what remains payable if you exit at month twelve of the term, what happens to the hardware, and how your menu, sales history, and customer data export at the end. A vendor confident in Friday night performance answers those questions plainly and puts them in the contract. Build one more protection into the first term: keep your menu and pricing documented outside the system, in a format your next POS could import. Restaurants change systems at the worst moments, a lease move, an ownership change, a second location with different needs, and the ones that switch cleanly are the ones whose data was never only inside the old till. The subscription difference between Toast and a month to month rival is a few hundred dollars a year; the exit difference, priced honestly, is the number that belongs in the comparison.

Verdict

Restaurants run on Friday night reliability, not feature lists. Toast is built for that room. My vendor rule stands: get the full contract, hardware, and payments quote in writing before the demo high wears off.
