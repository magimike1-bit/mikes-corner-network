Think of it as: payroll done the Canadian way, without paying for an HR suite you don't need.

Wagepoint was built in Canada for Canadian payroll. CPP/QPP, EI, federal and provincial tax tables, T4/T4A and RL-1 generation, and ROEs are all native, not adapted from a US product. Pricing is a flat base fee plus a small per-employee charge, across three plans (Lite, Core and Plus). That makes it one of the most predictable payroll costs a small business can budget.

Best for: Canadian small businesses (roughly 1 to 100 employees) that want straightforward, compliant payroll without a full HR platform.

Canadian note: This is the rare vendor on PlanPrice that bills in CAD natively, the invoice matches your books with no conversion. Being Canadian-built also means provincial edge cases (QST, Quebec parental insurance, Ontario ESA rules) are handled as first-class features rather than afterthoughts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Wagepoint cost for ten employees, all in?

CAD 100 per month on Core: CAD 40 base plus ten times CAD 6 per employee. That is CAD 1,200 a year before tax, with no per run charges on that plan.

Are T4s and ROEs included?

Yes. Year end forms and records of employment are core payroll work here, not a paid extra, which is part of why a Canadian built platform earns its fee.

How does per employee pricing work for seasonal staff?

You pay per employee per month, so seasonal headcount raises the bill in the months those staff are on payroll. Model your peak month, not your quietest, when you budget.

Does it handle Quebec payroll?

Wagepoint is built for Canadian payroll including Quebec requirements such as QPP and RL-1 reporting. Confirm your specific Quebec setup during onboarding, before the first run.

What happens if I run extra payrolls?

On Lite, the plan covers one run a month, so extra runs mean a plan change. On Core and Plus, runs are unlimited, which is why biweekly and weekly shops belong there.

Before You Sign: The Renewal Checklist

  • Keep the fee schedule. Wagepoint publishes its fees, which is rarer than it should be in payroll. Save the schedule you signed under, and diff it against any renewal notice. Our research rechecked it on 2026-11-13 and confirmed the Lite, Core, and Plus figures in the Wagepoint 2.0 schedule, last updated 2026-10-22; your copy is how you would know if that ever stops being true.
  • Model your peak headcount. Per employee pricing means the August roster, not the February one, sets the real annual cost for seasonal businesses. Budget the peak month honestly and the quiet months become a pleasant variance.
  • Decide the plan by run frequency, not headcount. Lite covers one run a month. Biweekly and weekly shops belong on Core or Plus regardless of size, because the plan boundary is runs, not people.
  • Confirm year end ownership. T4s, T4As, and ROEs are included, but someone still owns the review. Name who checks the year end package before it files; payroll software does the math, not the accountability.
