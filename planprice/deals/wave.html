Who It Fits, and Who It Does Not

Fits: Canadian sole proprietors and micro businesses that need free accounting plus invoicing, with native CAD handling from a Canadian company.

Does not fit: growing firms that need multi user depth, inventory, or an accountant ecosystem. Wave's ceiling is absence, not price: the features are simply not there when you grow into them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Wave really free in Canada?

Yes, for the core job. Starter covers accounting and invoicing at CAD 0. Wave earns on payments and paid add-ons, not on the ledger itself.

What does Pro add?

Pro, at CAD 25 per month, adds receipt scanning and removes the free tier's limits and ads. It is a convenience upgrade, not a deeper accounting system.

How much is Wave Payroll?

The payroll add-on starts from CAD 25 per month in our research readings. Price it at your headcount before you assume the free tier covers your payroll year.

Can Wave handle HST?

Wave tracks Canadian sales taxes on invoices and reports, which covers day to day HST handling for a micro business. Filing stays with you or your accountant.

When should I leave Wave?

When you need inventory, multi user depth, or reports your accountant asks for that Wave cannot produce. Graduate on evidence, not on a sales call.

Before You Sign: The Renewal Checklist

  • Define your graduation triggers now. Write down the three events that mean you have outgrown the free tier: a second user who needs real permissions, inventory, or an accountant asking for reports Wave cannot produce. Deciding the triggers while it costs nothing beats shopping in a panic later.
  • Keep the books exportable. The cost of leaving any accounting tool is the export. Download your data on a schedule while you are small, so a later move to QuickBooks, Xero, or Zoho Books is an import exercise and not an archaeology dig.
  • Price Pro honestly when it arrives. At CAD 25 per month, Pro buys receipt scanning and convenience, not depth. If the reason for upgrading is depth, the money belongs on a deeper tool instead.
  • Treat payments as the real price. Wave earns on payment processing, so a business that takes most revenue through Wave Payments should compare its effective processing cost, not celebrate a CAD 0 ledger. Both numbers can be good deals; only one of them is the bill.

Switching and Exit Notes

Wave's free price buys something else as well: a cheap exit. Because the ledger is simple, the data a micro business accumulates in its first years is small, and moving customers, invoices, and opening balances to a deeper tool is a weekend project rather than a consulting engagement. That calculus changes with time. Every year of history, every payroll run, and every receipt filed in Wave raises the cost of the eventual move, which is why the graduation triggers in the checklist matter more than the tier prices. Firms that leave Wave well share one habit: they kept exports current while they were small, chose a fiscal boundary for the move, and ran the new system in parallel for one month before trusting it. Firms that leave badly discover at year end that a report they need lives in a system they already cancelled. Keep the Wave account readable for a full fiscal year after any move; the cost of keeping it is trivial beside the cost of needing it.

Verdict

Free that is actually free for the core job. Every store starts somewhere, and starting here costs nothing but discipline. Graduate when the reports your accountant asks for take longer than the subscription would cost.
