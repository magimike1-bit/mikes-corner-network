Annual vs monthly billing

Definition: The choice between paying month-to-month or prepaying a year, usually for a discount. Annual billing trades commitment for a lower effective rate.

Why it matters when comparing prices

Only take the annual discount after a full cycle on monthly billing — you want proof the tool survives your worst month before you prepay. Seasonal businesses should be especially careful, and always check the cancellation terms: some annual plans refund nothing if you leave in month three.

What to watch for in Canadian pricing

For Canadian buyers, annual billing carries a currency dimension that US buyers never face. Prepaying a year in USD locks in both the discount and the exchange-rate risk: if the loonie slides during the year you cannot re-time your payments, and the card's ~2.5% foreign transaction fee hits the whole lump sum at once. If the vendor offers CAD annual billing, take it — the discount stays and the currency risk goes. If they do not, convert the USD annual total (plus the FX fee) and compare it honestly against twelve CAD monthly payments before committing.

Ask how refunds and credits work on annual plans before you prepay. Some vendors refund unused months, some convert them to account credit, and some refund nothing — and a refund issued in USD months later converts back at whatever rate applies then, which can be worse than the original charge. Also ask whether the annual price is guaranteed at renewal or whether the "annual discount" quietly shrinks in year two while the monthly price stays put.

A concrete example

Illustrative arithmetic, not a real vendor quote: say a tool costs CAD 30 a month on monthly billing or CAD 300 a year annually — the annual plan saves CAD 60, about 17%. But if you cancel in month four and the plan refunds nothing, you have paid CAD 300 for four months of use: CAD 75 a month effective, worse than the monthly plan you skipped. The discount was real; the commitment made it expensive.
