Contract term and auto-renewal

Definition: The length of your commitment and what happens when it ends — many B2B contracts auto-renew for another full term unless you cancel inside a notice window.

Why it matters when comparing prices

Put the cancellation deadline in your calendar the day you sign, because the auto-renewal notice window is easy to miss and expensive to discover. Check whether renewal pricing is locked or can rise, and prefer vendors whose renewal terms are stated up front over ones you have to ask about twice.

What to watch for in Canadian pricing

For Canadian buyers, the renewal clause deserves a currency check. An auto-renewal that reprices in USD exposes you to a year's worth of exchange-rate movement on the day it triggers — ask for the renewal price quoted in CAD, in writing, with the currency named in the contract. Ask for the renewal notice period and the cancellation window in calendar days rather than "before renewal," and put both dates in your calendar the day you sign.

Ask what the vendor can change at renewal without your signature: price, tier features, seat minimums, support level. The strongest contracts cap annual increases at a percentage you can live with and require written notice of any change; the weakest auto-renew "at then-current rates," which is effectively a blank cheque. If the vendor will not cap increases, shorten the term instead of signing a long one — a one-year term with a renewal cap beats a three-year term with none.

A concrete example

Illustrative arithmetic, not a real vendor quote: say a 12-person team signs a two-year contract at CAD 400 a month with auto-renewal "at then-current rates." At renewal the list price has risen to CAD 500 a month, so year three costs CAD 6,000 instead of CAD 4,800 — a CAD 1,200 increase the team never approved. A 5% annual cap would have held year three to about CAD 441 a month instead.
