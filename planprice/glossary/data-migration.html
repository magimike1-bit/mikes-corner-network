Data migration

Definition: Moving your existing data — customers, transactions, history — into the new tool. The most underestimated cost in any software switch.

Why it matters when comparing prices

Ask what migration help is included free versus billed extra, and test the import with a slice of real data during the trial — deduplication and custom-field mapping are where migrations stall. Just as important is the reverse: confirm you can export everything in a standard format before you import anything.

What to watch for in Canadian pricing

Migration cost shows up differently on Canadian pricing pages. US vendors often call migration "included" while defining the included scope as a basic CSV import — mapping your custom fields, Canadian tax codes, and CAD bank-feed connections is then billed as professional services. Ask for the migration scope in line items and in CAD: how many years of history, how many records, and whether deduplication and custom-field mapping are included or extra.

Currency and tax settings are where Canadian migrations quietly go wrong. Your old system stores amounts in CAD with Canadian tax codes; the new tool has to carry those over, not rebuild them from a US-default chart of accounts. During the trial, migrate one messy real account — duplicates, archived customers, old tax codes — and confirm the tax reports still reconcile. Ask who does the mapping work and what happens if the numbers do not tie out: free rework, or a paid second pass.

A concrete example

Illustrative arithmetic, not a real vendor quote: say a company is quoted CAD 2,000 for "migration assistance" covering 5,000 transactions at CAD 0.40 each. Their books actually hold 18,000 transactions across three years, so the overage is 13,000 × CAD 0.40 = CAD 5,200 — making the real migration cost CAD 7,200, not CAD 2,000. The per-record overage rate, buried in the fine print, was the number that mattered.
