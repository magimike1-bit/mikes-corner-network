Free trial

Definition: A time-limited period of full or nearly-full access before you pay. The honest way to evaluate software — if you use it properly.

Why it matters when comparing prices

A trial only works if you trial with real data and real workflows — sample projects flatter every tool. Check whether the trial requires a credit card up front and what happens when it ends: automatic billing, a hard stop, or a downgrade to a free tier. Set a calendar reminder two days before it expires.

What to watch for in Canadian pricing

Before the trial starts, pin down what happens in Canadian dollars when it ends. Trials often convert at the vendor's US list price in USD, so your first paid invoice arrives with the exchange rate and the ~2.5% card foreign transaction fee baked in. Ask sales for the post-trial plan price in CAD, in writing, valid through the trial period. Also check whether the trial requires a card up front: a card-up-front trial that auto-converts in USD can charge you before you have finished comparing alternatives.

Use the trial to verify the Canadian specifics, not just the features. Connect your CAD bank feed, create a test invoice with GST/HST or QST, and check the tax reports — a tool that cannot handle Canadian tax categories properly will cost you hours every quarter no matter how good the trial felt. Export your test data before the trial ends, too: some tools lock exports behind a paid plan, and you do not want to discover that after building your whole workflow inside.

A concrete example

Illustrative arithmetic, not a real vendor quote: say a 12-person team trials a scheduling tool, loves it, and converts to paid at CAD 12 per seat per month — 12 × 12 = CAD 144 a month, or CAD 1,728 a year. But the trial converted at the USD list price instead, landing at roughly CAD 165 a month after conversion and the card's FX fee — about CAD 250 a year extra for the identical plan. Asking for the CAD price before converting would have caught it.
