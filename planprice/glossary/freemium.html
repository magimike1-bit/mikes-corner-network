Freemium

Definition: A free tier that is genuinely useful, with paid upgrades for power features. The vendor bets you will grow into paying.

Why it matters when comparing prices

Evaluate a free tier by what it omits, not what it includes: HubSpot's free CRM, Wave's free accounting, Square's free POS software, and Calendly's free single event type are all real starting points. Find the exact trigger that forces the first paid invoice — a contact limit, a second event type, a feature gate — and price year two, not month one.

What to watch for in Canadian pricing

The free-to-paid jump is where Canadian buyers get surprised twice: first by the price, then by the currency. Free tiers are currency-free by nature, but the first paid invoice often arrives in USD — ask what the upgrade costs in CAD before you build your workflow on the free tier. Also check whether the free tier's limits are measured in a way that pushes you to upgrade sooner, such as contact or storage caps that count archived records the same as active ones.

Find the exact trigger that forces the first invoice and the exact price on the other side of it. A contact limit, a second user, an extra pipeline — whatever it is, model the month you cross it and price year two from there, not from the free tier. Also ask whether paid features can be bought individually or only as a full plan jump: the cheapest path off free is sometimes an add-on, not the next tier.

A concrete example

Illustrative arithmetic, not a real vendor quote: say a team runs free on a CRM for eight months, then crosses the contact limit and must upgrade at CAD 20 per seat per month for 12 seats — 12 × 20 = CAD 240 a month. Year one of paid use costs CAD 2,880, so the "free" year plus the upgrade makes the true two-year cost CAD 2,880, not zero. Price the crossing, not the free tier.
