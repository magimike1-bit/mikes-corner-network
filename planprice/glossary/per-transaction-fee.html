Per-transaction fee

Definition: A fee charged on each sale or payment processed, typically a small percentage plus a fixed amount. The core of payment-processor pricing.

Why it matters when comparing prices

Do not compare the headline rate; compare the effective rate, total fees divided by total volume, computed from a real month of your sales. Flat-rate structures like Square's suit smaller or spikier volume, while interchange-plus suits higher steady volume, and the crossover only shows up in your own numbers.

What to watch for in Canadian pricing

In Canada, the per-transaction fee hides a second layer. Interchange and card-brand costs work differently for Canadian merchants, and payments from US or international customers often carry an extra cross-border assessment on top of the headline rate, a flat advertised rate rarely includes it. Ask the processor for your effective rate broken into domestic cards, US and international cards, and debit, and ask specifically about Interac debit, which in Canada is typically a low flat fee per transaction rather than a percentage.

Settlement currency matters as much as the rate. If you invoice in CAD but the processor settles to you in USD, you are paying currency conversion on your own revenue before it reaches your account, on top of the transaction fee. Ask whether settlement is in CAD to a Canadian bank account, what the payout timing is (next-day versus two-day changes your cash flow), and whether chargebacks are billed in the transaction's original currency or converted at the processor's rate.

A concrete example

A Canadian online store sells CAD 30,000 a month across 1,000 orders on Stripe at 2.9% plus CAD 0.30 per transaction. The percentage takes CAD 870 and the fixed fees take CAD 300, total CAD 1,170, an effective rate of 3.9% on the month's volume once the fixed fee is counted. At a CAD 15 average ticket the same rates take CAD 1,470. The per-transaction fee is why ticket size changes the answer.
