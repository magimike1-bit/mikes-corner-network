Seat-based pricing

Definition: A pricing model where you pay per user login, sometimes called per-seat or per-user pricing. Adding a team member raises the bill; removing one lowers it.

Why it matters when comparing prices

Seat-based pricing punishes growth in headcount, so model the bill at your twelve-month team size, not today's. Watch for seat minimums — Monday.com requires three seats on paid plans — and contrast with unlimited-user models like Xero, where you pay for features instead of logins. Always ask what guests, viewers, and your accountant cost.

What to watch for in Canadian pricing

Canadian buyers should ask what currency each seat is priced in — and stay alert when the answer differs by tier. Some vendors advertise CAD prices for the base plan but bill add-on seats or premium seats in USD, so your per-seat cost quietly becomes two numbers on one invoice. Ask for a single CAD per-seat figure for every seat type you will buy: full users, managers, and your external accountant, whom some vendors charge as a full seat while others let in free.

Ask how seat changes are billed mid-cycle. Most vendors prorate additions immediately but only credit removals at renewal, which means seasonal or contract staff cost you the full term even if they work two months. If your headcount swings, ask whether the vendor supports true-ups, deprovisioning credits, or a smaller pool of flexible seats — and check whether "view-only" or guest access is genuinely free or simply a seat by another name.

A concrete example

Illustrative arithmetic, not a real vendor quote: say a CRM costs CAD 25 per seat per month. A 12-person team pays 12 × 25 = CAD 300 a month, or CAD 3,600 a year. Hiring three contractors in June and removing them in August still costs 3 × 25 × 12 = CAD 900 for the year if removals only credit at renewal — versus 3 × 25 × 2 = CAD 150 if seats prorate both ways. The removal policy was worth CAD 750.
