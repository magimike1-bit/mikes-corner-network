1Password vs Bitwarden for Business: Per-User Prices and the Family-Plan Crossover

Last updated 2026-10-11

"Bitwarden is cheaper" is the conventional wisdom — and it is wrong at small team sizes. 1Password's Teams Starter Pack has a flat-ish structure that beats Bitwarden per-user pricing up to 10 users, while Bitwarden's Enterprise tier undercuts 1Password Business at every size. This guide prices both with dated figures and works the crossover.

The Starter Pack anomaly: flat pricing at small sizes

1Password's Teams Starter Pack includes 10 members for roughly CA$29.95/month on annual billing — about CA$358.80 a year for a 10-person team. Bitwarden Teams at the same size costs US$480 a year. The Starter Pack wins because of its minimum-10 structure: you pay the 10-seat floor whether you have 6 people or 10. Up to 10 additional seats can be added at per-member pricing as the team grows, up to the 20-user cap.

At exactly 10 users, 1Password's Starter Pack is the cheapest business-grade option either vendor sells. Past the 20-user Starter cap, the comparison moves to the full business tiers — and there Bitwarden wins on price at every size: US$6/user/month for Enterprise (with SSO) against CA$10.99 for 1Password Business. The numbers aren't subtle at 25+ seats, but they aren't feature-for-feature either: 1Password Business includes developer tools (SSH key signing, CLI, CI/CD integrations) that Bitwarden's Teams tier doesn't match.

The family-plan temptation: what breaks without business features

Bitwarden Families costs $47.88/year for up to 6 users — a fraction of any business tier. Tempting, but the family plans lack what makes a business tier a business tier: SSO, SCIM provisioning, enterprise policies, and audit-grade activity logs. The moment you need centralized onboarding, offboarding a departing employee, or an audit trail, the family plan stops working. Price the business tier for what the business will need at 20 employees, not what it needs today.

What the cheaper option gives up

Bitwarden's price advantage comes with trade-offs buyers should name: 1Password generally wins on ease of rollout, polished autofill, and support responsiveness; Bitwarden wins on openness (open source, third-party audited) and the self-hosting option on Enterprise, which matters for data-residency or compliance requirements. 1Password is cloud-only. If a cheaper tool saves nothing but costs the IT owner several hours a month in onboarding and recovery work, the higher fee can pay for itself — price the labor, not just the licence.

Decision framework: three buyer profiles

  • Team of 6–10, wants the easiest rollout: 1Password Teams Starter Pack — cheapest at exactly 10 seats and the smoothest onboarding.
  • Growing past 20, budget-first: Bitwarden Teams or Enterprise — per-user pricing that scales linearly, with SSO and SCIM included where you need them.
  • Compliance or data-residency requirements: Bitwarden Enterprise — the self-hosting option is the deciding feature, not the price.

Note: this is a pricing explainer, not a procedure guide — no steps here, just the per-user math to check against your headcount and SSO requirements.

FAQ

Which is cheaper for a small business, 1Password or Bitwarden?

At 10 users or fewer, 1Password's Teams Starter Pack (CA$2.99/user/month with a 10-user minimum) is cheaper than Bitwarden Teams. Above that, Bitwarden's per-user pricing pulls ahead — and at the SSO tier, Bitwarden Enterprise at US$6/user/month undercuts 1Password Business at every size.

Can a business just buy a family plan instead of a business plan?

The family plans are cheaper but lack the business tiers' admin controls: SSO, SCIM provisioning, enterprise policies, and audit logs. The moment you need centralized onboarding or offboarding, the family plan stops working.

Does Bitwarden really offer self-hosting?

Yes — Bitwarden Enterprise includes the option to self-host, which matters for businesses with data-residency or compliance requirements. 1Password is cloud-only.
