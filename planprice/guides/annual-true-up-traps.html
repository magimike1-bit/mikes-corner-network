Annual True-Up Traps: The Clause That Turns Growth Into a Surprise Invoice

Published 2026-10-12

You contracted for 20 seats. Mid-year the team grew to 26. At the anniversary, the vendor reconciles the difference — that's the true-up. It sounds fair until you read the clause: when the overage is measured and from what date it's charged decide whether you owe a little or a lot. This guide covers the four clause types, the asymmetry built into all of them, and the audit that shrinks the bill before the vendor counts.

General information, not legal advice. True-up mechanics vary by contract — read your own agreement.

The four clause types

True-up clause types and what each one costs you.
Clause typeWhen measuredWhat you're billedExposure
Forward true-upAt the anniversaryAdditional seats, from the anniversary forwardLowest — you pay for what you'll use
Retroactive true-upAt the anniversary, backdatedAdditional seats from the date you first exceededHigh — up to a full year of backdated seats
Automatic band escalationContinuouslyThe whole account reprices to the next bandHigh and non-linear — one seat can move every seat
Then-current rate overageAt the anniversaryOverage at list price on the true-up dateUncapped — the rate isn't fixed in your contract

The cost formula: the gap between your count and the vendor's recalculated count, × the overage rate, × the number of months it's backdated.

The asymmetry: growth bills you, shrinkage doesn't

Here's the structural unfairness: there is rarely a corresponding "true-down" provision. You can grow into a higher tier mid-year and owe money for it, but you can't shrink out of one until renewal — and even then, the auto-renewal default re-locks you at the higher count. Headcount goes up, invoice goes up; headcount goes down, invoice stays put. The clause only moves in the vendor's favour.

The fix is negotiated before signing, not at the invoice. Push for four things: frequency — annual true-up on the anniversary, not quarterly (quarterly true-ups charge a seasonal workforce for its peak four times a year); a threshold buffer of around 10% before any true-up triggers, so normal hiring churn doesn't generate an invoice; a locked rate so added seats price at your original per-seat number rather than list — write the number into the clause; and symmetry — a headcount decline reduces the renewal baseline.

A retroactive true-up, worked in dollars

Take a 20-seat Slack Pro contract at US$7.25/user/mo billed annually (checked 2026-10-05): US$1,740/year. In month 3 the team grows to 26 seats and nobody tells procurement. At the anniversary, the vendor counts 26.

Under a forward true-up, you simply renew at 26 seats: 26 × US$7.25 × 12 = US$2,262/year going forward. Under a retroactive true-up, you also owe the backdated difference: 6 extra seats × 10 months × US$7.25 = US$435 — on top of the higher renewal. Under band escalation, if 25 seats is a pricing-band boundary, all 26 seats could reprice to the next band's rate, not just the 6 over. And under then-current-rate overage, those 6 seats bill at whatever list price the vendor publishes on the true-up date — not the US$7.25 you negotiated.

The same 6-seat growth costs US$0 extra under a forward true-up, US$435 under a retroactive one, and an unknown amount under the other two. The clause type is the entire difference.

The 90-day pre-true-up audit

Ninety days before the anniversary:

  1. Run the seat match. Export the user list, match every seat to a current human, and deactivate zero-activity seats before the vendor counts them.
  2. Downgrade the over-tiered. Active-but-light users move to cheaper tiers now, so the baseline the vendor measures is already right-sized.
  3. Document the count. Screenshot or export your own user count with a date. If the vendor's number disagrees with yours at true-up time, you have evidence — not a feeling.
  4. Push for a longer notice window in the next term. Standard contracts give 30 days to modify seat counts before renewal — rarely enough. Negotiate 90 days minimum so the audit, the reclamation, and the negotiation all fit before the vendor locks their numbers.

Frequently asked questions

What is a true-up in a SaaS contract?

The clause that reconciles your contracted seat or usage count against actual use during the term, generating an invoice when you've exceeded what you licensed. The expensive part isn't the concept — it's the clause type (forward, retroactive, band escalation, or then-current-rate).

Can you negotiate a true-up clause?

Yes — frequency, a threshold buffer (~10%), a locked overage rate, and true-down symmetry are all negotiable before signing. After signing, you're negotiating the invoice, which is a much worse position.

What's the difference between a true-up and an overage charge?

An overage charge bills excess usage as it happens (usually monthly); a true-up reconciles it periodically (usually annually). True-ups create larger, less frequent surprises — which is why the 90-day pre-true-up audit exists.
