AWeber Pricing Decoded for Canadian Small Businesses

Published 2026-10-22 · Last updated 2026-10-22

AWeber is one of the longest-running email platforms for small businesses — founded in 1998, per its own site — and one of the easiest to misprice from a search result. The headline figures on its pricing page are annual-billing numbers ($12.49 a month for Lite, $19.99 for Plus), the same plans cost $15 and $30 on monthly billing, and both figures apply only to the smallest subscriber band: the price climbs through fourteen published tiers as a list grows. This guide decodes the current structure from AWeber's official pricing page (checked October 22, 2026), with the full band ladder and the two mechanics that decide the real bill — a monthly send cap set as a multiple of list size, and an ecommerce transaction fee that differs by plan. All prices are USD as published; AWeber publishes no Canadian-dollar price list, so Canadian buyers should budget exchange on top of every figure below. General information only, not financial advice.

The plans as AWeber publishes them

Free is $0 with no time limit and no credit card: up to 500 subscribers, 3,000 emails a month, one email list, one landing page and one automation, with phone, chat and email support — and AWeber branding on sends. Lite is $15/month billed monthly, or $12.50/month billed annually, at the up-to-500-subscriber band: 5,000 emails a month at that band, three landing pages, three automations, three users and one saved custom segment. Plus is $30/month billed monthly, or $20/month billed annually, at the same band: 6,000 emails a month, unlimited lists, landing pages, automations and users, behavioral automation, priority support and free list migration. Done For You is the Plus plan plus a one-time setup fee — shown on the pricing page as $79, marked down from $599 — for which AWeber's team builds the branded template, landing page, signup form and welcome workflow, with a 1:1 setup call and 30 days of unlimited edits. Above the band ladder sits Unlimited, a flat $899/month for large lists. Lite and Plus both start with a 14-day free trial; one caution from Omnisend's 2026 review of AWeber is that payment information has to be handed over to access the trial, so the trial start date is worth diarising.

Support is a genuine structural difference from most rivals at this price: every plan, Free included, carries 24/7 chat and email support, with phone support from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. ET — hours that line up with the Canadian business day in the east. Plus and Unlimited add priority support, and AWeber's team migrates a list for free on Plus. Nonprofits get three months of Lite or Plus free, then 25% off, per AWeber's FAQ.

Two patterns matter for budgeting. First, the Lite–Plus gap narrows as the list grows: it is $15 a month at 500 subscribers, $20 at 2,500, $35 at 10,000 and only $25 at 50,000 — so the upgrade decision gets cheaper, relatively, exactly when a growing business is most likely to want Plus's unlimited automations and segments. Second, annual billing is a real discount, not a rounding trick: at 500 subscribers Lite costs $150 a year prepaid against $180 billed monthly (about 17% saved), and Plus costs $240 against $360 (about 33% saved). A business confident it will stay the year should take the annual price; a business testing deliverability should not let the discount rush the decision.

The two mechanics that move the real bill

The send multiplier. Paid plans do not cap subscribers — AWeber's table lists the subscriber limit as unlimited — they cap sends: 10 times the subscriber count per month on Lite, 12 times on Plus. At 2,500 subscribers that is 25,000 sends on Lite, enough for a weekly newsletter (about 10,000), a welcome series and regular campaigns — but a daily sender or a business running several lists' worth of automation through one account can hit the cap, and per AWeber's knowledge base as summarised by Sender, exceeding the send limit upgrades the account to the next tier automatically. The bill can therefore move in a month when the list did not grow, purely because sending did. The transaction fee. Selling through AWeber Ecommerce carries a platform transaction fee of 1.0% on Lite and 0.6% on Plus, on top of the subscription. That 0.4-point gap produces a clean crossover. At the 2,500-subscriber band, Lite costs $35/month and Plus $55/month, a $20 difference; the fee gap recovers that $20 at $5,000 of monthly sales through AWeber (0.4% of $5,000 = $20). Below that volume Lite is cheaper in total — at $2,000 of monthly sales, Lite totals $55 against Plus's $67. Above it Plus wins on fees alone — at $10,000 of monthly sales, Lite totals $135 against Plus's $115, a $240-a-year difference — before counting Plus's unlimited automations, segments and branding removal. A seller's first question is therefore not "which plan is better" but "how much revenue actually runs through AWeber's checkout."

Traps and fine print

Four cautions complete the decode. First, billing-mode confusion is the most common AWeber pricing error in the wild: $12.49 and $19.99 are annual-billing per-month figures, while $15 and $30 are the monthly-billing prices at the same band — any comparison that mixes the two modes is wrong by 17–33%. Second, the send cap is a billing trigger, not just a limit: automatic tier upgrades on overage mean a single heavy campaign month can raise the subscription. Third, a seasonal business that pauses email entirely still pays full price unless it acts — Sender's summary of AWeber's knowledge base lists a Hold Package at $4.99/month that pauses billing while keeping subscriber data, automations and landing pages. Fourth, Done For You's $79 setup is a marked-down price (list $599, per AWeber's own pricing page; one review source still quotes the $599 figure as the fee), so its value should be judged at the price on the page on the day of purchase, not at the struck-through anchor.

The decision in three questions

For a Canadian small business, the AWeber choice reduces to three questions. Is the list under 500 and the sending light? Free is a real product — 3,000 sends, an automation, support — and the correct price is $0 until it is outgrown. Does the business sell through AWeber's checkout, and at what volume? Under roughly $5,000 a month of AWeber-processed sales at small bands, Lite's lower subscription beats its higher transaction fee; above it, Plus pays for itself on the fee gap alone. How many lists, automations and team members are actually needed? Lite's caps (one list, three automations, three users, one segment) are the real Plus trigger for most service businesses — a second list or a fourth automation ends the debate regardless of price. Businesses that answer "one list, light selling, small team" stay on Lite the longest; everyone else should budget Plus from the start and take the annual price once the first quarter proves the fit.
