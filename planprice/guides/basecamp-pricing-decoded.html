Basecamp Pricing Decoded: The Flat Rate and the 20-Person Crossover

Published 2026-10-15 · Last updated 2026-10-15

Basecamp is the pricing outlier in project management: one per-user plan, one flat plan, no tier ladder, and no feature gates between them. That simplicity is the product's whole pitch — and it makes the buying decision unusually mathematical. There is a precise headcount at which the flat plan overtakes the per-user plan, a precise way the flat plan's effective per-person cost falls as a team grows, and a short list of flat-priced add-ons that apply to one plan and are bundled into the other. This guide works that math for Canadian teams, in US dollars as Basecamp publishes them, checked on 2026-10-15.

Two plans, no ladder

The per-user plan — sold as Basecamp Plus in some listings — costs US$15 per user per month, with unlimited projects, all features, and 500 GB of storage. The flat plan, Pro Unlimited, costs US$299 per month billed annually, or US$349 per month billed monthly, for unlimited users, with 5 TB of storage, priority support and one-to-one onboarding. Both plans include the full feature set; Pro Unlimited is not a higher tier with more features unlocked, it is the same product under a different billing model, plus more storage, priority support and onboarding. That distinction matters because it removes the usual upgrade anxiety entirely: no team ever has to re-buy Basecamp to unlock a view, a report or an automation. The only question Basecamp asks a buyer is how many people will use it — and it asks that question in a form that can be answered with arithmetic rather than a feature audit.

Two further rules complete the picture. Clients and contractors are free on both plans — only employees are billed on the per-user plan — which removes the guest-seat accounting that complicates most competitors. And there is a trial route in: either plan can be tried free for 30 days with no credit card required, which is long enough to run one real project end to end before the billing model has to be chosen. For teams burned by per-seat creep elsewhere, the appeal is not any single number; it is that the bill next year is predictable from the bill this year, with headcount growth on the flat plan costing nothing at all.

The crossover: 20 people

The arithmetic is exact. On the per-user plan, a team of N people costs 15 × N per month. Set that equal to the flat plan's US$299 annual-billing rate and the crossover lands at 19.9 people — so at 20 users, the two plans cost the same (20 × US$15 = US$300 against US$299 flat), and every person beyond 20 makes Pro Unlimited strictly cheaper. Below the crossover the per-user plan wins and the gap is real money for small teams: five people pay US$75 a month per-user against US$299 flat, a difference of US$2,688 a year that a five-person team should not spend on billing elegance. Above the crossover the flat plan's advantage compounds quickly, because its price does not move at all while every per-seat competitor's price rises with each hire.

The effective per-person rate on Pro Unlimited tells the same story from the other side. At 20 users it is about US$14.95 a person — no saving yet. At 50 users it is about US$5.98 a person per month. At 100 users it is US$2.99 a person. At 200 users, US$1.50. No per-seat tool in this category approaches those effective rates at scale, which is why Basecamp's flat plan is best understood not as a discount but as a different bet: the vendor bets that simplicity and retention are worth more than per-seat expansion revenue, and large teams collect the difference.

Add-ons: flat-priced, and bundled on the flat plan

Basecamp's add-on list is as short as its plan list, and priced the same way — flat, not per seat. On the per-user plan, the named extras cost US$50 per month flat each: the Timesheet add-on for time tracking, the Admin Pro Pack, and each additional terabyte of storage. On Pro Unlimited, the Timesheet and Admin Pro Pack upgrades are included in the flat rate rather than sold separately. A per-user team that needs both extras is therefore not really paying US$15 a seat; it is paying US$15 a seat plus US$100 a month, which moves its crossover point. Ten people with both add-ons pay US$150 + US$100 = US$250 a month — within US$49 of the flat plan that includes both extras, unlimited seats and ten times the storage. Any team on the per-user plan that adds Timesheet or the Admin Pro Pack should redo the crossover math with the add-ons included before renewing; the honest comparison is total against total, and the totals are closer than the plan prices suggest. This is also the one place the otherwise simple price list can surprise a buyer — not through per-seat creep, but through flat extras quietly stacking beside a per-seat base.

What the flat price does not buy

Flat pricing buys predictability, not depth, and the trade should be priced consciously. Basecamp deliberately leaves out much of what per-seat competitors compete on: there are no Gantt charts, no dependencies, no custom fields, no sprint tooling and only basic reporting, and the product ships essentially no native AI features — its maker has chosen to let external tools connect instead. A team whose work needs dependencies, capacity views or portfolio reporting will not fix that by paying more; no Basecamp tier adds them. The correct comparison is therefore two-step. First, does Basecamp's deliberately simple feature set cover the work? If not, its price — however attractive at scale — is irrelevant. Second, if it does cover the work, run the crossover math: per-user below 20 people without add-ons, flat at 20 and above, and flat earlier than 20 once the US$50 add-ons enter the per-user total. Teams that pass both steps get the most predictable software bill in the category. Teams that fail the first step should pay a per-seat competitor for the depth they actually need and stop comparing against a flat rate they cannot use.

The decision framework

This is a buying-decision guide, not a setup procedure — the framework below is the process.

  1. Gate on features first. No Gantt, dependencies, custom fields or native AI at any price — if the work needs them, Basecamp is out before pricing begins.
  2. Run the crossover at your real headcount. Per-user wins below 20 people; Pro Unlimited wins at 20 and above, and its effective rate falls with every hire.
  3. Include the add-ons in the per-user total. Timesheet and Admin Pro Pack at US$50 a month each can pull the crossover well below 20 people.
  4. Count only employees. Clients and contractors are free on both plans — do not inflate the headcount used in the math.
  5. Use the 30-day trial for one real project. Simplicity is the product; a live project is the only honest test of whether it is enough.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Basecamp cost?

US$15 per user per month on the per-user plan, or a flat US$299 per month billed annually (US$349 monthly) for unlimited users on Pro Unlimited. Clients and contractors are free on both plans.

When is Pro Unlimited cheaper than the per-user plan?

At 20 users: 20 × US$15 = US$300 a month against US$299 flat. Add-ons on the per-user plan — US$50 a month each for Timesheet and the Admin Pro Pack — can move the crossover lower.

Does Basecamp charge for guests or clients?

No. Clients and contractors can be invited without a licence on both plans; only employees count toward the per-user price, and Pro Unlimited has no per-person price at all.
