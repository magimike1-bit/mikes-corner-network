What Canadian Small Businesses Pay for Software & Services — 2026 Benchmark

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Every number on this page is computed from PlanPrice's own verified vendor dataset — the 49 vendors whose pricing our research team has checked against vendor pages between September 30 and October 25, 2026 — and from nothing else. No industry surveys, no borrowed statistics. That makes this a smaller dataset than the big annual reports, and a more checkable one: every median below can be traced to the vendor pages linked throughout this site.

Method and dataset

Dataset: 49 vendors across 9 categories, pricing verified 2026-09-30 to 2026-10-25 (each vendor page shows its own verification date; the verification log shows what was checked when). Method: for each category we took every published paid plan price in the dataset, converted plan prices to their stated monthly figure as published (annual-billing prices where the vendor leads with them), and report the median and the full range. Plan prices mix per-user and flat pricing exactly as vendors publish them — read the table as "what plans cost," not "what your bill will be"; the teardowns and comparison tool do the per-business math. Currency is as published per vendor (Canadian vendors in CAD; US vendors in USD as published).

The fee layer: payments

Published in-person card rates in the dataset cluster tightly: Square at 2.5% for credit and 0.75% + 7¢ for Interac debit (Canada, checked October 25, 2026), Lightspeed Payments at 2.6% + 10¢, and online rates at 2.9% + 30¢ (Stripe) to 2.9% + 60¢ (Wave Payments, Canada). The spread between the cheapest and most common in-person credit rate is 0.1 of a point — at $30,000 of monthly card volume that point is worth $30 a month, which is why the per-transaction cents and the debit rate matter more to small-ticket businesses than the headline percentage.

What this dataset cannot say

It cannot say what the average Canadian business actually spends — we track published prices, not invoices. Usage-based lines (card fees, SMS, extra contacts) move real bills well above plan medians; the business-profile teardowns model those explicitly. Prices also move: this report will be recomputed as the dataset's verification dates advance, and the per-vendor deal pages always carry fresher dates than this page.

How to use this benchmark (and how not to)

Use it for orientation: what a category's plans typically cost, where the free credible options are, and what the fee layer looks like before you price a specific vendor. Do not use it as a budget — medians mix per-user and flat pricing, currencies as published, and plans aimed at very different business sizes. Your budget lives in three other places on this site: the vendor pages (exact plans, dated), the comparison tool (your seat count against the same data), and the business teardowns (full stacks for six profiles, fees included). A second caution: "cheapest credible" is a floor, not a recommendation — several categories' cheapest options are free tiers with real limits, and the right question is which limit you'd hit first.

What we expect to move by the 2027 edition

Three lines in this dataset are visibly in motion. AI packaging: vendors are re-bundling AI features between tiers and add-ons faster than any other line — several 2026 price checks on this site are really AI-tier changes wearing a pricing costume. Payroll: Wagepoint's October 2026 schedule rebuild shows the category repricing around per-employee lines; rivals' next moves will show in this dataset within a sweep or two. Percentage-of-revenue pricing: more categories (booking, ecommerce, invoicing) are shifting from flat plans to transaction percentages, which medians capture badly — the 2027 edition will need a fee-weighted companion table. The dataset grows with every vendor page and sweep; the method stays the same so editions stay comparable.
