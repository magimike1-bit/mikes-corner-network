Capsule CRM Pricing Decoded for Canadian Small Businesses

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Search for Capsule CRM pricing and most results hand you one ladder — $18, $36, $54 and $72 per user per month — and stop there. Those numbers are real, but they are only the annual-billing half of the price list. Capsule also publishes a monthly-billing ladder, and it does not sit a tidy flat percentage above the annual one: the gap is about 14% on Starter, about 10% on Advanced, but only about 5% on Growth and about 4% on Ultimate. This guide decodes both ladders from Capsule's own published pricing statements and detailed plan breakdowns (checked October 25, 2026), maps the three gates that actually force an upgrade — sales pipelines, contact limits, and workflow automation with most of the AI tools — and works the number a Canadian small business actually pays at five users. All prices are USD as published; no Canadian-dollar price list was found in the sources checked, so budget exchange on top of every figure below. General information only, not financial advice.

The plans, as published

Capsule sells five editions. The Free plan costs $0 for up to 2 users and 250 contacts, with one sales pipeline and 10 AI content assists per month. Starter costs $18 per user per month billed annually, or $21 billed monthly: the contact limit jumps to 30,000, with one sales pipeline, one project board, 50 custom fields, one shared mailbox, five email templates and activity reporting. Growth costs $36 billed annually, or $38 billed monthly: 60,000 contacts, five sales pipelines, five project boards, 150 custom fields, 1,000 AI assists per month, three shared mailboxes and 50 email templates. Advanced costs $54 billed annually, or $60 billed monthly: 120,000 contacts, 50 pipelines and 50 project boards, 500 email templates and five shared mailboxes. Ultimate costs $72 billed annually, or $75 billed monthly: 240,000 contacts, 100 pipelines and 100 project boards, unlimited custom fields, ten shared mailboxes, 1,000 email templates, and premium support with a dedicated account manager and custom training. Every paid plan carries a 14-day free trial, with no credit card required to start it on the breakdowns checked.

Annual vs monthly: the uneven discount

The annual-billing saving is worth computing plan by plan, because Capsule's is not the flat 15–20% discount many trackers imply. On Starter, paying annually saves $3 per user per month against the $21 monthly price — about 14%. On Advanced, the $6 gap against $60 is 10%. On Growth, the gap shrinks to $2 against $38 — about 5% — and on Ultimate it is $3 against $75, about 4%. The practical consequence runs against the usual advice: the plan where annual commitment saves the least, in percentage terms, is Growth, which is also the plan most growing teams end up on. Annual billing still costs less on every tier, and at five users the saving is $180 a year on Starter and $360 a year on Advanced — real money, but not a reason by itself to lock in before the 14-day trial and a first month of real use have shown the team will live in the tool. A team unsure between Starter and Growth loses very little by starting monthly on Growth: the monthly premium there is only $2 per user.

The three gates: pipelines, contacts, and automation

With five editions, the upgrade question is really three separate questions. The first gate is pipelines: Free and Starter both allow exactly one sales pipeline, so a business that sells two different things in two different ways — new business and renewals, say, or products and services — needs Growth at $36 per user per month regardless of how few contacts it holds. The second gate is contacts: the ladder runs 250 on Free, then 30,000, 60,000, 120,000 and 240,000. The Free-to-Starter jump is the steep one in usefulness — 250 contacts is a trial-sized list, while 30,000 covers most small businesses for years — which makes Starter a genuinely usable paid tier rather than a toll booth. The third gate is automation and AI: workflow automation, advanced sales reporting, user roles and permissions, and business and contact enrichment arrive on Growth, and Capsule's own published summary puts its AI features from the $36 Growth price, listing AI Summaries, AI Contact Enrichment, AI Email Assist and the AI Pipeline Generator among its AI capabilities. Starter's 10 AI assists per month, against Growth's 1,000, tells the same story in numbers: the AI suite is a Growth feature, with a sample included below it.

Read the table as a decision, not just a total. A five-person team on Starter pays $1,080 a year for a single-pipeline CRM with basic reporting; the step to Growth exactly doubles the bill to $2,160, and what the extra $1,080 buys is specific: four more pipelines, workflow automation, advanced reporting, roles and permissions, enrichment, and a hundred times the monthly AI assists. If none of those is a live need, Starter is not a compromise — it is the correct tier, and the contact ceiling of 30,000 is unlikely to bite first. If any one of them is a live need, no amount of Starter savings helps, because the feature is simply not sold below Growth. Advanced and Ultimate, by contrast, are mostly headroom purchases — more contacts, pipelines, boards, templates and mailboxes, plus account management at the top — so they should be bought when a limit is actually approaching, not in anticipation.

What sits outside the seat price

The seat price is not the whole Capsule bill for every team. Email marketing is not built into the CRM tiers: it lives in Transpond, Capsule's sister email-marketing product, sold separately, so a team that expects campaigns, sequences and marketing automation inside its CRM subscription should price Transpond on top of the figures above before comparing Capsule against an all-in-one rival. This is the recurring pattern in SaaS pricing generally: the headline per-user figure covers the core product, and the integrations and companion tools a team actually relies on are where the second line item appears. The integrations themselves are a Starter-and-up strength — paid plans add premium integrations including Xero, QuickBooks, Mailchimp and Zapier — but a careful buyer confirms, for each tool in the existing stack, whether it connects on the tier being bought or waits for a higher one, rather than discovering the gate after the annual payment.

Who should buy Capsule CRM, and who should not

Buy Capsule CRM if the team is small, sells in one process today, and wants a paid CRM that is usable at the entry tier: Starter at $18 per user per month on annual billing, with 30,000 contacts, is a complete small-business CRM for a single-pipeline firm. Buy Growth from the start if there are two sales processes, if follow-up work needs workflow automation, or if the AI summaries and enrichment are part of the buying reason — those are $36 features, and trialling Starter first only postpones the real evaluation. Skip Capsule if email marketing must live inside the same subscription (that is the separate Transpond product), if the contact list already exceeds the tier ceilings at a team size that makes $54–$72 per user hard to justify, or if deep enterprise customisation is the requirement — Capsule's published shape is deliberately a small-and-mid-sized-business tool. And whichever tier fits, use the 14-day trial on the tier actually being considered: the gates in this product are structural, so a trial on the wrong tier proves very little.
