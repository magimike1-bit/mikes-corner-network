Cloudflare Plans Decoded: What Free vs $20 vs $200 Actually Unlocks

Published 2026-10-11

Most Cloudflare plan breakdowns are written for security engineers — tables of WAF rulesets and rate-limit counts that answer everything except the small-site question: what does a brochure site or a small store actually get for $20 a month that Free doesn't provide? This guide decodes the three plans in practical terms, prices the monthly-billing premium most guides omit, and flags the add-on layer that can bill on top of any plan.

What Free genuinely covers

Cloudflare's Free plan is unusually generous and covers the essentials for most small sites: unmetered DDoS mitigation, the global CDN (330+ data centers), a shared SSL certificate, a limited free managed WAF ruleset, one rate-limiting rule, Bot Fight Mode, and 3 page rules. Critically, Cloudflare charges no per-GB bandwidth fees on any plan, including Free — unlimited CDN bandwidth is included, which alone makes it the best free tier in the infrastructure business.

For a brochure site, a blog, or a small portfolio: Free is enough. The honest test is whether you've ever hit one of its limits — if you haven't configured a WAF rule in anger, you don't need the paid rulesets yet.

What $20/month Pro unlocks in practice

Pro costs $20/month billed annually, or $25/month billed monthly — the monthly premium is 25%, a detail most guides omit. Per zone (per domain), Pro adds:

  • The full WAF stack: Cloudflare Managed Ruleset plus the OWASP Core Ruleset, instead of Free's limited ruleset. This is the headline upgrade — real protection against the OWASP Top 10.
  • Image optimization (Polish) and mobile optimization (Mirage) — automatic image compression and mobile rendering tweaks that genuinely speed up image-heavy pages.
  • Super Bot Fight Mode (up from basic Bot Fight Mode) and 2 rate-limiting rules (up from 1).
  • 20 page rules (up from 3) — meaningful if you run redirects, caching exceptions, or forwarding rules.
  • Workers Paid — 10 million requests included per month (then $0.30 per additional million), with 50ms CPU per invocation instead of Free's 10ms.

In practical terms: Pro earns its $20 when your site is image-heavy enough that Polish pays for itself in speed, when you've outgrown one rate-limit rule (say, protecting both login and checkout), or when you're running Workers in production. A small store doing real transaction volume is the classic Pro customer — not because of the CDN, which Free already gives you, but because of the WAF.

Who needs $200/month Business (almost no small site)

Business costs $200/month billed annually, $250 monthly. It adds custom WAF rules, 5 rate-limit rules, custom SSL certificate uploads, bypass-cache-on-cookie, PCI compliance support, prioritized email support, 50 page rules, and a 100% uptime SLA with 25x reimbursement credit.

The small-site reality check: the features that justify Business are PCI compliance needs (payment data flowing through your own infrastructure), custom WAF rules for application-specific attacks, and the uptime SLA for revenue-critical sites. If none of those describe you, Business is $2,400/year for peace of mind you can get from Pro at $240. One pricing tracker reports the top tier dropped from $250 to $200/month in July 2026 — even at the lower price, it's an enterprise-adjacent tier wearing a small-business name.

The add-on layer above every plan

The plan price isn't the ceiling — Cloudflare sells metered add-ons that bill on top of any plan, including Free:

  • Argo Smart Routing — $0.10/GB: faster routing for dynamic content. At 100GB/month of dynamic traffic, that's $10/month on top of your plan.
  • Stream — $1 per 1,000 minutes of video delivered: video hosting without the YouTube branding. A small course site can outgrow its plan budget here fast.
  • Workers overages — beyond the included requests, usage-based billing applies.

One pattern worth flagging: SaaS vendors love charges that live outside the plan price — one provider billed per device connected even for read-only data access, a fee that never appeared on the rate card. Cloudflare's add-on layer is the same shape of surprise. Before enabling Argo or Stream, estimate the metered cost at your actual traffic; the plan comparison is only half the bill.

The decision, in one pass

  • Stay on Free if: brochure site, blog, portfolio — you haven't hit a Free limit and don't handle payment data directly.
  • Go Pro ($20/mo annual) if: image-heavy pages, a store with real transaction volume, you need the OWASP WAF ruleset, or one rate-limit rule isn't enough. Bill annually — monthly costs 25% more.
  • Go Business ($200/mo annual) if: PCI compliance requirements, custom WAF rules for your app, or the uptime SLA is worth $2,160/year more than Pro to you.
  • Watch the add-ons regardless of plan: Argo, Stream, and Workers overages are metered separately — estimate before enabling.

A note on format: this is a buying-decision guide, not a fix-it walkthrough, so there are no numbered setup steps — the decision framework above is the actionable part.

FAQ

Is Cloudflare Pro worth it for a small website?

For most small brochure sites, no — the Free plan's CDN, DDoS protection, and SSL cover the essentials. Pro's $20/month earns its keep when you need image optimization, the full WAF ruleset, or more than one rate-limiting rule.

Does Cloudflare charge for bandwidth?

No. Cloudflare charges no per-GB bandwidth fees on any plan, including Free — unlimited CDN bandwidth is included. Metered add-ons like Argo Smart Routing ($0.10/GB) and Stream are the exception, not the rule.

What is the difference between Cloudflare Pro and Business?

Pro ($20/month) adds image optimization, the full OWASP WAF ruleset, and 20 page rules. Business ($200/month) adds custom WAF rules, 5 rate-limit rules, PCI compliance support, custom SSL uploads, and a 100% uptime SLA with 25x credit.
