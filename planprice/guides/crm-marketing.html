How to Choose a CRM for a Small Business

Last updated 2026-10-01

A CRM earns its keep the week it stops a deal from slipping through the cracks. Until then it is just another login. The trick is picking one whose free starting point is genuinely useful, so the team builds the habit before the bill arrives.

HubSpot's CRM is free for unlimited users, which makes it a low-risk way to get off spreadsheets — the free tier covers contacts, deals, and basic email marketing, with paid Starter and Pro editions waiting if you outgrow it. This guide covers how to evaluate that path, and what to check in any CRM before your data lives there.

Start free, but read the upgrade path

A free CRM is only a good deal if the paid tiers make sense later. Map the journey: what does the free tier include, what triggers the first paid invoice — more contacts, automation, reporting — and what does the top tier you might plausibly need look like? A CRM that is free today and shocking in year two is not a bargain.

HubSpot's model is the template to compare against: free for unlimited users on the core CRM, with Starter and Pro editions adding sales and marketing automation. Whatever you choose, the upgrade path should be a slope, not a cliff.

Understand the contacts model

CRMs charge in different ways: per contact, per seat, or per feature tier. Unlimited users with contact-based pricing behaves very differently from per-seat pricing as your team grows. Model both for your headcount at twelve months, not just today.

Also ask what counts as a contact. Marketing contacts are often billed separately from CRM contacts, and the definition varies. If you plan to do email marketing from the CRM, get the contact math in writing before you import your list.

Check the email marketing limits

Most small-business CRMs bundle basic email marketing, but basic hides real limits: monthly send caps, template libraries, automation depth, and deliverability tooling. If email is a core channel for you, trial the email builder with a real campaign before you commit your list.

Deliverability is the quiet killer. Ask about list hygiene tools and what happens to your sender reputation if you import a stale list. A CRM that helps you send is good; one that helps you land in the inbox is better.

Plan the data migration

Your existing customer data lives in spreadsheets, inboxes, and someone's head. Before choosing, confirm the CRM imports from CSV cleanly, deduplicates on import, and lets you map custom fields. A painful migration is the number one reason CRM rollouts stall.

Run the migration as the trial: import a slice of real data, build one pipeline, send one campaign. If the trial feels like work, the rollout will feel like twice the work.

Quick checklist

  • Map the free-to-paid path: what triggers the first invoice, and what does year two look like?
  • Model pricing for your twelve-month headcount and contact volume, not today's.
  • Confirm what counts as a billable contact, especially for email marketing.
  • Trial the email builder with a real campaign before importing your list.
  • Test CSV import, deduplication, and custom-field mapping with real data.
  • Check integrations with your invoicing and scheduling tools.
