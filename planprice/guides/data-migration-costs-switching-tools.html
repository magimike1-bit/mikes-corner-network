Data Migration Costs When Switching SaaS Tools

Published 2026-10-12

Every vendor demo ends the same way: "migration is easy, just export and import." Every real migration tells a different story — custom fields that don't map, relationships that break, four months of cleanup for a one-afternoon export. The cost of leaving a tool is the most under-budgeted line in SaaS, and it is also the number that determines whether switching is worth it at all.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm migration terms on the vendor's own site or in a written quote before buying.

The invoice nobody budgets: leaving costs money

Migration cost scales with mess, not records. A clean 50,000-contact CRM move with standard fields can be a $5,000–$15,000 agency project; the same record count with years of custom objects, undocumented automations, and four messy legacy sources becomes a $25,000–$80,000 project taking six to twelve weeks. One practitioner documented a rushed 50,000-record CRM migration that landed at roughly $108,000 on a $40,000 budget — $25,000 of cleanup that had to happen after go-live instead of before, $15,000 in timeline overruns, $8,000 in emergency fixes, and $20,000+ in lost pipeline velocity.

The pattern repeats because budgets cover the move and forget the preparation. Migration vendors report projects running 50–100% longer than planned, and the overrun almost always traces to data quality nobody assessed before the clock started.

The three migration cost buckets

Bucket 1: labor. Export, cleanup, field mapping, import, validation. CRM consultants charge $150–$250 an hour. A worked HubSpot-to-Salesforce example for 50,000 contacts: 40 hours of export and cleanup ($6,000), 20 hours of import mapping ($3,000), 60 hours of integration rewiring ($9,000), 10 hours of training ($1,500) — $19,500 in contractor time alone, before licenses.

Bucket 2: the overlap tax. You pay for both systems during the transition, plus the weeks of dual entry or reconciliation while the team straddles them. Budget two to six months of the old tool's subscription as pure overlap — and keep the old system accessible in read-only mode for at least six months after migration, because historical data that didn't transfer cleanly will be needed.

Bucket 3: the lock-in penalty. Proprietary formats turn an export into a rebuild. Salesforce's data model creates proprietary object structures that don't translate cleanly to any other CRM, and custom code built in its platform works only inside it. After cancellation, Salesforce gives you 30 days of data access — custom objects, full history, attachments, relationships, everything — and then it's gone. A 30-day window for years of customer history is not a grace period; it's a deadline.

The "cheaper tool" is only cheaper after the migration line is paid off, and most switchers never compute the payback before they start.

The exit-cost screen: read the export before you sign

The cheapest migration is the one you price before buying the first tool. Before signing with any vendor, answer these:

  1. Can I export everything, in what format, and how fast? CSV and API exports are cheap to leave; proprietary formats are ransom notes.
  2. What happens to my data when I cancel? Get the post-cancellation access window in writing — 30 days is common and tight.
  3. How much of my setup is portable? Custom objects, automations, and integrations are rebuilt, not moved. Count them.
  4. What does the old system cost me during the move? Overlap is a real line item — price two to six months of dual subscriptions.
  5. Who owns the cleanup? Pre-migration cleanup cuts total project cost by 25–35% — do it before the contractor's clock starts.

A vendor that answers these clearly is a vendor whose exit is cheap. A vendor that waves them away is telling you the price of leaving — it's just not on the pricing page.

Frequently asked questions

How much does data migration cost for a small business?

Simple moves (clean contacts, standard fields) run from a few hundred to several thousand dollars. A typical small-business CRM migration with an agency runs $5,000–$15,000. Complex Salesforce-class migrations run $25,000–$80,000+. Budget 3–5× your first estimate — the export is the easy part.

How long does a CRM migration take?

A simple migration can finish in one to three weeks. Salesforce-class migrations typically take six to twelve weeks plus integration rebuild time. Timelines stretch when data quality is poor — audit before you commit to a date.

What is the biggest hidden cost in switching SaaS tools?

The cleanup nobody budgeted: fixing broken relationships, mapping custom fields, and deduplicating records that merged differently in each system. In one documented case it turned a $40,000 project into a $108,000 one.
