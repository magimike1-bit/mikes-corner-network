Deel vs Remote Pricing in Canada: EOR Costs and When You Don't Need One

Published 2026-10-12

Deel and Remote both sell the same headline: employer-of-record service from US$599 per employee per month. But before comparing the two, a Canadian employer should ask a question no global listicle asks: do you need an EOR at all? If the person you are hiring lives in Canada, domestic payroll costs a fraction of that US$599. The EOR conversation starts when the hire is abroad.

General information, not financial or legal advice. EOR fees exclude salaries and statutory employment costs. Confirm current pricing with each vendor before signing.

Do you even need an EOR? The Canadian employer's first question

An employer of record becomes the legal employer of your worker in a country where you have no entity — handling payroll, taxes, benefits, and compliance there. For a hire in Toronto, you don't need one: Canadian payroll providers run the whole thing (CPP, EI, T4s) for a small monthly fee per employee. An EOR buyer's guide from Wisemonk makes the point bluntly: neither Deel nor Remote is cheapest for single-market hiring — a specialist often wins on cost.

So the decision tree is: hiring inside Canada → domestic payroll. Hiring in the US, UK, Philippines, or anywhere you have no entity → EOR, and now Deel vs Remote is the right comparison.

What the US$599 does not include

The EOR fee is the management layer only. On top of it sit the worker's salary, statutory employment costs in their country (social charges, mandatory benefits, payroll taxes), and any country-specific extras. One comparison notes explicitly that service fees exclude salary and statutory employment costs — confirm the current agreement, because the extras vary wildly by country. Neither vendor charges onboarding or setup fees for basic use, though deposits can apply for high-risk regions.

1 EOR employee + 3 contractors: the 12-month math

A common small setup — one full-time hire abroad, three international contractors — for one year:

  • Deel: (US$599 × 12) + (3 × US$49 × 12) = US$7,188 + US$1,764 = US$8,952/yr (≈ CA$12,767)
  • Remote (annual billing): (US$599 × 12) + (3 × US$29 × 12) = US$7,188 + US$1,044 = US$8,232/yr (≈ CA$11,741)
  • Remote (monthly billing): (US$699 × 12) + US$1,044 = US$9,432/yr (≈ CA$13,452)

Two takeaways: the contractor tier is where Remote wins the TCO — US$20 less per contractor per month adds up to US$720/year at three contractors. And Remote's annual-vs-monthly spread is US$1,200/year on a single EOR employee; pay monthly only if you are unsure the role lasts the year.

Where they actually differ

Price aside, the structural difference is the entity model: Remote directly owns its legal entities in its EOR countries, while Deel uses a mix of owned entities and in-country partners (Remote's comparison table). Deel counters with a broader product ecosystem — immigration, IT, PEO — and faster self-serve onboarding. User-experience scoring from Employ Borderless gives Remote 4.8 vs Deel 4.2 on UX and 4.6 vs 4.3 on support, with Deel ahead 4.7 vs 4.5 on features.

The guarantee terms differ too: Remote offers a 100% money-back guarantee for the first 60 days if you cancel before onboarding completes; Deel has no broad formal refund policy and handles disputes case by case (Global Work Guide).

The Canadian billing wrinkle

Both vendors invoice in USD. Canadian buyers should budget the exchange swing (CAD estimates above use 1 USD ≈ CA$1.4262, checked 2026-10-05) and ask their accountant about GST/HST on imported services — cross-border B2B services can carry indirect-tax obligations that don't appear on the vendor's pricing page.

Decision: which setup buys which

Hiring inside Canada only → skip both; use domestic payroll. Hiring abroad with a contractor-heavy mix → Deel's broader platform and US$599 flat EOR rate suit fast-moving teams. Hiring full-time employees abroad where compliance risk keeps you up at night → Remote's owned-entity model, lower contractor fees, and money-back guarantee. Either way, run the 12-month math with your actual headcount mix before the demo — the headline rate is the smallest part of the decision.
