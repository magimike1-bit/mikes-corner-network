DocuSign vs Dropbox Sign: Per-Envelope Math and the API-Tier Trap

Published 2026-10-11

E-signature pricing comparisons stop at the seat price and miss the number that decides the bill: the envelope. DocuSign sells seats with an annual envelope cap; Dropbox Sign sells flat plans with unlimited envelopes. At low volume the seat math wins; at high volume the envelope math flips the answer. This guide works both, with Canadian DocuSign prices.

The three pricing models in e-signatures

Every vendor's pricing page fits one of three models. Per user, per month: DocuSign Standard at CA$33/user/month — you pay for a seat for every sender, every month, used or not. Per envelope, pay as you go: pure usage pricing for spiky senders. Flat plan with an allowance: Dropbox Sign Essentials at $15/month with unlimited envelopes for small teams. Know which model you are looking at before you compare a single number.

The cap that matters: 100 envelopes per user per year on annual plans (10 per user per month on monthly plans), with published overage of $3.00–$5.80 per envelope. Metered extras: SMS delivery from CA$0.50 and ID verification from CA$3.30 per attempt.

Dropbox Sign: the unlimited-envelope model

Dropbox Sign (formerly HelloSign) takes the opposite approach: Essentials at $15/month with unlimited envelopes for small teams, and Standard at $25/user/month. There is no per-user annual cap to plan around. The trade: lighter workflow depth than DocuSign — fewer advanced IDV options and simpler audit tooling — in exchange for a bill that never surprises you at volume.

Many senders with low volume is where per-seat plans are at their least bad. And a flat $8/month plan with a 100-invite annual cap (SignNow Business, ~$96/year) can beat both for the lowest-volume case — with its own cap to watch.

The API-tier trap

If you plan to send envelopes from your own app, budget a second product. DocuSign's API is not included in dashboard plans: developer plans start around $600/year with request-volume tiers (40–100 envelopes/month on entry tiers, embedded signing on higher tiers). Dropbox Sign's API is likewise a separate product, from around $75/month on entry tiers. Teams discover this after buying dashboard seats — price the API line item before you commit to either vendor.

Decision framework: which model for which team

  • Few senders, steady volume under the caps: DocuSign Standard — the workflow depth is real and the caps won't bite.
  • High or spiky volume, any team size: Dropbox Sign Essentials — unlimited envelopes removes the planning problem entirely.
  • Sending from your own software: price the API tiers first — the dashboard comparison is the wrong comparison.

Note: this is a pricing explainer, not a procedure guide — no steps here, just the per-envelope math to run against your sending volume.

FAQ

How many envelopes does DocuSign include per user?

100 envelopes per user per year on annual plans (10 per user per month on monthly plans). Overage runs $3.00–$5.80 per envelope.

Does Dropbox Sign have an envelope cap?

Essentials ($15/month) offers unlimited envelopes for small teams; Standard is $25/user/month. There is no per-user annual cap the way DocuSign structures it.

Is API access included in DocuSign plans?

No — API access is a separate product with developer plans starting around $600/year, billed by request volume. Budget it as a second purchase.
