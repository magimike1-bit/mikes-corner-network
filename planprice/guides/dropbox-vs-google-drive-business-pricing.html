Dropbox Business vs Google Workspace Pricing: The Storage Math Nobody Does

Published 2026-10-11

Every comparison of Dropbox and Google Workspace publishes the same per-user price table and stops there. But the per-user price is the least informative number on the page: what matters is what each seat buys in storage, how that storage pools across your team, and what the USD list price becomes in Canadian dollars. This guide does that math for real team sizes, using prices checked in October 2026.

The published prices, October 2026

Google Workspace sells three Business tiers, all priced per user per month on annual billing (flexible month-to-month billing costs more): Starter at $7, Standard at $14, and Plus at $22, all in USD. Annual billing saves about 16% versus the flexible rate. New customers may see lower intro prices on their first 20 users for 12 months — that discount expires, so budget at the standard rate.

Dropbox's team plans (checked against Dropbox's plans page in September 2026): Standard at $15 per user per month billed annually, and Advanced at $24 per user per month billed annually, both in USD. Both carry a 30-day free trial. This guide compares price and storage only — it doesn't rate the bundled apps on either side.

The storage math nobody does: what 5 seats actually buy

Here's where the comparison gets real. Both vendors pool storage, but they count it differently:

  • Google Workspace adds every user's allowance into one shared org-wide pool. Five seats on Business Standard share 10 TB (5 × 2 TB). One designer can fill most of it while the inbox-only users barely register — nobody gets a per-user warning.
  • Dropbox Standard pools 3 TB plus 1 TB per extra active license. Dropbox Advanced pools 15 TB plus 5 TB per extra active license, with a three-license minimum and a 1,000 TB ceiling.

Now the money, for a 5-person team on annual billing:

  • Dropbox Standard: 5 × $15 = $75/month (~$107 CAD)
  • Workspace Standard: 5 × $14 = $70/month (~$100 CAD), with 10 TB pooled

At 10 seats the gap is $150 vs $140 per month — Workspace stays about 7% cheaper per seat while delivering about two-thirds more pooled storage at the Standard tier (20 TB vs 12 TB for a 10-person team). Dropbox only wins the storage argument at Advanced, where the 15 TB base pool suits media-heavy teams — at $24 per seat, about 70% more per seat than Workspace Standard.

Per-seat vs pooled: how each vendor counts

Both vendors charge per seat, but a seat means different things. On Dropbox, storage scales with active licenses — add a fourth person and the pool grows by the per-license increment; remove someone and it shrinks. On Workspace, each seat contributes a fixed allowance (30 GB, 2 TB or 5 TB) to the shared pool, and Business plans cap at 300 users — past that you're on Enterprise with custom pricing. Practical consequence: on either platform, don't buy seats for people who don't need accounts just to get storage. And before you add seats for storage reasons, check the next tier up — the jump from Workspace Standard to Plus more than doubles per-user storage (2 TB to 5 TB) for $8 more per seat, which is often cheaper than seats you don't need.

The Canadian double-hit

Both vendors bill in USD. At the October 2026 mid-market rate of about 1.43 CAD per USD, Workspace Standard's $14 seat is really ~$20 CAD, and Dropbox Standard's $15 seat is ~$21.50 CAD — before your card's foreign-transaction markup. The per-seat comparison you do in your head in CAD is wrong by the FX gap on every seat, every month. Convert before you compare, and remember the rate moves: a 5% swing in the loonie moves a 10-seat Workspace Standard bill by about $10 CAD a month.

Which team should pick which

Pick Google Workspace Standard if per-seat price and pooled storage are the deciding factors — $70/month for a 5-person team with 10 TB pooled is the strongest price-per-TB in this comparison. Pick Dropbox Advanced if you need its 15 TB base pool and are willing to pay $24/seat for it. Dropbox Standard at $15/seat is the hardest tier to justify on storage math alone: it costs more per seat than Workspace Standard ($15 vs $14) while its 5-seat pool (7 TB) is smaller than Workspace's (10 TB).

A note on structure: this is a price-comparison explainer, not a repair procedure — there are no settings to change, only the storage math to run before you buy.

FAQ

Is Dropbox Business cheaper than Google Workspace?

Per seat, no — Workspace Business Standard ($14/user/month annual) undercuts Dropbox Standard ($15/user/month annual), and Workspace's 5-seat pooled storage (10 TB) beats Dropbox Standard's (7 TB). This guide compares price and storage only.

How does pooled storage work on each platform?

Workspace adds every user's allowance into one org-wide pool (five Standard seats = 10 TB shared). Dropbox Standard pools 3 TB plus 1 TB per extra active license; Advanced pools 15 TB plus 5 TB per extra active license, with a three-license minimum.

Do Canadians pay these prices in CAD?

No — both vendors bill in USD. At the October 2026 mid-market rate of about 1.43 CAD per USD, a $14 USD seat costs roughly $20 CAD before any card FX fee. Always convert before comparing.
