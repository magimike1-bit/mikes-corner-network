Best Email Marketing for Canadian Small Business

Last updated 2026-10-05

Four platforms cover almost every Canadian small-business case: Mailchimp, Brevo, Kit, and Canadian Cyberimpact. This guide compares them on what decides the bill: the free-tier limits, how the pricing model treats your list size, and whether the platform helps you meet Canada's anti-spam rules.

The four worth comparing

Mailchimp is the biggest name and the most polished builder, with the largest template library. It prices by contact count: Free ($0, up to 250 contacts, 500 sends a month), Essentials from US$13/month at 500 contacts (≈ CA$19 at current rates), Standard from US$20/month (≈ CA$29), Premium from US$350/month (≈ CA$499). Costs climb with your list — Essentials runs about US$45/month at 2,500 contacts (≈ CA$64) and US$110/month at 10,000 (≈ CA$157). Canadian accounts are billed in CAD. The most criticized policy: every stored contact counts toward your bill, including unsubscribed ones.

Brevo prices by email volume, not contacts: contacts are unlimited (up to 100,000 on free). The free plan allows 300 emails per day; Starter starts at US$9/month for 5,000 emails a month (billed in USD, ≈ CA$13 at current rates), Standard at US$18/month (≈ CA$26). Automation is gated to Standard, and Brevo branding is forced on free-plan emails — logo removal is a separate US$10.80/month add-on (≈ CA$15).

Kit (formerly ConvertKit) is built for creators — newsletter writers, course sellers, podcasters. Its free Newsletter plan is the most generous of the four: up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited sends. Creator, at US$39/month for 1,000 subscribers (billed in USD: ≈ CA$56 at current rates, or ≈ CA$47 on annual billing at US$33), adds visual automations and sequences; Creator Pro is US$79/month (≈ CA$113).

Cyberimpact is the Canadian option, built around CASL compliance: consent tracking, automated unsubscribe management, and the required legal footer are part of the workflow. Contact data is stored on Canadian servers, the interface and support are bilingual (English and French), and billing is in CAD. Free covers up to 250 contacts with unlimited mailings and unlimited users; paid tiers start at C$22.04/month (Basic, up to 500 contacts), Plus at C$38.59/month, Pro at C$49.61/month.

The free tier that decides small budgets

Free plans look similar until you read the limits. Mailchimp: 250 contacts, 500 sends a month, no scheduling or A/B testing — and new accounts start on a US$20/month Standard trial (≈ CA$29) that auto-charges unless you downgrade to free within five days. Brevo: unlimited contacts but 300 emails a day, so a 20,000-subscriber list can't be reached in one send. Kit: 10,000 subscribers and unlimited sends but only one visual automation, no sequences — real automation needs Creator. Cyberimpact: just 250 contacts, but unlimited mailings, unlimited users, no daily send wall.

The moment you need scheduled sends, A/B testing, or automations, you're on a paid plan.

CASL: what a Canadian sender has to get right

CASL is the federal law governing commercial electronic messages sent to Canadians. The CRTC requires three things: consent, sender identification, and an unsubscribe mechanism. General information, not legal advice.

  • Consent records. Express opt-in lasts until withdrawn; implied consent from a purchase covers two years, an inquiry six months. You must be able to prove consent, so the tool should record how and when each address was obtained.
  • Identification. Every message must identify your business — check the platform's footer templates make this easy.
  • Unsubscribe that works. Requests must be honoured within 10 business days. One-click, no login.
  • Data residency and French support. Quebec customers make French templates and support matter; Canadian servers simplify the privacy conversation under Quebec's Law 25. A Canadian-built platform starts ahead here.

Penalties reach C$10 million per violation for businesses and C$1 million for individuals, enforced by the CRTC, the Competition Bureau, and the Office of the Privacy Commissioner.

Which one should you pick?

  • Creator business, growing audience, minimal budget: Kit. The free plan's 10,000-subscriber cap carries most early-stage lists; move to Creator when you need automations.
  • Big list, low send frequency, tight budget: Brevo. Unlimited contacts with volume-based pricing keeps large-but-quiet lists cheap — respect the 300/day wall on free.
  • Canadian business wanting compliance by default: Cyberimpact. CAD billing, bilingual support, Canadian servers, built-in consent tooling — reasonably priced for small lists.
  • Rich promotional emails, biggest template library: Mailchimp. Accept per-contact billing (unsubscribed contacts included) and USD list prices (billed in CAD for Canadian accounts), and price your actual list size before committing.

FAQ

Is there a free email marketing tool that works well for a Canadian small business?

Yes, with trade-offs. Kit's free plan is the most generous overall (10,000 subscribers, unlimited sends) but lacks automation. Cyberimpact's free plan is small (250 contacts) but includes unlimited mailings and built-in CASL tooling. Brevo's free plan suits larger lists that send infrequently, within the 300-emails-per-day limit.

Does Mailchimp charge for contacts who unsubscribed?

Yes — billing counts all stored contacts, including unsubscribed ones, toward your contact tier. Archiving dead contacts is the only way to keep the bill aligned with your real audience.

Do I need consent before emailing customers in Canada?

Yes. CASL requires consent — express opt-in that lasts until withdrawn, or implied consent in defined cases such as a purchase (two years) or an inquiry (six months). Every message must identify your business and include a working unsubscribe mechanism.
