FreshBooks vs Wave vs Xero Pricing: Canadian Service-Business TCO

Published 2026-10-12

Three accounting tools dominate the Canadian small-business shortlist — and two of them are Canadian companies. But comparing plan prices alone misleads: the real cost is plan price plus payment-processing fees on the invoices you collect plus the seat and client-count rules. This guide computes the total cost of ownership for a 4-person Canadian service business.

The two hometown vendors: why Canada matters here

FreshBooks was built in Toronto in 2003 for service businesses that bill by time or project — and the product still reflects it: time tracking connects straight to invoicing, which no other Canadian platform does as cleanly, and pricing follows your active client count (Gestisoft). Wave is also Canadian and free at the core — a genuine $0 starting point with unlimited invoices.

The Canadian practicality: both understand GST/HST natively, and FreshBooks offers phone support where Xero handles Canadian queries by ticket. But being local doesn't erase the trade-offs — Wave has no inventory at all, and FreshBooks offers neither a payroll nor an inventory module, which rules both out the moment you carry stock or hire.

Payment fees: the line item that decides it

Here's the math the plan-price comparisons skip. If clients pay you through the app's payment processing, the processing fee applies to every dollar you invoice — and it dwarfs the subscription. FreshBooks charges 2.9% + 30¢ per credit card transaction; Wave charges 2.9% + 60¢ (3.4% + 60¢ for Amex), though Wave Pro waives the 60¢ fixed component on the first 10 card transactions each month (fee breakdown; 2026 invoicing guide).

A service business collecting US$10,000/month by card at 2.9% pays roughly US$3,500/year in processing fees — against a US$228–300/year subscription. The subscription is the rounding error; the payment rail is the bill. Pay by bank transfer instead of card and the cut drops to ~1% (ACH) — or nothing if you handle payments separately.

That reframes the "Wave is free" story: free accounting with paid payment processing is still dramatically cheaper than a US$300/year subscription with the same processing fees — the subscription saving is real, it's just small next to the rail. Compare the rails, not the stickers.

Seat and client rules: where the sticker price lies

Xero's quiet superpower is unlimited users on every plan — a 4-person team pays the same US$25/month as a solo user. FreshBooks prices by active clients, so the tier you need follows how many people you invoice: an agency with a long client list and small invoices climbs tiers fast. Wave's free core covers the team, but Gestisoft's warning is worth hearing: Wave is a starting point businesses outgrow on a schedule, and the first employee starts the clock — multi-currency is thin and multi-entity reporting has no route at all.

A 4-person service business, one year: the real bill

Assume 4 users, 40 active clients, US$8,000/month collected through in-app payments:

  • Wave: US$0 subscription + payment processing on US$96,000 ≈ ~US$2,800/yr in fees, US$0 in subscription
  • FreshBooks: ~US$19–38/mo depending on client tier (US$228–456/yr) + the same ~US$2,800 in processing
  • Xero Starter: US$25/mo = US$300/yr + the same ~US$2,800 in processing

The subscription spread across all three is under US$500/year; the processing is ~US$2,800 regardless. So choose on capability — invoicing elegance (FreshBooks), zero subscription (Wave), or accounting depth with unlimited users (Xero) — not on the sticker.

What reviewers say

A long-time user on Hacker News who ran Wave for three years after FreshBooks since its early days puts it plainly: Wave free "is huge," FreshBooks is "great at invoicing, but it pretty much falls down at everything else," and Xero is "great" — but "wasn't $30/month better than my Wave workflow." The same user warns Wave's reporting "stinks" and exports to CSV instead. Fahimai's 2026 hands-on test agrees: Wave wins on upfront cost for under-$100K service businesses, and their ratings table scores FreshBooks 4.5, Xero 4.4, and Wave 4.0.

Decision: which business buys which

Buy Wave if you're a freelancer or micro-business under ~US$100K invoicing services, with no inventory and no immediate hiring plans — free is free. Buy FreshBooks if you bill by the hour or by project and want the cleanest time-to-invoice flow in the category, and your client list fits the lower tiers. Buy Xero if you're growing: unlimited users, real inventory, multi-currency for US clients, and depth your accountant will respect. Sell products from stock? Skip all three questions and shortlist Xero or QuickBooks — neither FreshBooks nor Wave does inventory.

Frequently asked questions

Is Wave really free?

The core accounting — invoicing, expense tracking, basic reports — is free. Wave makes money on payment processing (per-transaction fees when clients pay you through Wave) and paid add-ons like payroll. "Free" means no subscription, not no cost.

Which is best for a Canadian freelancer?

For a solo service freelancer, Wave (free, Canadian) or FreshBooks (best invoicing, client-count pricing that stays cheap with few clients). Xero's depth is wasted until you grow.

Can I switch later if I outgrow Wave?

Yes — export your data and move. The common path is Wave → Xero when you hire, add inventory, or bill US clients in USD. Start free; upgrade when the business, not the marketing, says so.
