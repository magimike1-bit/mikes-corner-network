Freshdesk Pricing Decoded for Canadian Teams (2026)

Published 2026-11-01 · Last updated 2026-11-01 · By Mike, Editor

"Freshdesk costs $19 per agent" is wrong twice. The $19 is in US dollars, and it is only the first of three meters Freshworks runs: the agent licence, a per-agent AI add-on, and AI sessions bought by the pack. A Canadian team that budgets the headline number alone will underbudget its first year, this decode prices all three meters, in USD as published and in approximate Canadian dollars, checked against Freshworks' own pricing page on November 1, 2026.

Freshworks publishes no CAD price list, and its payment terms state that offline payments follow USD pricing. Canadian teams are billed in US dollars and wear the exchange rate on every renewal. The approximate CAD figures above use 1 USD = 1.3977 CAD (November 1, 2026), rounded to the nearest dollar; the approximate CAD is the product price only, and your card's conversion spread and any applicable tax are extra. There is no permanent free plan: sign-up starts a 14-day trial of the Enterprise plan, and the standing free offer is a Free Program capped at 2 agents for 6 months.

Freshdesk or Freshdesk Omni? Two ladders, one name

Freshworks sells two help desks under nearly one name. Classic Freshdesk (the ladder above) is email-first. Freshdesk Omni adds chat and messaging channels in the same inbox and costs more on every tier: Growth $29, Pro $79, Enterprise $119 per agent/month billed annually (≈ C$41, ≈ C$110, ≈ C$166). Comparison pages routinely mix the two ladders into one table, which is how a team ends up budgeting $19 for a product it priced at $29. The deciding question is channel mix: if your customers reach you by email and a portal, classic Freshdesk is the buy; the Omni premium only earns its keep when live chat and messaging carry real ticket volume.

The second and third meters: Copilot and AI sessions

Meter two is Freddy AI Copilot, the agent-assist add-on: $29 per agent/month billed annually (≈ C$41), or $35 on monthly billing, and it is sold only on Pro and Enterprise. The saving grace is assignment: Freshworks' own FAQ confirms Copilot can be purchased and assigned to any number of agents you choose, so a team can license it for the two agents who handle complex tickets and leave the rest unlicensed. Budget it per agent who will actually use it, not per headcount.

Meter three is Freddy AI Agent sessions. Every Growth, Pro and Enterprise account gets 500 free sessions, once, not per month, not per agent. After they run out, additional sessions cost $49 per pack of 100 ($0.49 a session). Know what a session is before forecasting: a unique interaction between an end-user and the AI agent, and for email the window is generous, a 72-hour window from the customer's first email, with every AI reply inside it counting as the one session. A chatty three-day email thread is one session; three separate customers are three.

Two smaller meters sit underneath. Day passes for occasional agents cost $2 a day on Growth, $7 on Pro, $12 on Enterprise (three free on every plan), the right tool for a bookkeeper who helps at month-end, instead of a full licence. And connector app tasks, the units consumed when Freshdesk syncs with outside apps, run $80 per 5,000 tasks, with MCP actions at $15 per 1,000. PlanPrice's pricing evaluations keep landing on the same lesson: standard subscription fees are rarely the surprise in a SaaS bill, the per-connection and per-integration charges are, because they scale with how wired-in the tool becomes.

Change the assumptions and watch the total move. The same team on Growth with no AI add-ons pays $1,140 a year (≈ C$1,593), the honest entry price. License Copilot for all five agents instead of two and the Pro total rises to $6,804 (≈ C$9,510). The spread between those outcomes, roughly $1,100 to $6,800 a year for the same five people, is decided entirely by meter two and meter three choices, which is why the seat price is the least useful number on the pricing page.

Traps: the same-plan rule, stale prices, and the monthly-billing penalty

Every agent sits on the same plan. One team lead who needs Pro's custom roles or multiple SLA policies moves the whole account to Pro pricing, there is no mixed-tier account. Price the plan by the most demanding agent, not the average one.

Stale prices are everywhere. Freshworks raised plan prices in January 2026. Growth went from $15 to $19 per agent/month billed annually, Pro from $49 to $55, Enterprise from $79 to $89, after raising AI session prices in November 2025. Any guide still quoting $15, $49 or $79 is describing a price that no longer exists; budget from the current ladder.

Monthly billing is a quiet tax. Month-to-month, the plans list at $23, $66 and $107 per agent, so choosing monthly over annual costs an extra $48 per agent per year on Growth, $132 on Pro and $216 on Enterprise. For a five-agent Pro team that is $660 a year for the flexibility to leave, on a product with no cancellation fees whose cancellation simply takes effect at the end of term. Pay annually unless you are genuinely piloting.

When Freshdesk is the wrong buy

Skip it, or at least price the alternatives first, in three situations. If support arrives mainly by live chat and messaging, the real comparison is Omni at $29 to $119 against chat-first tools, not classic Freshdesk at $19. If two agents handle everything, the 2-agent, 6-month Free Program and genuinely free tiers elsewhere deserve a look before any per-agent bill starts. And if nobody on the team will own the AI setup, meter three is a cost with no return, buy Growth, skip the session packs, and revisit when ticket volume justifies an agent who tends the bot. For an email-first Canadian team of three to twenty agents that will use routing and SLAs, though, Pro at $55 USD (≈ C$77) with Copilot assigned only where it earns is a defensible, budgetable bill, as long as the budget was built from all three meters.
