Google Workspace Pricing in Canada: The CAD Ladder Decoded (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Canadian buyers are used to USD price lists and exchange-rate surprises, which makes Google Workspace a pleasant exception: Google publishes a Canadian-dollar ladder. Business Starter is C$9.20 per user/month, Business Standard C$18.40, Business Plus C$28.70 (checked October 18, 2026). This decode covers what each step up actually buys — because the ladder doubles twice, and both doublings need a reason.

The tiers, as a spec sheet

TierCAD/user/moHeadline gates
Business StarterC$9.2030 GB pooled storage per user, standard support, core apps
Business StandardC$18.402 TB pooled storage, Meet recording and larger meetings, appointment booking pages
Business PlusC$28.705 TB pooled storage, enhanced security controls, eDiscovery/Vault

Storage is the marketed gate; meeting recording is the practical one. Most small teams hit "we want to record client calls" long before they fill 30 GB a head, which makes Standard — not storage math — the default working tier for client-facing firms. Plus is a compliance purchase: buy it when retention, legal hold or device-management requirements are named, not speculatively.

Against Microsoft 365

Microsoft's Canadian ladder is a near-mirror: Business Basic C$9.50, Standard C$19.00, Premium C$29.80 per user/month on annual commitment (checked October 18, 2026). The per-seat gaps are $0.30–$1.10 — small enough that the choice should be made on ecosystem (desktop Office apps and Windows management pull toward Microsoft; browser-first collaboration pulls toward Google) rather than price. The full history, including Microsoft's July 1, 2026 US price increase and what it signalled, is in our Workspace vs Microsoft 365 guide; the seat math for a 3-person firm is in the consultancy teardown.

Budget notes

CAD billing removes the FX line that USD tools carry — at ten seats, Standard is a flat C$184 a month with no exchange wobble, which simplifies the annual software budget more than the modest per-seat difference suggests. Taxes: GST/HST applies on top per your province and is recoverable for registrants (HST provinces) — see the province pages.

Five-seat and fifteen-seat math

Five seats on Standard: C$92/month, C$1,104 a year — the tier most client-facing small firms land on because of Meet recording. Fifteen seats: C$276/month. The Plus jump at fifteen seats costs an extra C$154.50 a month over Standard; the question to answer before paying it is whether Vault/eDiscovery or the enhanced security controls map to a named requirement (a client contract, an insurer's questionnaire, a regulator). "More secure" is not a requirement; "we must retain and produce email for matters under legal hold" is. Microsoft's Premium tier asks the identical question at a near-identical price, which is why this comparison is best made once, at the ecosystem level, and then left alone for years.

The annual-commitment footnote

Google's published per-user prices assume annual plans in most presentations; flexible monthly plans price higher per seat. For a firm with seasonal or contract staff, the mix matters: core staff on annual seats, short-term collaborators on flexible seats (or as guests where the product allows) — a blend that routinely saves 10–15% against putting everyone on the flexible plan for convenience. Read your renewal date the way you read a lease: seat reductions happen at term boundaries, not mid-year, and licence ghosts accumulate exactly the way the Microsoft decode describes.

Frequently asked questions

Are these prices per user or per business? Per user per month — every seat with its own mailbox and storage allocation counts. Shared inboxes and aliases have their own rules inside the admin console.

Does Google charge GST/HST on top? Tax applies per your province and registration details on the Google invoice; registered businesses recover GST/HST as ITCs in HST/GST provinces. See our province pages.

Can I mix tiers across my team? Google has historically limited mixing within the Business family in some configurations — check the current admin rules before designing a mixed-tier rollout; Microsoft allows mixing more freely.
