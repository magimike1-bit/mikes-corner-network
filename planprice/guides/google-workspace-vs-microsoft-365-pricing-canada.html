Google Workspace vs Microsoft 365 Pricing in Canada: The CAD Math

Published 2026-10-18 · Last updated 2026-11-15 · By Mike, Editor

Most comparisons of Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 are written in US dollars, where the two lineups now match almost exactly, and they stop there. Canadian buyers do not pay the US list. Priced from the vendors' Canadian lists, the suites are close at entry but not above it, Microsoft's prices moved on July 1, 2026 and its published Canadian lineup changed again by November 2026, in ways that invalidate older comparisons, and the decision is really made by three things the dollar-for-dollar tables miss: the AI now bundled into both mid tiers at very different prices, an entry tier where the storage allowances differ by a factor of thirty, and Canadian data residency. This guide works the Canadian math for small and mid-sized teams. All prices are Canadian dollars on annual billing, checked on 2026-11-15.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change, always confirm the current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

The Canadian price lists, side by side

Google's Canadian pricing page lists Business Starter at CA$9.20 per user per month on a one-year commitment, Business Standard at CA$18.40 and Business Plus at CA$28.70, with higher flexible monthly rates for teams that will not commit. Microsoft Canada lists Business Basic at CA$9.50, Business Standard with Copilot at CA$31.90 and Business Premium with Copilot at CA$43.40 per user per month paid yearly, plus tax. The without-Copilot Standard and Premium prices Microsoft listed in October (CA$19.00 and CA$29.80) no longer appear on its public pricing pages. Google is therefore the cheaper published list at every tier in Canada, by CA$0.30 at entry and by CA$13.50 and CA$14.70 at the mid and top tiers, where Microsoft's price now includes Copilot. The US lists (US$7, US$14 and US$22 on both sides) suggest a dead heat that the published Canadian lists no longer offer. Both families cap their business plans at 300 users; above that, both vendors move buyers to quote-based enterprise tiers.

The date on these numbers matters more than usual. Microsoft updated its commercial suite pricing globally on July 1, 2026, applying to new and renewing customers, so any comparison written earlier in 2026, including several still ranking for this query, is quoting a Microsoft price list that no longer exists. Google's structure carries a different caution: its headline rates assume the annual commitment, and its promotional introductory pricing for new customers expires, so a first-year invoice is not the steady-state cost.

Storage: the entry tier is not the same product

The largest hidden difference sits at the cheapest tier. Google's Business Starter includes 30 GB of pooled storage per user, shared across the organisation, so a ten-person team pools 300 GB in total. Microsoft 365 Business Basic includes 1 TB of cloud storage per user. For a team whose work is email and light documents, 30 GB pooled per person is workable; for a team sharing photo, video or design files, the Google entry tier is exhausted quickly and the real comparison becomes Business Standard, where Google's allowance jumps to 2 TB pooled per user, ahead of Microsoft's still-flat 1 TB, against Business Standard. Business Plus raises Google to 5 TB pooled per user. Storage, not the CA$0.30 headline gap, is what should pick the entry-tier winner, and it cuts in opposite directions at different tiers: Microsoft at entry, Google from the mid tier up.

The app model differs the same way. At the mid tier, Microsoft's CA$31.90 buys the installable desktop Office applications alongside the web versions, with Copilot included; Google's suite is browser-based at every tier, with full Gemini assistance from Business Standard up, in Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Meet and Chat. A team anchored to desktop Excel or Outlook is buying something Google does not sell at any price; a team that lives in the browser is paying Microsoft for installers it will never run.

AI: bundled on both sides, priced very differently

The widest real price gap in this comparison is the AI bundle. Gemini is included in Google Workspace Business Standard and above at the standard list price. On the Microsoft side, Copilot is now bundled into the published tiers themselves: Microsoft Canada lists Business Standard with Copilot at CA$31.90 and Business Premium with Copilot at CA$43.40 per user per month, and no longer publishes a Standard or Premium price without it. The AI-for-AI comparison at the mid tier is therefore the list comparison itself, CA$18.40 against CA$31.90, a CA$13.50-per-user monthly difference, or CA$1,620 a year at ten seats. That does not settle which AI is better; it settles that the suites are not the same price once AI is counted, and that a Microsoft buyer who wants Copilot for five power users rather than the whole team should price selective licensing through a reseller, because the published tiers bundle it for every seat.

Data residency in Canada

For teams handling regulated or contract-sensitive data, one non-price fact can end the comparison. For a tenant provisioned in Canada, Microsoft 365 keeps Exchange Online, SharePoint, OneDrive and Teams data at rest in Canada, in its Toronto and Quebec City regions. Google Workspace offers data regions of the US, the EU or no preference, Canada is not a selectable region. A Canadian firm whose clients, insurer or provincial privacy obligations (Quebec's Law 25 is the usual trigger) ask where data rests will find one straightforward answer and one workaround. This is not a pricing argument and neither vendor's list price reflects it; it belongs in the buying decision precisely because no feature grid surfaces it.

The top tiers answer different security questions, and should not be treated as equivalents either. Microsoft's Business Premium bundles Intune device management and Defender for Business threat protection into the CA$43.40, tools a Google shop would assemble separately, while Google's Business Plus answers with Vault retention and eDiscovery, Secure LDAP and advanced endpoint management. A security-parity comparison by a Canadian MSSP puts Microsoft ahead on bundled cost at 50 seats once equivalent endpoint and device controls are added to the Google side; a retention-and-discovery-led comparison runs the other way. Price the controls the business actually needs, on the side that bundles them.

Year-one math at 10 and 25 seats

At Canadian list on annual billing, a 10-person team pays CA$1,104 a year on Google Business Starter against CA$1,140 on Microsoft Business Basic; at the mid tier, CA$2,208 against CA$3,828; at the top SMB tier, CA$3,444 against CA$5,208. At 25 seats the entry gap is CA$90 a year, the mid-tier gap CA$4,050 and the top-tier gap CA$4,410. The pattern: the entry tiers are within a few percent, and above entry the decision is made by the Copilot bundle, storage shape, desktop apps and residency, in whichever order a given business ranks them.

The decision framework

This is a buying-decision guide, not a setup procedure, the framework below is the process.

  1. Price in CAD, dated after July 1, 2026. Discard any comparison quoting pre-July Microsoft pricing, the without-Copilot prices Microsoft stopped publishing in late 2026, or US lists; the Canadian gap is small at entry and wide above it.
  2. Let storage pick the entry tier. Heavy file sharing rules out Google's 30 GB pooled Starter; light email-and-docs teams should not pay for Microsoft's 1 TB they will not use.
  3. Decide the AI question explicitly. Gemini is in the Google mid-tier price; Copilot is bundled into Microsoft's published mid tier, which sits CA$13.50 per user above Google's. If only some seats need Copilot, price selective licensing through a reseller instead of the published bundle.
  4. Check residency obligations before features. If Canadian data-at-rest is a client, insurer or Law 25 requirement, Microsoft's Canadian regions answer it directly and Google does not offer a Canada region.
  5. Match the top tier to the controls actually needed. Intune plus Defender (Microsoft Premium) and Vault plus eDiscovery (Google Plus) solve different problems, buy the bundle that matches the audit, not the longer feature list.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Workspace cheaper than Microsoft 365 in Canada?

At entry, yes, slightly: CA$9.20 vs CA$9.50, checked 2026-11-15. At the mid and top tiers the published gap is wide, CA$18.40 vs CA$31.90 and CA$28.70 vs CA$43.40, because Microsoft's published Standard and Premium now include Copilot while Google's tiers include Gemini. Once equivalent device management and endpoint security are added to the Google side, a security-parity comparison can favour Microsoft at the top tier.

Why do most comparisons say the two cost the same?

They compare US list prices, which are aligned at US$7, US$14 and US$22. The Canadian lists are set separately, Microsoft's Canadian prices changed on July 1, 2026, and its published lineup changed again by November 2026, so older comparisons are stale on three counts.

Does either suite keep data in Canada?

Microsoft 365 keeps core data at rest in Canada (Toronto and Quebec City) for Canada-provisioned tenants. Google Workspace data regions are the US, the EU or no preference; Canada is not selectable. Teams with residency obligations should verify the current region options with each vendor before signing.

Worked Example: Ten People On Business Standard

Google Workspace Business Standard lists at CAD 18.40 per user per month on annual billing in Canada (checked 2026-11-15). Ten people cost CAD 184 per month, CAD 2,208 per year. Microsoft 365 Business Standard is now published only with Copilot, at CAD 31.90 per user per month paid yearly (checked 2026-11-15): CAD 319 per month, CAD 3,828 per year. The gap is CAD 1,620 a year, and most of it is the AI bundle, Copilot on every Microsoft seat against Gemini included on Google's. The entry tiers tell a different story: Workspace Starter at CAD 9.20 against Microsoft 365 Business Basic at CAD 9.50 (both checked 2026-11-15), a CAD 36 a year gap at ten seats where price really does not decide it. One caution: comparisons written before November 2026 quote Microsoft's without-Copilot Standard at CAD 19.00; that price is no longer published on Microsoft's Canadian pages.
