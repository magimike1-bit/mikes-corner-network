Google Workspace vs Microsoft 365 Pricing in Canada: The CAD Math

Published 2026-10-18 · Last updated 2026-10-18

Most comparisons of Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 are written in US dollars, where the two lineups now match almost exactly — and they stop there. Canadian buyers do not pay the US list. Priced from the vendors' Canadian lists, the suites are close but not tied, Microsoft's prices moved on July 1, 2026 in a way that invalidates older comparisons, and the decision is really made by three things the dollar-for-dollar tables miss: AI that is included on one side and upsold on the other, an entry tier where the storage allowances differ by a factor of thirty, and Canadian data residency. This guide works the Canadian math for small and mid-sized teams. All prices are Canadian dollars on annual billing, checked on 2026-10-18.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm the current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

The Canadian price lists, side by side

Google's Canadian pricing page lists Business Starter at CA$9.20 per user per month on a one-year commitment, Business Standard at CA$18.40 and Business Plus at CA$28.70, with higher flexible monthly rates for teams that will not commit. Microsoft Canada lists Business Basic at CA$9.50, Business Standard at CA$19.00 and Business Premium at CA$29.80 per user per month on an annual subscription, plus tax. Google is therefore the cheaper list at every tier in Canada — by CA$0.30, CA$0.60 and CA$1.10 per user per month — even though the US lists (US$7, US$14 and US$22 on both sides) suggest a dead heat. Both families cap their business plans at 300 users; above that, both vendors move buyers to quote-based enterprise tiers.

The date on these numbers matters more than usual. Microsoft updated its commercial suite pricing globally on July 1, 2026, applying to new and renewing customers, so any comparison written earlier in 2026 — including several still ranking for this query — is quoting a Microsoft price list that no longer exists. Google's structure carries a different caution: its headline rates assume the annual commitment, and its promotional introductory pricing for new customers expires, so a first-year invoice is not the steady-state cost.

Storage: the entry tier is not the same product

The largest hidden difference sits at the cheapest tier. Google's Business Starter includes 30 GB of pooled storage per user — shared across the organisation, so a ten-person team pools 300 GB in total. Microsoft 365 Business Basic includes 1 TB of cloud storage per user. For a team whose work is email and light documents, 30 GB pooled per person is workable; for a team sharing photo, video or design files, the Google entry tier is exhausted quickly and the real comparison becomes Business Standard — where Google's allowance jumps to 2 TB pooled per user, ahead of Microsoft's still-flat 1 TB — against Business Standard. Business Plus raises Google to 5 TB pooled per user. Storage, not the CA$0.30 headline gap, is what should pick the entry-tier winner, and it cuts in opposite directions at different tiers: Microsoft at entry, Google from the mid tier up.

The app model differs the same way. At the mid tier, Microsoft's CA$19.00 buys the installable desktop Office applications alongside the web versions; Google's suite is browser-based at every tier, with full Gemini assistance from Business Standard up — in Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Meet and Chat. A team anchored to desktop Excel or Outlook is buying something Google does not sell at any price; a team that lives in the browser is paying Microsoft for installers it will never run.

AI: included on one side, an upsell on the other

The widest real price gap in this comparison is not in the table's first three rows. Gemini is included in Google Workspace Business Standard and above at the standard list price. On the Microsoft side, the included AI is Copilot Chat — capable, but not grounded in the team's own work content. The work-grounded Copilot that operates inside Word, Excel, Outlook and Teams is sold as an add-on or through with-Copilot bundles: Microsoft Canada lists Business Standard with Copilot at CA$31.90 and Business Premium with Copilot at CA$43.40 per user per month. The honest AI-for-AI comparison at the mid tier is therefore CA$18.40 against CA$31.90 — a CA$13.50-per-user monthly difference, or CA$1,620 a year at ten seats. That does not settle which AI is better; it settles that the suites are not the same price once AI is counted, and that a Microsoft buyer who wants Copilot for five power users rather than the whole team will usually spend less by licensing it selectively than by moving every seat to a bundled tier.

Data residency in Canada

For teams handling regulated or contract-sensitive data, one non-price fact can end the comparison. For a tenant provisioned in Canada, Microsoft 365 keeps Exchange Online, SharePoint, OneDrive and Teams data at rest in Canada, in its Toronto and Quebec City regions. Google Workspace offers data regions of the US, the EU or no preference — Canada is not a selectable region. A Canadian firm whose clients, insurer or provincial privacy obligations (Quebec's Law 25 is the usual trigger) ask where data rests will find one straightforward answer and one workaround. This is not a pricing argument and neither vendor's list price reflects it; it belongs in the buying decision precisely because no feature grid surfaces it.

The top tiers answer different security questions, and should not be treated as equivalents either. Microsoft's Business Premium bundles Intune device management and Defender for Business threat protection into the CA$29.80 — tools a Google shop would assemble separately — while Google's Business Plus answers with Vault retention and eDiscovery, Secure LDAP and advanced endpoint management. A security-parity comparison by a Canadian MSSP puts Microsoft ahead on bundled cost at 50 seats once equivalent endpoint and device controls are added to the Google side; a retention-and-discovery-led comparison runs the other way. Price the controls the business actually needs, on the side that bundles them.

Year-one math at 10 and 25 seats

At Canadian list on annual billing, a 10-person team pays CA$1,104 a year on Google Business Starter against CA$1,140 on Microsoft Business Basic; at the mid tier, CA$2,208 against CA$2,280; at the top SMB tier, CA$3,444 against CA$3,576. Add work-grounded AI for all ten seats and the mid-tier comparison becomes CA$2,208 against CA$3,828 for Business Standard with Copilot. At 25 seats the base gaps widen modestly — the top-tier difference reaches CA$330 a year — while the AI difference reaches CA$4,050. The pattern holds at every size: the suites are within a few percent on base price, and the decision is made by storage shape, desktop apps, residency and AI licensing, in whichever order a given business ranks them.

The decision framework

This is a buying-decision guide, not a setup procedure — the framework below is the process.

  1. Price in CAD, dated after July 1, 2026. Discard any comparison quoting pre-July Microsoft pricing or US lists; the Canadian gap is small but real.
  2. Let storage pick the entry tier. Heavy file sharing rules out Google's 30 GB pooled Starter; light email-and-docs teams should not pay for Microsoft's 1 TB they will not use.
  3. Decide the AI question explicitly. Gemini is in the Google mid-tier price; Copilot is a CA$12.90–13.50 per-user step on the Microsoft side. Licence Copilot selectively if only some seats need it.
  4. Check residency obligations before features. If Canadian data-at-rest is a client, insurer or Law 25 requirement, Microsoft's Canadian regions answer it directly and Google does not offer a Canada region.
  5. Match the top tier to the controls actually needed. Intune plus Defender (Microsoft Premium) and Vault plus eDiscovery (Google Plus) solve different problems — buy the bundle that matches the audit, not the longer feature list.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Workspace cheaper than Microsoft 365 in Canada?

On base list price, yes, slightly: CA$9.20 vs CA$9.50 at entry, CA$18.40 vs CA$19.00 at the mid tier and CA$28.70 vs CA$29.80 at the top SMB tier, checked 2026-10-18. Once work-grounded AI is added for every seat, the gap widens in Google's favour because Gemini is included from Business Standard while Copilot is a paid step. Once equivalent device management and endpoint security are added to the Google side, a security-parity comparison can favour Microsoft.

Why do most comparisons say the two cost the same?

They compare US list prices, which are aligned at US$7, US$14 and US$22. The Canadian lists are set separately and are not aligned — and Microsoft's Canadian prices changed on July 1, 2026, so older comparisons are stale on two counts.

Does either suite keep data in Canada?

Microsoft 365 keeps core data at rest in Canada (Toronto and Quebec City) for Canada-provisioned tenants. Google Workspace data regions are the US, the EU or no preference; Canada is not selectable. Teams with residency obligations should verify the current region options with each vendor before signing.
