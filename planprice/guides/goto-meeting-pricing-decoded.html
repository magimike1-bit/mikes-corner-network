GoTo Meeting Pricing Decoded for Canadian Small Businesses

Published 2026-10-24 · Last updated 2026-10-24

Search for GoTo Meeting pricing and you will find $12, $14, $16 and $19 per organizer per month — all of them real, none of them the whole story. Unlike rivals that sell a ladder of tiers, GoTo's official pricing page sells exactly two plans, and the four numbers in the trackers are just those two plans read at two different billing terms. This guide decodes the structure from GoTo's own pricing page (checked October 24, 2026), separates the annual price from the monthly one, and works the number a Canadian small business actually pays — which, because GoTo licenses organizers rather than attendees, is usually smaller than the headcount suggests. All prices are USD as published; GoTo publishes no Canadian-dollar price list on the page checked, so budget exchange on top of every figure below. General information only, not financial advice.

The plans, as GoTo publishes them

Professional costs $12 per organizer per month, billed annually at $144 per organizer per year: meetings with up to 150 participants, priced per user for unlimited video and audio meetings with no caps on meeting time, screen sharing or chat functions. Business costs $16 per organizer per month, billed annually at $192: the participant cap rises to 250, and the tier adds meeting lock, transcripts and — in the pricing page's own words — free unlimited recording and transcripts for Business accounts. There is no permanent free plan; what GoTo offers instead is a free trial with access to its top features, a notification before the trial expires, and no payment information required to start it. Larger events are not a third Meeting tier at all: GoTo's own FAQ answers the question of hosting more than 250 participants by pointing at GoTo Webinar, which covers 500, 1,000 or even 3,000 participants. Volume and enterprise pricing are handled by contacting sales.

Why the trackers disagree: $12 vs $14 is a billing term, not a price war

The conflicting numbers reconcile cleanly once the billing term is attached. Review trackers that headline $14 for Professional and $19 for Business — Software Finder's current summary is one — are quoting the month-to-month price. Trackers quoting $12 and $16, including G2's pricing data (which G2 states is provided by GoTo Meeting itself), are quoting the annual-billing price from GoTo's page. GetVoIP's pricing breakdown lists both terms side by side and confirms the split exactly: $14 and $19 billed monthly, $12 and $16 billed annually. The practical consequence: monthly billing costs roughly 17–19% more per organizer than the annual price for the identical plan, so a firm that stays month-to-month out of caution is paying a real premium for the flexibility — worth it for a trial month, hard to justify in month thirteen. The second tracker artifact is the phantom Enterprise tier: several comparison sites still list a GoTo Meeting Enterprise plan at custom pricing for up to 3,000 participants. That shape is out of date against GoTo's current page, which sells two plans and routes the 500-to-3,000-participant need to GoTo Webinar instead. If a quote or a comparison table shows you a Meeting Enterprise tier, check which product is actually being priced before comparing it against anything.

The per-organizer rule: the line that decides the bill

GoTo licenses the organizer, not the audience. The organizer is the person who hosts the meeting; participants do not even need a GoTo Meeting account to join, which is why there is no extra charge for them. For a small business this is the single most valuable line in the pricing structure: licences track the handful of people who schedule and run meetings, not the whole payroll. A twelve-person firm where five people host client calls needs five paid seats, not twelve. Add-ons are priced per organizer too, and they are worth seeing before assuming dial-in is cheap: GetVoIP's breakdown lists toll-free numbers and the Call Me feature at an additional $5 per month per user, and GoTo Webinar Lite — the webinar add-on for Meeting accounts — at $20 per month. The contrast with Google Meet matters here: Meet is not sold standalone — it rides inside Google Workspace, where every user pays (Business Starter at CA$9.20 per user per month on annual commitment, per Google's Canadian page) whether or not they ever host, though in exchange the seat also carries email, storage and the rest of the suite. Neither model is wrong; they meter different things, and which one is cheaper depends entirely on your host-to-staff ratio.

The feature gates that actually matter

With only two plans, the upgrade question is unusually clean: what does the extra $4 per organizer per month buy? On GoTo's own page, Business is the tier where recording and transcripts live — free unlimited recording and transcripts are included for Business accounts, alongside meeting lock. G2's vendor-supplied pricing data fills in the rest of the Business list: unlimited cloud storage, the smart assistant, meeting diagnostic reports, and keyboard and mouse sharing. Review coverage of the two tiers (Tech.co's plan breakdown) adds the operational items: unlimited cloud recording, auto-transcriptions and the ability to include co-organizers are the features named as the big Professional-to-Business differences. Read together, the gate is really about records: a firm whose client calls must be recorded, transcribed and kept — advisers, recruiters, anyone whose meetings become documents — is a Business firm at $192 per organizer per year, and the Professional tier's $48 annual saving per organizer is not available to it no matter how small the team. A firm that hosts live conversations and keeps its records elsewhere can honestly run on Professional.

Read the table with its caveats attached. The Google row buys a whole office suite in Canadian dollars, so it is the right comparison only for a firm that would otherwise buy Workspace anyway — for that firm, Meet's marginal cost is effectively zero and GoTo has to justify itself on features. For a firm buying meetings alone, GoTo Professional undercuts Zoom Pro per host ($12 against $14.16 on annual billing), and Business undercuts Zoom Business ($16 against $18.33) — GoTo is, on published annual prices, the cheaper paid meeting tool of the two, which is the opposite of the premium reputation its tracker coverage sometimes gives it. Webex sits in the same band (Starter at $144 per user per year, Business at $204, checked October 23, 2026), so the realistic shortlist for a meetings-only purchase is a three-way comparison in a $144–$204 per-host band, decided by the recording gate and the participant cap, not by headline price. And note what is missing at the free end: Zoom and Webex both publish a free tier and GoTo does not — the 14-day trial is the only $0 route, so a firm that wants a permanent free fallback for occasional meetings cannot get it here.

Who should buy GoTo Meeting, and who should not

Buy GoTo Meeting if your hosts are few and your meetings are long, if recording and transcripts are deliverables (price Business from the start), or if you want the cheapest published annual per-host price among the major meeting tools and do not need the wider suite around it. Skip it if your team already pays for Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 (a meeting tool is already in the building), if you need a permanent free tier for occasional use, or if your events run past 250 participants — that need belongs to GoTo Webinar's pricing, not this page's. And whichever way the decision goes, take the annual price only after the trial and a month on monthly terms if needed — the $180-a-year gap on five Business seats is the price of committing early to a tool your team has not lived in yet.

Frequently asked questions

How much does GoTo Meeting cost in Canada? GoTo publishes prices in USD: Professional is $144 per organizer per year ($12/month billed annually, or $14 billed monthly), and Business is $192 per year ($16/month billed annually, or $19 billed monthly). Canadian buyers pay those USD figures plus exchange; there is no CAD price list on the pricing page. Do attendees need licences? No — GoTo licenses organizers. Participants join without a GoTo Meeting account and at no extra charge, up to the host plan's cap (150 on Professional, 250 on Business). Is there a free GoTo Meeting plan? No permanent free plan. GoTo offers a free trial with access to its top features and no payment information required. What does Business add over Professional? A 250-participant cap (up from 150), meeting lock, and the recording package: free unlimited recording and transcripts, with unlimited cloud storage, the smart assistant, meeting diagnostic reports and co-organizers in the vendor's fuller feature list.
