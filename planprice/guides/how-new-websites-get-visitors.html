How New Websites Actually Get Visitors (and the Cheapest Ways to Get More)

Published 2026-10-12 · Last updated 2026-10-12

Nobody visits a new website out of loyalty — nobody knows it exists. Every first visit is an interception: someone had a question or a job to do, and a surface — a search result, a pin, a forum thread, a shared link — put your page in front of them at that moment. Understanding that one fact tells you where to spend effort, and the recent data tells you how the ground is shifting underneath it.

Most searches are questions, not shopping

The classic log study of web search behaviour (Jansen, Booth & Spink, analysing over 1.5 million queries) classified more than 80% of queries as informational — someone looking to learn or solve something — with roughly 10% navigational (looking for a specific site) and 10% transactional. A 2022 replication on a different query set found a similar split. The implication for a new site is structural: you cannot compete for navigational traffic, because people can't search for a name they've never heard. You compete for the informational slice — the questions your customers actually ask — and that is where almost all first visits come from (Search Engine Land's summary of the intent research).

The uncomfortable part: search is shrinking, and small sites feel it most

Three measured findings frame everything else in this guide:

  • Small publishers lost about 60% of their search referral traffic over two years (sites with 1,000–10,000 daily pageviews), versus 47% for mid-sized publishers and 22% for large ones, according to Chartbeat data reported in March 2026. ChatGPT referrals grew over 200% in the same period but still account for under 1% of publisher pageviews (Search Engine Journal).
  • About 68% of US Google searches now end with no click at all (SparkToro/Similarweb clickstream data, January–April 2026), up from 60.45% in 2024 and 49% in 2019 (SparkToro).
  • AI summaries roughly halve the click-through that remains. Pew Research tracked about 69,000 real searches and found a traditional result was clicked in 8% of searches showing an AI summary versus 15% without one — and links inside the summaries themselves were clicked in only 1% of visits (Search Engine Land on the Pew study).

None of this makes search optional — for a new site with no audience, organic search is still typically 60–90% of what little traffic exists, simply because there is no direct, email, or social base yet. It means the returns now go to narrower, more specific, harder-to-summarise content. Ahrefs' data is a useful reality check on timelines: only 1.74% of newly published pages reach Google's top 10 within a year, and top-10 pages are typically years old (Ahrefs). Expect 3–6 months to first movement.

Two refinements matter. First, get cited by AI answers: the one rigorous study of generative-engine optimisation (Princeton/Georgia Tech, presented at KDD 2024, ~10,000 queries) found that adding quotations, statistics, and source citations lifted a source's visibility in AI-generated answers by up to about 40%, with lower-ranked sources gaining most — while keyword stuffing hurt (summary of the study). Treat AI referrals as brand visibility, not a traffic channel: they're under 1% of pageviews today. Second, don't neglect Bing: it powers Copilot and other AI surfaces, competition is thinner, and inclusion is free via Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow. No solid study quantifies Bing's payoff for small sites — the case rests on the near-zero cost.

What to skip

Facebook Page link posts reach about 1.65% of a Page's followers organically on average in 2025 benchmarks, with link posts favoured least — a channel in name only. Product Hunt launches without an existing audience to mobilise repeatedly report near-zero results in founders' own post-mortems (one launch day produced about 6 real visitors after bot filtering); the durable gain came later, from directory listings. And buying cheap traffic to flip into advertising revenue fails its own arithmetic — we work the numbers in the arbitrage guide.

The practical order of operations

  1. Publish narrow, genuinely complete answers to the specific questions your customers ask — with original numbers, tables, and cited sources where you can.
  2. Submit to Bing Webmaster Tools and enable IndexNow; keep Google Search Console as your measurement baseline.
  3. Put an email capture offer on every page from day one, so the visitors search does send become an audience you own (the next guide covers this).
  4. Add one free tool or calculator matched to your niche's most-searched task.
  5. If your niche is visual, automate Pinterest with templates.
