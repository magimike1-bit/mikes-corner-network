HubSpot Service Hub Pricing Tiers: Starter vs Professional vs Enterprise

Published 2026-10-12

HubSpot Service Hub's pricing page shows a clean per-seat number — about US$15, US$90, or US$150 a month depending on tier. The actual first-year invoice tells a different story: mandatory onboarding fees of US$1,500 to US$3,500, plus a second price list for AI credits that can exceed the seat cost itself. This guide does the real math — seats, onboarding, and credits — for the team sizes that actually buy it.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm the current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

The tiers: what each costs per seat

Service Hub prices per seat on annual billing, checked October 2026. Free (US$0) covers basic ticketing, live chat, and a shared inbox with HubSpot branding. Starter runs about US$15/seat/month annually (US$20 month-to-month): ticketing, shared inbox, live chat, basic bots, and two ticket pipelines. Professional is US$90/seat/month annually (US$100 monthly) and adds the features growing teams actually buy for — help-desk automation, knowledge base, customer portal, surveys, SLAs, and the Breeze customer agent. Enterprise is US$150/seat/month with custom objects, conversation intelligence, playbooks, and advanced permissions.

The 6x jump from Starter to Professional is where most evaluations begin — and where the onboarding fee lands. Professional carries a mandatory one-time US$1,500 onboarding fee; Enterprise carries US$3,500. These are non-negotiable for new customers and hit before a single agent is onboarded.

Year-one math: the invoice nobody quotes

Run the totals. A 3-agent team on Professional: 3 × US$90 × 12 = US$3,240 in seats, plus US$1,500 onboarding = US$4,740 in year one — 46% above the seat price alone. A 10-agent team on Professional: US$10,800 + US$1,500 = US$12,300. The same ten agents on Enterprise: US$18,000 + US$3,500 = US$21,500, before AI credits. In year two the onboarding fee drops off, so quote every comparison as year-one vs year-two, not as a monthly number.

Starter has no onboarding fee, which makes it the honest choice for simple ticketing — but Starter lacks workflow automation, SLA management, the knowledge base, and the Breeze customer agent. The features most growing teams need live one tier up, which is exactly how HubSpot designed the ladder.

Breeze credits: the second price list

HubSpot's AI runs on HubSpot Credits, a consumption pool on top of seats. The Breeze Customer Agent costs about US$0.50 per resolved conversation (overage around US$0.45), with modest included allowances — roughly 3,000 credits on Professional (about 60 resolved conversations a month) and 5,000 on Enterprise (about 100). Capacity packs cost US$10 per 1,000 credits a month.

Do the deflection math before you budget: a team deflecting 5,000 conversations a month through the Breeze agent would pay about US$2,500 a month in resolution fees alone — more than the US$1,350 a month a 15-agent Professional team pays in seats. AI support can cost more than the humans it assists. Model your conversation volume against the credit allowance before committing, because this line item is the hardest to forecast and the easiest to blow past.

What reviewers say

Service Hub holds 4.4 out of 5 on G2 across roughly 2,920 reviews, with ease of setup and the CRM integration the most praised themes. Hemanthkumar M., a business development analyst, gave it 4.5/5: the shared inbox, knowledge base, and automated ticketing "make it easy to deliver fast, personalized support at scale." Ravish K., a QA manager, gave 5/5 for the way it "seamlessly connects customer support with CRM, giving us a unified view of the customer journey."

The consistent caveat matches this guide's math: advanced features are costly and locked behind higher tiers, and several reviewers flag that pricing becomes hard to justify against alternatives once automation and AI credits are in play. One honest framing from a 2026 analysis: the product reviews well; the billing model is what draws complaints.

The decision framework

This is a buying-decision guide, not a setup procedure — the decision framework near the end is the whole process.

  1. Quote year one, not per month. Seats × 12 + onboarding + estimated AI credits is the only number that matters.
  2. Choose the tier by operating model, not headcount. One shared inbox and simple ticketing: Starter. Bots, surveys, automation, knowledge base: Professional — even if the team is small.
  3. Model Breeze credits against real conversation volume before buying; the included allowance covers only ~60 resolutions a month on Pro.
  4. HubSpot-stack teams get the discount that matters — the CRM context in every ticket — which standalone help desks can't replicate at any price.
  5. Renegotiate at renewal, not mid-contract. The onboarding fee is once; the leverage is at the annual renewal.

Frequently asked questions

Is HubSpot Service Hub free?

The free tools (basic ticketing, live chat, one shared inbox) are genuinely free, with HubSpot branding. Anything involving automation, SLAs, or the knowledge base requires a paid tier.

Can you skip the onboarding fee?

For new Professional and Enterprise customers, no — US$1,500 and US$3,500 respectively are mandatory and non-negotiable. Budget them into year one.

How much does the Breeze Customer Agent cost?

About US$0.50 per resolved conversation on top of seats, drawn from a monthly credit allowance (roughly 3,000 credits on Pro, 5,000 on Enterprise). Overage runs about US$0.45 per resolution; extra capacity packs are US$10 per 1,000 credits a month.

When does Starter stop being enough?

When you need workflow automation, SLA management, a knowledge base, a customer portal, or AI-assisted support — all Professional-and-up features. Most teams hit this wall by the time support becomes a real function rather than a shared inbox.
