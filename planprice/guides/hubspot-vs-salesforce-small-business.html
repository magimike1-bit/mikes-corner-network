HubSpot vs Salesforce Pricing for Small Business: The 5-Person First-Year TCO

Published 2026-10-12

HubSpot vs Salesforce comparisons usually read like enterprise buyer's guides. But if you have five people, the question isn't "which platform scales to 5,000 users" — it's "what will this cost us in year one, all-in?" This guide computes that number: licenses plus the onboarding and implementation fees that never appear in the pricing tables.

The free tiers: what's actually free and what triggers the jump

HubSpot's free CRM is genuinely usable — contacts, deals, basic email, forms, live chat. The paid jump triggers when you need real automation, more seats, or marketing contacts at volume: Starter at US$15–20/seat/month is the first step, and the invoice grows with seats and contact tiers. Salesforce has no free CRM; Starter Suite at US$25/user/month is the floor, limited to 10 users with basic marketing tools and no Flow automation. The jump to Pro Suite (US$100/user/month) is where quoting and deeper automation unlock.

5 people, 12 months: the first-year TCO

  • HubSpot Sales Hub Pro: 5 × US$90 × 12 = US$5,400 + US$1,500 onboarding = US$6,900 year one
  • Salesforce Pro Suite: 5 × US$100 × 12 = US$6,000 + US$5,000–15,000 implementation = US$11,000–21,000 year one
  • HubSpot Starter: 5 × ~US$18 × 12 ≈ US$1,080/yr — if basic automation suffices
  • Salesforce Starter Suite: 5 × US$25 × 12 = US$1,500/yr — if 10-user cap and basic tools suffice

The license gap at the mid tier is modest (US$5,400 vs US$6,000). The chasm is everything around the license: HubSpot's onboarding is a fixed US$1,500 line item; Salesforce's implementation typically runs US$5,000–15,000+ and often needs an admin's salary on top.

What reviewers say

G2 ratings checked August 2026 put both at 4.4/5 — Salesforce Sales Cloud and HubSpot Sales Hub effectively tied on satisfaction (Salesflare's comparison). The qualitative split, summarized from G2 reviews, is familiar: HubSpot praised as user-friendly and ideal for SMBs, dinged for costs that grow as you add capabilities; Salesforce praised for scaling with enterprise complexity, dinged for a steep learning curve, the need for devoted admin manpower, and — quoted verbatim — "elaborate price models; costs are well-hidden in detailed quotes."

The honest question: does a 5-person team need either?

Ask the three questions a recent honest comparison uses: is marketing automation a primary requirement? Will you have a dedicated administrator? Is your process genuinely unusual or regulated? If the answers are no, no, and no — a twelve-person sales team with a simple pipeline is better served by Pipedrive or Zoho than by either giant, and two of their seven buyer profiles recommend neither platform. HubSpot without marketing needs is an expensive contacts database; Salesforce without an admin degrades reliably. Buy the tool for the team you have, not the enterprise you might become.

Decision: which team buys which

Buy HubSpot if you're under ~100 people, want marketing and sales in one place, have no dedicated admin, and value the genuinely usable free tier as a starting point — Starter's transparent per-seat pricing keeps the bill predictable. Buy Salesforce if your sales motion is genuinely complex (CPQ, territory management, multi-currency, regulated processes) and you have — or will hire — someone to own it. And if neither description fits, price Pipedrive or Zoho before signing either: the best CRM deal is often the one you don't overbuy.

Frequently asked questions

Is HubSpot really free?

The core CRM is free and genuinely usable. Costs appear when you add paid seats (Starter US$15–20/seat/month), marketing contacts at volume, or higher-tier hubs — plus a one-time onboarding fee (US$1,500 for Sales Hub Pro) when you step up.

Why is Salesforce so expensive for small business?

The licenses are only part of it: Starter Suite is US$25/user/month, but most teams land on Pro (US$100) or Enterprise (US$175), and implementation plus admin staffing multiply the license cost several times over in year one.

Can you use HubSpot and Salesforce together?

Yes — many companies run HubSpot for marketing and top-of-funnel with Salesforce as the sales system of record, via HubSpot's native Salesforce integration. Budget for ongoing data-hygiene work: duplicates and sync conflicts are the tax on the hybrid setup.
