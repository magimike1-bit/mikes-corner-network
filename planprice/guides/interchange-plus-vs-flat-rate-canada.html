Interchange-Plus vs Flat-Rate Pricing in Canada: Which Model Wins at Your Volume

Published 2026-10-26 · Last updated 2026-10-26

$173.25 a month. That is the gap, on one stated basket, between a published Canadian interchange-plus schedule and a published Canadian flat rate at $25,000 a month in in-person card sales — $371.25 against $544.50, worked line by line below from rates checked October 26, 2026. At $5,000 a month the same gap shrinks to $34.65, an amount most owners would trade for simpler statements. The model question is therefore not which pricing is better in principle. It is at what volume, ticket size and debit share the gap stops being pocket change. General information only, not financial advice.

Two models, one set of underlying costs

Every card transaction carries the same three layers, whichever model bills it. Interchange goes to the card-issuing bank and is set by the card networks. Network fees go to Visa, Mastercard or Interac. The processor markup is the only layer your processor sets. Flat-rate pricing bundles all three into one percentage. Interchange-plus pricing passes the first two through at cost and adds the markup as a separate, visible line.

That distinction drives everything else. On flat rate, a cheap transaction and an expensive one cost you the same percentage, so the processor prices the blend high enough to cover the expensive cards. On interchange-plus, each transaction costs what it costs: a standard consumer card in person is cheap, a premium rewards card online is not, and Interac debit is priced in cents rather than percent. Canadian interchange averages illustrate the spread — standard Visa and Mastercard cards average about 1.40%, premium rewards cards about 1.90%, corporate cards about 2.30%, and Interac debit about $0.04 to $0.07 per transaction before network fees and markup.

Head to head: the published Canadian schedules

Three schedules anchor the comparison. Helcim sells interchange-plus only, with five volume tiers set on a three-month rolling average; the entry tier, for $0 to $50,000 a month, adds a margin of 0.40% + 8¢ in person and 0.50% + 25¢ online or keyed on top of interchange and network fees. Its Canadian fee disclosure combines those layers into per-card rates, which is what the worked math uses: Visa Consumer at 1.75% + 8¢ in person, Mastercard Core at 1.42% + 9¢, Interac tap at 12.5¢ flat. Square sells flat rate in Canada at 2.5% for in-person credit with no per-transaction fee, 2.8% + 30¢ online, and Interac debit priced separately at 0.75% + 7¢. Moneris publishes both models side by side: a flat rate of 2.65% + $0.10 in person with Interac at $0.12 per transaction, and a Simplified Pricing plan that is its interchange-plus model, priced by quote rather than from a published markup table. Online, Stripe's flat 2.9% + CA$0.30 is the reference rate most Canadian web sellers know.

Read the Helcim row carefully. Those combined percentages already contain interchange and network fees for those specific card types; a premium card costs more on the same schedule — Visa Infinite is 2.07% + 8¢ in person on Tier 1 — because its interchange is higher. That variability is the trade: the bill moves with your customers' cards, in both directions.

At $25,000, the arithmetic runs like this. Visa sales of $12,500 at 1.75% cost $218.75, plus 250 transactions at 8¢ ($20). Mastercard sales of $7,500 at 1.42% cost $106.50, plus 150 transactions at 9¢ ($13.50). One hundred Interac taps at 12.5¢ cost $12.50. Total: $371.25, an effective rate of about 1.49%. Square prices the same basket at $500 for the $20,000 of credit and $44.50 for the debit — $37.50 in percentage plus $7 in per-transaction fees — for $544.50 all in. Annualised, the $25,000 gap is $2,079; the $50,000 gap is $4,158.

Two sensitivities matter more than the headline. Shrink the average ticket and the per-transaction cents on interchange-plus bite harder, while Square's in-person credit rate carries no per-transaction fee at all — a $10-ticket seller gives back a visible slice of the percentage saving in 8¢ and 9¢ charges. Shift the mix toward premium rewards cards and the interchange-plus total rises toward the flat rate, because the pass-through is honest about what those cards cost. Published Canadian break-even bands reflect exactly that: flat rate is often fine under $10,000 a month, the $10,000 to $25,000 range is case-by-case, interchange-plus is usually cheaper from $25,000 to $40,000, and above $40,000 it is almost always cheaper. The basket above lands inside those bands, not above them.

Interac debit: where the models split widest

Debit deserves its own section because it is where the two models stop being close. On interchange-plus, an Interac tap is a cents-priced transaction: 9¢ by chip and PIN or 12.5¢ by tap on Helcim's entry tier. Square prices Interac at 0.75% + 7¢, which on a $50 purchase is about 45¢ — roughly three and a half times the tap rate. Moneris flat rate prices debit at a flat $0.12, close to the interchange-plus figure, which shows the split is not really flat versus interchange-plus: it is whether debit is carved out of the credit percentage or billed through it.

A debit-heavy store — a convenience shop, a quick-service counter, a grocer — should run its own debit share before it runs anything else. At 20% debit the debit lines above differ by $32 a month at $25,000 volume. At 50% debit on the same volume, the debit gap alone approaches $80 a month, and it arrives on top of the credit gap, not instead of it.

What you can negotiate, and what you cannot

Interchange is not negotiable. The networks set it, the issuing bank collects it, and no processor can discount it, whatever a sales call implies. Network assessments are likewise fixed. On an interchange-plus quote, the only negotiable number is the markup — the percentage and per-transaction cents the processor adds — plus any monthly account charges around it. Helcim publishes its markup and drops it automatically across its five tiers, from 0.40% + 8¢ in person at the entry tier to 0.15% + 6¢ above $1,000,000 a month. Moneris, by contrast, publishes no markup for Simplified Pricing: the quote is the negotiation, so it has to be obtained in writing and compared as a markup, not as a blended estimate.

Watch the floor under the saving, too. Some interchange-plus accounts carry a monthly minimum — Moneris lists a $5.00 minimum merchant discount among its incidental fees, a trivial figure, but larger minimums exist in quoted contracts — and a minimum is charged in the months you process least. A cheap markup paired with a high monthly floor can lose to flat rate in a quiet January even while it wins the year. Price the minimum against your slowest real month, not your average one, and count any software or statement fees in the same pass.

Run this check on one statement before you switch

This is a buying-decision guide rather than a settings procedure, so the actionable part is a five-step check on paperwork you already have:

  1. Compute your effective rate. Take one recent statement, add every processing-related charge, and divide by that month's card volume. That percentage, not the advertised rate, is your current price.
  2. Split the mix. Note the debit share and the premium-card share. Debit above roughly a third of transactions, or a low premium share, points toward interchange-plus; the reverse narrows its edge.
  3. Re-price the basket. Run that same month through a published interchange-plus schedule, card type by card type where the schedule allows it, exactly as the table above does.
  4. Price the slow month. Apply any monthly minimum, software fee and statement fee to your quietest month's volume and confirm the new model still wins there.
  5. Get the markup in writing. On any quoted interchange-plus plan, require the markup as a percentage plus cents, separate from interchange. A blended estimate cannot be compared, and cannot be held.

Success looks like this: one page where your current effective rate, the re-priced basket total and the slow-month total sit side by side, all from the same month's real transactions. If interchange-plus wins that page by less than the cost of an hour or two of your time each month, flat rate's simplicity is a rational purchase. If it wins by the $170 to $350 a month the mid-volume baskets above produce, the switch pays for its own paperwork quickly.
