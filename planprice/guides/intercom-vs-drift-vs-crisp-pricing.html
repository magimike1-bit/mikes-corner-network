Intercom vs Drift vs Crisp Pricing: Per-Resolution vs Flat at Real Volume

Published 2026-10-12

Live-chat pricing used to be simple: dollars per agent per month. Then AI arrived and the models diverged — Intercom charges per AI resolution, Crisp charges a flat workspace fee with AI included, and Drift… well, Drift is the reason this guide starts with a flag, not a price. Here's the honest 2026 math.

The Drift flag: one of these you can't buy anymore

Drift no longer publishes pricing — since its acquisition by Salesloft, it's quote-only and sales-led. Worse for buyers, the product's future is openly questioned: HelpCrunch's 2026 verdict is blunt — "Drift is no longer a tool you'd adopt today, it's one you'd migrate away from" — citing very high pricing at scale and a 2025 supply-chain data breach. If you're choosing a chat platform in 2026, this is effectively a two-horse race between Intercom and Crisp, with Drift relevant only to existing customers planning a migration.

Per-resolution vs flat: 1,000 conversations a month

The AI pricing model is the whole TCO story. Intercom's Fin charges US$0.99 per resolution — and a resolution isn't a message, it's an outcome (the customer confirms the issue is solved, a handoff completes, a prospect is disqualified). There's also a 50-outcome monthly minimum when Fin runs on top of another helpdesk. Crisp charges a flat workspace fee with AI included.

Alhena's September 2026 AI-cost comparison works the monthly AI bill at three volumes (assuming ~70% of conversations resolve):

  • 300 conversations/mo: Crisp US$95 · Intercom Fin US$207
  • 1,000 conversations/mo: Crisp US$295 · Intercom Fin US$693
  • 3,000 conversations/mo: Crisp (no public price at this volume) · Intercom Fin US$2,079

Per-resolution is fine at ten conversations a day; it's a financial trap at hundreds. The G2 and Reddit echo on Fin bills is consistent — Featurebase cites a widely shared review describing a jump from US$4k to US$9k a month as AI volume grew. Budget Fin against your conversation forecast, not your seat count.

Crisp's free tier: the small-team default

Crisp offers the most functional free tier in the live-chat market: two seats, unlimited contacts, a chatbot builder, and a knowledge base — at zero cost (LaunchBuff). For a pre-revenue or early-revenue team that just needs to answer the "does this work with Shopify?" question on the pricing page, that's the whole decision. Paid tiers run ~US$25–95/month per workspace — flat, not per seat — so adding your fifth team member doesn't change the bill the way Intercom's per-seat model does.

What reviewers say

HelpCrunch scores G2 at Intercom 4.5/5 and Drift 4.4/5, Capterra at 4.5/5 for both — close on ratings, far apart on trajectory. On support, Featurebase's comparison finds Crisp known for fast, personal support with replies in minutes even on the free plan, while Intercom leans on deep self-service documentation and slower human response times on lower tiers. Intercom remains the pick for larger service operations that need mature multi-team routing and deep admin controls — the platform, not just the chat.

Decision: which team buys which

Buy Crisp if you're a small team that needs live chat now: start free, upgrade to the flat workspace tier when you need AI and campaigns, and never think about per-seat math. Buy Intercom if you're running a real support operation at volume and need the ecosystem — but forecast your Fin resolutions first, because the AI bill can exceed the seat bill. And Drift? If you're already on it, plan the migration; if you're choosing today, it's not on the shortlist.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Intercom cost for a small business?

From US$29/seat/month for the platform, plus Fin AI at US$0.99 per resolution. A small team should budget both lines — at 1,000 AI conversations a month, Fin alone runs ~US$693/month.

Is Crisp really free?

The free tier covers 2 seats with live chat, a chatbot builder, and a knowledge base — genuinely usable for an early-stage team. Paid workspace tiers (~US$25–95/month) add AI features, email sequences, and campaigns.

What happened to Drift's pricing?

Drift stopped publishing pricing after its acquisition by Salesloft; it's now quote-only and sales-led. Reviewers in 2026 describe it as a product to migrate away from rather than adopt.
