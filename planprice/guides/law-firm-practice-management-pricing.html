Law Firm Practice Management Pricing: The Per-Lawyer Math

Published 2026-10-12

Legal practice management software is priced per user, not per lawyer — and every comparison table shows the "from" price instead of what a real firm pays. This guide prices Clio, MyCase, PracticePanther, and CosmoLex for a concrete 5-lawyer Canadian firm, flags which prices are published and which hide behind a sales call, and names the add-ons that move the total. Prices checked October 2026; all figures USD as listed by vendors (Canadian firms are USD-billed).

General information, not legal or financial advice. Prices change — always confirm current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

Why the "from" price lies

Clio — the Vancouver-based vendor, 4.6/5 from roughly 1,000 G2 reviews — now publishes exactly one price: Clio Manage Starter from US$49/user/month. Core, Signature, and Elite all carry a "Get pricing" button instead of a number (confirmed August–September 2026). Older guides still circulate stale tier names and figures (US$79–89 / US$99–119 / US$129–169 have all circulated), so check any number you read against Clio's current page before trusting it.

The pattern repeats across the category: headline entry prices look close together, but the tier a working firm actually needs sits one or two steps up. PracticePanther is the exception — it publishes all four tiers ($49/$69/$89/$114 per user/month), which is why budget-conscious firms keep shortlisting it. MyCase lists annual ($50/$100/$130) and monthly ($60/$120/$150) tiers; CosmoLex lists $49/$99/$129.

Over on Reddit's r/LawFirm, community reviews put Clio at 4.3 from 24 reviews and MyCase at 3.9 from 15 — a smaller but practitioner-direct sample.

The quote wall: what to demand in writing

For Clio's Core/Signature/Elite, Smokeball, and Filevine, the price only exists inside a sales conversation. Before you compare, get in writing: the per-seat price at your headcount on the tier you need, what's outside the per-seat figure (implementation, data migration, training, payment processing), the renewal price (not just year one), and the cancellation terms. A quote you can't put in a spreadsheet next to PracticePanther's published US$89 isn't a quote — it's a sales call.

Trust accounting is the real filter

For Canadian firms, the purchase decision rarely turns on the subscription delta above — it turns on trust accounting. Clio includes IOLTA-compliant trust accounting on every plan; CosmoLex's whole pitch is integrated trust + business accounting in one tool (it also publishes its tiers, US$49/$99/$129). If your firm carries meaningful trust balances, shortlist on the trust-accounting model first and the subscription math second. And keep the firm's total tech budget in view: 85% of small law firms spend US$10,000 or less annually on their entire technology stack, so a US$8,400 practice management bill leaves little room for everything else.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Clio cost per user?

Clio publishes one price: Clio Manage Starter from US$49/user/month (2026). Core, Signature, and Elite require a quote — older published figures circulating online use retired plan names and should not be trusted.

Which practice management software is cheapest for a small firm?

On published pricing, PracticePanther's full tier ladder is the most transparent value play (US$49–114/user/month). MyCase's entry annual tier is US$50/user/month. Clio Starter looks cheapest at US$49, but most working firms need a quote-gated tier above it.

Do I pay per lawyer or per user?

Per user — every seat, including paralegals, legal assistants, and bookkeepers, pays the full per-user price. Model your total on staff headcount, not lawyer headcount, and add intake/CRM products and payment processing separately.
