Mailchimp's 15% Discount: Who Actually Qualifies and What It's Worth (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Mailchimp's homepage promotion — 15% off your first 12 months — is the kind of offer that reads as universal and isn't. It applies to accounts with 10,000 or more contacts, on the Standard and Premium plans. Below that list size, the discount is worth exactly zero, and the honest response to the promo is to ignore it. This guide prices both sides of the eligibility line.

Above the line

Premium at 10,000 contacts lists at $350 a month in our dataset. Fifteen percent off for twelve months is $52.50 a month — $630 across the year. Standard at the same list size prices lower, so the saving shrinks proportionally, but the structure is the same: the discount is meaningful precisely because the qualifying plans are the expensive ones. If you're near 10,000 contacts, note what the threshold rewards: importing a padded list to cross it would be a mistake — unengaged contacts raise deliverability risk and, on contact-based billing, raise every future invoice too.

Below the line

At 500 contacts, Mailchimp Essentials is $13 a month and Standard is $20 in our data (checked October 20, 2026) — no promotion applies, and none is needed to justify the tool at that size. The budget question for small lists isn't discounts; it's the contact-count billing model itself, which charges for stored contacts in ways rivals like Klaviyo (active profiles) and MailerLite structure differently. Our Mailchimp vs Klaviyo comparison works that angle.

The renewal cliff

Month thirteen bills at full price. A discount that justifies a platform switch has to survive its own expiry: at $350 a month undiscounted, Premium needs to be earning its keep on automation depth, not on a $52.50 introductory break. Price the tool at list; treat the 15% as a switching subsidy, not a price.

The adjacent question: should a 10,000-contact account switch for this?

If you're already on a rival at 10,000+ contacts, the $630 first-year saving is a switching subsidy with real migration costs against it: list import and re-permissioning hygiene, template rebuilding, automation/flow reconstruction, and a deliverability warm-up period on new infrastructure. For a store whose email program is a meaningful revenue channel, those costs — mostly your time — can exceed $630 quickly, and the discount expires in month thirteen while the migration is forever. The cleaner use of the offer: accounts already planning to land on Mailchimp (new programs, consolidations) should time the start to capture it, and accounts courted by it should demand the comparison be made at undiscounted prices, because that's the price they'll actually live at.

Small lists: where the money actually goes

Below the eligibility line, the budget story isn't promotions — it's contact-count discipline. Mailchimp bills on contacts in ways that reward hygiene: archived and unsubscribed contacts handled per plan rules, duplicate imports, and dead addresses all inflate the tier you're priced into. A 2,000-contact list with 30% dead weight can sit a full price band above a cleaned 1,400-contact list while performing worse. The quarterly list clean is the small sender's version of the enterprise discount — it's just paid in time instead of eligibility.

Frequently asked questions

Does the 15% apply to the Essentials plan? As published, the promotion covers Standard and Premium for qualifying (10,000+ contact) accounts. Essentials accounts should not budget for it.

What counts as a 'contact' for the 10,000 threshold? Mailchimp's audience/contact definitions govern — the same definitions that drive its billing. Check your audience total in-account rather than your raw email list size.

Is there a similar standing offer for small lists? Not in the promotion checked on October 25, 2026. Small lists save through billing structure (contact hygiene, the right plan tier) rather than promotions.

One more eligibility nuance

Promotions like this are typically for new subscriptions or new accounts rather than existing customers mid-term — the exact eligibility language lives on Mailchimp's offer page and in-account, and it's worth reading before planning a migration around the 15%. Existing Standard and Premium customers near the threshold should check their account dashboard for whether the offer surfaces there; if it doesn't, the honest assumption is that it doesn't apply. Either way, the structural guidance stands: price Mailchimp at list, treat any discount as a windfall, and let the contact-billing model — not a promotion calendar — drive the platform decision.
