Mailchimp's Contact Pricing: The Archived-Contact Trap and the Real Cost per 1,000

Published 2026-10-11

Mailchimp doesn't charge for emails sent. It charges for contacts stored — including unsubscribes from years ago and signups that never confirmed. That single design decision is the difference between the price on the pricing page and the number on your invoice. This guide does the unit economics: what 1,000 contacts really cost at each tier, the archived-contact trap, and the cleanup that cuts the bill.

The billable-contact trap

Mailchimp counts subscribed, unsubscribed, and non-subscribed contacts toward your billing total. Only archived, cleaned, or deleted contacts are excluded. A store that has collected 5,000 unsubscribes over five years pays for 5,000 ghosts — contacts that generate zero revenue and will never open another email. The bill is set by the highest contact count held during the billing period, so a one-time import spike can raise a whole month's invoice.

The pattern repeats across SaaS pricing: the headline fee is rarely the surprise — the surprise is the meter nobody advertised. On Mailchimp, the hidden meter is your own list hygiene.

Cost per 1,000 contacts at each tier

Current US prices, verified October 2026: the free plan covers 250 contacts with 500 monthly sends; Essentials starts at $13/month for 500 contacts with 10x monthly sends and 3 users; Standard starts at $20/month for 500 contacts with 12x sends and 5 users; Premium starts at $350/month for 10,000 contacts with 15x sends. Annual billing saves 20%.

Unit cost falls as the list grows — but only if the contacts are real. Every thousand ghosts in the count drags your effective cost per mailable contact up. And the Standard premium over Essentials is $7/month at 500 contacts but $25/month at 5,000 and 10,000 — the upgrade gets more expensive exactly as your list gets valuable.

The archive workflow that cuts the bill

The fix is archiving, not deleting. Archive unsubscribed contacts and they leave your billable total while Mailchimp keeps the suppression record — so a sloppy future import can't resubscribe someone who asked to leave. Delete them and that record is wiped, which is a compliance risk on top of a billing one.

  1. Go to Audience → All contacts and filter by Email Marketing Status = Unsubscribed.
  2. Export the segment as a backup CSV if you keep unsubscribes for analytics or legal records.
  3. Select all and Archive (not Delete). Repeat for non-subscribed contacts you will never market to.

What success looks like: your billable contact count drops on the next billing cycle, with opt-out history intact. Run this quarterly — unsubscribes accumulate silently.

The TCO: a dirty list vs a clean list

Worked example. You have 6,500 total contacts: 4,500 engaged subscribers and 2,000 unsubscribes you never archived. At 6,500 billable contacts you sit in the 10,000-contact tier: $110/month on Essentials, $1,320/year. Archive the 2,000 ghosts and you drop to the 5,000-contact tier at $75/month, $900/year. The cleanup saves $420/year for about twenty minutes of work. On Standard the same cleanup saves $35/month too ($135 down to $100).

At 25,000 contacts with 40% dead weight, archiving 10,000 ghosts moves you from the 25,000 tier ($270/month Essentials) to the 15,000 tier ($180/month) — $1,080/year recovered. Mailchimp raised paid prices roughly 11–13% in April 2026, so every ghost costs more than it did last year.

Essentials vs Standard: the $7 decision

At 500 contacts the difference is $7/month. Buy Standard when you need what Essentials withholds: multi-step automation (Essentials caps flows at four steps), the extra two seats (5 vs 3), and 12x instead of 10x monthly sends. Stay on Essentials if your automation is simple — a welcome email, an abandoned-cart nudge — and your send volume fits inside the 10x allowance.

Decision rule: if more than one person runs email and you use automation beyond a single welcome flow, Standard pays for itself at the $7 entry gap. If you're a solo operator sending a weekly newsletter to a clean list, Essentials is the cheapest sufficient tier.

When the math says leave Mailchimp

Run your own cost per 1,000 mailable contacts: monthly bill ÷ (billable contacts minus archived ghosts) × 1,000. If that number sits above $20 and you don't need Mailchimp-specific features, price the alternatives — several competitors charge by sends rather than contacts, which flips the math for large, infrequently-mailed lists.

FAQ

Does Mailchimp charge for unsubscribed contacts?

Yes. Subscribed, unsubscribed, and non-subscribed contacts all count toward your billable total. Only archived, cleaned, or deleted contacts are excluded — so unsubscribes keep costing you until you archive them.

Should I archive or delete old contacts in Mailchimp?

Archive, don't delete. Archiving removes the contact from your billable count while preserving the suppression record, so a future import can't accidentally resubscribe someone who opted out. Deleting wipes that record entirely.

What does Mailchimp cost per 1,000 contacts in 2026?

At 5,000 contacts, Essentials ($75/month) works out to $15 per 1,000 contacts per month and Standard ($100/month) to $20 per 1,000. At 10,000 contacts the unit cost falls to $11 and $13.50 per 1,000 respectively. Prices in USD, verified October 2026.
