MailerLite Pricing Decoded: Comfort, Power and the June 2026 Rebuild

Published 2026-10-20 · Last updated 2026-10-20

MailerLite rebuilt its pricing in June 2026: the Growing Business plan became Comfort, Advanced became Power, prices rose at every tier, and the free plan shrank to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month. Most “MailerLite pricing” pages still quote the old ladder — $10 and $20 starting prices, a 500-subscriber free plan with 12,000 emails — which makes them wrong in the way that matters most: at the moment a growing list crosses a tier. This guide decodes the current structure from MailerLite's own pricing page (checked October 20, 2026), with the tier ladder filled in from September 2026 published pricing summaries, and the billing mechanics — cumulative active counting and automatic tier upgrades — that decide what you actually pay. Prices are USD as published; Canadian buyers should budget the exchange rate on top.

The four plans as MailerLite states them

MailerLite's own summary: the Free plan includes up to 250 subscribers, 2 user seats and 2,500 monthly emails, plus campaigns, automations, a website, a landing page, signup forms and all three email editors. Comfort (starting at $12/month) adds unlimited templates, dynamic emails, campaign auto-resend, multivariate testing, the unsubscribe page builder, a preference centre, an AI writing assistant and smart sending, “with sensible sending and usage limits.” Power (starting at $25/month) adds unlimited user seats, monthly emails, automations, forms, digital products and bookings, multiple automation triggers, partner discounts and 24/7 live chat support. Enterprise is custom-priced for lists over 200,000 subscribers, with a dedicated success manager, deliverability support and a dedicated IP as an add-on. One structural point the vendor states plainly: pricing is based on usage, not feature access — you pay for subscribers and sending volume, which is why the tier ladder, not the feature list, is the real price document.

At 5,000 subscribers the gap narrows in percentage terms ($49 vs $69) while the send ceiling (50,000) starts to bind for anyone mailing more than twice a week — which is exactly where Power's unlimited sending becomes the deciding feature rather than a luxury.

The three billing mechanics that move the real price

1. Cumulative active counting. Billing is based on active subscribers — unsubscribed and bounced addresses do not count — but the count is cumulative within the billing cycle: any address active at any point in the cycle counts toward the limit even if deleted later, and the count resets on your billing anniversary. Deleting subscribers mid-cycle does not lower this cycle's tier. The cleanup timing that works is therefore “before the anniversary, with the reset in mind,” not “whenever you notice the bill.”

2. Automatic tier upgrades. On paid plans, crossing your subscriber limit automatically moves you to the next tier and charges the card, with an in-account notification as the limit approaches. There is no overage grace period to negotiate — list growth is billed as it happens, which makes import discipline (no stale purchased or scraped lists, ever) a billing control as well as a deliverability one. On the free plan, crossing 250 active subscribers locks sending — campaigns and automations — until you upgrade or remove subscribers.

3. The Comfort send ceiling is 10× the tier, not 10× your list. Comfort allows ten times the top of your subscriber tier each month: on the up-to-1,000 tier you get 10,000 emails whether you have 200 subscribers or 900. Hitting the ceiling mid-month leaves two options — upgrade to Power for unlimited sending, or wait for the cycle reset. High-frequency senders should do this arithmetic before choosing, not during a launch.

What changed in June 2026 — and why old guides mislead

The 2026 restructure did three things at once: renamed the plans (Growing Business → Comfort, Advanced → Power), raised entry prices ($10 → $12 and $20 → $25, with the 1,000-subscriber steps rising $15 → $19 and $30 → $39), and tightened the free plan in stages — first to 500 subscribers, then in June 2026 to 250 subscribers and 2,500 monthly emails, down from 12,000. Existing paid accounts moved to the new plans at their next renewal; manual plan changes after June 16 landed on the new structure immediately. The practical consequence: any pricing comparison written before June 2026 — including ones still ranking today — understates MailerLite's current cost by roughly 10–30% depending on tier, and overstates the free plan by 4× on subscribers. Who should pay for Power, who should not

The decision framework is short because the vendor made the meters explicit. Stay on Comfort if: your list is under 10,000, you mail at most weekly (the 10× ceiling holds), three-ish seats cover your team, and email support response times are acceptable. Move to Power when any one of these breaks: sending volume regularly exceeds 10× your tier, the team needs unlimited seats, you sell digital products or bookings through the platform (a Power-gated feature set), or a launch calendar makes a mid-month send lock an unacceptable risk. Nonprofits should price both plans at the published 30% nonprofit discount before doing any of this math. And every account gets a 14-day premium trial — use it to test the Power-only features you are actually considering paying for, because after the trial those features switch off (your data, subscribers and automations are not removed).

FAQ

Does MailerLite charge for unsubscribed contacts? No — billing counts active subscribers only; unsubscribed and bounced addresses are excluded. But the count is cumulative within a billing cycle, so an address deleted mid-cycle still counts until the anniversary reset.

What happens if I exceed my subscriber limit? Paid plans auto-upgrade to the next tier and charge the card on file (with an in-account warning as you approach the limit). Free accounts have sending locked until they upgrade or trim the list below 250 active.

Is the free plan still usable? For a genuine starter list, yes: 250 subscribers, 2,500 emails a month, automations and a landing page is a real toolkit. It is no longer the stay-on-it-for-a-year plan it was at 1,000 subscribers and 12,000 emails — budget the first paid tier from the start.

Where is data stored, and does it matter for Canadian senders? MailerLite states its data centre is in the European Union with ISO 27001 certification, and that it is GDPR compliant. Canadian senders remain responsible for their own CASL compliance (consent records, identification, unsubscribe handling) regardless of where the platform stores data.
