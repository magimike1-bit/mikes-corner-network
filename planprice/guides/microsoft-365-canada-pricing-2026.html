Microsoft 365 Pricing in Canada for 2026: CAD Tiers and the Annual Trap

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Microsoft publishes Canadian-dollar pricing for Microsoft 365 Business, and in 2026 the fine print matters as much as the figures. The ladder: Business Basic C$9.50, Business Standard C$19.00, Business Premium C$29.80 per user/month — on annual commitment (checked October 18, 2026). Monthly-commitment pricing runs higher, so the first decode is simple: the price you see assumes a year.

What each tier is for

TierCAD/user/mo (annual)The deciding feature
Business BasicC$9.50Web/mobile Office apps, Exchange email, Teams — no desktop apps
Business StandardC$19.00Desktop Office apps (Word, Excel, Outlook) — the tier most firms actually need
Business PremiumC$29.80Device management (Intune) and advanced threat protection

The Basic-to-Standard jump ($9.50 per seat) is the desktop-apps toll: if your team lives in installed Excel and Outlook, Basic was never an option and Standard is the real entry price. Premium's jump buys IT control — justified when you manage devices for staff who handle client data, decorative otherwise.

The July 2026 signal

Microsoft raised US Business Standard pricing from $12.50 to $14.00 on July 1, 2026 (tracked in our research data). Canadian list prices in the check above post-date that change. The lesson for renewals: Microsoft moves prices on its own calendar, annual commitment locks yours for the term, and a renewal landing just after an increase announcement is worth a hard look at term length. The side-by-side with Google Workspace (C$9.20/$18.40/$28.70) is in our comparison guide.

Seat hygiene

At C$19 a seat, a departed contractor's unremoved licence costs C$228 a year. Quarterly seat audits — comparing the licence list against the payroll list — are the cheapest cost control in this entire category, and annual commitment makes them more valuable, not less: you can't shrink mid-term, but you can stop the renewal from carrying ghosts.

Worked example: an 8-person office

Eight seats on Business Standard: C$152/month, C$1,824 a year on annual commitment. The same office with two licence ghosts (a departed employee, a contractor from a finished project) pays C$190/month — C$456 a year for nothing, which over a three-year span exceeds the cost of the office's entire hardware refresh cycle for one workstation. Premium for the same office: C$238.40/month; the C$86.40 monthly premium over Standard buys Intune device management and advanced threat protection — worth it the day the office handles client data on laptops that leave the building, hard to justify for a fully office-bound team. These are the two audits to calendar: seats quarterly, tier annually, both against the payroll list rather than the IT list.

New Canadian wrinkle: price protection at renewal

After a headline increase year, renewal mechanics matter more than list prices: annual terms lock pricing for the term, monthly terms float. A firm burned by a mid-cycle increase on flexible billing should read the July 2026 increase as the argument for term discipline — not longer terms blindly, but terms aligned to how certain the headcount is. Contractors and seasonal staff belong on the flexible edge; the permanent core belongs on the annual price. Microsoft's partner channel (most small firms buy through an IT provider) can also surface promotional pricing the public list doesn't show — ask, in writing, before the renewal processes.

Frequently asked questions

Is Microsoft 365 ever billed in CAD? Yes — the prices decoded here are Microsoft's published Canadian-dollar prices. Confirm your tenant's billing currency in the admin centre; USD tenants exist and cost more after conversion.

What's the difference between Business and Enterprise plans? Enterprise (E3/E5) is a different ladder for larger organizations with different licensing rules. Small firms belong on Business tiers — the per-user math and the 300-seat Business cap make that the intended lane.

Can I pay monthly without annual commitment? Yes, at a higher per-seat price — the flexible premium. It's the right choice for genuinely temporary seats and the wrong one for your permanent core.
