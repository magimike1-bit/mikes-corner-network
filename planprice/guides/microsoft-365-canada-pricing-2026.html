Microsoft 365 Pricing in Canada for 2026: CAD Tiers and the Annual Trap

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-11-15 · By Mike, Editor

Microsoft publishes Canadian-dollar pricing for Microsoft 365 Business, and in 2026 the fine print matters as much as the figures. The ladder Microsoft now publishes in Canada: Business Basic C$9.50, Business Standard with Copilot C$31.90, Business Premium with Copilot C$43.40 per user/month, paid yearly (checked November 15, 2026). Standard and Premium are now listed only with Copilot included; the without-Copilot prices Microsoft published in October (C$19.00 and C$29.80) no longer appear on its public pricing pages. Monthly-commitment pricing runs higher, so the first decode is simple: the price you see assumes a year.

What each tier is for

TierCAD/user/mo (annual)The deciding feature
Business BasicC$9.50Web/mobile Office apps, Exchange email, Teams, no desktop apps
Business Standard with CopilotC$31.90Desktop Office apps (Word, Excel, Outlook) plus Copilot, the tier most firms actually need
Business Premium with CopilotC$43.40Device management (Intune), advanced threat protection and Copilot

The Basic-to-Standard jump (C$22.40 per seat) is the desktop-apps and Copilot toll: if your team lives in installed Excel and Outlook, Basic was never an option and Standard is the real entry price. Premium's jump buys IT control, justified when you manage devices for staff who handle client data, decorative otherwise.

The July 2026 signal

Microsoft raised US Business Standard pricing from $12.50 to $14.00 on July 1, 2026 (tracked in our research data). The bigger Canadian change came later in 2026: Microsoft's public pages stopped listing Standard and Premium without Copilot, so the published ladder is now the Copilot ladder above. The lesson for renewals: Microsoft moves prices on its own calendar, annual commitment locks yours for the term, and a renewal landing just after an increase announcement is worth a hard look at term length. The side-by-side with Google Workspace (C$9.20/$18.40/$28.70) is in our comparison guide.

Seat hygiene

At C$31.90 a seat, a departed contractor's unremoved licence costs C$382.80 a year. Quarterly seat audits, comparing the licence list against the payroll list, are the cheapest cost control in this entire category, and annual commitment makes them more valuable, not less: you can't shrink mid-term, but you can stop the renewal from carrying ghosts.

Worked example: an 8-person office

Eight seats on Business Standard with Copilot: C$255.20/month, C$3,062.40 a year paid yearly. The same office with two licence ghosts (a departed employee, a contractor from a finished project) pays C$319/month, C$765.60 a year for nothing. Premium for the same office: C$347.20/month; the C$92 monthly premium over Standard buys Intune device management and advanced threat protection, worth it the day the office handles client data on laptops that leave the building, hard to justify for a fully office-bound team. These are the two audits to calendar: seats quarterly, tier annually, both against the payroll list rather than the IT list.

New Canadian wrinkle: price protection at renewal

After a headline increase year, renewal mechanics matter more than list prices: annual terms lock pricing for the term, monthly terms float. A firm burned by a mid-cycle increase on flexible billing should read the July 2026 increase as the argument for term discipline, not longer terms blindly, but terms aligned to how certain the headcount is. Contractors and seasonal staff belong on the flexible edge; the permanent core belongs on the annual price. Microsoft's partner channel (most small firms buy through an IT provider) can also surface promotional pricing the public list doesn't show, ask, in writing, before the renewal processes.

Frequently asked questions

Is Microsoft 365 ever billed in CAD? Yes, the prices decoded here are Microsoft's published Canadian-dollar prices. Confirm your tenant's billing currency in the admin centre; USD tenants exist and cost more after conversion.

What's the difference between Business and Enterprise plans? Enterprise (E3/E5) is a different ladder for larger organizations with different licensing rules. Small firms belong on Business tiers, the per-user math and the 300-seat Business cap make that the intended lane.

Can I pay monthly without annual commitment? Yes, at a higher per-seat price, the flexible premium. It's the right choice for genuinely temporary seats and the wrong one for your permanent core.
