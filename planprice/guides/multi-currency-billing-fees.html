Multi-Currency Billing Fees: What USD Pricing Costs Canadian Buyers

Published 2026-10-12

The pricing page says $99 a month. Your credit card is charged in Canadian dollars — and the number is always higher than $99 times the exchange rate. Between the network conversion, the vendor's spread, and your card's foreign-transaction fee, USD-denominated SaaS carries a currency tax of roughly 3–5% on every invoice. Across a whole tool stack, that's a thirteenth month of software you're paying for and never using.

General information, not financial advice. Exchange rates move — always confirm how a vendor bills Canadian accounts before buying.

The sticker is in USD; your bill is not

Most SaaS vendors price in US dollars and bill your Canadian card in USD, leaving the conversion to your card network and issuer. That means the "$99 a month" tool is really three costs: the USD price, the exchange rate, and the markup layered on top. At an exchange reference of about 1.4262 CAD per USD, $99 USD converts to roughly $141.19 CAD — before any fees.

Buyers notice the gap but rarely name it. On a long-running forum thread about foreign transaction fees, users reported that card companies add 1–3% even on USD purchases from companies headquartered outside the US — including internet services billed in dollars. The fee is disclosed in the cardholder agreement, as required, but nobody reads the cardholder agreement before buying software.

The three layers of currency cost

Layer 1: the exchange rate itself. Visa or Mastercard converts the USD charge at its own daily rate, which sits close to the wholesale rate banks trade at. This part is fair — it's the market price of the dollars.

Layer 2: the vendor's spread. Vendors that convert for you — billing "in CAD at checkout" — use their own rate, which includes a margin. Shopify bills Canadians in CAD at checkout at its own exchange rate; the convenience is real, and so is the spread. When a vendor offers CAD billing, compare their rate against the day's wholesale rate before assuming it's cheaper.

Layer 3: your card's foreign-transaction fee. Most Canadian credit cards add about 2.5% on foreign-currency purchases (the range runs roughly 1–3% depending on the card), calculated on the converted CAD amount. A fourth trap, dynamic currency conversion — the vendor offering to bill you in CAD at their terminal rate — adds the merchant's own markup on top and is almost always worse than letting your card convert.

Worked: one team's real CAD stack cost

Take a ten-person team: Slack Pro at $7.25 USD a user a month (billed annually, USD-billed) plus one $99 USD-a-month tool. Sticker: $72.50 + $99 = $171.50 USD a month. At 1.4262, that's $244.59 CAD — and then the card's 2.5% foreign-transaction fee adds about $6.11, for a true cost of roughly $250.70 CAD a month, or about $3,008 a year.

Which vendors bill you in CAD

The simplest fix is buying from vendors that bill Canadians in Canadian dollars. Shopify bills CAD at checkout. Xero Canada's plans are CAD-denominated (Starter $25 a month). Domestic billing removes the card FX fee entirely — though the vendor's own conversion spread still applies when the list price originated in USD, so the "CAD price" deserves the same scrutiny as any converted price.

Three ways to cut the FX markup

  1. Prefer CAD-billed vendors. No foreign-currency charge means no 2.5% card fee. Worth a tiebreak between otherwise equal tools.
  2. Use a no-foreign-transaction-fee card for SaaS spend. Several Canadian cards waive the fee — you pay the network's near-wholesale rate and nothing more. Route all USD subscriptions through it.
  3. Never accept dynamic currency conversion. If a checkout offers to bill you in CAD "for convenience," decline — the merchant's rate carries a markup your card's conversion doesn't.

And budget in CAD, not USD. A stack priced at "$2,000 a month" is really $2,930 CAD at current rates plus fees — plan the cash flow in the currency that leaves your account.

Frequently asked questions

Do Canadian credit cards charge foreign transaction fees on USD SaaS?

Almost always: most Canadian cards add about 2.5% (range roughly 1–3%) on purchases billed in a foreign currency, calculated after conversion to CAD. The fee applies even when the vendor bills in USD — it's about the currency, not the vendor's location.

Which SaaS tools bill Canadians in CAD?

Shopify bills CAD at checkout, and Xero Canada's plans are CAD-denominated (Starter $25 a month). Always check the vendor's billing currency for Canadian accounts before assuming — many USD-listed vendors still bill in USD.

Is it cheaper to pay annually to reduce currency fees?

Marginally: one annual charge incurs the FX fee once instead of twelve times, but the fee is a percentage, so the saving is only the avoided per-transaction minimums (which are usually zero). The real lever is the card you pay with, not the billing frequency.
