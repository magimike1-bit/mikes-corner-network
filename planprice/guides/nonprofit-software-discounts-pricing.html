Nonprofit Software Discounts & Pricing: The Free Layer First

Published 2026-10-12

Nonprofit software guides list discount programs; none prices the actual stack a small nonprofit runs. This guide stacks the free layer first — Salesforce's Power of Us, Google for Nonprofits, Microsoft's nonprofit program — then prices the paid tiers that actually carry the work at a 10-person organization, with October 2026 figures.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

Start with the free layer

Through Salesforce's Power of Us program, eligible nonprofits get 10 free licenses of Nonprofit Cloud — the single biggest line item a small org can zero out. Google for Nonprofits and Microsoft's nonprofit program cover the productivity layer (email, docs, and donated or steeply discounted Microsoft 365). Apply for all three before you price anything paid: the free layer defines what you actually need to buy.

The trap, flagged repeatedly by G2 reviewers: the 10 free licenses are praised, but organizations report aggressive billing as they grow past them. Salesforce's Canadian nonprofit pricing page lists Nonprofit Cloud Enterprise at US$60/user/month, Unlimited at US$100, and Agentforce editions at US$135. The 11th seat costs US$720/year — and Salesforce implementations are famously not free, with reviewers citing external implementation and customization costs as the real expense.

When "free" stops being free

Two tripwires. First, the seat count: the moment you need seat 11, you're on the US$60/user/month ladder, and Salesforce's per-user pricing compounds fast at 20+ staff. Second, the ecosystem: the CRM is free, but the implementation partner, the data migration, and the admin training are not — budget them before you celebrate the $0 license line. Bloomerang and DonorPerfect look expensive next to $0 until you price a Salesforce implementation; then the US$1,188–1,500/year all-in looks like the deal it is.

Bloomerang vs DonorPerfect vs Zeffy at scale

On reviews, Bloomerang leads the paid pair: 4.6/5 from 914 G2 reviews (ease of use its most-cited strength), 94% recommend from 2,200 reviews on SelectHub, with users praising fast onboarding and patient chat support. DonorPerfect: 4.4/5 (566 G2 reviews), 89% recommend (1,507) — valued for affordability, auctions, and QuickBooks sync, with a steeper learning curve and a dated email module. Zeffy is the structural outlier: a genuinely $0 platform — no subscription, no platform fee, no processing fee — funded entirely by optional donor tips at checkout. For a budget-conscious nonprofit of any size, Zeffy sets the price floor that every paid option has to justify against: what does US$1,500/year buy that $0 doesn't? Usually the answer is support depth, reporting, and integrations — worth it for some orgs, not for all.

Frequently asked questions

How do nonprofits get Salesforce for free?

Through the Power of Us program: eligible nonprofits get 10 free Nonprofit Cloud licenses. Apply via the Power of Us portal with a Trailblazer ID. Beyond 10 seats, Nonprofit Cloud Enterprise is US$60/user/month.

Bloomerang vs DonorPerfect: which is cheaper?

DonorPerfect's entry is estimated around US$99/month (tiers are quote-gated); Bloomerang CRM is US$125/month published. Over two years that's roughly US$2,376 vs US$3,000 before add-ons — but DonorPerfect's add-on pricing is less transparent, so get the full quote.

Is Zeffy really free?

Yes — no subscription, platform, or processing fees, in the US, Canada, UK, and Australia. It's funded by optional donor tips at checkout. The trade-off is a smaller integration ecosystem and less hand-holding than paid CRMs.
