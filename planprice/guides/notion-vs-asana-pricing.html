Notion vs Asana Pricing: The Canadian Small-Team Math

Published 2026-10-12

Notion and Asana sit next to each other on every "best project tools" list, and their entry prices look nearly identical — US$10 against US$10.99 per user per month. But the invoice a Canadian team actually pays depends on three things the pricing pages bury: the annual-vs-monthly spread, the free-tier cliff, and a quiet 2026 change to how Notion sells AI. This guide works the real numbers for a 6-person team.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm the current rates on each vendor's own pricing page before buying.

The free-tier cliff: what a 4-person team pays on day one

Here is the asymmetry no comparison table prices. Notion's free tier covers individuals with up to 10 guests — a 4-person team collaborating on docs can genuinely run free. Asana's free Personal tier caps at 2 users, so the same 4-person team starts paying immediately: 4 × US$10.99 = US$43.96/month (≈ CA$62.70) on the annual plan, or US$53.96/month (≈ CA$76.96) month-to-month.

A 30-day firsthand test by a 4-person team, published on Medium, put the same split in real terms: Notion at US$40/month for the team against Asana at US$121.96/month — because their team needed Asana's pricier tier for basic reporting. The lesson: the tier you need is decided by features, not by the entry price.

AI pricing: the line item nobody had a year ago

Notion quietly changed its AI packaging in 2026: the standalone Notion AI add-on is gone, full AI now ships inside the Business plan (US$20/user/mo annual), and Plus gets only a trial. If your team wants AI across the board, budget Business — US$1,440/year for six people (≈ CA$2,054), not the US$720 Plus sticker.

Asana's AI story is plan-dependent and keeps shifting, so treat any number here as perishable and check the current add-on terms. The structural point stands: "AI for everyone" is a doubling of the per-seat cost on Notion, and a moving target on Asana. Price it as a line item, not a footnote.

6 people, 12 months: the real invoice

For a 6-person Canadian team, annual billing:

  • Notion Plus: 6 × US$10 × 12 = US$720/yr (≈ CA$1,027)
  • Asana Starter: 6 × US$10.99 × 12 = US$791.28/yr (≈ CA$1,129)
  • Asana Starter, monthly: 6 × US$13.49 × 12 = US$971.28/yr (≈ CA$1,385) — the annual prepay saves ~18.5%
  • Notion Business (with AI): 6 × US$20 × 12 = US$1,440/yr (≈ CA$2,054)
  • Asana Advanced: 6 × US$24.99 × 12 = US$1,799.28/yr (≈ CA$2,566)

The entry-tier gap is US$71/year — noise. The real fork is what happens when you need more: Asana's Advanced tier runs 25% more per seat than Notion's Business tier, and Asana buyers report needing the higher tier sooner for reporting. A five-week parallel evaluation found Asana's Timeline view was the decisive factor for teams juggling dependencies across 8+ concurrent tasks — worth the premium if that is your work, wasted money if it isn't.

The Canadian wrinkle

Both vendors bill in USD, so the CAD number on your card moves with the exchange rate — budget a buffer. The deeper question is fit: a buy-or-skip matrix from The Stack Architects puts it cleanly — teams managing structured projects, deadlines, and dependencies belong on Asana; teams combining projects with SOPs, briefs, and meeting notes belong on Notion; teams choosing on price alone should compare required features first, because the headline per-seat prices are not equivalent tiers.

Decision: which team buys which

Buy Notion if your team's day is docs plus light task tracking, you want one workspace for knowledge and projects, and you can invest the setup time — the Medium testers spent six hours in week one building their system, then dropped Google Docs entirely. Buy Asana if your day is task execution: dependencies, timelines, workload views, and clear ownership, with minimal setup. And if the budget is the deciding vote, remember the free-tier cliff: under 3 people, Asana is free; past it, Notion's free tier stretches much further.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, Notion or Asana?

At entry level, nearly identical: US$10 vs US$10.99 per user/month on annual billing. The difference appears at the free tier (Notion stretches further), at monthly billing (Asana costs ~23% more), and at upper tiers (Asana Advanced US$24.99 vs Notion Business US$20).

Can Notion replace Asana for project management?

For many small teams, yes — Notion now supports task databases, subtasks, dependencies, timelines, and dashboards. Asana remains more purpose-built for structured execution, portfolio workload views, and standardized multi-project work.

Do either bill in CAD?

Both list and bill in USD. Canadian buyers pay the converted amount, so the CAD invoice varies with the exchange rate — check your card statement's effective rate, not just the USD sticker.
