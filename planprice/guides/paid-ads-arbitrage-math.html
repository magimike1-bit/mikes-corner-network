Should You Buy Traffic? The Paid-Ads Arbitrage Math, Worked Honestly

Published 2026-10-12 · Last updated 2026-10-12

"Buy cheap traffic, show ads, pocket the difference" sounds like a business model. It is, mostly, an arithmetic error — and it's worth doing the arithmetic properly once, because the same formula tells you when paying for traffic can make sense.

The revenue side: what a visitor is worth

A display-ad page earns Page RPM ÷ 1,000 per pageview. Practitioner-reported typical ranges run about $5–20 Page RPM for most content niches on entry and mid-tier ad networks (AdSense at the low end; premium networks pay more but gate entry — Mediavine's main programme now requires roughly $5,000/year in ad revenue, with its "Journey" on-ramp starting around 10,000 sessions), finance-type niches up to about $30, and entertainment as low as about $3. At a $15 RPM, one pageview is worth $0.015.

The cost side: what a visitor costs

Even "cheap" social and native clicks typically cost $0.03–0.30+, and search clicks in anything commercial cost far more — Google Ads' average CPC rose 12.88% in 2025, with 87% of industries up (WordStream data). Compare the two sides and the model inverts: the click costs more than the pageview it buys can earn.

The break-even formula

Maximum CPC = (Page RPM ÷ 1,000) × pageviews per visit. At $15 RPM and a strong 3 pageviews per visit, break-even CPC is $0.045. At the 1.2 pageviews per visit that bought traffic realistically produces (paid visitors browse less), it's $0.018 — below almost any legitimate click price in Canada, the US, or other top-tier geographies. And that's before invalid traffic (about 20% of impressions in one 2025 fraud analysis), ad-network clawbacks, and the account risk of traffic patterns that look like arbitrage. The spread is collapsing from both sides: click prices rising about 13% a year while monetisation policies tighten (2026 arbitrage analysis).

When micro-budget paid traffic can pay

  • High-RPM niche + deep asset: a finance, insurance, or legal tool flow producing 5+ pageviews at $40+ RPM can tolerate roughly a $0.20 CPC from cheap native or social placements in narrow geographies. The math works because both inputs are at the favourable extreme.
  • Buying subscribers, not pageviews: if a lead magnet converts and each subscriber's lifetime pageview and purchase value is calculable (our email list value calculator), a small budget is an investment in an owned audience, not arbitrage.
  • Seeding and testing: $50–100 to test a creative or validate a niche before you build more on it. You're buying information, not traffic.

As a standing traffic source for ad-supported articles, though, the verdict of the numbers is simple: don't. The cheapest traffic is the kind this site's other marketing guides cover — search, tools, email, and (for visual niches) Pinterest.
