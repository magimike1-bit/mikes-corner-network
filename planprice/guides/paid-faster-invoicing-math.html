Getting Paid Faster: The Real Cost of Card-on-Invoice (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

"Why would I pay 2.9% to get my own money?" Because the alternative isn't free either — it's just unbilled. This piece prices both sides: the card fees on invoice payments at the rates in our dataset, against the float and labour cost of waiting for cheques and e-Transfers that arrive when the client remembers.

The percentage dominates at large invoices (the 30¢ gap between Wave and Stripe is worth 30¢, not a strategy); the fixed fee dominates at small ones — on a $25 invoice, Wave's 60¢ is 2.4% on its own. Fee shape matters most to businesses that bill small, often.

The waiting side

Price your own float: an invoice paid 12 days sooner on $10,000 of monthly billings returns roughly $10,000 × (12/365) × your cost of money — at a 6% line-of-credit rate, about $20 a month. Small. The bigger cost is labour: fifteen minutes of reminder-and-reconcile time per late invoice, at any sane hourly valuation, exceeds the float saving quickly, and card-on-file autopay removes the reminder entirely for recurring clients. The honest total for a firm sending 40 invoices a month: card fees of roughly $290 at these rates if everyone pays by card, against perhaps $200–400 of combined float and chasing labour avoided — which is why the winning policy is usually hybrid: card offered (and pre-authorized for recurring work), e-Transfer stated as the default on terms, large invoices nudged off card where the relationship allows. A policy template that survives real clients

The hybrid policy, written the way firms actually state it: "Invoices are payable by Interac e-Transfer (preferred), credit card via the link on the invoice, or pre-authorized payment for recurring engagements. Card payments include a convenience option — clients choosing card accept the processing cost is reflected in our rates." Three details do the work. E-Transfer named first and marked preferred (default effects are powerful). Card present and easy (you're buying speed where the client values it). Recurring work moved to pre-authorization (the reminder problem deleted, not managed). What not to do in Canada: improvised card surcharging — card-network rules and provincial consumer law constrain it, and the informal "3% card fee" line on an invoice creates more disputes than the float it recovers. If you want card costs covered, price them into your rates openly, the way the template above does.

The metric to watch

Days sales outstanding — average days from invoice to cash — is the single number this whole decision lives in. Measure it for a quarter before changing anything (your accounting app reports it: FreshBooks, Wave and QuickBooks all surface invoice aging), then a quarter after adopting card links plus terms changes. A five-day improvement on $30,000 of monthly billings frees about $5,000 of permanent working capital; at any cost of borrowing, that's the honest yardstick for the fee line — not the fee line alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is it legal to pass card fees to clients in Canada? Surcharging credit card payments is constrained by card-network rules and, for consumers, provincial law — the permitted path (since the 2022 network rule changes) requires registration, notice and caps, and excludes Interac debit. Most small firms are better served pricing fees into rates openly rather than surcharging informally.

What's a good default invoice term? Net 14 for services is common and defensible; Net 30 is a concession to larger clients' payables cycles. The term matters less than the reminder system and the payment rails attached to the invoice.

Do accounting apps charge extra for card payments on invoices? The app is usually free to send; the payment rail charges the percentage. FreshBooks, Wave and Stripe all follow this shape — the fee is in the money movement, not the software.
