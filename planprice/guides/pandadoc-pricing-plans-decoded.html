PandaDoc Pricing Plans: Starter, Business, and the CRM Gate

Published 2026-10-12

PandaDoc's pricing page leads with US$19 a seat — and that number has misled a lot of sales teams. The plan most sales teams actually need costs US$49 a seat, because CRM integrations, bulk sending, deal rooms, and approval workflows are all gated behind the Business tier. This guide maps the real feature gates, runs the per-team math, and shows when PandaDoc replaces DocuSign rather than adding to it.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm the current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

Free, Starter, Business, Enterprise: the real split

PandaDoc sells per seat — every user who sends documents needs one — with prices checked October 2026. Free (US$0) covers up to 60 documents a year with the drag-and-drop editor, signing order, and audit trail. Starter is US$19/seat/month billed annually (US$35 month-to-month): unlimited documents and e-signatures, the rich-media editor, real-time tracking, and five templates. Business is US$49/seat/month annually (US$65 monthly): everything in Starter plus CRM integrations, custom branding, the content library, deal rooms, approval workflows, web forms, and bulk send. Enterprise is custom-quoted, per seat or per document, adding CPQ, SSO, and API access.

Annual billing saves up to 46% on Starter — one of the steepest annual discounts in the category — which tells you how much PandaDoc wants the commitment.

The CRM gate: what US$19 doesn't buy

This is the most common pricing complaint in PandaDoc reviews, and it is worth stating plainly: the Starter plan has no CRM integration. If your sales process runs through HubSpot, Pipedrive, or Salesforce, Starter is a document-signing tool, not a proposal tool — the integration that makes PandaDoc valuable starts at Business. The same gate holds custom branding, the content library, approval workflows, and bulk send.

For a five-person team, the Starter-to-Business step adds US$1,500 a year (5 × US$30 × 12). Teams that sign up on Starter and discover the gap after onboarding either pay the step-up or rebuild their workflow — the expensive version of the same lesson. Price the plan your workflow needs on day one, not the plan the headline advertises.

Year-one math: 5- and 10-sender teams

On Business, annual: five senders cost 5 × US$49 × 12 = US$2,940 a year; ten senders cost US$5,880. The same teams month-to-month pay US$3,900 and US$7,800 — the annual commitment saves roughly a third. Note the license-splitting strategy real teams use: only the people who build and manage proposals need full seats; occasional reviewers of pricing or legal language can often work with fewer licenses, though that adds planning overhead.

Now the stack math that matters: most sales teams buy the CRM seat and the document seat as a pair, so price the proposal stack together — the per-seat document cost is only half the invoice once the CRM subscription is included.

What reviewers say

PandaDoc holds 4.7 out of 5 on G2 from over 3,400 reviews — the top proposal-software rating in G2's Spring 2026 rankings, with a 93% likelihood-to-recommend score — and 8.7 out of 10 on TrustRadius from 570+ verified reviews. A Deputy Head of Marketing gave it 4.5/5 in July 2026: the Content Library "made the biggest difference" for keeping proposals consistent, while flagging that the Business-tier gate forced them to split licenses. The counterweight: Trustpilot sits at 3.3/5, where billing and support complaints concentrate — the same per-seat compounding this guide models. PandaDoc is loved by the salespeople who use it daily and questioned by the finance teams who pay per seat for it.

PandaDoc vs DocuSign: the replace-not-add calculation

PandaDoc's best financial argument is consolidation. If PandaDoc's unlimited e-signatures let you cancel a DocuSign subscription, the comparison is not PandaDoc vs nothing — it is PandaDoc Business vs DocuSign's per-envelope plans. A team already paying for DocuSign should net the cancelled subscription against PandaDoc's seat cost before judging the US$49 number. If you need both — DocuSign for legal-grade agreement workflows and PandaDoc for proposals — budget them as separate tools and don't expect one to replace the other.

The decision framework

This is a buying-decision guide, not a setup procedure — the decision framework near the end is the whole process.

  1. Start pricing at Business (US$49), not Starter. If you need CRM integration, branding, or approvals — and sales teams do — Starter is not your plan.
  2. Go annual. The 46% Starter / 25% Business annual discount is among the largest in SaaS; month-to-month is an evaluation tool, not a strategy.
  3. Split licenses deliberately between proposal builders and occasional reviewers — but count the admin overhead of doing so.
  4. Net out any DocuSign cancellation before judging the seat price; consolidation is PandaDoc's real discount.
  5. Read the Trustpilot complaints before the G2 praise — billing disputes cluster there, and they preview your renewal negotiation.

Frequently asked questions

Does PandaDoc have a free plan?

Yes — a free e-sign plan covering up to 60 documents a year with the editor, signing order, and audit trail. It is a genuine free tier, not a trial, but it lacks the document editor's paid features and all CRM integrations.

Does PandaDoc integrate with HubSpot on the Starter plan?

No. CRM integrations — HubSpot, Pipedrive, Salesforce — require the Business plan at US$49/seat/month annually. This is the single most important feature gate in PandaDoc's pricing.

How much does PandaDoc cost for 10 users?

On Business with annual billing: 10 × US$49 × 12 = US$5,880 a year. On Starter: US$2,280 a year — but without CRM integration, branding, or bulk send.
