Payment Links vs Invoices: Fee and Speed Differences for Small Sellers (Canada, 2026)

Published 2026-11-03 · Last updated 2026-11-03 · By Mike, Editor

$3.10. That is the processing fee on a $100 sale paid online through Square in Canada, whether the customer arrived through a payment link or paid an invoice, at the rates Square published on November 3, 2026: 2.8% plus 30 cents. Sellers who choose between the two tools expecting a fee saving are solving the wrong problem. The tools differ in workflow, speed of setup, and what happens when a customer pays late, not in the headline rate.

Rates are from Square Canada's fee schedule and its Invoices pricing page, checked November 3, 2026. Two rows deserve attention. Charging a card on file through an invoice costs more than sending the invoice for the customer to pay online, $2.35 more on a $500 invoice. And an invoice that ends up paid in person at the counter prices on the cheaper in-person rates, including the Interac debit rate. The channel the money actually travels through sets the fee, not the document that asked for it.

The runbook: links for fixed offers, invoices for named bills

  1. List what you sell repeatedly at a fixed price. A class seat, a standard service, a product with one price. These belong on payment links.
  2. Create the link. From Square Dashboard, Payment Links, choose collect a payment, sell an item, or accept donations, add the title, amount and any custom fields, then share the link, QR code or buy button. Square's Canadian buy button page confirms the standard rate of 2.8% plus 30 cents, no monthly fees, and Apple Pay, Google Pay and Afterpay at checkout.
  3. List what you bill to a named customer after the work. Custom jobs, deposits, balances due, anything with a due date. These belong on invoices.
  4. Send the invoice on the free plan first. Square Invoices Free, $0 a month, includes unlimited invoices, estimates and contracts, recurring invoices, deposits, automatic payment reminders, real time status tracking, and the ability to set a late payment fee.
  5. Upgrade only for the named features. Invoices Plus is $30 a month. It adds milestone based payment schedules, batch invoicing, multi-package estimates, auto-conversion of accepted estimates to invoices, and custom templates and fields. If none of those is a weekly need, Plus buys nothing, because it does not change the processing rate.
  6. Review the mix monthly. Count card on file charges. Each one paid a premium over the online invoice rate for the convenience of not asking the customer to click. Sometimes that trade is worth it. Make it a decision, not a default.

Speed, honestly separated

A payment link is faster to create and faster to reuse. One link serves every buyer, can sit in a social post, an email signature or a printed QR code, and needs no customer record before the sale. An invoice is slower to issue, because it is addressed to someone, with line items, a due date and a paper trail. That slowness buys control: deposits before work starts, reminders after the due date passes, a late fee if you set one, and a record of what is paid and unpaid.

Reviewers on Capterra Canada describe Square Payment Links in similar terms, praising how easy it is to collect card payments and the absence of monthly fees, while noting the per transaction charge as the cost of that simplicity. That matches the math above. The link is a convenience tool priced per use.

The kill criterion for the wrong tool

Stop using payment links for billed work if two or more payments a month arrive late, unlabelled, or hard to match to a job. At that point the business is not saving time, it is spending it on reconciliation, and invoices with reminders and deposits are the cheaper tool even at the same rate. Stop paying for Invoices Plus if a full month passes without a milestone schedule, a batch run, or a multi-package estimate. The $30 buys features, not status, and $360 a year is real money at small seller scale.
