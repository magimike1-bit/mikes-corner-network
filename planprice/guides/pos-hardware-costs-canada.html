POS Hardware Costs in Canada: Readers, Terminals and Registers Priced

Published 2026-10-29 · Last updated 2026-10-29

What does a card reader actually cost in Canada — $69, $429, or $34.95 a month forever? All three answers are on sale right now, and the difference is not the plastic. It is the ownership model. Some vendors sell a device once. One major Canadian processor rents its terminal by the month. That choice, repeated over two or three years, matters more than the sticker on any single reader. The rented Go Terminal passes the purchase price of a Helcim Smart Terminal ($429) at roughly month 13, and passes a Square Terminal ($399) before the end of month 12. By month 24 the rental has cost $838.80 for a device the merchant still does not own. Rental is not automatically a bad deal — it can bundle replacement and support, and it keeps the upfront cost near zero in an opening month when cash is tight. The trap is signing a rental without doing this multiplication, or discovering the term and any return or restocking conditions only when switching processors. Contract length and termination terms are silent costs in payment deals generally: check them on the hardware line as carefully as on the processing line.

The hardware is tied to the processor

A Square Reader does not run another processor's payments. A Helcim Smart Terminal is a Helcim device. Moneris Go hardware runs Moneris. That lock-in is the real cost driver behind the table: a processor switch usually means a hardware switch too. Work on processor migrations at retailers makes the point concretely — a change of acquirer has repeatedly arrived together with a new POS rollout, new terminals included, rather than as a settings change on the old ones.

So price hardware on the life of the processing relationship, not the life of the device. A $69 reader you replace in a year because you outgrew the processor was cheap. A $999 register bought a month before a processor switch was not, however well it was built. Before buying the top of any vendor's range, ask one question: if the processing rate stopped suiting the business in 18 months, what in this setup could be kept? On most of these stacks, the honest answer is the cash drawer and little else.

Buy, rent, or start with a reader?

A short decision sequence:

  1. Start with the cheapest device that takes the payments you actually get. Tap and chip at a market stall or a single counter is a $69 Square Reader or a $239 Helcim Reader paired with a phone or tablet you already own — not a register.
  2. Buy a terminal when receipts and a standalone device matter. At $399 to $429 CAD, a Square Terminal or Helcim Smart Terminal is a one-time cost that ends. Compare any rental quote against that line first.
  3. Rent only with the term in front of you. Total the monthly figure over the full term ($34.95 × 24 = $838.80), confirm what happens at the end, and confirm the return conditions before signing.
  4. Step up to a register last. The $999 Square Register (2nd generation) earns its price at a busy, fixed counter with a customer-facing screen — and is the most money stranded if the processor changes.

Success looks like this: the hardware total on the quote, multiplied out over 24 months and in CAD, is smaller than the processing fees it supports — and nobody on the team is surprised that changing processors next year means changing devices too.
