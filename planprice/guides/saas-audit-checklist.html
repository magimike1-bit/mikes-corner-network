The SaaS Audit Checklist for Small Business

Published 2026-10-12

Somewhere in your stack is a subscription nobody has opened in months — and it's renewing itself. The fix is a two-hour audit you can run this afternoon with a spreadsheet, your card statements, and admin access to your apps. This guide gives you the procedure, the math that justifies it, and the renewal calendar that keeps the savings permanent.

Size the prize first

Before spending an afternoon on this, know what the afternoon is worth. Zylo's 2026 SaaS Management Index puts average license waste at US$1,785 per employee per year, with 25–30% of total SaaS spend effectively wasted. Vertice's Q2 2026 data is starker: 65% of licenses are unused or underutilized — 14% fully unused, 51% underused. And per-employee SaaS spend jumped 21.9% year over year to US$4,830, driven by AI line items and renewal price hikes.

For a 12-person team, the Zylo average implies roughly US$21,400 a year in waste. Now the procedure.

The 2-hour audit procedure

Work in this order — each step feeds the next:

  1. Build the inventory (30 min). Pull every recurring software charge from 90 days of card statements and expense reports, plus anything in your identity provider's app list (Google Workspace: Security > Access and data control > Third-party apps; Microsoft 365: Entra ID > Enterprise applications). Record vendor, plan, seats, price, and who pays.
  2. Match every seat to a human (25 min). Export each app's user list and line it up against your current roster, employees and contractors. Flag every seat assigned to someone who left, changed roles, or never joined. These are your zombie seats; they're the single most common thing a first audit turns up.
  3. Check last-login dates (20 min). Most admin consoles (Microsoft 365 Admin Center, Zoom's Adoption report) show per-user activity. Anything billed but untouched for 60+ days is a removal or downgrade candidate. Use simple thresholds: mailboxes with fewer than five weekly sends, seats with under ten monthly logins.
  4. Hunt duplicates (15 min). Compare by category, not vendor name: two video tools, two project trackers, two email platforms.
  5. Check the hidden meters (15 min). Seats aren't the only meter. Some vendors charge per device connected or per integration — a cost per device "even if it was just reading data." Scan invoices for integration, API, and device-count line items, not just seat counts.
  6. Build the renewal calendar (15 min). For every surviving subscription, record the renewal date and the cancellation-notice deadline (usually 30–90 days earlier), and set a reminder 45 days before each. Tropic reports that 80% of its customers have had a contract auto-renew without their knowledge — the calendar prevents that.

The zombie-seat math: a 12-person team

Take a typical 12-person team paying for Slack Pro (US$7.25/user/mo billed annually, checked 2026-10-05), Zoom Workplace Pro (US$13.33/user/mo, checked 2026-10-11), and Microsoft 365 Business Standard (CA$17.40/user/mo on annual commitment in Canada, checked 2026-10-05). The audit finds: two Slack seats for departed staff, three Zoom licenses held by people who only ever use chat, and two M365 seats on a tier nobody's advanced features justify.

The cleanup: deactivate the two Slack seats (US$174/year recovered), move the three chat-only users off Zoom Pro (US$480/year), and right-size the two M365 seats. That's over US$650 a year from three apps — before touching duplicates or the renewal calendar. Downgrading M365 E5-style seats that skip advanced security tools to E3-class tiers can cut the per-user cost by up to 42%.

From audit to renewal calendar

Every renewal is a decision: renew, resize, renegotiate, consolidate, or cancel — and the calendar turns that into a date with a name on it. When a reminder fires 45 days out, re-run the seat match for that vendor, check whether a cheaper tier now covers your usage, and get a competing quote if the price moved. That fifteen-minute ritual, repeated per renewal, is what keeps the savings from decaying.

Assign one owner. If nobody owns the calendar, nobody will have the evidence — or the nerve — to cancel anything when the reminder fires.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a small business audit SaaS subscriptions?

Quarterly for the seat match (30 minutes once the inventory exists), annually for the full audit including duplicates and tier reviews. Re-run the seat check 45 days before any major renewal.

What percentage of SaaS licenses go unused?

Zylo's 2026 index reports 36% of licenses unused against recommended utilization levels; Vertice's Q2 2026 data puts combined unused and underutilized licenses at 65%.

Do I need SaaS management software to run an audit?

No. Card statements, expense reports, your identity provider's app list, and each tool's admin console cover the full procedure above. Management platforms automate the repetition, which matters past a few dozen apps — but the first audit is a spreadsheet job.
