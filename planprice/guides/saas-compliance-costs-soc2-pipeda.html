SaaS Compliance Costs: SOC 2, PIPEDA and What Vendors Charge For

Published 2026-10-12

Every enterprise RFP asks for the SOC 2 report. Almost nobody asks what that badge costs — the vendor pursuing it, or the customer buying from them. Compliance has two bills: the vendor's (audits, platforms, hundreds of staff hours) and yours (the tier gates, the tooling upgrades, the security reviews). For a Canadian small business, PIPEDA adds a third. Here is where compliance actually shows up in your SaaS spend.

General information, not legal advice. Compliance obligations depend on your industry, province, and data — confirm requirements with qualified counsel.

Compliance has two bills: theirs and yours

The vendor's bill first, because it explains the prices you see. Getting SOC 2 compliant typically runs $30,000–$50,000 for most SaaS teams, and can climb toward $150,000 with readiness work, tools, and internal effort. The pieces: Type 2 audit fees of $7,000–$50,000, readiness assessments from $10,000, compliance-automation platforms at $5,000–$30,000 a year, and 100–200 staff hours. A five-person startup's first year runs $20,000–$60,000 all-in.

Founders who have paid it describe landing $30,000–$40,000 all-in deals by timing purchases near quarter-end — and note that buyers who need the badge eventually are often fine with a credible commitment and a timeline today, rather than waiting for the certificate. That is useful intelligence if you're the buyer being asked to wait: a vendor "working toward SOC 2" with a dated plan is normal; a vendor with no plan is a risk you price.

What 'SOC 2 certified' costs you as the customer

The vendor's compliance spend lands in your bill three ways. List prices carry it. Audits and platforms are overhead; overhead is priced into tiers. Security features are tier-gated. SSO/SAML, audit logs, and data-loss-prevention controls routinely sit one or two tiers above the plan you'd otherwise buy — Slack gates SSO to Business+ at $15 a user a month, for example. The compliance feature you need can force a 2× tier jump on its own.

Your own stack gets upgraded. The compliance process forces tooling upgrades buyers rarely budget: higher Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace tiers, endpoint protection at roughly $10–$20 per computer a month, device management at $5–$10, vulnerability scanning, patch management. None of it is the SaaS subscription — all of it is the cost of being compliant while using SaaS.

PIPEDA: the Canadian buyer's actual obligations

PIPEDA applies to any business handling personal information in commercial activity, at any size — there is no small-business exemption. The duties in practice: name a privacy lead, identify why you collect each piece of data before you collect it, get meaningful consent, limit use and retention, safeguard the data, publish your practices, answer access requests within 30 days, and report breaches with a real risk of significant harm. Critically, you stay responsible for the data even when a vendor processes it for you — outsourcing to SaaS does not outsource accountability.

The good news: the minimum viable posture for a ten-person organization is six concrete elements — a named privacy officer, a written privacy policy, a complaint procedure, encryption on devices, a basic retention practice, and a 30-day access-request process. Proportionality applies to how you implement them, not whether they exist. That's a policy afternoon and an encryption check, not a consulting engagement.

Five compliance questions before you buy

  1. Which tier includes the SOC 2 report, SSO, and audit logs? Price the compliant configuration, not the entry tier.
  2. Where is my data stored, and can I get Canadian residency? PIPEDA's accountability principle follows your data across borders — know where it goes.
  3. What does your DPA cover on breach notification? You owe the OPC and affected individuals a report; your vendor's contract should support that timeline.
  4. What happens to my data at cancellation? Retention and deletion terms are a compliance question, not just an exit question.
  5. Do I actually need the badge yet? If no customer or regulator requires SOC 2 from your vendors today, a credible vendor roadmap beats paying the tier premium now.

Frequently asked questions

How much does SOC 2 certification cost?

For the company pursuing it: typically $30,000–$50,000 all-in for a SaaS team, up to $150,000 with readiness work and tools. A five-person startup's first year runs $20,000–$60,000. As a buyer, you don't pay this directly — you pay it through tier pricing and gated security features.

Does PIPEDA apply to small businesses?

Yes — PIPEDA covers any business handling personal information in commercial activity, with no size exemption. The minimum posture for a ten-person firm is six elements: a named privacy officer, a written privacy policy, a complaint procedure, device encryption, a retention practice, and a 30-day access-request process.

Why do compliance features cost extra in SaaS pricing?

Because they are expensive to build and certify: the vendor's audit, platform, and engineering costs are real, and vendors recover them from the customers who need the features — which is why SSO, audit logs, and DLP controls cluster in higher tiers.
