Vendor Lock-In and Exit Costs: Price the Escape Before You Sign

Published 2026-10-12

A vendor's sales demo answers one question: does this tool solve your problem today. It never answers the question that matters two years later: how hard is it to leave? Lock-in isn't a fee — it's the slow disappearance of your bargaining power. This guide prices the escape before you sign: the five exit-cost lines, a worked estimator, and the pre-sign audit that keeps you free.

General information, not financial or legal advice. Exit terms vary — verify them in your own contract.

The five exit-cost lines

Leaving a SaaS vendor costs money in five places, and the subscription was never the biggest one:

  1. Data export and migration labor. Exporting, cleaning, mapping, and importing your data. Data transformation alone can eat around 20% of a total migration budget.
  2. Integration rewiring. Every integration has to be rebuilt on the new platform. A small team might have five — each one is still a project.
  3. Retraining. The team has to learn the new tool. Small per person, real in aggregate.
  4. Business disruption. Downtime, parallel runs, the weeks where two systems are half-alive — lost productivity, not an invoice.
  5. Contractual exit penalties. Early-termination fees, forfeited prepaid value, auto-renewal clauses that fire mid-migration. Multi-year agreements often carry steep early-exit penalties.

These hidden costs can exceed the savings that motivated the switch — price the exit before signing, not during the breakup.

Exit-cost estimator: worked examples

Example 1 — switching CRMs. A HubSpot-to-Salesforce migration for a 50,000-contact CRM breaks down as: data export and cleanup, 40 hours (US$6,000 at contractor rates); import mapping, 20 hours (US$3,000); integration rewiring, 60 hours (US$9,000); training, 10 hours (US$1,500). Total: US$19,500 — before either subscription. A 10-person team on Salesforce Starter at US$25/user/mo (checked 2026-10-11) eyeing Pipedrive Growth at US$39/user/mo annual (checked 2026-10-11) should budget a migration project in the low thousands even before the subscription delta.

Example 2 — the proprietary-format penalty. Notion stores data in a proprietary JSON structure; Airtable uses a custom relational format. The trap: start with "portable" data, adopt the proprietary features (databases, automations), and discover the data has become non-exportable. Test the export before you commit: if your data doesn't come out in a usable standard format, price the lock-in as part of the purchase.

Example 3 — the 30-day window. After cancellation, Salesforce gives you 30 days of data access. Everything — custom objects, full activity history, attachments — must be exported in that window. Miss it and years of customer history are gone. Full mid-market Salesforce migrations regularly run US$25,000–$80,000+. The export window is the deadline that matters.

The pre-sign lock-in audit

Run these five checks before committing to any tool that will hold important data:

  1. Export format. Export a sample of your real data today (most tools offer a trial). If it comes out clean in CSV/JSON, the exit is cheap. If it's proprietary or partial, the exit is a project — price it.
  2. Exit window. How long do you have data access after cancellation? Thirty days is common; confirm it in writing and calendar the deadline from day one.
  3. Integration inventory. List every integration you'd have to rebuild. Each one is a line item on the exit bill — and a reason to prefer tools with standard APIs.
  4. Contract exit terms. Auto-renewal clauses with short cancellation windows, early-termination penalties, and price-increase caps (or their absence) — the same flags as any contract review, read with exit in mind.
  5. Integration pricing. Some vendors charge per device connected or per integration — a meter "even if it was just reading data." Every paid integration is another dependency to rebuild, so check integration pricing before adopting, not when leaving.

Lock-in is leverage

The deepest cost of lock-in isn't the migration bill — it's your negotiating position. When leaving is painful enough, you stop comparing alternatives and stop negotiating; you renew not because it's the best option but because escape is too expensive. A small business that can't credibly walk away has lost its leverage — and a vendor that knows you can't leave has little reason to keep earning you.

The exit brief is also a renewal tool: a team that can show a tested export, a current integration list, and a priced migration walks into renewal with a credible walk-away — and vendors negotiate differently with buyers who have one.

Frequently asked questions

What is vendor lock-in in SaaS?

The accumulated cost of data in proprietary formats, workflows built around vendor-specific features, and integrations that only exist because of one vendor's API — making switching expensive enough that you stop considering it.

How much does it cost to switch CRM?

A 50,000-contact HubSpot-to-Salesforce migration runs about US$19,500 in labor at contractor rates; mid-market Salesforce exits regularly cost US$25,000–$80,000+. Small-team switches cost less in absolute terms but follow the same five cost lines.

How do I avoid SaaS vendor lock-in?

Run the pre-sign audit: test the export format, confirm the exit window, inventory integrations, read the exit terms, and check integration pricing. Prefer standard data formats and APIs — and keep the exit brief current so the walk-away stays credible.
