Seat-License Optimization: Cutting Per-Seat Waste Without Cutting Tools

Published 2026-10-12

Per-seat pricing has a ratchet: seats get added when people join and rarely get removed when they leave, change roles, or quietly stop using the tool. Vertice's Q2 2026 data puts 65% of licenses as unused or underutilized — 14% fully unused, 51% underused. Reclaiming seats is the least disruptive saving available: nobody loses a tool they use. This guide shows where the waste hides, which lever to pull per seat, and what the cleanup is worth.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm the current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

The four zombie-seat categories

Waste seats aren't one thing — each has a different fix:

  1. Zero-activity seats. No logins in the review window — departed employees, ended contractors, or people provisioned for a finished project. Reclaim these outright.
  2. Low-activity seats. Occasional logins but no real use. These need a conversation, not an automatic cancellation — sometimes occasional use is legitimate.
  3. Over-tiered seats. Active users on a plan more expensive than their usage requires — nobody loses access, you're just stopping payment for capability nobody touches. Downgrading M365 E5-class seats that skip the advanced security tools to E3-class tiers can cut the per-user cost by up to 42%.
  4. Duplicate and admin seats. The extra paid account an admin created "just in case," or the second seat for someone who already has one under a different email. Pure bookkeeping errors that bill every month.

Use simple thresholds to sort them: mailboxes with fewer than five weekly sends, seats with under ten monthly logins. Export the admin console's user list, merge it with the billing table, and sort by wasted spend.

Deactivate vs downgrade vs reassign

Three levers, and picking the wrong one either wastes money or annoys people:

Which seat lever to pull, by seat category.
Seat categoryLeverWhat happens on the invoice
Zero activity, 60+ daysDeactivateSeat drops off at the next billing event; nothing to migrate
Active but light usageDowngrade tierSame login, cheaper plan (e.g., Zoom Business → Basic for chat-only users)
Role changed, still employedReassignMove the seat to the new hire or the role that needs it; buy nothing new
Dormant on fair-billing vendorsDo nothingSlack checks daily and credits dormant users pro-rata — the vendor already did it

Slack's fair-billing policy checks daily for active versus dormant users and credits accounts pro-rata for dormant ones — so before deactivating Slack seats, check whether the credit already landed. For vendors without fair billing, deactivation is the move — but check the contract first: some annual commitments won't reduce the seat count mid-term, and the saving lands at renewal instead.

Worked example: a 15-person team

The team pays for Slack Pro (US$7.25/user/mo billed annually, checked 2026-10-05), Zoom Workplace Pro (US$13.33/user/mo, checked 2026-10-11), and Microsoft 365 Business Standard (CA$17.40/user/mo on annual commitment in Canada, checked 2026-10-05). The seat match finds: three Slack seats for people who left, four Zoom Pro licenses used only for chat, and three M365 seats where nobody touches the premium features.

Cleanup math: 3 × US$7.25 × 12 = US$261/year off Slack. Moving four chat-only users off Zoom Pro saves 4 × US$13.33 × 12 = US$640/year (less whatever a cheaper tier costs, if they need one at all). Right-sizing three M365 seats at the E5-to-E3-style 42% reduction saves roughly 3 × CA$17.40 × 12 × 0.42 ≈ CA$263/year. Total: over US$900 a year plus CA$263 — from seats, not tools. Nobody lost access to anything they used.

When cutting seats backfires

One trap to check before you cut: volume-discount removal. Many contracts grant a per-seat discount contingent on a seat-count minimum. Drop below the tier and you don't just save on the bill; you can lose the volume discount on the remaining seats, so the effective per-seat cost goes up. The vendor will helpfully point this out and recommend renewing at the higher count.

The counter: renegotiate the tier explicitly rather than accepting the contract's default fallback rate — most buyers don't know the tier itself is negotiable. Model the cut both ways — at the current rate versus the fallback rate — before deciding the cleanup is worth it.

Frequently asked questions

Should I deactivate or downgrade unused licenses?

Deactivate zero-activity seats (60+ days untouched). Downgrade active-but-light users to a cheaper tier. Reassign seats when someone changes roles instead of buying new.

How do I find zombie seats?

Export each app's user list with last-login dates and match every seat against your current roster. Anything assigned to someone who left, or untouched for 60+ days, is the starting list.

Does Slack charge for inactive users?

Slack's fair-billing policy checks daily for active versus dormant users and credits accounts pro-rata for dormant ones — so inactive users on paid plans are largely credited automatically. Most vendors don't do this; for them, deactivation is on you.
