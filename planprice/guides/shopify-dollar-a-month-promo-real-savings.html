Shopify's $1-a-Month Promo: What It Actually Saves (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Shopify's standing offer for new merchants — three days free, then $1 a month for your first three months — is easy to dismiss as a token. Whether it is depends on the tier you pick, because Shopify's Canadian list prices are not token-sized: Basic is C$49 a month, Grow is C$132, and Advanced is C$517. This guide prices the promo honestly at each tier, including the part nobody mentions: the decision the promo is really paying you to postpone.

On Basic the promo is a nice discount on a cheap plan. On Advanced it is $1,548 — real money — which is exactly the trap: the promo's value scales with the tier, nudging new stores to start two tiers above their traffic. An Advanced plan's extra reporting and duty/tax features earn nothing for a store with eleven orders. Start on the tier your store needs, bank the matching saving, and upgrade when a feature gate — not a discount — says so.

What the promo doesn't discount

The $1 covers the platform subscription. It does not touch transaction economics: payment processing rates apply from the first sale, paid apps bill on their own cycles, and a premium theme is a one-time cost the promo ignores. Budget the store's first quarter as $3 of Shopify plus everything else at full price.

After month three

The plan renews at full list. The right use of the promo window is a decision deadline: three months of real orders tells you whether the store works and which tier it needs — set the downgrade/upgrade review for week ten, not the day the first full invoice lands. Offer terms are Shopify's and change; the Shopify page carries the current status as last checked.

Frequently asked questions

Does the promo apply if I downgrade later? The promotion attaches to the new-merchant signup period; changing plans is a Shopify account action — confirm current terms on Shopify's own pages before relying on any interaction between the promo and a plan change. Is the $1 charged in CAD? Canadian merchants on CAD billing are charged in Canadian dollars per Shopify's billing setup; the saving table above uses CAD list prices, so the arithmetic holds either way. Can existing stores get it? No — it's a new-merchant offer; the standing discount levers for existing stores are annual-billing savings on the subscription and app-stack discipline, both covered in our Shopify page and app-cost guides. What should I actually do with the three months? Treat them as a paid experiment with a deadline: launch on Basic, spend the saved $144 on the store's first real photos or its first ad test, and let week-ten data — not the promo's expiry pressure — choose the tier you renew on.

Frequently asked questions

Does the promo include Shopify Payments rates? No — the promotion covers the platform subscription. Payment processing applies at the standard rates for your plan and country from the first sale.

What if I pick the wrong tier during the promo? Plan changes are self-serve in Shopify; because the promo's value scales with tier, there's no benefit to starting high 'to use the discount' — start at need and adjust as data arrives.

Is annual billing a better saving than the promo? After the promo window, yes — annual terms on the subscription are the standing lever, typically worth about a month or more per year against monthly billing at list. The two stack sequentially: promo first, annual at renewal if the store has proven itself.

The decision the promo should actually fund

There is one more honest way to read the $144–$1,548: it's Shopify paying for your experiment. The stores that use the window well spend it answering the only question that matters — will strangers buy this, at this price, through this checkout? — with a real product page, real traffic from one channel, and real orders. The stores that use it badly spend it perfecting a theme. If the experiment fails, the promo has saved you from learning the same lesson at $49–$517 a month. If it succeeds, the tier conversation takes care of itself at renewal. Either way, the promo is best understood as research funding with an expiry date — spend it on evidence.
