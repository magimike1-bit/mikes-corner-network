SignNow Pricing: Business, Business Premium, and the Invite Cap

Published 2026-10-12

SignNow inverts the usual SaaS model: every plan includes unlimited users, but signature invites are metered — 100 per user per year on standard plans. That makes it the cheapest credible e-signature tool for teams with many occasional senders, and a trap for teams with a few heavy ones. This guide explains the invite model, the real plan costs, and the compliance ceiling.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm the current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

Business, Business Premium, Enterprise, Site License

SignNow's plans, checked October 2026 (USD). Business: US$8/user/month billed annually (US$20 monthly) — unlimited templates, mobile apps, cloud storage integrations. Business Premium: US$15 (US$30 monthly) — adds bulk sending, reminders, signing links, and document groups. Enterprise: US$30 (US$50 monthly) — smart fillable fields, conditional documents, advanced threat protection. Site License: from US$1.50 per signature invite — usage-based, for high-volume deployments, with full API access and CRM integrations.

Annual billing saves up to 60% versus monthly — the Business plan's US$20-to-US$8 drop is the steepest annual discount in the e-signature category. There is no permanent free plan, only a 7-day free trial (plus a 30-day money-back guarantee).

The invite model: 100 per user per year, explained

Here is the number that decides your bill. Standard plans include 100 signature invites per user per year (10 a month on monthly plans), with unlimited users, templates, and storage. Invite usage is shared across the account, and going over the allowance costs extra per invite.

The budgeting question is therefore not "how many people need accounts" but "how many signature invites will the account use this year." A 30-person company where each person sends 20 documents a year uses 600 invites — comfortably inside a single Business plan's allowance. A 3-person sales team sending 500 contracts a year blows past 300 invites and needs the overage math or the Site License. Unlimited users helps the first team enormously and the second team not at all.

High-volume senders: when the Site License wins

At US$1.50 per invite, the Site License beats per-seat plans once invite volume is high and user count is higher still — the classic case is HR onboarding or property management, where dozens of staff each send a steady stream of documents. API plans start around US$146 a month for 1,000 yearly invites for teams embedding signing in their own product. If your sending is concentrated in a few power users, though, a per-seat rival with unlimited sending (Dropbox Sign) or a higher invite allowance may price better — run both models at your actual volume.

What reviewers say

On G2's SignNow pricing page, reviewers consistently describe it as the value choice for small businesses and solo professionals who need reliable e-signatures without DocuSign or Adobe pricing — with real estate, insurance, legal services, accounting, and non-profits the most cited industries. Mid-market reviewers highlight Business Premium for bulk sending and reminders; budget-conscious organizations cite unlimited document sending and templates as the reasons they chose it over pricier alternatives. The consistent undertone: SignNow wins on price, and buyers who need advanced workflow routing or API depth look upmarket.

The compliance ceiling

All plans comply with the US ESIGN Act and the EU's eIDAS regulation, and every document carries an audit trail. But HIPAA compliance with a signed BAA is not included in the standard plans — healthcare and other regulated buyers need to confirm the compliance path (reported as the quote-only Site License tier) before shortlisting. This is the detail that disqualifies SignNow late in regulated evaluations, so check it first if it applies to you.

The decision framework

This is a buying-decision guide, not a setup procedure — the decision framework near the end is the whole process.

  1. Budget invites, not seats. Estimate annual signature invites first; the user count barely matters.
  2. Go annual. The 60% annual discount is the largest in the category — month-to-month is for evaluation only.
  3. Check the compliance ceiling first if HIPAA or a signed BAA is required; standard plans don't include it.
  4. Model the Site License once the team passes ~1,000 invites a year with many senders.
  5. Compare against unlimited-sending rivals (Dropbox Sign) when a few power users drive most of the volume.

Frequently asked questions

Is SignNow free?

No — there is no permanent free plan, only a 7-day free trial and a 30-day money-back guarantee. Every paid plan includes unlimited users, templates, and storage.

What is a signature invite?

Each request sent for signature. Standard plans include 100 invites per user per year (shared across the account); additional invites cost extra, roughly US$0.96–3.60 each depending on tier.

Is SignNow legally binding?

Yes — signatures collected with SignNow are legally binding under laws like the US ESIGN Act and the EU's eIDAS regulation, and each document includes an audit trail.
