Slack Pricing Decoded for Small Canadian Teams (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Slack's price list is two numbers — Pro at $7.25 per user/month billed annually, Business+ at $15 (checked October 11, 2026) — wrapped around a free plan whose real price is amnesia: message history on the free tier is limited to the most recent 90 days. This decode is about the moment that limit, not the seat price, writes your budget.

The free plan's hidden invoice

A five-person team can run on free Slack happily until the day someone searches for a decision made four months ago and finds the wall. By then the workspace holds the team's working memory, and upgrading is no longer a choice but a ransom — Pro at $36.25 a month for five seats, billed annually. The strategy this suggests: decide early whether Slack is a chat tool (free forever, memory lives elsewhere) or the company's record (budget Pro from the start). Both are legitimate; drifting between them is the expensive option.

Pro vs Business+: who needs the $15 tier

Business+ doubles the seat price for enterprise-shaped features: SAML single sign-on, compliance exports, and richer admin controls. A small Canadian team needs exactly one thing on that list to justify it — SSO mandated by a client security review. Everything else is headroom. If nobody can name the feature, you're on Pro.

The comparison that matters

Slack's real rival for small teams isn't another chat app — it's the suite you're already paying for. Microsoft Teams is bundled into Microsoft 365 seats (from C$9.50/user/month), and Google Chat rides with Workspace (from C$9.20). A team already on either suite pays an effective $0 for bundled chat; Slack has to be $7.25-a-seat better than free, which for many teams it is — channels, huddles and integrations are genuinely ahead — but the comparison to make is "Slack vs $0 bundled," not "Slack vs Teams' sticker." Full numbers on the Slack page and the Slack vs Teams guide.

Five-seat math, and the huddle question

Five seats on Pro: $36.25/month billed annually, $435 a year. Ten seats: $72.50/month. The features small teams actually cite when renewing are rarely the enterprise list — they're huddles (drop-in audio that replaces "quick call?" scheduling), channel structure that keeps client work separated, and an integration shelf (Google Drive, calendars, the CRM) that makes Slack the office's hallway rather than another inbox. Test those in the trial deliberately: if your team's work doesn't route through channels daily, Slack becomes a $435-a-year room nobody enters, and the bundled chat in your existing suite was the right answer all along.

Retention as a business decision

The 90-day free-tier wall deserves a second look from the business side: message history is institutional memory — pricing decisions, client commitments, incident timelines. A team that treats chat as its archive has a records question regardless of tier (retention settings, export rights and legal-hold needs differ by plan and matter in disputes). Decide where the company's memory lives — docs/wiki for decisions, chat for conversation — and Slack's tier choice gets much easier: chat that isn't the archive can stay free far longer, and the archive tool gets the budget instead.

Frequently asked questions

What happens to messages older than 90 days on the free plan? They become inaccessible on the free tier's rolling window; upgrading restores access to history that was retained. Treat the free plan as conversation, not archive, from day one.

Is Slack worth it if we're already on Microsoft 365? Only if the team genuinely lives in channels and huddles more than in Teams. The bundled tool costs $0 extra; Slack must beat free, and for many teams it does — but run the trial with real work, not enthusiasm.

Do guests cost extra on paid plans? Slack's guest model (single-channel guests historically free in set ratios, multi-channel guests billable) is part of the seat audit — external collaborators are where unnoticed seat costs gather.
