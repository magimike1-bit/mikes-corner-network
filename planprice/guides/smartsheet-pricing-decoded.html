Smartsheet Pricing: Pro, Business, and the Member Model

Published 2026-10-12

Smartsheet doesn't sell seats — it sells Members. That one-word difference decides your bill: viewers are free, guests are free, but anyone who edits internally can silently become a paid Member. Add a Pro plan restricted to new customers and premium add-ons that stack on top, and the US$9 headline needs unpacking. This guide unpacks it.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm the current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

Pro vs Business vs Enterprise: published vs custom

Smartsheet's published prices, checked October 2026 (USD). Pro: US$9 per Member per month billed annually (US$12 monthly) — unlimited sheets, Gantt views, 250 automation runs a month, 20 GB storage. Business: US$19 per Member per month annually (US$24 monthly) — unlimited automations, 1 TB storage, resource management, workload tracking, advanced reporting. Enterprise (10+ Members) and Advanced Work Management are custom-quoted, adding SSO, portfolios, and scenario planning. There is a 30-day free trial with no credit card required — and no free plan.

The Pro plan carries two gates the pricing page footnotes: it is available to new customers only, and it caps at 10 Members. An existing customer can't buy it, and a growing team can't stay on it. Most professional Smartsheet users land on Business — which is exactly where the published pricing ends and the custom quotes begin.

Members, viewers, guests: who bills and who doesn't

The member model is the real price list. Members are paid — anyone who creates or edits sheets internally. Contributors can view, comment, and attach files without a paid seat. Guests — qualifying external collaborators — can edit shared items for free on Business and Enterprise. The trap is provisional membership: when an internal viewer starts editing, Smartsheet can convert them to a provisional Member, and provisional Members become paid. The bill grows when people's behavior changes, not when you buy seats.

Practical rule: license the creators, keep everyone else as viewers or contributors, and audit the member list quarterly. The cheapest Smartsheet deployment is the one where the member count matches the actual editors.

Premium add-ons: where the bill grows

The seat price is not the total cost of a mature deployment. Resource Management, Dynamic View, Data Shuttle, Control Center, Brandfolder, premium connectors, and advanced support can all add expense depending on workflow — one 2026 analysis estimates realistic teams land at US$25–40 per user per month once add-ons are in play. Before choosing a plan, map the complete process you want to run: a team that only needs schedules and dashboards may stay within Business, while an enterprise PMO will lean on Advanced Work Management. Price the workflow, not the seat.

10- and 25-seat year-one math

On Business, annual: ten members cost 10 × US$19 × 12 = US$2,280 a year; twenty-five members cost US$5,700. The same 25 people on Trello Premium would cost US$3,000 a year — Smartsheet runs nearly double, which is the premium for Gantt charts, dependencies, and resource management that Trello simply doesn't have. There is no monthly-billing escape hatch worth taking: US$24 vs US$19 is a 26% penalty for flexibility.

What reviewers say

Smartsheet holds 4.4 out of 5 on G2 from over 22,000 reviews — one of the largest review bases in project management. Reviewers in construction, manufacturing, and IT praise it as a reliable workhorse for structured project execution, with the spreadsheet-familiar interface the most cited strength. The recurring critiques are consistent: UX complexity, advanced features that demand significant configuration and training, and a dated visual interface. It is the tool Excel-native teams graduate to — and the learning curve is the tuition.

The decision framework

This is a buying-decision guide, not a setup procedure — the decision framework near the end is the whole process.

  1. Count editors, not employees. Viewers and contributors are free — the member list should match the people who actually build sheets.
  2. Don't plan around Pro unless you're new and staying under 10 members. Existing customers and growing teams start at Business.
  3. Budget add-ons separately. If the workflow needs Resource Management or Control Center, the seat price is the down payment, not the total.
  4. Audit provisional members quarterly — behavior-driven seat growth is the silent budget killer.
  5. Buy it for Gantt-and-dependencies work. If the work is really kanban, Trello at half the price wins; Smartsheet earns its premium on structured project execution.

Frequently asked questions

Does Smartsheet have a free plan?

No — only a 30-day free trial with no credit card required. The old free tier is gone; every deployment starts on a paid plan.

What is the difference between a Member and a viewer?

Members are paid licenses for people who create and edit sheets. Viewers (and Contributors, who can also comment and attach files) are free. External Guests can edit shared items for free on Business and Enterprise plans.

Why can't I buy the Pro plan?

Pro is currently offered to new customers only and caps at 10 Members. Existing customers, or teams that will grow past ten editors, start at Business (US$19/member/month annually).
