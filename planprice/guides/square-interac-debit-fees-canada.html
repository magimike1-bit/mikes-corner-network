Square's Interac Debit Fees in Canada: The 0.75% + 7¢ Math (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Most pricing pages quote Square's 2.5% in-person rate and stop. In Canada there's a second rate that matters more to small-ticket businesses: Interac debit at 0.75% + 7¢ per transaction (Square Canada, checked October 25, 2026). Because the two rates scale differently — one pure percentage, one percentage plus a fixed fee — which customers you nudge toward debit, and at what ticket size, changes your processing bill by hundreds of dollars a year.

Debit wins at every ticket size in the table — the 7¢ fixed fee only threatens the percentage advantage below a toonie, and even there debit is a hair cheaper. The gap widens with the ticket: at $60, each debit transaction saves about a dollar against credit.

What that means monthly

A café doing 2,800 transactions a month at a $12 average ticket, currently 90% credit, pays about $762 in processing. Moving the mix to 60% debit — a prompt at the till and tap-to-pay debit enabled — cuts that to roughly $590. The $170 monthly saving is larger than most software subscriptions on this site, and it requires no new vendor, no contract, and no hardware: it is a behaviour change priced by a rate card most businesses never read past the headline.

The caveats

Customers choose their payment method — you can prompt, default and display, not refuse credit without cost to goodwill (and card-network rules limit surcharging in Canada; Interac debit also requires the customer to have it enabled for tap). Refunds and chargebacks behave differently on debit. And online sales are a different rate card entirely (Square's online rates differ from in-person; see the Square page). Model your own mix, not this example: the formula is (debit volume × 0.75%) + (debit transactions × $0.07) versus (credit volume × 2.5%).

Worked example: a bakery at two mixes

A bakery processes $22,000 a month across 1,900 transactions (average ticket ~$11.60). Mix A — 85% credit, 15% debit: credit $18,700 × 2.5% = $467.50; debit $3,300 × 0.75% = $24.75 plus ~285 debit transactions × $0.07 = $19.95. Total ≈ $512. Mix B — 55% credit, 45% debit: credit $12,100 × 2.5% = $302.50; debit $9,900 × 0.75% = $74.25 plus ~855 × $0.07 = $59.85. Total ≈ $437. Moving 30 points of mix saves about $75 a month — $900 a year — at bakery scale, with no vendor change. The levers are mundane: debit enabled on the reader (including tap), staff who ask "debit or credit?" instead of defaulting, and signage where it's natural. None of them cost the customer anything; Interac debit is how many Canadians prefer to pay for small purchases anyway.

Where debit doesn't help

Online sales price on a different card (Square's online rates differ from in-person; the debit advantage is an in-person story). Refund-heavy businesses should note debit refunds return to the account on Interac timelines, which customers experience differently than credit reversals. And if your average ticket is very large — a jeweller, an appliance shop — the percentage dominates entirely: at $500 tickets, mix shifts matter less than negotiating the credit rate itself or moving deposits to e-Transfer. The debit lever is a small-and-mid-ticket lever; that's precisely the till most Canadian independents run.

Frequently asked questions

Does the debit rate apply to tap payments? Yes — Interac debit transactions, including tap, price on the debit rate in Square's Canadian rate card; the payment method, not the gesture, sets the fee.

What about Interac e-Transfer for invoices? Different rail, different price: e-Transfer typically costs a flat banking fee (often ~$1.50 or included) regardless of amount — the cheapest way to collect large invoices, as our invoicing-math guide shows.

Do credit-card rewards change the customer's choice? They do — points collectors prefer credit, and that's their right. The prompt strategy works on the undecided middle, which at most tills is the majority.

Is there a minimum ticket where debit loses? Effectively no at Canadian price points: even a $2 sale prices at $0.085 by debit versus $0.05 by credit — the single case where credit is cheaper. Below a toonie, don't sweat the mix.
