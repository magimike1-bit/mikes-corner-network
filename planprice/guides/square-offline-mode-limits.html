Square Offline Mode: Limits, Risks and What a Dead Connection Costs (Canada, 2026)

Published 2026-11-03 · Last updated 2026-11-03 · By Mike, Editor

Can Square keep selling when the internet drops? Yes, within limits that are stricter than most sellers expect. Square Canada's offline payments help page, checked November 3, 2026, says offline payments process automatically when you reconnect within 24 hours, and no later than 72 hours. Past 72 hours, a pending payment expires. It cannot be retrieved or reprocessed.

The limits, verified against Square's own page

LimitWhat Square Canada states
Upload windowReconnect within 24 hours, no later than 72 hours
ExpiryPending payments expire after 72 hours and cannot be retrieved or reprocessed
DeclinesNo decline notifications while offline; declines appear only after reconnecting
LiabilityThe seller is responsible for expired, declined or disputed offline payments
Not available offlineAfterpay, Square Gift Cards, Tap to Pay on iPhone and Android, and manually entered card numbers
RefundsA pending offline payment cannot be cancelled or refunded until it has uploaded

Fees are not the surprise here. Square states that your current processing fees apply to offline transactions based on payment type, so an offline credit sale prices like an online one. The risk sits in whether the payment exists at all when the connection returns.

The Canadian limit most guides miss: Interac debit

Square's Canadian offline payments preparation guide states that Interac debit cards do not work offline, and that any offline transaction attempted with an Interac debit card will auto-decline on regaining connectivity. That matters more in Canada than in most Square markets. A market stall or food truck whose regulars mostly tap debit is not covered by offline mode in the way the headline feature suggests. Credit cards carry the offline session. Debit customers need cash, or a connection.

Plan for that split before an outage, not during one. A small sign and a cash float turn a debit decline into a minor delay. Discovering the rule after a queue has paid is a different conversation.

What a dead connection can cost, worked

Take a market day as a labelled scenario, not a promise. Assumptions: 120 credit sales at a $30 average ticket, $3,600 taken offline, Square's in-person credit rate of 2.5% from its Canadian fee schedule, checked November 3, 2026.

Square's own offline processing guide makes the middle row plain: if no payment goes through, Square does not charge a transaction fee, but the seller is out of pocket for the cost of goods or services already handed over. The third row is the real tail risk. Expiry is total, and Square states it cannot provide customer contact information or contact customers on your behalf for declined or expired offline payments. There is no collection path built into the product.

Actions that destroy pending payments

While payments are pending, Square warns sellers not to sign out of the POS app, delete the app, switch modes, switch locations, or factory reset the device or hardware. Pending payments are permanently lost if you do, and the funds will not be captured. You can restart or power off a device, but it has to be powered back on and reconnected before the payments expire.

That list should be printed for staff. The instinctive troubleshooting move during an outage, sign out and back in, or delete and reinstall, is exactly the move that erases the day's offline sales.

Set it up before you need it

  1. Allow offline payments in advance. In Square Dashboard or the POS app, allow offline payments and choose whether they apply during an internet outage, a Square service disruption, or both. The setting covers every device assigned to that mode.
  2. Brief the debit rule. Tell every staff member that Interac debit does not work offline in Canada, and decide the cash or wait policy before a queue forms.
  3. Watch the banner and the clock. An offline session shows a banner, and for internet outages a countdown clock showing the remaining upload time. Treat 24 hours as the working deadline and 72 hours as the cliff.
  4. Protect the device state. No signing out, no deleting the app, no switching modes or locations, and no factory reset while payments show as pending.
  5. Reconnect and review. Reconnecting uploads the payments with no other action required. Then open the transaction history and check for declined or failed payments, because no decline notice arrived while you were offline.
  6. Collect a signature where possible. Square's safety guidance asks sellers to obtain a signature for offline transactions where possible and check it against the card.

Success looks like this: every pending payment has uploaded well inside 24 hours, the transaction history shows no unexplained declines, and any declined sale is identified the same day, while the customer and the goods are still traceable in your own records.

Who should rely on it, and who should not

Offline mode suits a seller with mostly credit sales, modest tickets, and a connection that usually returns within hours. It is a bridge across a short outage. It suits a seller poorly when debit is the dominant tender, when tickets are large enough that one decline hurts, or when the selling location can stay offline for days. For those sellers, the honest backup is a second connection, a mobile hotspot or a different network, priced as insurance against the $3,600 row of the table above, not as an IT luxury.
