Stripe vs Square Canadian Rates: In-Person, Online, Invoicing, and the Crossover

Last updated 2026-10-11

Most Stripe-vs-Square comparisons quote US rates and call it a day. Canadian merchants face different numbers: Square Canada publishes 2.5% for in-person credit, and Stripe Canada publishes 2.9% + CA$0.30 for online domestic cards. This guide puts the published Canadian rates side by side — in-person, online, and invoicing — and works the ticket-size math.

In-person: Square wins at every ticket size

This one is not close. Square Canada charges a flat 2.5% with no fixed cents on in-person credit; Stripe Terminal charges 2.7% plus 5¢. There is no crossover — Square is cheaper by 0.2 percentage points plus 5¢ on every ticket. On Interac debit, Square's 0.75% + 7¢ is in a different league from credit-card pricing entirely. If your business is a counter, a market stall, or a service van, the rate card favors Square.

Online: nearly a tie, slight Square edge

Online, the fixed fee is identical (30¢) and the percentage differs by a tenth of a point: Square 2.8%, Stripe 2.9%. On $100 of online volume, that is a 10¢ difference — real but tiny. The deciding factor online is rarely the rate card: it is whether you need Stripe's developer tooling, subscription billing (0.7% of billing volume on Stripe Billing), and international payment methods, or Square's no-code setup with a free online store.

Invoicing and the extras

Stripe Invoicing is not free: Starter adds 0.4% per paid invoice and Plus adds 0.5%, each on top of the 2.9% + 30¢ processing fee. A $2,000 invoice on Stripe Invoicing Starter costs roughly $58 in processing plus $8 in invoicing fees. Square does not publish a separate per-invoice percentage on its Canadian pages the way Stripe does — invoices fall under its standard card-not-present pricing. Hardware: Square readers start at $69 CAD with $0 monthly; Stripe Terminal readers (from $59 for the Reader M2) are a separate hardware purchase on top of a developer integration.

The ticket-size crossover math

The fixed-cent fee decides small tickets more than the percentage does. Worked for in-person credit:

Online, the 30¢ fixed fee is identical on both, so the 0.1-point gap scales linearly: Square saves $1 per $1,000 of online volume. Neither gap is decisive — which is exactly the point: pick on product fit, not on the rate card.

Decision framework: three merchant profiles

  • The counter business (retail, café, market stall): Square — the 2.5% in-person rate and $0 monthly fee are hard to beat, and debit at 0.75% + 7¢ is a genuine edge.
  • The online store: a wash on rates — choose Stripe for developer control, subscriptions, and 135+ currencies, or Square for no-code setup with a free store.
  • The invoicing freelancer/contractor: Square — no per-invoice percentage on top of processing, versus Stripe's 0.4–0.5% invoicing add-on.

Note: this is a rate explainer, not a procedure guide — no steps here, just the published-rate math to check against your channel mix before you commit.

FAQ

Which is cheaper in Canada, Stripe or Square?

On published rates, Square wins in person (2.5% vs Stripe Terminal's 2.7% + 5¢) and slightly online (2.8% + 30¢ vs 2.9% + 30¢). Stripe wins where developer tooling, subscriptions, or international payments matter more than the percentage point.

Does Square charge extra for international cards in Canada?

Yes — an additional 1.5% applies to transactions with cards issued outside of Canada, on top of the 2.5% in-person rate.

How much does Stripe Invoicing cost on top of processing?

Stripe Invoicing adds 0.4% per paid invoice on Starter or 0.5% on Plus, on top of the 2.9% + 30¢ processing fee.
