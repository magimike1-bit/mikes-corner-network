Tap to Pay on Phone: Fees and Limits for Canadian Sellers

Published 2026-10-29 · Last updated 2026-10-29

$0. That is the hardware price when a phone becomes the terminal — no reader, no rental, nothing to ship. The fee per tap is where the offers separate, and in Canada they separate in a specific way: one provider charges its normal in-person rate, another adds a per-authorization charge on top of its in-person rate, and two more publish the $0 hardware line without putting a Tap-specific processing rate beside it. General information only, not financial advice.

Square: the regular in-person rate, on the phone

Square's Canadian fee schedule lists Tap to Pay on iPhone and Tap to Pay on Android inside its standard card-present rates, checked October 29, 2026: 2.5% of the transaction for credit or prepaid cards, and 0.75% + 7¢ per transaction for debit. There is no separate Tap surcharge in that schedule. Square's Tap to Pay on Android page puts the same numbers beside $0 in monthly fees and no long-term contract, and states the conditions plainly: a compatible Android device, an NFC-enabled phone, and an internet or cellular connection.

On iPhone, the hardware condition is the model, not the app. Square's Tap to Pay on iPhone in Canada runs on iPhone XS or later, inside the Square POS, Retail or Appointments apps. A phone older than that is not a terminal, whatever the app store page suggests.

Stripe: in-person rate plus a Tap charge

Stripe prices the phone differently. Its Canadian pricing page, checked October 29, 2026, lists in-person payments at 2.7% + CA$0.05 per successful charge for most cards, and then adds CA$0.15 per authorization for Tap to Pay. A Stripe tap therefore carries a 20¢ fixed component before the percentage — 5¢ on the base rate plus 15¢ for using the phone. The same page prices Interac debit through Terminal at CA$0.15 per transaction. Stripe is also a build-it-yourself route for most small sellers: Terminal and Tap to Pay are developer-led products, typically reached through an app or integration built on Stripe, rather than a single off-the-shelf POS app.

Moneris and Shopify: $0 hardware, rate to confirm

Moneris lists Tap to Pay on Android and Tap to Pay on iPhone starting at $0/month on its Canadian POS machines page, and its card-machine page describes Tap to Pay on iPhone with the Moneris Go app as accepting in-person contactless payments with no extra readers or hardware. What that page does not state is a Tap-specific processing rate. Moneris publishes a general flat rate elsewhere — 2.65% + 10¢ card-present credit and $0.12 per Interac debit transaction, checked October 26, 2026 — but this guide does not assume the phone rate equals the terminal rate. Confirm the rate that will apply to Tap transactions in the Moneris quote or account before modelling a month on it.

Shopify's route is an eligibility list rather than a rate card. Its Help Center, checked October 29, 2026, requires a store located in Canada (among other supported countries), a paid Shopify plan, Shopify Payments as the processor, the Shopify app, a passcode set on the device, and — on Android — NFC activated. No Tap-specific per-transaction rate is published on that activation page, so Shopify sellers should read their own plan's in-person rate as the figure to verify, not a number quoted here.

One name does not belong on a Canadian Tap list at all: Helcim. Its POS documentation describes Tap to Pay on iPhone for US merchants only. A Canadian Helcim account still needs the card reader or terminal.

At 1,000 taps it is $280 a month on the same mix — the point at which a $69 or $79 physical reader, which does not carry the Tap charge on Stripe's schedule, starts looking less like hardware and more like a fee cut.

The limits that end the sale

Tap to Pay is contactless only. A customer whose card has no tap, or who needs to insert and enter a PIN, cannot be served by the phone alone. That interacts with a Canadian ceiling: Interac contactless payments are available for transactions of up to $250 at participating merchants, with lower limits on some older cards. Above that ceiling the cardholder inserts the card and enters a PIN — a move a phone-as-terminal cannot offer. A seller whose typical ticket sits above $250, or whose customers regularly pay by chip, needs a physical reader in the drawer regardless of how good the phone rate looks.

Connectivity is the second limit. Square states the condition directly: an internet or cellular connection is required. A basement market hall or a rural service call with no signal is not a Tap to Pay location, and there is no chip fallback to fall back on. Processor migrations at retailers underline why the backup matters in practice: the payment setup that fails in front of a queue costs the sale, not just the fee.

Set it up, then test the edges

  1. Check the device first. iPhone XS or later for Square on iPhone; a compatible NFC-enabled Android phone for Android routes; a passcode set, and NFC switched on where the provider requires it.
  2. Enable Tap inside the provider's app — Square POS, the Moneris Go app, the Shopify app with Shopify Payments active, or the Stripe-based app the business uses — and confirm the account's Tap rate on screen before the first real sale.
  3. Run three test sales: a small credit tap, a debit tap, and the largest ticket the business normally takes. Note where the debit tap stops and a PIN would be demanded.
  4. Decide the backup. If any test fails on payment type, ticket size or signal, price a basic physical reader as part of the same setup, not as a later emergency purchase.

Success looks like this: every payment type the business actually receives has been tapped, inserted or deliberately routed to the backup reader in a test — and the per-tap rate in the account matches the published rate the month was modelled on.
