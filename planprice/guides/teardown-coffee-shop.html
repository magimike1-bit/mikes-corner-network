Coffee Shop Software & Fees: Full Monthly Cost Teardown (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

A single-location coffee shop lives on small tickets and card volume, so its software bill is really two bills: a short list of subscriptions, and a card-processing line that dwarfs them. This teardown prices the realistic stack for a Canadian café from PlanPrice's verified vendor data — every line names its source and check date — then totals it and prices a cheaper variant.

Model assumptions (illustrative — not a real business): One location, owner plus 3 staff; $30,000/month in card sales across ~2,800 transactions, modelled as $28,000 credit and $2,000 Interac debit (300 debit transactions). Card-fee lines use the stated monthly card volume and transaction counts. Taxes (GST/HST/PST/QST as applicable) are extra on subscription lines. Province tax pages explain how the tax layer applies.

Variant total: $768 per month — $30 less than the recommended stack. The variant drops QuickBooks for Wave's free accounting and saves the $30 subscription — the trade is fewer accountant-facing features and no multi-currency on the free plan. The bigger lever is not software at all: at these volumes, every $1,000 of monthly sales that moves from credit (2.5%) to Interac debit (0.75% + 7¢) saves roughly $16, which is why cafés that prompt for debit come out ahead of cafés that negotiate software.

What moves this total most

Sensitivity, in order. Card mix is the largest lever: at this model's volume, shifting 10% of credit sales to Interac debit saves roughly $49 a month — more than the accounting and scheduling lines combined. Card volume is second: the processing line scales almost linearly, so a $40,000 month costs about $245 more in fees than a $30,000 month, while subscriptions don't move at all — budget the fee line as roughly 2.5% of card sales, not as a fixed cost. Headcount is third: each additional employee adds $4 a month on Wagepoint Lite, which is noise next to the fee line. The practical conclusion for a café is unfashionable but true: your payment setup deserves more negotiation energy than your software subscriptions, because that's where the money is.

First-year costs outside the monthly stack

Three one-time or annual items sit outside the totals above. Hardware: a Square countertop setup (reader, stand, printer, cash drawer) is a one-time purchase, with readers from $69 — budget a few hundred dollars for a complete till, less for a single reader-and-tablet setup. Menu and catalogue setup: your own time, typically an afternoon; there is no vendor fee. Accounting year-end: if an accountant closes your year, that fee is professional services, not software — the QuickBooks subscription is what makes their job (and their invoice) smaller. None of these change the monthly comparison, but a first-year budget that ignores them underestimates year one by roughly the hardware plus any accountant time you buy.
