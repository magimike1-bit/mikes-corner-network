3-Person Consultancy Software Costs: Full Monthly Teardown (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

A three-person consultancy sells time, so its software stack is small, per-seat, and judged by one test: does it protect billable hours or win work? This teardown prices the realistic stack from verified vendor data — workspace, CRM, accounting, meetings and the invoice-payment fees most firms forget to model — and totals it against a cheaper variant.

Model assumptions (illustrative — not a real business): Three consultants; $22,000/month invoiced across 12 invoices, modelled as half paid by client credit card and half by Interac e-Transfer. No retail card volume. Card-fee lines use the stated monthly card volume and transaction counts. Taxes (GST/HST/PST/QST as applicable) are extra on subscription lines. Two trades in the variant: FreshBooks Plus becomes Wave (free, but no double-entry depth or multi-currency polish), and Zoom disappears into the Meet licence the firm already pays for. The honest framing is Sterling's rule for this profile: at three people the stack is a rounding error next to one billable day — the variant is worth it only while revenue is thin; the recommended stack is worth it the first time a client relationship needs the CRM discipline and invoicing polish it buys. Note the largest line in both stacks is not software at all: it is the card fees on client invoices, which is a payment-terms decision (e-Transfer default) disguised as a software cost.

What the stack looks like at five people

The per-seat lines scale predictably — Workspace becomes $46, a second Zoom licence appears, CRM seats stop being free (Zoho's free tier ends at 3 users; Capsule Starter at $18/user or Pipedrive from $14/user become the realistic lines, adding roughly $70–$90 at five seats). The structural change is the invoice-fee line: at $40,000+ of monthly billings, payment terms become a finance policy, not an admin habit. Firms this size routinely add a card surcharge equivalent into their rate card or steer anchor clients to pre-authorized payment — either move is worth more than any subscription negotiation in the stack. Budget the five-person stack at roughly $550–$650 a month on the recommended pattern, with the invoice-fee line still the largest single component.

The tool nobody budgets: the contract layer

Consultancies live on agreements — proposals, MSAs, SOWs — and e-signature/document tools (PandaDoc, SignNow, DocuSign class) are the stack's usual unbudgeted guest, typically $15–$50 per user per month when they arrive. This teardown excludes them deliberately: at three people, FreshBooks' proposals and standard PDF signing workflows cover most firms, and the honest moment to add a document platform is when signature-chasing measurably delays starts. When it does, add it as its own line with its own math — documents sent per month against the per-envelope or per-seat price — rather than letting it ride inside "software, misc."
