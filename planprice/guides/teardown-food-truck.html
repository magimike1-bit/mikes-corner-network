Food Truck Software & Fees: Full Monthly Cost Teardown (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

A food truck's software footprint is small — which makes the card-processing line almost the whole story. This teardown prices the realistic stack for a Canadian truck working markets and lunch rushes, from verified vendor data, and totals it with the seasonal caveat that matters more than any subscription.

Model assumptions (illustrative — not a real business): Owner plus 1 employee on payroll; $18,000/month in card sales across ~1,600 transactions in season, modelled as $16,200 credit and $1,800 Interac debit (200 debit transactions). Card-fee lines use the stated monthly card volume and transaction counts. Taxes (GST/HST/PST/QST as applicable) are extra on subscription lines. Province tax pages explain how the tax layer applies.

Variant total: $429 per month — $31.50 less than the recommended stack. The variant changes no software at all — it changes the prompt at the till. Moving $3,200 a month of volume from credit (2.5%) to Interac debit (0.75% + 7¢) is worth about $31 a month here; trucks with bigger average tickets save more per shifted dollar. The seasonal caveat: most of these subscriptions are monthly and pausable, but the card line scales with revenue — budget it as a percentage of sales (about 2.4% of card volume in the recommended mix), not as a fixed cost.

The off-season version of this stack

Food trucks are seasonal in most of Canada, and the stack should be too. The card line self-adjusts (no sales, no fees). The subscriptions are the decision: Wagepoint Lite's $28 continues monthly whether or not the truck rolls, so an operator who fully closes for winter should ask what pausing or cancelling costs in setup time come spring — payroll accounts carry year-end obligations (T4s) even for a short season, which argues for keeping the account alive at its small monthly cost rather than rebuilding it annually. Wave's $0 line has no such dilemma. The model to carry: divide the annual subscription total by your operating months, not by twelve, to get the true in-season monthly cost — here, roughly $34 a month across an eight-month season for the payroll line's year-round $336.

Event and catering days break the model (upward)

A single private event can bill like a week of curbside sales, usually on one or two large invoices rather than hundreds of taps. Those invoices shouldn't ride the in-person rate card: online invoice payments price differently (Square's online rates and Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢ both apply to card-paid invoices), and for invoices in the thousands, an Interac e-Transfer request costs effectively nothing against 2.5–2.9% of the ticket. The teardown's monthly model assumes curbside mix; a truck doing regular events should model a second line — event revenue × the rail the client actually pays on — because at event scale the choice of rail is worth more than every subscription in the stack combined.
