Retail Store POS & Software Costs: Full Monthly Teardown (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Retail is where POS choice stops being a preference and becomes a rate card. This teardown prices a small Canadian retail store two ways — a retail-native POS (Lightspeed) and a generalist (Square) — from verified vendor data, because the hardware, the register software and the processing rate are one decision, not three.

Model assumptions (illustrative — not a real business): Owner plus 2 staff; $42,000/month in card sales across ~2,600 transactions, modelled as $38,000 credit and $4,000 Interac debit in the Square variant. Lightspeed Payments is modelled at its published 2.6% + 10¢ across all card volume. Card-fee lines use the stated monthly card volume and transaction counts. Taxes (GST/HST/PST/QST as applicable) are extra on subscription lines. Province tax pages explain how the tax layer applies.

Variant total: $1,089 per month — $440 less than the recommended stack. On pure price the Square variant wins by roughly $440 a month at this volume — the gap is the Lightspeed subscription plus its 10¢ per-transaction fee, which punishes small baskets. What the recommended stack buys for that premium is retail-specific depth: multi-location inventory, purchase orders and vendor management that a generalist POS approximates at best. The spec verdict: a single-location store with simple inventory should take the variant and bank the difference; a store whose inventory complexity is the business should pay for Lightspeed and stop re-litigating it monthly. Hardware is excluded from both stacks on purpose — register hardware is a one-time purchase (Square readers from $69; Lightspeed hardware is quoted per setup), not a monthly line.

Reading the two stacks as a spec sheet

The functional ledger, briefly. Inventory: Lightspeed handles variants, matrices, purchase orders and multi-location transfers natively; Square covers single-location inventory well and approximates the rest. Reporting: Lightspeed's retail reporting is deeper out of the box; Square's is adequate and simpler. E-commerce: both connect an online store; Lightspeed's eCom tie-in is tighter for inventory-synced selling, Square Online is faster to launch. Staff: both price team features in higher tiers than this teardown uses. The pattern to notice: every Lightspeed advantage is an inventory-system advantage, and every Square advantage is a cost or simplicity advantage. That is why the verdict splits on inventory complexity rather than on store size alone — a small store with 4,000 SKUs and size/colour matrices is a Lightspeed store; a larger store with 300 SKUs is not.

The second-location breakpoint

Adding a second location re-prices everything: POS subscriptions multiply or step up a tier, inventory transfers become a real feature requirement (Lightspeed's home ground), and payroll crosses into Wagepoint Core territory as headcount grows. The processing comparison, however, barely changes — rates are rates at both locations, and the $440/month Square advantage in this model roughly doubles with doubled volume. A retailer planning a second store within two years should weight the inventory-system question more heavily than this single-store teardown does, because migrating a POS with a year of inventory history costs far more than the subscription difference ever will.
