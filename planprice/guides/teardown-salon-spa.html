Salon & Spa Software & Fees: Full Monthly Cost Teardown (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

For a salon, the software stack is a revenue tool before it is a cost: the booking system decides how full the chairs are. This teardown prices a four-person salon's realistic monthly stack from verified vendor data — booking, payments, accounting and payroll — totals it, and shows the cheaper variant, so the cost of keeping chairs full is explicit.

Model assumptions (illustrative — not a real business): Owner plus 4 staff; $16,000/month in card sales across ~900 transactions, modelled as $14,000 credit and $2,000 Interac debit (250 debit transactions). Card-fee lines use the stated monthly card volume and transaction counts. Taxes (GST/HST/PST/QST as applicable) are extra on subscription lines. Province tax pages explain how the tax layer applies.

Variant total: $444.50 per month — $20 less than the recommended stack. The variant swaps Acuity for Setmore's free tier and saves $20 a month. Whether that trade is worth it is a revenue question, not a software question: reminder automation that prevents a single no-show a month in a $90 chair pays for Acuity several times over, which is why the recommended stack keeps paid booking and the variant is listed as the budget floor, not the advice.

The revenue side of this stack

A salon stack should be audited against chairs filled, not just dollars spent. The booking line ($20) is the only subscription whose job is directly revenue: online booking captures the appointment that a ringing phone during a cut would have lost, and reminder automation defends it. Model it concretely: if Acuity's reminders prevent two no-shows a month in a $90 chair, that's $180 of defended revenue against a $20 cost — the best ratio in the stack. The accounting line defends margin more quietly (expense capture at tax time), and the payroll line buys back the owner's Sunday evening. When a salon owner asks which line to cut first, the honest order is: scheduling last, because it's the only one that sells.

What changes as the salon grows

Two breakpoints. At five-plus staff, Setmore's free tier (4 staff accounts) stops covering the team, so the variant's $0 booking line becomes a paid line — the variant advantage narrows to the Acuity-vs-paid-Setmore gap. At higher card volume, the processing line follows the same rule as every business on this page: it scales with revenue while subscriptions stay flat, so a busier salon should re-run the debit-mix question yearly — Canadian clients tap debit for small services more readily than owners assume, and every point of mix shift is worth about 1.75 points of margin on that volume.
