Trades Contractor Software & Fees: Full Monthly Cost Teardown (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

A trades contractor's stack is built around the job, not the office: dispatch, invoicing from the truck, payroll for the crew, and card fees on invoices big enough that the processing line rivals the software. This teardown prices it from verified vendor data and totals a recommended stack and a cheaper variant.

Model assumptions (illustrative — not a real business): Owner plus 5 crew on payroll; $35,000/month invoiced, modelled as $20,000 collected by online card payment across 40 invoices and $15,000 paid in person by credit card. Card-fee lines use the stated monthly card volume and transaction counts. Taxes (GST/HST/PST/QST as applicable) are extra on subscription lines. Province tax pages explain how the tax layer applies.

The variant steps Housecall Pro down to Basic and accounting down to free Wave, saving $120 a month — worth taking until the crew size or job volume needs Essentials' extra seats and features (seat caps on Housecall Pro tiers have changed over time; confirm the current cap on the vendor page before choosing). The structural point stands in both stacks: at $35,000 a month invoiced, payment collection choices (card vs e-Transfer vs cheque timing) move more money than every software decision on this page combined.

Where this stack leaks money

Three leaks, in size order. Invoice collection lag: at $35,000 a month billed, every week of average collection delay is roughly $8,000 of working capital permanently lent to clients; card-on-file and deposit policies (both supported by the job software in this stack) attack that number directly, and the interest saved can exceed the card fees they incur. Payroll errors: manual timesheets for five crew mis-record hours in the employer's disfavour often enough that time-tracking inside the job app — a feature difference between the Basic and Essentials tiers — pays for the tier gap by itself at this crew size; that, more than dispatch polish, is the real Essentials argument. Subscription drift: the smallest leak — an annual audit of the stack (who still uses what) typically recovers one forgotten seat or tier per year.

Scaling to a second crew

Doubling the crew roughly doubles payroll's per-employee line ($70 → $130 on Core) and pushes job management firmly into the tier with the seats and dispatch depth — the variant's Basic tier stops fitting. Card fees scale with billed volume, not headcount, so a second crew that's mostly subcontracted labour changes the stack less than one that's fully employed. The planning rule: budget software per crew as its own mini-stack (job app seats + payroll per-head + their card volume), because the trades stack is really N copies of a per-crew pattern plus one accounting core.
