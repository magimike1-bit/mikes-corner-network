Trello Business Pricing Breakdown: Standard, Premium, Enterprise

Published 2026-10-12

Trello's pricing looks like the simplest page in project management: four tiers, one price per seat, done. Most teams end up paying more than the sticker price once billable guests, monthly-vs-annual billing, and Enterprise's seat floor enter the picture. This guide breaks down what each Trello plan actually costs in 2026 — and the two line items the pricing page never mentions.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm the current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

The four tiers: what each costs per user

Trello bills per user, checked October 2026. Free (US$0) gives unlimited cards but caps the workspace at 10 boards, with 250 automation runs a month and a 10 MB attachment limit. Standard is US$5/user/month billed annually (US$6 month-to-month): unlimited boards, advanced checklists, custom fields, and 1,000 automation runs. Premium is US$10/user/month annually (US$12.50 monthly): the Calendar, Timeline, Table, Dashboard, and Map views plus unlimited automation runs. Enterprise is US$17.50/user/month, annual billing only, with a mandatory 50-seat minimum — a US$10,500-a-year entry ticket regardless of how many people you actually license.

Annual billing trims roughly 17–20% off Standard and Premium versus month-to-month. Enterprise has no monthly option at all, and its per-seat rate falls at higher seat counts through volume discounts — get a quote at your actual headcount rather than extrapolating.

Billable guests: the hidden second line item

Here is the charge that surprises finance teams. Trello bills paid workspaces per member and per multi-board guest: a guest who touches only one board is free, but a guest on two or more boards in the same paid workspace becomes billable at the full per-user rate. A consultancy with five clients each invited to two project boards just added five phantom seats.

Audit your guest list before every renewal. Consolidating a client's work onto a single board, or moving external collaborators to a free workspace for viewing, are the two levers that keep this line item at zero. It is the single most common reason Trello bills exceed the headcount math.

Annual vs monthly: a 15-person team

The billing-cycle math is straightforward and worth doing once. Fifteen people on Premium, annual: 15 × US$10 × 12 = US$1,800 a year. The same team month-to-month: 15 × US$12.50 × 12 = US$2,250 — a US$450 annual penalty for flexibility. On Standard the spread is US$900 vs US$1,080. Once the team is committed and the board count has stabilized past the free tier's 10-board cap, annual billing is close to free money.

The real upgrade trigger is rarely automation — it is the board limit. Teams hit the paid boundary when the 10-board cap collides with how they organize work, and no amount of clever archiving changes that. If you are constantly merging boards to stay under ten, you have already outgrown Free.

What reviewers say

Trello holds 4.4 out of 5 on G2 across about 14,040 reviews and 4.5 out of 5 on Capterra from 23,545 reviews — one of the most-reviewed tools in the category. Nitin V., a full-stack engineer, gave it 4.5/5 in June 2026: the boards-and-cards system is "simple and highly visual," Butler automation is "quite good" — but "Trello can get a bit messy when a project grows," and "for complex projects with dependencies between tasks, Trello falls short."

That is the honest shape of the reviews: beloved for adoption speed and simplicity, outgrown when projects need dependencies, resource planning, or serious reporting. Trello's lowest unit price in the category is real — the question is whether your work stays board-shaped.

The decision framework

This is a buying-decision guide, not a setup procedure — the decision framework near the end is the whole process.

  1. Stay on Free until the 10-board cap actually binds. Merging boards to dodge the cap is a sign you've outgrown it, not a strategy.
  2. Audit multi-board guests quarterly — they bill at full seat price and are the #1 source of surprise charges.
  3. Go annual once the team is stable — the ~20% saving is real and the switching cost is low.
  4. Price Enterprise at 50 seats minimum (US$10,500/yr floor), not at your headcount — then negotiate volume pricing.
  5. Leave when the work stops being board-shaped. Dependencies, capacity planning, and cross-project reporting are the exit signals, and no tier adds them.

Frequently asked questions

Is Trello really free?

Yes, for up to 10 boards per workspace with unlimited cards — genuinely usable for individuals and tiny teams. The cap that pushes teams to paid is boards, not users.

Do guests cost money on Trello?

Single-board guests are free. A guest added to two or more boards in a paid workspace becomes billable at the full per-user rate — audit this before renewal.

How much does Trello Enterprise cost?

US$17.50 per user per month, annual billing only, with a 50-seat minimum — so US$10,500 a year to start, before volume discounts at higher seat counts.
