Wagepoint's New Fee Schedule Explained: Lite, Core and Plus (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Wagepoint quietly rebuilt its price list on October 22, 2026, and if you run Canadian payroll, the new schedule changes what you pay. The old Solo and Unlimited plans are gone. In their place: Lite at $20 base + $4 per employee, Core at $40 + $6, and Plus at $80 + $10, per month. This guide works the math at the team sizes Canadian small businesses actually have, and flags the one comparison that matters — your old plan's price against its replacement tier.

The shape matters more than the numbers: Wagepoint's per-employee line rises steeply by tier — $4 to $6 to $10 — so tier choice costs more as you grow. A 20-person firm on Plus pays $280 a month; the same firm on Core pays $160. The $120 gap has to buy features the firm actually uses (advanced reporting, time-off depth, priority handling), not features that sound good in a demo.

What happened to Solo and Unlimited customers

Existing customers are migrated to the new tiers; the practical question is which tier your old plan maps to and whether the mapping is the cheapest legal fit for how you actually run payroll. If you were on a light plan and land on Core by default, check whether Lite covers your runs — at 10 employees that single decision is $40 a month, $480 a year, forever. Payroll pricing compounds quietly because nobody re-shops it; a fee-schedule change is the one moment re-shopping is free.

How Wagepoint now compares

Against the other Canadian payroll options in our dataset, Wagepoint stays the small-team value pick: the base-plus-per-employee shape keeps a 3-person payroll at $32–$58 a month depending on tier. The fuller comparison — including Knit People (from $40 + $6/employee, checked September 30) and Humi under Employment Hero (quote-based) — is on the Wagepoint page and in Deel vs Wagepoint for the cross-border case.

Bottom line: the new schedule is simpler and, at Lite, fairly priced. The risk isn't the headline — it's landing on a higher tier than your payroll complexity needs. Price your exact headcount on the table above before your next run.

Worked example: a 7-person café payroll

Seven employees, bi-weekly runs. On Lite: $20 + 7 × $4 = $48/month, $576 a year. On Core: $40 + 7 × $6 = $82/month, $984 a year. The $408 annual gap is the entire decision. Core earns it only if the café uses what sits above Lite — deeper reporting, more hands-on support, the tier's extra HR-adjacent features as published. A café running one location, hourly staff and a bookkeeper who handles year-end questions will rarely touch them; a multi-location group with salaried managers and complex deductions plausibly will. Payroll is the stickiest subscription a business owns — switching means re-onboarding every employee's banking and tax data — so the tier decision deserves the ten minutes this table takes, once, rather than a default that renews 26 times a year.

Questions to ask before your first run on the new schedule

Which tier did my account land on? (Migration mappings are the vendor's default, not your optimum.) What does my per-employee line become at next season's headcount? Are year-end items (T4s, summaries) inside the tier or billed separately? — the schedule's fine print, not its headline, answers this, and January is the wrong month to learn it. Does my accountant work in Wagepoint? If they do, ask which tier they prefer their clients on; their answer is data, because they absorb the friction of the wrong one.

Frequently asked questions

Did my payroll get more expensive on October 22? Only if your migrated tier prices above your old plan at your headcount — run your employee count through the new table (Lite $20 + $4, Core $40 + $6, Plus $80 + $10) against your last invoice to see your actual delta.

Can I move down a tier? Tier changes are an account action with Wagepoint; if your runs don't use the higher tier's features, ask — the per-employee gradient makes over-tiering the most expensive default in Canadian payroll.

How does this compare to doing payroll in QuickBooks? QuickBooks bundles payroll as an add-on to accounting; Wagepoint is payroll-only and priced per employee. Firms already on QuickBooks should price the add-on at their headcount against the table above before assuming either is cheaper.
