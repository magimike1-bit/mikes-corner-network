Wave (Free) vs QuickBooks Online: The Break-Even Math for Canadian Freelancers

Last updated 2026-10-05

Freelancers comparing Wave and QuickBooks need the math, not another feature list: at what point does paying for QuickBooks beat free Wave? This guide runs the numbers with dated Canadian pricing, works a concrete break-even example, and names the feature gates that force a switch.

The real cost of "free": Wave's current pricing

Wave's Starter plan is free: unlimited invoices, estimates, bills, and bookkeeping records at $0, checked on Wave's pricing page on 2026-10-05. Clients paying by card pay 2.9% + CA$0.60 per transaction (3.4% + CA$0.60 for Amex) on Starter. Wave bills Canadian customers in CAD, per its Canadian payments terms. Wave Pro (CA$25 per business per month) changes two things: processing drops to 2.9% + CA$0 for the first 10 transactions monthly (then CA$0.60), and you get automatic bank imports via Plaid, auto-merge and categorization, receipt capture, and automated late-payment reminders. Payroll is a separate add-on starting at CA$25/month. All Wave prices exclude applicable tax.

QuickBooks Online's CAD tiers

QuickBooks Online Canada has four CAD tiers, confirmed on Intuit's Canadian pricing page on 2026-10-05: EasyStart CA$30/month (1 user), Essentials CA$70/month (3 users), Plus CA$110/month (up to 5 users), Advanced CA$220/month (up to 25 users). Every tier includes access for your accountant. For a freelancer the feature ladder is short: EasyStart covers invoicing, expenses, GST/HST tracking, and standard reports; Essentials adds bill management, time tracking, and multi-currency; Plus adds inventory, budgets, and project profitability. Intro promos are common — 50% off was advertised on the higher tiers — so the first year often costs less than list.

Once you want automatic bank imports, Wave costs CA$25 — only CA$5 less than QuickBooks EasyStart at CA$30. "Free vs CA$360 a year" is the wrong comparison; the real one is CA$300 vs CA$360 a year.

The worked example: when CA$30/month pays for itself

Take a freelance designer in Toronto billing CA$75 an hour, sending 10 invoices a month and taking most payments by card (all figures in CAD).

Cost side. Wave Pro runs CA$25 × 12 = CA$300 a year; QuickBooks EasyStart runs CA$30 × 12 = CA$360 a year. The gap is CA$60 a year — CA$5 a month.

Processing side. On 10 card invoices a month, Wave Pro's discounted rate saves CA$0.60 per transaction versus Starter — CA$6 a month, CA$72 a year — wiping out the Starter-vs-Pro gap for a card-heavy freelancer.

Time side. This is where QuickBooks can win on price. If EasyStart's automated bank reconciliation, receipt matching, and reporting save you 30 minutes a month compared with manual Wave Starter bookkeeping, that's 6 hours a year. At your CA$75 billable rate, that's CA$450 of your time against a CA$360 subscription — the software pays for itself, and the math gets better the higher your hourly rate climbs.

Illustrative arithmetic, not financial advice — rerun it with your own invoice count and hourly rate. The subscription gap between the serious tiers is tiny: the decision turns on features.

The switch gates: five triggers that force the move

If even one of these is true, the free option has stopped being right. Switch now — migrating under tax-season pressure is the one cost neither plan lists.

  1. You hire your first person. Payroll becomes real work, and you'll want time tracking (QBO Essentials) to record employee hours.
  2. You bill by the hour. Wave has no time tracking on any plan; QuickBooks Essentials does, and billable time flows straight onto invoices.
  3. You sell physical products. Wave has no inventory tracking at all; QuickBooks Plus does.
  4. Your accountant asks for more. Every QuickBooks tier includes accountant access plus an internal audit log of every edit — Wave lets you delete a paid invoice, which can cascade errors through your books and leaves less to show in a tax audit.
  5. You outgrow manual entry. Want automatic bank imports? You're choosing between Wave Pro at CA$25 and QuickBooks EasyStart at CA$30 — choose on features, not price.

Verdict: start free, switch on a trigger

For a Canadian freelancer with simple service income and no employees, free Wave Starter is the rational starting point — the subscription math doesn't justify QuickBooks until your time or your complexity says otherwise. Revisit the decision the month any gate above trips. When it does, move early: migrating a year's books at tax time is the most expensive mistake in this whole comparison.

FAQ

Is Wave really free? Wave's Starter plan is free with unlimited invoicing and bookkeeping — you pay only for card processing (2.9% + CA$0.60 per transaction), payroll, or the CA$25/month Pro plan. Prices checked 2026-10-05.

Does Wave have time tracking? No — not on any plan. Freelancers who bill hourly need a separate app, or QuickBooks Essentials and up.

Can I switch from Wave to QuickBooks later? Yes. Export your Wave data and import it into QuickBooks; the process is well documented by both vendors and migration specialists. Do it before tax season, not during.
