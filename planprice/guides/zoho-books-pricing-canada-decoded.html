Zoho Books Pricing Canada: Plans, the Free Tier, and Per-Org Math

Published 2026-10-12

Almost every accounting tool charges per user. Zoho Books charges per organization — one flat price covers the whole company, and the plan that includes ten users costs less per month than a single seat of most rivals. That structural difference changes every comparison it appears in. This guide maps the six tiers, the free plan's real limits, and the per-org math for Canadian small businesses.

General information, not financial advice. Prices change — always confirm the current rates on the vendor's own pricing page before buying.

The plans: Free through Ultimate, per org

Zoho Books sells per organization, not per seat, checked October 2026 (USD). Free (US$0): 1 user plus 1 accountant, 1,000 invoices and expenses a year, for businesses under US$50,000 in annual revenue. Standard: US$15/month billed annually (US$20 monthly): 3 users, 5,000 invoices a year. Professional: US$40 (US$50): 5 users, 10,000 invoices, and this is where inventory management, purchase orders, and multi-currency arrive. Premium: US$60 (US$70): 10 users, 25,000 invoices, budgets, and a vendor portal. Elite: US$120 (US$150): 10 users, 100,000 invoices, advanced inventory. Ultimate: US$240 (US$275): 15 users with advanced analytics. Annual billing saves 13–25% depending on tier.

The number to internalize: Premium at US$60 a month covers ten users. That is US$6 per user per month — against per-seat rivals charging US$25–90 for one. The per-org model is Zoho's entire pricing strategy, and it is devastating at headcounts of three and up.

The free tier: who actually qualifies

The free plan is genuinely free — not a trial — but the gates are specific: under US$50,000 in annual revenue, one user plus your accountant, 1,000 invoices a year. A freelancer billing US$40,000 with 300 invoices fits comfortably; a growing agency does not. It includes invoicing, expense tracking, bank reconciliation, and the client portal — a fuller free tier than most paid entry plans elsewhere.

Two Canadian caveats. First, Zoho bills in USD — convert every figure at your card's rate (about CA$1.43 per US$1 recently), so Standard is really about CA$21.50 a month. Second, the revenue threshold is in USD, which at current rates is roughly CA$71,500 — slightly more generous for Canadians than the round number suggests.

Per-org math: 3-, 5-, and 10-user scenarios

Run the annual numbers. Three users on Standard: US$15 × 12 = US$180 a year — US$60 per user per year. Five users on Professional: US$40 × 12 = US$480 a year, under US$100 per user per year, with inventory included. Ten users on Premium: US$60 × 12 = US$720 a year. Compare that against Sage 50 Pro at CA$916 a year for one user, or Xero Starter at CA$300 a year per user. At ten users, Zoho Books Premium costs less than three Xero Starter seats.

The trade is depth for price. Zoho Books handles invoicing, expenses, bank reconciliation, inventory, and 70+ financial reports — but Sage 50's Canadian payroll, job costing, and multi-location inventory depth are a level above. Buy Zoho for the price structure; buy Sage when the accounting work itself demands it.

What reviewers say

On Capterra, Zoho Books scores 4.4 for ease of use, 4.3 for features, and 4.4 for value across 672 reviews. A verified 5.0 review from August 2026 — an e-commerce operations manager — praised the automatic bank connection for saving time on expense tracking, calling it "useful for keeping everything organized in one place" for a small business. The honest counterpoint sits one review away: a 1.0 review from October 2025 citing poor customer support and paid extras beyond the plan price. The pattern across platforms is consistent — strong value scores, with support quality the swing factor.

The decision framework

This is a buying-decision guide, not a setup procedure — the decision framework near the end is the whole process.

  1. Count the org, not the seats. The plan decision is users-included vs users-needed: Standard covers 3, Professional 5, Premium 10.
  2. Start free only if you're under US$50K revenue — and plan the paid migration before you cross it.
  3. Convert to CAD before comparing. USD billing plus the FX spread narrows the gap with CAD-billed rivals.
  4. Buy Professional the moment inventory matters — it is the first tier with stock tracking, purchase orders, and multi-currency.
  5. Weigh support risk against the saving. The value scores are excellent; the support complaints are real. Test support responsiveness during the trial.

Frequently asked questions

Is Zoho Books really free?

Yes, for businesses under US$50,000 in annual revenue: 1 user plus 1 accountant, 1,000 invoices a year, with invoicing, expenses, bank reconciliation, and a client portal. It is a permanent free tier, not a trial.

How is Zoho Books priced — per user or per company?

Per company (organization). Standard at US$15/month covers 3 users; Premium at US$60 covers 10. The per-user equivalent falls as the team grows, which inverts the usual SaaS math.

Does Zoho Books work for Canadian taxes?

It handles multi-currency and standard sales-tax tracking from the Standard tier up. For deep Canadian payroll (T4s, RL-1s, ROEs), Sage 50 or a dedicated payroll provider remains the stronger choice.
