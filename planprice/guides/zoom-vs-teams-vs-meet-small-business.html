Zoom vs Microsoft Teams vs Google Meet Pricing: The 10-Person Team Bill

Published 2026-10-12

Zoom, Teams, and Meet don't really compete as standalone products — they compete as parts of suites you may already pay for. That makes the pricing question simpler than the feature tables suggest: which suite do you already own, and what's the meeting-AI line item nobody prices? This guide works the 10-person annual bill for all three.

Free tiers: who can stay free

The free limits decide more purchases than the paid prices. Zoom's 40-minute cap on group calls is the most irritating in the category — one reviewer, after 200+ client calls in 2025, notes every important meeting hits it. Google Meet and Teams both allow 60 minutes free, which covers most real meetings. All three give unlimited one-on-one call time on free plans — the cap only kicks in at 3+ participants. A team that mostly does quick internal huddles can stay free on any of them; a team running hour-long client sessions can't stay free on Zoom.

10 people, one year: the bundled bill

  • Zoom Workplace Pro (annual): 10 × ~US$150 = US$1,500/yr (monthly billing: 10 × US$15.99 × 12 = US$1,918.80)
  • Teams Essentials: 10 × US$4 × 12 = US$480/yr — meetings only
  • Teams via M365 Business Basic: 10 × ~US$6.50 × 12 ≈ US$780/yr — meetings plus email and Office
  • Meet via Workspace Starter: 10 × US$7 × 12 = US$840/yr — meetings plus Gmail and Drive

Zoom is the only one sold as meetings alone, and it's the most expensive per seat by a wide margin. Teams and Meet look cheap because the meeting tool rides inside a suite — but only if the suite is one you'd buy anyway.

Meeting AI: the line item that flips it

This is the pricing distinction most comparisons miss entirely (Next Perimeter's breakdown): Zoom's AI Companion — meeting summaries, action items — is included in paid plans at no extra cost. Google's Gemini "take notes for me" is included in Workspace Business Standard (US$14) and above, but not in Starter (US$7). Microsoft charges for it: Teams Premium is a US$10/user/month add-on — US$1,200/year for ten people — for intelligent recap, AI notes, and translated captions.

Add AI to the 10-person bill and the ranking moves: Teams via Business Basic (US$780) + Teams Premium (US$1,200) = US$1,980/year — suddenly more than Zoom Pro's US$1,500 with AI included. If meeting summaries matter to your team, price the AI, not just the seats.

The suite you already own decides

For most small businesses this isn't a three-way choice. On Microsoft 365 already → Teams is the answer; the only question is whether AI summaries are worth the Premium add-on. On Google Workspace → Meet, and the jump from Starter to Standard (US$7 → US$14) buys both recording and Gemini notes. Buying meetings standalone → Zoom Pro is the premium pick for reliability and external meetings, and the one worth paying for if your calls are with clients, not colleagues.

What reviewers say

G2 ratings via ClickUp's 2026 alternatives table: Google Meet 4.6/5 (2,900+ reviews), Microsoft Teams 4.4/5 (17,700+ reviews) — Meet scores higher with far fewer reviewers. SelectHub's synthesis of a year of Zoom user reviews: reliability and ease of use remain the strengths; pricing and less-seamless integrations are the weaknesses, making it "less attractive for smaller businesses or those heavily reliant on competing ecosystems." The 200-call reviewer at GuideTechly lands the practical verdict: Zoom the most reliable on flaky internet, Meet the best free option, Teams the right pick inside Microsoft 365.

Decision: which team buys which

Microsoft shop → Teams (price the Premium add-on only if you want AI recaps). Google shop → Meet via Workspace; consider Standard over Starter for recording plus Gemini. Client-facing team on neither suite → Zoom Pro for the frictionless guest join and the included AI Companion. And if your meetings are short internal huddles, check the free tiers first — 60 free minutes on Meet or Teams covers most of them.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheapest for a small business?

Teams Essentials at US$4/user/month is the cheapest paid option; via M365 Business Basic (~US$6–7) or Workspace Starter (US$7) you get meetings plus the full suite. Zoom Pro (~US$150/user/year) is the most expensive per seat but includes AI summaries.

Is Zoom still worth it in 2026?

For external and client-facing meetings, yes — reviewers still rate it the most reliable and the easiest for guests to join. For internal meetings inside a Microsoft or Google shop, the bundled tools are usually the better buy.

Do I have to pay extra for AI meeting notes?

On Zoom paid plans and Google Workspace Standard+, no — they're included. On Teams, AI recaps need the US$10/user/month Teams Premium add-on. On Workspace Starter and all free tiers, no AI notes.
