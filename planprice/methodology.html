How PlanPrice Ranks And Verifies

What PlanPrice Is

PlanPrice is a pricing directory for business software. It tracks list prices and current promotions for tools small businesses buy, in categories including accounting, CRM and marketing, ecommerce, point of sale and payments, scheduling, field service, HR and payroll, work management, and SEO and marketing tools.

PlanPrice does not sell software. It reads the prices vendors publish, records them with the date they were checked, and shows buyers where each price came from. This page explains how that work is done, in the order it is done.

How Products Are Selected

Products are added when they are business software that small businesses buy on a subscription or per transaction basis and the vendor publishes pricing, or a clear quote only position, in public.

Three rules govern the tracked list:

  1. The vendor must publish enough public information to check. If a vendor publishes no list price, the product can still be tracked, and its listing is marked Custom. If nothing about pricing can be verified in public, the product is not listed with a price.
  2. Products are tracked because buyers compare them, not because a vendor asked to be listed. No vendor can pay to be added, and no product is removed because a vendor asked for removal of an accurate price.
  3. Readers can suggest a product or a category through the Contact page. Suggestions go onto a tracking shortlist and are added when they meet the first rule.

What Is Checked, And In What Order

Every product goes through the same checks. The order matters, because later steps rely on what the earlier steps recorded.

Step 1, the vendor's own pricing page. The starting price and the price of each published plan tier are read from the vendor's own published pricing and recorded with the date of the check. Vendor submitted list prices, press releases, and third party price listings are not sources. If the vendor publishes no price, the listing is marked Custom and no price is estimated.

Step 2, the current promotion. The vendor's own offer pages are checked for a public promotion. When a public promotion exists, the deal page shows the promotion terms and links to the vendor's own offer page. When no public promotion exists, the deal page says so plainly instead of presenting standard list prices as a deal.

Step 3, the review scores. The product's aggregate user ratings on G2 and Capterra are recorded with the score, the number of reviews behind it, the source, and the date checked. How those scores are used, and how they are not used, is set out in the Review Scores section below.

Step 4, publication with dates. The deal page is published or updated with the check date printed next to the price and next to the scores. A page keeps its older date wherever its pricing has not been re-checked. PlanPrice never re-dates a page it did not actually re-verify.

Step 5, the public record. Each check is entered in the Verification Log with the date, the product, the type of check, and what was found. Price and promotion changes between sweeps are recorded on the Price Changes page, and every deal page carries its own price history table. The method is published worked, not just described: the Canadian SaaS Pricing Report 2026 applies it to the full store of dated checks.

Review Scores From G2 And Capterra

Deal pages show aggregate user ratings from two third party review sites, G2 and Capterra. Those scores are labelled with their source on every page where they appear, for three reasons:

  1. They are not PlanPrice ratings. They describe other users' experience of the product, collected by G2 and Capterra, not by PlanPrice.
  2. They are shown as found. PlanPrice records the average score and the review count and does not adjust, round in its own favour, or reweight them.
  3. They are checked on a date, like prices. Each score shows the date it was last checked, and scores are re-checked during the monthly sweep described below.

How the scores are weighted in PlanPrice's own ordering: scores do not change any price shown on this site, and they do not decide the default order of the directory. They have exactly two jobs. First, they inform the reader, next to the price, because a cheap tool with poor user ratings and an expensive tool with strong ratings are different purchases. Second, alternatives tables on comparison pages are ordered by aggregate review score, and those tables say so. A high score on an expensive tool does not make it the right tool for a tight budget, and no score is presented as a recommendation on its own.

How Listings Are Ordered

Order is never sold. No vendor can pay to appear on PlanPrice, to rank higher, or to be featured. There are no sponsored placements anywhere on this site.

The default order on category and directory pages is an editorial featured order set by the editor. PlanPrice does not use a weighted scoring formula, and this page will not present the order as one. What a reader can check on every listing is the verified starting price, or the Custom marker where the vendor publishes no list price, and a plain statement of what the tool is best for, so the order can be judged against the prices themselves.

Two exceptions to the default order are mechanical and are labelled where they appear. The deals directory can be re-sorted by price by the reader. Alternatives tables on comparison pages are ordered by aggregate review score, as described above.

What makes one product sit above another in the featured order is editorial judgement about fit for the small businesses this site serves, applied by the editor named below. It is never payment, never an affiliate relationship, and never the review score alone.

The Monthly Price Sweep

PlanPrice runs a full price sweep every month. In each sweep, the list price and promotion status of every tracked product are re-checked against the vendor's own pricing and offer pages, and the review scores are re-checked against G2 and Capterra.

Deal pages show the date each price was last verified, per product. The Verification Log keeps the full record of each sweep and of checks made between sweeps, including corrections and expired promotions. When a sweep finds that a vendor changed a starting price or ended a promotion, the change is recorded on the Price Changes page with its date, and the affected deal page is updated with the new check date.

Between sweeps, vendors can and do change prices and promotions without notice. That is why every price on this site carries its check date, and why the final price is always the one on the vendor's own checkout page.

Corrections

Corrections are welcome, and pricing corrections take priority over other reader mail because an out of date price is the one thing this site cannot afford to carry.

To send a correction, use the Contact page and include the vendor and plan name, the price you saw, the date you saw it, and a link to the vendor's pricing page where you saw it. A screenshot of the vendor's pricing page helps when a price differs by region or account type.

Each correction is re-checked against the vendor's own site. If the correction checks out, the page is updated and its verified date moves. If the price shown here is still the price the vendor lists, the reply will say so. Corrections that change a price are entered in the Verification Log like any other check.

Who Reviews What

PlanPrice is edited by Mike, a retail IT professional who has spent more than ten years in IT vendor management. His author page gives his role and background.

Mike directs what PlanPrice covers and reviews the content before it is published, including the prices and scores recorded in the checks above and the check dates printed with them. AI tools assist with content production on this site. Mike directs that work and reviews the content before it is published.

If a check cannot be supported by the vendor's own published information, it does not go on a page as a verified price. That rule produced the one public exception in PlanPrice's record to date: where a guide's prices could only be checked against published guides rather than a live vendor page, the guide says so on its face instead of claiming the standard check.

No Hands-On Testing

PlanPrice does not do hands-on product testing. It does not buy, install, or run the products it lists, and it publishes no test results of its own.

What PlanPrice does is narrower, and it is the whole offer: it reads vendor published pricing carefully, dates every check, labels borrowed review scores with their source, and corrects the record in public when a price changes or a reader finds an error. Buyers who need hands-on evaluation should use free trials, which most tracked vendors offer, and the first party reviews on G2 and Capterra.

Affiliate Status And Ordering

Affiliate status is labelled per listing. No affiliate links are live today, so no listing earns PlanPrice a commission. When that changes, each affected listing will say so. The full position is set out in the Affiliate Disclosure.

Affiliate status, present or future, does not affect which products are tracked, the prices recorded, the scores shown, or the order of listings.

How Other Directories Rank

On Capterra, vendors pay referral fees for sponsored profiles, and Capterra's own transparency page says sponsored profiles may appear first on its product listings, identified by their link out buttons. On G2, vendors can buy advertising in sponsored positions on category and competitor pages; G2 says paying does not change its organic ranking or Grid scores. PlanPrice publishes those mechanics because buyers deserve to know how any directory they use makes its ordering decisions, including this one.
