Reading fee-change fine print

List prices are the visible part of a software bill. The changes that cost real money usually arrive as fee and term changes, announced in emails and help-centre notices rather than on pricing pages. When a vendor announces a change, five things are worth checking before the effective date:

  • Grandfathering. Does the change apply to your current plan, or only to new subscribers? Notices often say existing customers keep their rate for a period, sometimes until the next renewal. The period and its end date are the operative facts.
  • Renewal repricing. Many plans renew at the then-current rate. A price rise announced mid-term may not touch you until renewal, which makes the renewal date, not the announcement date, your decision deadline.
  • Per-transaction fees. Payment and usage fees can change separately from subscription prices: a percentage point here, a fixed cent amount there. On high transaction volume, a small fee change can outweigh a subscription change.
  • What counts toward your tier. Tiered plans reprice when a meter's definition changes, for example which contacts, seats, or transactions count. A definition change can move your bill without any price changing.
  • The notice itself. Change notices are usually binding under the terms you already accepted, and continued use after the effective date counts as acceptance. Save the notice, note the effective date, and compare at least one alternative before it arrives.
