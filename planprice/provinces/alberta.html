Alberta GST-Only & Software Costs: The 5% Province (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Alberta is the control case for Canadian software pricing: 5% GST and nothing else. No PST, no HST, no provincial return. A registered Alberta business recovers the 5% through input tax credits, which makes the province the one place in Canada where a software stack's true cost is simply its list price — the benchmark every other province's tax layer is measured against on this site.

Why it still matters to model

Two reasons. First, cash flow: on a $500/month stack the $25 GST is recovered, but on your return cycle, not at purchase — annual filers carry it longest. Second, expansion: the moment an Alberta business sells into other provinces, place-of-supply rules attach the customer's tax — 13% HST to Ontario, GST + QST treatment to Quebec, PST exposure in BC — and the invoicing setup has to apply it correctly from the first out-of-province invoice. Alberta businesses also occasionally discover a vendor charging a provincial tax line that doesn't exist here; a "PST" charge on an Alberta invoice for software is worth a question to the vendor.

Alberta checklist

Register for GST at the $30,000 threshold or voluntarily for ITC recovery. No provincial registration exists to match it. Selling out of province: configure your invoicing tool per-customer-province before you need it, and re-check when a province changes a rate — Nova Scotia's HST cut to 14% in April 2025 is the recent example.

General information only — not tax advice. Tax treatment depends on your registration status, what you sell, and where your customers are. Confirm your situation with the CRA, Revenu Québec, or your provincial tax authority, or a Canadian tax professional.

Worked example: the same stack, four provinces

Hold the business constant — $700 a month of subscriptions, fully registered, all tax recoverable where the system allows it — and only the province changes. Alberta: $35 GST charged, $35 recovered, true cost $700. Ontario: $91 HST charged, $91 recovered, true cost $700 (more cash in transit). Quebec: $35 GST + $69.83 QST charged, both recovered, true cost $700 (most cash in transit). British Columbia: $35 GST recovered, but $49 PST charged and kept — true cost $749. The table is the answer to "does province matter if I can claim it all back?": in three provinces it's a timing question; in BC it's a real 7% on the software line, and Alberta is the only province where the question doesn't arise at all.

Alberta traps (they're about other provinces)

First out-of-province sale, wrong tax — invoicing tools default to the seller's rate unless configured; an Alberta seller's first Ontario invoice at 5% undercharges 8 points of HST. Assuming no PST obligations anywhere — selling into BC or Quebec can create collection duties there even for an Alberta business. Rate drift — provinces change rates (Nova Scotia, April 2025); an invoicing template set once and never revisited silently goes stale. A five-minute annual check of your invoicing tool's provincial rate table is the whole defence.

Why Alberta stacks look cheaper in national comparisons

National "average software cost" figures quietly bake in other provinces' tax layers, which is why Alberta readers sometimes find published benchmarks $30–$80 a month above their own experience at the same stack size. When comparing your bills against any benchmark — including this site's 2026 benchmark, which reports pre-tax prices — Alberta is the baseline: your true cost is the benchmark figure, while a BC reader's is the figure plus 7% and an Ontario reader carries more cash timing. Vendors know this, incidentally: "plus tax" in Alberta means plus 5%, the smallest asterisk in the country.
