Atlantic Canada HST & Software Costs: 15% — and Nova Scotia's 14% (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Atlantic Canada is HST country with a recent split. New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Prince Edward Island charge 15% HST (5% federal + 10% provincial). Nova Scotia charges 14% (5% + 9%) since April 1, 2025, when the province cut its portion — the only rate change in the region in years, and one that older pricing pages and invoicing templates still get wrong. If a vendor's checkout applies 15% to a Nova Scotia address today, it is overcharging by a point.

Cost effect for software buyers

Like Ontario, the HST on business software is recoverable through input tax credits for registered businesses, in all four provinces — so for a registered business the Atlantic rates are a cash-flow layer, not a cost. The rate matters most to non-registered small suppliers (under the $30,000 threshold), who absorb 14–15% on every subscription with no recovery, and to any business pricing its own services into the region: place-of-supply rules put the customer's province's rate on the invoice, so an Ontario firm invoicing a Halifax client charges 14%, not 13%.

Atlantic checklist

Confirm your invoicing tool's Nova Scotia rate is 14% (templates set before April 2025 may still say 15%). Register for GST/HST at the threshold or voluntarily for ITCs. Note the regional point-of-sale rebates that exist on specific goods (for example certain children's items) — they do not apply to business software, so don't model them into a SaaS budget.

General information only — not tax advice. Tax treatment depends on your registration status, what you sell, and where your customers are. Confirm your situation with the CRA, Revenu Québec, or your provincial tax authority, or a Canadian tax professional.

Worked example: a Halifax café's stack

A registered Halifax café pays $450 a month across POS, accounting, payroll and scheduling. Nova Scotia HST at 14% adds $63 — fully recoverable as ITCs, so the true cost is $450, with $63 of cash timing until the return settles it. The same café in 2024 would have carried $67.50 at the old 15% rate; the April 2025 cut is worth $4.50 a month on this stack — small, but it is also the kind of change that breaks invoicing templates: a café whose POS still applies a hard-coded 15% "Nova Scotia" rate is overcharging every customer by a point and accumulating a correction problem. Check the rate your systems actually apply, not the rate you remember.

Regional notes

Tourism seasonality makes Atlantic software budgets lumpy — subscriptions are flat while revenue isn't, so the stack's share of revenue doubles in the off-season; annual-billing discounts are worth more here than in steadier markets, provided cash allows. Selling into the region from elsewhere: place of supply means a mainland firm invoicing Atlantic clients applies 15% (or 14% for Nova Scotia), and clients will notice a wrong rate — Atlantic businesses check, because their own rate changed recently enough to be memorable.

Filing rhythm in HST provinces

Atlantic businesses file GST/HST on the same cycles as Ontario — annual, quarterly or monthly by revenue and election — and the software-tax recovery rides that cycle entirely: there is no separate provincial claim for the HST on your subscriptions. Two practical consequences. First, a business that buys a year of software upfront in an annual-billing discount sees the whole HST amount on one invoice and recovers it on the next return — a timing lump worth planning around, not a cost. Second, annual filers with big software renewals cluster should check whether quarterly filing smooths their cash position enough to be worth the extra returns; for software-heavy small firms it frequently does.
