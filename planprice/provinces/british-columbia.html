BC GST + PST & Software Costs: Why the 7% PST Is a Real Cost (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

British Columbia looks cheaper than Ontario on the sticker — 5% GST + 7% PST = 12% — and is quietly more expensive for software buyers, because of one rule: BC's PST applies to software, including software accessed remotely (SaaS) when it is used in BC, and PST paid on business inputs is generally not recoverable. GST paid is recoverable through input tax credits for registered businesses; the 7% PST line is a true cost that stays in your expense column.

The real comparison

On a $500/month stack: BC adds $25 GST (recoverable) + $35 PST (not recoverable) — true cost $535. Ontario's 13% HST adds $65, all recoverable — true cost $500. A BC business comparing two tools on list price alone understates its software budget by 7% of every PST-taxable line, every month. When PlanPrice totals a cost stack for a BC reader, the PST layer belongs in the total, not in a footnote; our business teardowns flag taxes as extra precisely because this layer varies by province.

Who charges it

Vendors selling software for use in BC are generally required to register and collect BC PST on those sales, including many out-of-province and non-resident vendors. If a vendor doesn't charge it, a BC buyer may owe the tax by self-assessment — the liability doesn't disappear because the invoice was silent. Software bought partly for use inside and outside BC can need allocation; keep the seat/usage records that support yours.

BC checklist

Budget software at list + 7% PST as the working figure. Register for GST when you pass the $30,000 threshold (PST registration is the vendor's side for software sales; buyers register for PST in specific cases such as bringing taxable goods in). If you sell software or taxable services to BC customers from another province, check your own PST collection obligations — BC expects registration from sellers with BC customers in many cases.

General information only — not tax advice. Tax treatment depends on your registration status, what you sell, and where your customers are. Confirm your situation with the CRA, Revenu Québec, or your provincial tax authority, or a Canadian tax professional.

Worked example: a Vancouver retailer's software year

A BC retailer spends $12,000 a year on POS software, accounting, payroll and scheduling subscriptions that are PST-taxable. The PST line is $840 a year — not recoverable, not deductible against the PST itself, simply cost. Run the same stack in Alberta and the tax cost is $0 (GST recovered); in Ontario, $1,560 of HST is charged and $1,560 recovered. BC's $840 is therefore the most expensive software-tax outcome of the three for a registered business, despite BC's mid-pack headline rate. It also changes vendor comparisons: a tool that's $20 a month cheaper before tax but PST-taxable can tie with a rival after the 7% is applied to both — always compare BC software prices tax-in.

BC traps for software buyers

Budgeting at list price — the 7% miss compounds across every subscription; a $500 stack is a $535 stack. Assuming a US vendor's invoice is final — if PST wasn't charged on software for BC use, self-assessment may be owed. Partial-use software — a tool used by staff in BC and another province needs a defensible allocation; keep seat records by location. Selling software or SaaS into BC — sellers can owe PST collection on BC customers' purchases even without a BC office; check before your first BC sale, because registering late doesn't erase earlier periods.
