Ontario HST & Software Costs: What 13% Means for Your SaaS Bill (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Ontario is the simplest large province to buy software in: one tax, one return, and — for a GST/HST-registered business — the tax on your software bill is mostly a pass-through. Ontario charges a single Harmonized Sales Tax of 13% (5% federal GST + 8% provincial portion), administered by the CRA. SaaS subscriptions, POS software, accounting platforms and the other services PlanPrice tracks are taxable supplies, so the 13% is added to the vendor's price — and a registered business claims it back as an input tax credit (ITC) on the same return where it remits the HST it collected.

What that does to a real stack

Take a $500/month software stack bought from vendors who charge tax properly. Ontario adds $65 of HST. A registered business recovers the full $65 as an ITC, so the true cost stays $500 — the $65 is a cash-flow timing item, not an expense. Two groups feel the tax for real: businesses under the $30,000 small-supplier threshold who choose not to register (they pay HST on inputs with no ITC recovery, but also don't charge it on sales), and any business buying from a vendor whose invoice doesn't show the tax separately, which complicates the ITC claim. If a subscription invoice lacks a GST/HST registration number or a tax breakdown, ask the vendor for a corrected one before claiming.

Place of supply: your customers' provinces set their rate

The HST you charge is not set by your Ontario address. Under the CRA's place-of-supply rules, the rate follows where your customer receives the goods or services: an Ontario seller billing a BC client charges 5% GST plus BC PST treatment as applicable, and an Ontario client of an Alberta supplier is charged 13% HST. Software that bills by seat gets this wrong quietly — check that your invoicing tool applies the customer's province, not yours.

Ontario checklist

Register for a GST/HST account once taxable supplies pass $30,000 over four consecutive quarters (registration is voluntary below that, and voluntary registration is what unlocks ITCs). File on your assigned cycle (annual, quarterly, or monthly). Keep vendor invoices showing the HST amount and the supplier's registration number — they are your ITC evidence. Software bought from non-resident vendors: many now register under the simplified GST/HST regime and charge the tax; if one doesn't, a registered buyer may need to self-assess the Ontario portion.

General information only — not tax advice. Tax treatment depends on your registration status, what you sell, and where your customers are. Confirm your situation with the CRA, Revenu Québec, or your provincial tax authority, or a Canadian tax professional.

Worked example: a Toronto service firm's stack

Take a registered Toronto consultancy paying $800 a month across Workspace seats, a CRM, accounting software and project tools, all invoiced with tax shown. Ontario HST adds $104. The firm claims the full $104 as ITCs on its quarterly return, so the stack's true cost is $800 — but its cash position carries the $104 until the return is filed and the net remittance settled. Quarterly filers feel this as a small permanent loan to the timing of the system; monthly filers barely notice it. The same firm unregistered (under $30,000, by choice) pays the $104 as a genuine cost and charges no HST on its own invoices — competitive on price to consumers, expensive on inputs, and the trade only makes sense while input costs stay small relative to sales.

Ontario mistakes worth avoiding

Three recur. Claiming ITCs from invoices without the supplier's GST/HST number — the CRA can deny the credit; get the corrected invoice. Applying 13% to out-of-province customers — place of supply sets their rate, and overcharging a BC or Alberta client their wrong tax is a refund-and-correct exercise. Assuming US SaaS is tax-free — many non-resident vendors now charge GST/HST under the simplified regime, and where they don't, self-assessment can apply to the Ontario portion; "no tax on the invoice" is not the same as "no tax owed."
