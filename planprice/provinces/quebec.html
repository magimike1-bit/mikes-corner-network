Quebec GST + QST & Software Costs: 5% + 9.975% Explained (2026)

Published 2026-10-25 · Last updated 2026-10-25

Quebec runs its own system. A Quebec small business pays 5% GST plus 9.975% QST — a combined 14.975% — on the software and services it buys, and Revenu Québec administers both taxes inside the province. The QST is charged on the price before GST (the two taxes no longer compound). For a registered business, both halves are recoverable: GST through input tax credits, QST through input tax refunds (ITRs), filed on the combined return.

The registration wrinkle that matters for software

Since 2019, specified non-resident suppliers — the category that captures many foreign SaaS vendors selling to Quebec consumers and non-registered buyers — must register for QST under a simplified system and charge it, even with no physical presence in Quebec. In practice: a Quebec business buying software from a US or overseas vendor will often see QST on the invoice where a buyer in another province sees only GST or nothing. For a QST-registered buyer that tax is recoverable via ITR; for a non-registered small supplier it is a real 9.975% cost on top of the sticker price — worth modelling before choosing the cheaper-looking foreign tool over a Canadian one that itemizes taxes you can recover.

What it does to a monthly stack

On a $500/month stack, Quebec adds $25 GST + $49.88 QST = $74.88. Registered: both amounts come back. Not registered: the stack's true cost is $574.88 — nearly 15% more than the Alberta equivalent, and the gap grows with every seat. This is the province where the register-voluntarily calculation most often favours registering early.

Quebec checklist

Register with Revenu Québec for GST and QST (one process, administered provincially) when taxable supplies pass $30,000, or voluntarily for recovery. File the combined return on your assigned cycle. If you sell into Quebec from another province, the place-of-supply rules generally put the QST on Quebec customers — and specified-registration rules can pull out-of-province suppliers in; get advice before your first Quebec invoice, not after.

General information only — not tax advice. Tax treatment depends on your registration status, what you sell, and where your customers are. Confirm your situation with the CRA, Revenu Québec, or your provincial tax authority, or a Canadian tax professional.

Worked example: a Montréal studio's stack

A registered Montréal design studio pays $600 a month in subscriptions. Its invoices show $30 GST and $59.85 QST. Both come back — GST as ITCs, QST as ITRs — on the combined Revenu Québec return, so the true cost is $600, with $89.85 of cash timing in between. The unregistered version of the same studio pays the full $689.85 as cost. At what point does voluntary registration pay? Roughly when recoverable tax on all inputs (software, rent where taxable, equipment, subcontractors' taxes) exceeds the administrative cost of filing plus the competitive effect of adding 14.975% to prices for non-registered clients — for B2B studios whose clients recover the tax themselves, that point usually arrives almost immediately.

Quebec-specific traps

Assuming the CRA is your counterparty — in Quebec it's Revenu Québec for both taxes, including your GST return. Missing ITRs on US SaaS — the QST a foreign vendor charges under specified registration is recoverable for registrants; leaving it unclaimed is a quiet 9.975% donation on every foreign subscription. Selling into Quebec from outside — place-of-supply plus the specified-registration rules can require QST collection by vendors with no Quebec presence; out-of-province sellers should confirm their obligations before their Quebec sales grow, not at audit time.
