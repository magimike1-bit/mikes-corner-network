Canadian SaaS Pricing Report 2026: What Our Own Price Checks Actually Show

Published 2026-11-13 · Last updated 2026-11-13 · By Mike, Editor

Mike is a retail IT professional with more than 10 years in IT vendor management. PlanPrice tracks what Canadian small businesses actually pay for software, and this report is built only from price checks we ran and stored ourselves.

Key Findings At A Glance

  • Most prices did not move. Of 196 plan labels we checked two or more times between August and November 2026, 190 read exactly the same at the latest check as at the first check.
  • Six readings differed. After adjudication, five are list price changes across three vendors: Zoho, Canva, and Basecamp. One, a Hostinger introductory rate, is promotion movement, not a list change.
  • The bigger story is restructuring, not headline prices. Basecamp rebuilt its plan ladder, Canva withdrew Teams for new buyers, ClickUp added a second, higher pricing line beside its legacy line, and Katana and Workiz stopped publishing prices at all.
  • Annual billing saved a median of 20.0 percent against monthly billing across 58 matched plan pairs from 28 vendors.
  • Canadian list prices are usually set below a straight currency conversion. Across 13 matched Canadian and US plan pairs outside one exception vendor, the Canadian price implied an exchange rate of 1.22 to 1.37, while the market rate we recorded in the same period ran 1.3977 to 1.42671.
  • At least 40 tracked vendors offer a free plan, and 51 tracked vendors have a paid entry plan under $15 per month on the definitions set out in Section 5.

1. How This Report Was Built

What The Dataset Is

This report uses one source: the PlanPrice research store, our own file of dated price checks. At the time of writing the store holds 184 tracked entities, with 1,149 stored price readings and 711 stored facts. Of the 184 entities, 170 hold at least one price reading. Each reading records the plan label, the value, the currency, the date we checked it, and the source page it came from, across 407 distinct source pages. Every figure in this report traces to readings in that store, cited by entity, plan label, and check date in the tables and in Appendix A. The 49 products with full deal pages on PlanPrice are a subset of these 184 entities.

The readings carry check dates from 2026-08-04 to 2026-11-13. The bulk of the checking happened in fall 2026: 918 readings are dated October 2026 and 219 are dated November 2026, with 10 dated September 2026 and 2 carried readings dated 2026-08-04 (entity hootsuite). Where a plan was checked more than once, this report compares the earliest and latest readings of the identical label and currency.

In total, 124 vendors have price readings on two or more distinct dates, and 79 vendors have at least one identical plan label checked two or more times. Those repeat checks form 196 label groups, and they are the only basis for the change findings in Section 2. Appendix A reproduces all 196 groups so any reader can audit every number in this report against the store.

What This Dataset Cannot Show

Readers should hold five limits in mind.

First, the history is short. Four months of spot checks cannot show seasonal patterns, and a vendor with no observed change may simply not have been rechecked near a change date.

Second, these are list prices before promotions and before taxes, unless a reading is explicitly labelled as a promotion or an introductory rate. Section 2 shows why the distinction matters: several readings that first looked like price changes turned out to be promotion prices, headline prices, or corrections to an earlier third party reading. Appendix C lists those cases.

Third, coverage is uneven. Some vendors, such as Shopify, Xero, and QuickBooks, carry readings from several dates and both currencies. Others carry a single check. A finding stated for the vendors we could match is not a finding about all 184 entities.

Fourth, most readings come from vendor pricing pages, and the rest come from reputable secondary pages that cite the vendor page. Where the two kinds of source disagree, we say so beside the figure rather than averaging them.

Fifth, we never fill gaps. If the store holds one reading, the report says one reading. If a pattern needs two readings and only one exists, the pattern is dropped, and Section 2 gives one prominent example.

3. Monthly Versus Annual Billing

Where a vendor publishes the same plan on both billing periods, annual billing was cheaper in every matched pair we found. We matched 58 plan pairs across 28 vendors where the store holds both a monthly billing reading and an annual billing reading for the same plan, on a per month basis. The saving from paying annually, measured against the monthly billing rate, ran from 4.0 percent to 60.0 percent, with a median of 20.0 percent. Two readings of this table matter for buyers. First, the gap is a real budget line: on a ten seat team, the median gap of 20.0 percent is the difference between a manageable renewal and a surprise. Second, the gap is not uniform, so the generic advice to always pay annually is wrong at the edges. Capsule CRM gives back only 4.0 to 14.3 percent across its four tiers (entity capsule-crm, all readings checked 2026-10-25), while Grammarly gives back 60.0 percent. One related trap showed up in the Salesflare change in Section 2: a vendor can raise the monthly rate and leave the annual rate alone, which quietly widens the gap and pushes buyers toward annual terms. The annual price staying flat is not the same as pricing staying flat.

The pattern across these 13 pairs is consistent: implied rates of 1.22 to 1.37, all below the recorded market range of 1.3977 to 1.42671. A Canadian business that pays the Canadian list on these platforms pays less than it would by paying the US list on a US dollar card at market exchange, before card foreign transaction fees widen the gap further. The practical rule follows directly: if a vendor offers a Canadian list, price the contract in Canadian dollars first, and treat a US dollar quote as the option that needs to justify itself.

One vendor breaks the pattern, and the exception is instructive. Zoho Books prices Canada independently rather than by conversion (entity zoho-books, Canadian readings checked 2026-11-03, US readings checked 2026-10-12). Standard carries the same numeral in both currencies, 15 CAD against 15 USD. Professional and Premium carry lower numerals in Canada, 30 CAD against 40 USD and 40 CAD against 60 USD. The top tiers reverse, with Elite at 165 CAD against 120 USD and Ultimate at 290 CAD against 240 USD, implied rates of 1.38 and 1.21. There is no single conversion rule to learn here. Each market is priced on its own, tier by tier, which is exactly why a Canadian buyer should never estimate a Canadian price by converting the US page.

One pair we could not use: the store holds two readings for 1,000 Klaviyo SMS sends in Canada on the same date, 2026-10-11, one labelled CAD and one labelled USD, both at 18 (entity klaviyo). Until that conflict is resolved by a fresh check, no Canadian versus US claim is made for Klaviyo messaging rates.

Entry Prices Under $15 Per Month

For entry pricing we used the latest reading for each vendor and counted the cheapest genuine subscription plan: a per user, per seat, per agent, or flat per month plan. We excluded per transaction rates, usage units, add ons, hardware, introductory promotion rates, and personal or family plans. Duplicate store entities for the same product were merged before counting. On that basis, 51 tracked vendors have a paid entry plan under $15 per month. Appendix B lists all 51 with their citations.

The shape of the entry market, by example: password managers start lowest, with NordPass Teams from 1.79 USD per user per month billed annually (entity nordpass, label NordPass Teams (from, annual billing), checked 2026-10-20), Proton Pass Essentials at 1.99 USD (entity proton-pass, checked 2026-10-20), and Bitwarden Teams at 4 USD (entity bitwarden, checked 2026-11-13). Core team tools cluster between 7 USD and 11 USD: Google Workspace Business Starter at 7 USD (entity google-workspace, checked 2026-10-16), Microsoft 365 Business Basic at 7 USD (entity microsoft-365, checked 2026-10-11), Slack Pro at 7.25 USD billed annually (entity slack, checked 2026-10-27), Notion Plus at 10 USD (entity notion, checked 2026-10-11), and Asana Starter at 10.99 USD billed annually (entity asana, checked 2026-10-23). Canadian dollar entry points exist but are rarer in the store: 1Password Teams Starter Pack at 2.99 CAD per user per month on annual billing (entity 1password, checked 2026-10-11) and DocuSign Personal at 13 CAD per month on annual billing (entity docusign, checked 2026-11-13) are the clearest examples, and the Wave Starter plan at 0 CAD (entity wave, checked 2026-10-27) remains the strongest free entry in Canadian accounting.

The honest limit on this section: entry price is the floor of a product, not its cost. Seat minimums, contact tiers, and usage units move real bills well above the entry figure, and the store holds many of those ladders for readers who need the full picture on a specific vendor.

6. What This Means For A Small Business

I have sat on the buyer side of vendor renewals for more than a decade, and the pattern in our own data matches what I saw across the table: the list price is the quiet part. It barely moved in four months. The money moves in the structure around the price, and structure is where a small business should spend its attention.

Budget renewals at the structure, not the headline. When Basecamp rebuilt its ladder, a business on the old per user plan did not face a price increase, it faced a different product with caps it had never had. When ClickUp opened a second pricing line beside the legacy line, the question for existing customers became which line their renewal lands on. Put a calendar note 60 days before every annual renewal and spend that window confirming which plan, which line, and which caps apply to your account. That single habit is worth more than negotiating the rate.

Price both billing periods for the actual plan, every time. The median annual saving in our data is 20.0 percent, which is real money, but the range runs from 4.0 percent to 60.0 percent, and the low end does not justify locking cash into a year of a tool you may drop. Run the two numbers for your plan and your seat count, and let that arithmetic, not a rule of thumb, make the call.

Buy in Canadian dollars where a Canadian list exists. On the platforms we could match, the Canadian list was priced below a straight conversion every time. Paying a US dollar price on a Canadian card stacks a worse exchange outcome on top of card fees. If a vendor will only quote in US dollars, that is a cost to name in the negotiation, not a detail to absorb quietly.

Treat free tiers as a trial with better marketing. Forty vendors in our store offer one, and two vendors in this same report period withdrew or ended free access. Use free tiers to learn a tool and to prove a workflow. Do not build a process your business depends on, client records, billing, customer history, on a tier the vendor can retire with a page update.

Keep your own price record. Every finding in this report exists because someone wrote down a price, a plan name, and a date, and then checked again later. A small business can do the same in a spreadsheet in an afternoon: plan, rate, billing period, renewal date, and the price page saved. At renewal time, that record is the difference between accepting a vendor account of what you agreed to pay and knowing what you agreed to pay.

We will keep checking. This report will be updated as the store grows, and the first additions on our list are the US coverage gaps named in Section 2 and a longer change history for the vendors our readers use most.
