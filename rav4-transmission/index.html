2012 Toyota RAV4 · 2.5L 4-cylinder & 3.5L V6

Change your RAV4's transmission fluid — properly, at home.

The 2012 RAV4 still has a transmission dipstick, a drain plug, and a serviceable strainer — which makes it one of the last genuinely DIY-friendly automatic transmissions Toyota sold. These guides walk through the drain-and-fill step by step, with the right fluid (Toyota ATF WS, nothing else), the right torque, and the safety steps that matter.

Start with the step-by-step guide →

Toyota's own service bulletin calls ATF WS a fluid that doesn't require changing "during the life of the vehicle" under normal use — and in the same breath warns that the wrong fluid can damage the transmission. Both things are true, and neither means you should ignore 14-year-old fluid. This site explains what the factory actually says, when a fluid change makes sense, how to do a careful drain-and-fill (or a pan drop with a new strainer), and which symptoms fresh fluid can fix — and which it can't.

Safety first, on every page of this site: transmission fluid comes out hot enough to burn. Support the vehicle on jack stands on level ground — never work under a vehicle held up by a jack alone. Let used ATF cool, seal it in a jug, and take it to a recycling depot; never pour it down a drain or onto the ground.
