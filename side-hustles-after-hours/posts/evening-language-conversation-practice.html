Evening Language Conversation Practice: What the Commission Tiers Leave You

Skill hustles · 7 min read

Published 2026-11-03 · Last updated 2026-11-03

Disclaimer: General information only, not financial, tax or legal advice. Earnings vary and are never guaranteed. Commission figures below were checked on 2026-11-03 from the platforms' own pages and change. Check your employment contract before starting.

The figure repeated most often about italki, a flat 15% commission, is out of date. italki charged a flat 15% until late 2024, and its current schedule, taken from its own help and policy pages and checked on 2026-09-30, is tiered: 0% on trial lessons, 21% on single lessons, and 19%, 17% or 15% on packages depending on size (s1). If you speak a language other people want to practise, that schedule, not the old flat figure, is the business. Here is the rest of it, starting with the reasons to walk away.

Who should not do this, and the stop line

Do not start if your fluency is conversational rather than confident, if you cannot correct errors kindly and clearly, or if you need the income to be steady in the first month. Students book a person, at a specific evening hour, in a specific language. That is a narrow shopfront, and it fills slowly.

The stop line, set before the first lesson: after eight weeks of holding at least three evening slots a week, add up everything actually withdrawn, or withdrawable, and divide by every hour spent teaching, preparing and messaging. If the result sits below the rate those evenings earn in work you can already get, close the profile or reprice once, properly, for new students only. Do not respond to a low effective rate by adding more slots at the same price.

Two platforms, one evening: what the tutor keeps

Both platforms split their teachers the same basic way. italki lists Professional Teachers, who hold qualifications or teaching experience, and Community Tutors, who are native or near-native speakers focused on conversation (s3). Conversation practice is the community tutor product: no curriculum promises, no exam guarantees, just structured talking with correction. That honesty is also the marketing. Read the trial row twice, because it decides the first month. On italki a trial lesson pays you in full at your trial price (s1). On Preply, the commission for every trial lesson with a new student is 100%, and the platform frames that lesson as your chance to gain a regular student (s2). Neither is a trick. They are two different prices for student acquisition, and an evening tutor with six slots a week feels the Preply version much more sharply than a full time tutor does.

The withdrawal is a second, smaller commission

italki pays earnings in credits tied to US dollars, the minimum withdrawal is $30, and while italki charges no withdrawal fee itself, the payment providers do: PayPal at about 2%, Hyperwallet at 1.5%, and Payoneer at a $3 flat fee per transfer (s1). For an evening tutor, the practical lesson is boring and valuable. Withdraw less often, in larger amounts, and the flat fee stops mattering. Withdraw every small balance through a percentage route and the second commission quietly becomes a third one, once currency movement is counted, which italki leaves with the teacher (s1).

Price geography matters as much as fees. italki lessons start from $4 according to its own pricing page, with most conversation practice sitting between $4 and $25, across more than 150 languages (s3). A common language puts you in a global price pool. A less commonly taught language, or a specific dialect and exam free conversation niche, is where an evening rate can hold.

A labelled evening scenario at a $20 lesson

Scenario, not a forecast. You list conversation practice at $20 a lesson. On italki, four single lessons in a week gross $80 and keep $63.20 after the 21% single lesson commission (s1). The same four lessons sold as part of larger packages keep more, up to $68 at the 15% package tier (s1), which is why steady students on packages are worth more than a churn of one off bookings.

On Preply, starting out, four regular lessons at $20 keep $53.60 after the 33% opening commission (s2). Add one new student's trial lesson that week and you taught five lessons for that same $53.60, because the trial paid nothing (s2). None of these figures count preparation, profile upkeep or messages, and the stop line above exists precisely because those hours are real. Count them from week one, in a simple log, or the scenario flatters you.

Setting up, in order

  1. Pick one platform and one language offer first. Splitting a thin evening schedule across two platforms splits your reviews, which are the asset that raises your rate.
  2. Write the profile around conversation outcomes. Business small talk, travel confidence, interview practice. Specific beats fluent.
  3. Record the short introduction video the platforms ask for. For conversation practice, the video is the product sample. Speak the way a lesson will feel.
  4. Open three evening slots, the same three every week. Students in other time zones build routines around a tutor who is reliably there. Reliability is a feature a day job accidentally provides, since your evenings are already structured.
  5. Set a package friendly price. On italki, packages carry the lower commission tiers (s1), so a price that works as a single lesson works better as a package. Know both numbers before you publish.

What success looks like: a student finishes a lesson, books the next one inside the platform before signing off, and your log shows the hour, the commission tier and the amount you will actually keep.

The decision rule after eight weeks

Keep the platform where repeat students, not trials, fill your slots. Reprice the other one or close it. Conversation practice is a good evening hustle for a fluent speaker with a scarce language or a clear niche, and a poor one for anyone competing on price in a crowded language at the single lesson tier. The commission tables above, checked on 2026-11-03, tell you which of those you are before your calendar does.
