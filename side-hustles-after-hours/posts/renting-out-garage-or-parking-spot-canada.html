Renting Out Your Garage or Parking Spot in Canada: Rules and Real Returns

Hustle ideas · 7 min read

Published 2026-10-29 · Last updated 2026-10-29

Disclaimer: General information only — not financial, tax or legal advice. Earnings vary and are never guaranteed. Check your employment contract and local rules before starting.

A parking spot feels like the simplest rent in the world: no tenants in your kitchen, no plumbing, just a rectangle and a monthly payment. That simplicity is the misconception. In Canada, renting a garage bay or a driveway spot still creates rental income, still runs into condo, strata and municipal rules, and still puts someone else's property — or someone else's car — on yours. The money can be sensible. It is only sensible after three checks, in this order.

Check one: are you allowed to rent it at all?

Start with permission, not price. Work through the layers that can each say no on their own:

  1. If you rent your home, read your lease first. Subletting or assigning use of a parking space you do not own is a lease question before it is an income question. Get the landlord's answer in writing.
  2. If you own a condo or strata unit, read the bylaws and rules for parking. Parking stalls are often common property or limited common property, and many corporations restrict rentals to residents of the building. Ask the board or manager, in writing, whether renting to a non-resident is permitted.
  3. Check the municipal layer. Driveway widening, front-yard parking and the number of vehicles that may be stored on a lot are municipal matters, and they differ by city. A spot that is legal to use yourself is not automatically legal to rent.
  4. Consider tenancy law in your province. A standalone parking rental is a different arrangement from parking supplied with a rented home, and which rules apply depends on how the spot is supplied. If the answer is unclear, that uncertainty is itself a reason to pause and get local advice before taking money.

What success looks like: you can point to a written yes — or at least no prohibition — at every layer that applies to you. One verbal okay from one layer is not a pass.

Check two: insurance and liability for what sits in the space

A garage used for storage holds someone else's goods. A driveway spot holds someone else's vehicle. Either way, two questions decide whether a small rent is worth the exposure. First, does your home insurance policy treat renting part of the property as a change it needs to know about? Ask the insurer directly and keep the answer. Second, who carries the loss if stored goods are stolen or damaged, or if a renter's vehicle damages your property — or the reverse? Put the allocation in the written agreement (below), because goodwill is not a policy.

Do not skip this check because the rent is small. The rent is monthly and modest; a liability dispute is neither.

Check three: the tax position

The Canada Revenue Agency names space rentals, including parking spots, as a form of sharing-economy activity, and income from the sharing economy must be included on your return; GST/HST may also need to be collected and paid depending on your situation (s1). More generally, rental income must be reported, and related expenses may be deductible — with current expenses claimed in the year they are incurred and capital expenses amortized over several years (s2).

Renting part of a home makes it a mixed-use asset, which can affect both the expense calculation and the principal residence exemption, and the guidance is to report 100% of rental income, including parking fees, even when it is paid in cash or by a family member; rental income from a home is generally reported on CRA Form T776 (s3). None of that is a reason not to rent a spot. It is a reason to keep records from the first payment: dates, amounts, the agreement, and receipts for any expense you intend to claim.

The agreement: terms that prevent the common fights

Keep it short, written and specific. The terms that do the work are: what exactly is rented (which stall, bay or marked area, and what may be stored there); the monthly amount and due date; the notice period for either side to end it; access hours and how the renter gets in; a ban on storing hazardous or illegal material; who is responsible for snow, lighting and keeping the access clear; and the insurance allocation from check two. Most disputes in this kind of rental are not about the rent — they are about access, stored clutter creeping beyond the agreed area, and how quickly the space must be vacated. Answer those three in writing and the arrangement usually stays boring, which is the goal.

The return model: gross rent minus the real costs

The displaced-parking line is the one most people omit, and it changes verdicts. Renting out the only garage bay while paying to park your own car elsewhere can turn a tidy gross figure into a near-zero trade. Count it every time.

The decision rule

Proceed only if all three checks pass and the net line, with displaced parking counted, still beats keeping the space for your own use. Set a kill criterion before the first renter arrives: if the arrangement produces a bylaw complaint, an insurance problem, or two late payments in the first three months, end it on the notice period you wrote into the agreement. Small, boring, reversible — that is the standard this hustle has to meet, because the gross rent will never be large enough to justify a fight.

Questions about renting a spot or garage

Is parking income taxable if it is paid in cash? Yes on the sources used here: report 100% of rental income including parking fees, even if paid in cash or by a family member (s3), and sharing-economy income must be included on your return (s1).

Which CRA form is used? Rental income from a home is generally reported on Form T776 (s3). Expenses follow the current-versus-capital distinction described by CRA (s2).

What rent should a spot fetch? No sourced local rate is carried in this piece — that figure has to come from current listings where the spot actually is. Use the model above rather than a number copied from another city.
