Slow cooker beef stroganoff: temper the sour cream, thicken before the dairy, and keep the noodles separate

Beef · Published Nov 17, 2026 · 7 min read

Total time: about 8 hours 20 minutes on LOW or 5 hours 20 minutes on HIGH (about 20 minutes hands on, the rest cooking and finishing) · Difficulty: easy · Yield: 6 servings, sauce and beef over noodles

Stroganoff is not a dump everything in dinner, however often it is written that way. The sour cream cannot go in at the start. Sour cream that cooks for hours curdles and turns grainy, and a sauce thickened after the dairy goes in never quite recovers. The order is the recipe: beef and mushrooms first, cornstarch second, noodles in their own pot, and sour cream last, tempered and off the heat.

The three failures, and what causes each

Split sauce comes from dairy meeting prolonged heat. The fix is timing, not a different brand of sour cream. Thin sauce comes from thickening too late. Mushroom and beef juices keep coming out of the pot all afternoon, so a slurry stirred in before the dairy has 15 to 20 minutes on HIGH to do its work while the sauce can still boil safely. Mushy noodles come from cooking them in the cooker, where they keep absorbing sauce with no moment when they are exactly done. One checked recipe puts it plainly: egg noodles turn to mush in a slow cooker, and 10 minutes on the stove is the right way.

Tough beef is the fourth complaint, and it usually means the clock was trusted over the fork. Chuck in large chunks needs the long window below. Small stew meat finishes sooner. The piece size written into a recipe is part of its timing, which is why the checked recipes disagree by two hours and can all be describing their own pots accurately.

Ingredients and equipment

For the main route, in a 5 to 6 quart (4.7 to 5.7 L) slow cooker:

  • 900 g (2 lb) boneless beef chuck, trimmed, cut into 3.5 cm (1½ inch) chunks
  • 15 mL (1 tablespoon) olive oil
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 340 g (12 oz) cremini or button mushrooms, thickly sliced
  • 360 mL (1½ cups) low sodium beef broth
  • 15 mL (1 tablespoon) Worcestershire sauce
  • 15 mL (1 tablespoon) Dijon mustard
  • 2.5 mL (1/2 teaspoon) dried thyme
  • 2.5 mL (1/2 teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
  • 2.5 mL (1/2 teaspoon) ground black pepper

To finish: 30 mL (2 tablespoons) cornstarch mixed with 30 mL (2 tablespoons) cold water, and 180 g (3/4 cup) full fat sour cream brought toward room temperature. To serve: 340 g (12 oz) dry egg noodles and 30 mL (2 tablespoons) chopped fresh parsley. Substitutions, labelled as such: pre-cut stew beef follows the same route at a similar size. Sirloin is named as a swap in the checked recipes, with a caution attached to lean meat in general: lean steak can become dry, while chuck softens because its connective tissue breaks down over the long cook.

The method, step by step

  1. Brown the beef, about 10 minutes. Pat the chunks dry. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium high heat and brown the beef in batches, about 2 minutes per side, leaving space between the pieces. The beef does not need to cook through at this stage. Browning builds flavour, and the step can be skipped on a rushed day with that cost understood.
  2. Load the cooker, 5 minutes. Scrape the browned bits from the skillet with a little of the measured broth and tip that liquid in. Add the beef, onion, garlic, and mushrooms, then the remaining broth, Worcestershire sauce, Dijon, thyme, salt, and pepper. The liquid will not completely cover the beef. The ingredients release moisture as they cook.
  3. Cook on LOW for 7 to 8 hours, or HIGH for 4 to 5 hours. Keep the lid in place as much as possible to retain heat. For comparison, a checked small piece version using 450 g (1 lb) of stew meat cut to about 1.25 cm (1/2 inch) finishes in about 6 hours on LOW, which is a statement about piece size, not a competing rule.
  4. Check the beef with a fork, not only the clock. A chunk should pull apart with a fork pressing against it, with no firm centre. If it resists, give the pot another 30 minutes and check again. The safety floor sits well below that point: FoodSafety.gov lists beef roasts and similar whole cuts at 63°C (145°F) with a 3 minute rest, and fork tender chuck in a braise will have passed it long before it shreds.
  5. Thicken before any dairy, 15 to 20 minutes. Stir the cornstarch slurry into the hot cooking liquid, cover, and cook on HIGH until the sauce thickens slightly. This step has to happen while the sauce can still be heated hard.
  6. Cook the noodles separately, about 10 minutes. Boil the egg noodles in a separate pot following the package directions and drain them well. Keeping them apart stops them absorbing too much sauce and turning overly soft.
  7. Temper the sour cream, 3 minutes. Turn the slow cooker off. Put the sour cream in a bowl and whisk in several spoonfuls of the hot sauce, one at a time, then stir the warmed mixture back into the pot. Stir gently until the sauce looks smooth and creamy. Do not boil the sauce after this point, because excessive heat can make it separate.
  8. Serve over the noodles. Spoon the beef, mushrooms, and sauce over the drained noodles, finish with parsley, and taste for salt at the table stage, not earlier.

What success looks like: beef that yields to a fork, a sauce that coats the back of a spoon in an even layer with no grainy flecks and no pool of thin liquid underneath, and noodles that still have a distinct bite under the sauce.

Choose the dairy finish on purpose

The checked recipes finish the same pot three different ways, and the differences are large enough to change the dish. Pick by what the sauce needs, not by habit.

Mushrooms get the same kind of choice. Thick slices added at the start hold up through the long cook and give a soft, sauce soaked result. For firmer mushrooms, saute them separately and add them during the final 30 minutes of cooking. Neither is a correction of the other. They are two textures, decided before the lid goes on.

Ground beef belongs in a separate note, not in the swap list. It appears in checked variations, browned and drained first, with a shorter cook of about 4 hours on LOW for 450 g (1 lb). Its safety floor is also different, at 71°C (160°F) for ground meat on the FoodSafety.gov chart, against 63°C (145°F) for whole cuts. Treat it as its own recipe.

Storage, reheating, and freezing

Store leftover beef and noodles separately, so the noodles keep their texture instead of soaking up the sauce in the fridge. Transfer leftovers to shallow, covered containers within 2 hours. FoodSafety.gov gives soups and stews with meat added 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator at 4°C (40°F) or below, and 2 to 3 months in the freezer at minus 18°C (0°F) or below, with freezer time counting for quality rather than safety. One checked recipe claims 5 days in the fridge. The official chart is the safer number to plan around, so use 3 to 4 days.

Reheat leftovers to 74°C (165°F), the FoodSafety.gov temperature for leftovers of any type, and warm a sour cream sauce gently with frequent stirring rather than boiling it, which risks the same separation the tempering step avoided. For the freezer, the strongest route in the checked recipes is to freeze the cooked beef and mushroom mixture before the sour cream goes in, then thaw it in the refrigerator, reheat it, and finish it with fresh sour cream and freshly cooked noodles.
