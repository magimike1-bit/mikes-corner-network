Slow cooker jambalaya: rice by type, shrimp last, and the simmering base rule

Chicken and shrimp · Published Nov 17, 2026 · 8 min read

Total time: about 6 hours 45 minutes on LOW then HIGH for the rice, or about 4 hours on HIGH throughout (about 20 minutes hands on) · Difficulty: easy to moderate · Yield: 6 servings

Three clocks run inside one pot of jambalaya, and they do not agree. The chicken and sausage base needs hours. The rice needs less than an hour, or a little over an hour, depending on the type in the cupboard. The shrimp need minutes. Recipes that quote a single rice time without naming the rice are where the trouble starts, because the checked methods range from 5 to 10 minutes for instant rice to 1 to 1½ hours for regular long grain, for the same dish.

Three clocks, one pot

The technique is staging. Cook the base until its liquid is actively simmering, add the rice into that hot liquid, and add the shrimp only when the rice is nearly tender. Converted, or parboiled, rice is the forgiving middle route: it has been partially cooked before packaging, holds its shape in the uneven heat of a slow cooker, and appears in two of the checked methods with windows that overlap once the checking interval is counted. Regular long grain and instant rice both work, on their own clocks.

Short grain rice sits outside the table on purpose. The checked methods agree it turns sticky or mushy in a covered pot, and one converted rice method says plainly to avoid instant, minute, and short grain rice inside its own timing. Instant rice has its own working method, listed above. It is not a substitute measured into the converted rice row.

Ingredients and equipment

For the converted rice route, in a 6 quart (5.7 L) slow cooker:

  • 30 mL (2 tablespoons) olive oil
  • 340 g (12 oz) andouille sausage, sliced into coins
  • 570 g (1¼ lb) boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into bite size pieces
  • 1 large yellow onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 2 celery stalks, diced
  • 3 to 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 can (411 g / 14.5 oz) diced tomatoes, with their juices
  • 30 mL (2 tablespoons) tomato paste
  • 30 mL (2 tablespoons) Cajun seasoning, or 10 mL (2 teaspoons) of a low salt blend in the second checked method
  • 5 mL (1 teaspoon) smoked paprika
  • 5 mL (1 teaspoon) dried thyme, or 2.5 mL (1/2 teaspoon) in the second checked method
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 625 mL (2½ cups) chicken broth, divided, plus up to 125 mL (1/2 cup) extra hot broth if the rice needs it
  • 280 g (1½ cups) uncooked converted long grain white rice
  • 340 to 450 g (12 oz to 1 lb) raw shrimp, peeled and deveined, thawed and patted dry

To finish: sliced green onions and chopped fresh parsley. Substitutions, labelled as such: another fully cooked smoked sausage can replace the andouille, with the spice and smoke level changing with the swap. Seasoning blends vary a lot in salt, so the finished dish gets tasted before any salt is added. This version with tomatoes is the Creole style described in the checked recipes. A Cajun style version omits the tomatoes and paste, browns the meats harder, and adds about 125 mL (1/2 cup) extra broth.

The method, step by step

  1. Brown the sausage and sear the chicken, about 10 minutes. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium high heat. Brown the andouille coins for 3 to 4 minutes until the edges crisp, then sear the chicken pieces for 4 to 5 minutes until the outside colours. Neither needs to cook through here. Andouille is already smoked and cooked, so browning it is for flavour and rendered fat, not for safety.
  2. Soften the vegetables in the same pan, about 5 minutes. Cook the onion, bell pepper, and celery in the rendered fat until the onion turns translucent, stir in the garlic for 1 minute more, and scrape everything, including the browned bits from the pan, into the cooker.
  3. Cook the base. Add the tomatoes with their juices, tomato paste, seasoning, paprika, thyme, bay leaves, and 125 mL (1/2 cup) of the broth. Cover and cook on LOW for 5 to 6 hours, or on HIGH for 3 hours. A second checked method cooks the base on HIGH for 3 to 4 hours, so begin checking near 3 hours on HIGH either way.
  4. Check the chicken before the rice goes in. The thickest chicken pieces should be tender and read 74°C (165°F), the FoodSafety.gov temperature for all poultry. Just as important, the tomato base should be actively simmering. Rice added to a merely warm base cooks unevenly, so wait for the simmer.
  5. Add the rice, then leave the lid alone. Remove the bay leaves. Turn the cooker to HIGH, stir in the rice and the remaining 500 mL (2 cups) of broth, and press any exposed grains beneath the hot liquid. If there is not enough liquid to submerge them, add a small splash of the extra hot broth. Cover and cook for 40 to 45 minutes, checking first at 30 minutes. The wider checked window runs 35 to 55 minutes, checked every 5 to 10 minutes after the first check.
  6. Add the shrimp last, 10 to 20 minutes. When the rice is nearly tender, stir in the thawed shrimp, cover, and cook on HIGH until they curl and turn opaque pink. One checked method gives 15 to 20 minutes for large shrimp, another gives 10 to 15 minutes and a thermometer figure of 63°C (145°F). FoodSafety.gov describes shrimp as done when the flesh is pearly or white and opaque, with no numeric temperature on its chart, so use the opaque flesh as the main test and stop as soon as it is met.
  7. Rest and finish, 5 minutes. Remove the bay leaves if any remain, stir in the green onions and parsley, and taste before adding salt. If the dish looks too wet with the rice already tender, let it stand uncovered for about 5 minutes instead of cooking it longer under the lid.

What success looks like: separate rice grains with no hard centres and no standing liquid, sausage coins with browned edges, chicken pieces that are tender without falling apart, and shrimp that are pink, opaque, and still curved loosely rather than curled tight.

When the rice or the shrimp goes wrong

Crunchy rice at the end of its window usually means the base was not simmering when the rice went in, or the grains were not submerged. Press the grains back under the liquid, add hot broth a little at a time only when the pot is drying out, re-cover, and check every 5 to 10 minutes. An automatic top up at the start can leave the finished dish brothy, which is the opposite problem.

Mushy rice means the rice clock ran too long for its type, or the wrong row of the table was used. That is a next batch fix, not a same pot fix, and it is the reason the rice type gets named before the timing does. Watery jambalaya with tender rice gets the uncovered rest in step 7, or a short spell on HIGH with the lid propped open a crack to let steam escape.

Rubbery shrimp were added too early, or added frozen. Frozen shrimp should be thawed and patted dry first, both because they release extra liquid into the pot and because they cool the mixture they land in. For a batch cooked mainly for leftovers, one checked method suggests holding back half the shrimp and adding them fresh at reheating, because shrimp firm up further on the second day.

Leftovers: freeze the base, finish the shrimp fresh

Refrigerate leftovers promptly in shallow, airtight containers. FoodSafety.gov gives cooked meat and poultry leftovers 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator at 4°C (40°F) or below, and soups and stews 3 to 4 days refrigerated and 2 to 3 months in the freezer at minus 18°C (0°F) or below, with freezer time counting for quality. The checked recipes split around that chart, at 2 to 3 days, 3 to 4 days, and up to 4 days. Planning on 3 days keeps a rice and shrimp dish inside every version, including the most cautious one.

Reheat leftovers to 74°C (165°F), the FoodSafety.gov temperature for leftovers of any type, adding a splash of broth or water because the rice dries out as it sits, and keep the reheating as short as that temperature allows so the shrimp do not toughen further. For freezing, the texture saving route is to freeze the chicken and sausage base before the rice and shrimp are added, then finish a thawed base with fresh rice and shrimp. A fully assembled batch can be frozen, and one checked method gives up to 3 months, with the warning that the rice softens slightly on thawing.
