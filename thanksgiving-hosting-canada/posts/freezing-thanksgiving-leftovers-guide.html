Freezing Thanksgiving leftovers properly: turkey, sides, gravy, and pie

Leftovers · 9 min read · Published Oct 15, 2026 · Last updated Oct 15, 2026

Thanksgiving dinner was Monday, October 12, and the fridge has been carrying the surplus ever since. Freezing is how the rest of that meal survives October — but only if it happens while the food is still inside its fridge timeline, and only if each food gets the container and wrap it actually needs. A block of turkey frozen in its roasting juices, an unlabelled bag of stuffing, and a pie in a loose box will all technically freeze; they just will not come back as dinner. This guide works food by food — turkey, stuffing, mashed potatoes, gravy, cranberry sauce, and pie — with the method for each, a comparison table of what freezes well and what does not, and the official timelines that decide how long each package is worth keeping.

Food-safety note: Freezing pauses the clock; it does not rewind it. Only freeze food that was refrigerated promptly after the meal and is still within its fridge life. When in doubt about a container's age or history, throw it out instead of freezing it. Timelines below are from Health Canada's safe food storage chart, checked October 15, 2026.

Freeze on day three, not day six: timing the session

Love Food Hate Waste Canada's core freezing advice is to decide, right after taking stock, what can reasonably be eaten within three to four days — and freeze the rest right away, because the sooner food is frozen, the better it tastes. Health Canada's chart explains why the decision cannot wait for the weekend: cooked poultry keeps three to four days in the fridge, and cooked vegetable and egg dishes the same. October 15 is day three after a Monday dinner, which makes today the last comfortable freezing day for most of the spread. The freezer itself should be at −18°C (0°F) or lower, the temperature Health Canada's chart assumes; a half-empty chest freezer or the back of the fridge freezer, away from the door, holds that temperature most steadily.

The method, food by food

Turkey. Take the meat off the bones first — it saves space, portions more easily, and thaws more evenly. Wrap portions tightly in freezer paper or plastic wrap, then place them in a freezer bag or airtight container, pressing out as much air as possible; trapped air is what dries surfaces out into freezer burn. Slice or shred the meat in the portion sizes future meals will use — sandwich portions, soup portions — rather than freezing one large lump. Bones and the carcass go into their own bag for stock, kept separate from the meat.

Stuffing. Cool it completely first, then pack it into airtight containers or freezer bags with the air pressed out. Flattened bags of stuffing stack neatly and thaw faster than a deep tub. Stuffing should have been stored separately from the turkey meat since Monday — that separation, recommended in Canadian leftover guidance, is also what makes it freezable as its own portion now.

Mashed potatoes. This is the borderline food, and the deciding factor is fat. Mashed potatoes made with butter freeze well; low-fat mash is on the not-recommended list because its texture breaks down. Even good mash can come back slightly grainy — the fix at reheating time is to stir in extra butter or cream, which helps restore a smoother texture. Freeze mash in flat portions so it reheats evenly.

Gravy. Cool it completely, then use the ice-cube method: pour gravy into ice cube trays, freeze solid, and transfer the cubes to a freezer bag. Cubes thaw in the exact quantity a single meal needs, so a pot pie for two never requires melting a litre of gravy. Flour-based gravy freezes well; make sure the cubes go into the bag the same day, labelled, before they pick up freezer odours.

Cranberry sauce. The easiest item on the table. Cranberry sauce freezes exceptionally well in a simple airtight container — leave a little headroom for expansion, label it, and it will thaw tasting freshly made.

Pie. Type decides everything. Pumpkin pie and apple or other fruit pies freeze well — fruit pies generally freeze better than cream-based pies. Wrap the pie (whole or in slices) tightly in plastic wrap, then in aluminum foil, before it goes in. Meringue-topped and custard pies are on the not-recommended list: the meringue weeps and the custard separates, so those slices are better eaten fresh this week.

One honest cross-check: the United States Department of Agriculture gives a slightly more conservative best-quality figure for leftover turkey — use it within four months — while Health Canada's chart allows four to six months for cooked poultry. Both agree on the principle underneath: frozen leftovers remain safe well past these windows, but they dry out and lose flavour, so the timelines are quality deadlines, not safety cliffs. Planning to use the turkey by the four-month mark satisfies both sources.

The freezing session, step by step

  1. Confirm everything is still in date. Check each container against its fridge timeline — three to four days for most cooked foods. Anything past its limit, undated, or left out for hours on Monday gets discarded, not frozen.
  2. Cool anything still warm completely. Cool food thoroughly before freezing to avoid condensation, which becomes ice crystals and freezer burn on the surface.
  3. Portion before packaging. Divide each food into the sizes a future meal will actually use — single lunches, a family soup night — so thawing never means thawing everything.
  4. Wrap and seal with the air out. Wrap tightly and use airtight containers or freezer bags, pressing air out of bags and laying them flat. Separate each food into its own package; keep different types of leftovers separate, as Health Canada advises for storage generally.
  5. Label every package with contents, portion count, and today's date — October 15, 2026. Adding a short reheating note on the label (for example, “reheat to 74°C”) makes the package self-explanatory in January.
  6. Freeze flat, then stack. Lay bags flat until solid, then stack them like files. Place packages toward the back or centre of the freezer, away from the door, where the temperature is steadiest.

What success looks like: the freezer holds flat, labelled, dated packages — nothing in a roasting pan, nothing anonymous, nothing warm — and a quick count of the packages matches a rough meal plan for using them within the timelines in the table. If a package cannot be identified without opening it, the labelling step was skipped and needs redoing before the door closes.

Thawing and reheating frozen leftovers

Health Canada's thawing rule for leftovers is the refrigerator or the microwave — never the counter — and thawed food should be consumed or cooked immediately after thawing. Food defrosted in the microwave should not be refrozen. Thawing flat bags in the fridge overnight is the low-effort route and another reason portion size matters: a flat package thaws evenly by morning, a thick block does not. Reheat everything to a safe internal temperature of 74°C (165°F), checked with a digital thermometer, and bring gravy and soups to a full, rolling boil while stirring. Gravy cubes can go straight into a saucepan from frozen. Discard whatever is reheated and not eaten, and stir the extra butter or cream into reheated mash at this stage, not before freezing.

What not to freeze — and what to do with it instead

The not-recommended list is about texture, not safety: milk- and cream-based dishes, mayonnaise-based dishes, dressed salads and lettuce, fried foods, very tender vegetables, cucumbers, radishes, gelatin-based dishes, potatoes without fat, and meringue or custard pies all come back diminished. The right move for these is a plan for this week, not the freezer: custard pie slices become tonight's dessert, dressed salad is finished at lunch, and cream-based leftovers are eaten within their shorter fridge life or shared with guests in take-home containers rather than frozen in hope.

FAQ

Can leftovers be frozen on day three? Yes — day three after cooking is still inside the three-to-four-day fridge window for most cooked foods, and freezing sooner rather than later gives the best flavour and texture.

Why did frozen turkey come back dry last time? Air is the usual cause. Meat wrapped loosely or stored in a part-empty container loses surface moisture to freezer burn; tight wrapping, air pressed out, and using the turkey within four months all protect it.

Does freezing make old leftovers safe again? No. Freezing preserves food in the state it went in. Food that was already past its fridge life, or sat out too long, should be thrown out — the freezer is a pause button, not a reset.
