How long does it really take to learn touch typing?

Planning · 6 min read

The short answer: plan on about a month to become a functional touch typist — and expect to be slower than your old method for the first two weeks. The long answer depends on where you're starting and how many minutes a day you actually practice. Most online timelines assume a specific starting point, which is why they contradict each other. This guide does it differently: find your starting point first, then read your calendar.

Step 0: find your starting point (60 seconds)

Open a free typing test — monkeytype or 10fastfingers are the usual picks — and type normally, the way you always type, for one minute. Write down two numbers: your speed (WPM) and your accuracy. Typical hunt-and-peck typists land between 27 and 37 WPM. Where you sit in that range changes everything about the timeline below.

The hour math behind every timeline

Research summaries put the total practice needed to convert from hunt-and-peck to touch typing at roughly 20 to 40 hours of structured practice. That's the engine behind every timeline; the calendar is just hours divided by minutes per day:

  • 15 min/day (about 1.75 hours a week) → 20-40 hours lands in 3 to 6 months.
  • 20 min/day (about 2.3 hours a week) → 2 to 4 months.
  • 30 min/day (about 3.5 hours a week) → 6 to 12 weeks.

And missed days count: if you practice 4 days a week instead of 7, multiply every estimate by about 1.75. Consistency is the whole game — short daily sessions beat occasional marathons for motor learning.

These bands come from multiple independent sources, not one person's story: a documented 30-day journey went from 15 to 50+ WPM in four weeks (15→25→35→45→50+, week by week), a complete guide gives the same stage markers (1 week positions, 2-4 weeks accuracy, 1-3 months 60-80, 6-12 months 100+), and another learner logged 87 WPM at 98% accuracy after a month of 3-5 hours a week. Different people, same shape of curve.

The decision shortcut

Match your situation:

  • Hunt-and-peck at ~30 WPM, 15 min/day available: give yourself 8-12 weeks to be a comfortable touch typist beating your old speed. This is the most common path.
  • Starting from ~15 WPM, 20-30 min/day: expect 50+ WPM in about a month — this is the documented 30-day journey exactly.
  • Already at 50 WPM touch typing, want 70+: plan another 2-3 months; the gains get slower and plateaus are normal.
  • Chasing 100 WPM: think in months, not weeks — around 10 months of regular practice is the published average.

What slows people down (and what speeds them up)

  • Chasing speed too early is the #1 delay. Every error you practice is a mistake your muscles memorize. Build accuracy first; speed arrives on its own.
  • Looking at the keys resets your progress — it breaks the hands-off habit the whole skill depends on.
  • Using the skill daily speeds everything up. Learners who switch their real work (emails, notes, code) to touch typing as soon as the basics are in report much faster gains than drill-only practicers.
  • Fixing weak keys deliberately — the letters that always trip you — breaks plateaus that brute-force repetition doesn't.

When to change the plan

If you've been practicing daily for three weeks and your speed hasn't moved at all, something structural is wrong — usually peeking at the keys or rushing before accuracy is solid. Slow down, re-check your finger positions, and give it another week. If you're improving but slower than the bands above, that's normal variance, not failure: the ranges are wide because people are.
