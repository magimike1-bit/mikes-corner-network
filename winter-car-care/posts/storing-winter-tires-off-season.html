Storing Winter Tires in the Off-Season: Cleaning, Bagging, and Marking Rotation

Tires · 5 min read

Published October 27, 2026 · Last updated October 27, 2026

Clean them, dry them completely, mark each one’s position, and seal each tire in its own airtight bag — then store them by the rule that matches whether they are still on rims. That sequence, assembled from a Canadian retailer’s guidance and two specialist storage guides, is the whole protocol. Most storage advice stops after the bagging. The step that actually forks — mounted versus unmounted — is the one sources most often state without its condition, and it is covered in full below.

The storage procedure

  1. Mark the positions before removal. While each tire is still on the car, mark its position — FL, FR, RL, RR for front left, front right, rear left, and rear right. Marking after removal is guessing; the record only works if it is made while the positions are still known.
  2. Clean with soap and water. Wash off salt and brake dust, and remove foreign matter lodged in the tread. Canadian retailer guidance gives the method plainly: soap and water, foreign matter out, before anything goes into a bag.
  3. Dry completely. Let each tire dry fully after washing. Bagging a damp tire seals the moisture in with it for the whole off-season.
  4. Skip the tire dressing. Do not apply tire dressing products before storage — a recommendation the specialist guide attributes to Continental. A stored tire needs to be clean and dry, not dressed.
  5. Bag one tire per bag, airtight. Store each tire in an airtight bag, one tire per bag, to limit air and ozone exposure during storage.
  6. Set the position by mounting state. Apply the mounted or unmounted rule from the table below. This is the fork in the procedure, and it is decided by whether the tire is on a rim, not by the space available.
  7. Place the set in its storage environment. Indoors, in a dry and ventilated place, within the 10–25°C range, away from heat and ozone sources and away from solvents, fuels, and ozone-producing equipment.
  8. Calendar the unmounted rotation. If the tires are unmounted, set a reminder: rotate each one a quarter turn every four to six weeks while it stands in storage.

What success looks like: four bagged tires, each marked with the position it came off, clean and fully dry inside its own airtight bag, stored indoors in the 10–25°C range away from heat, solvents, fuels, and ozone sources — mounted sets stacked flat or hung by the rim, unmounted tires standing upright with a quarter-turn rotation every four to six weeks on the calendar. Every item on that list can be checked in one visit to the storage spot.

State first, position second.

The same stack that is correct for a mounted set is the wrong answer for an unmounted one. Sources that give a single instruction — always stack, or always stand them up — are giving half a rule. That ordering is the decision framework for the whole storage question, and it costs nothing to apply.

The environment, in numbers

The Canadian retailer’s guidance sets the place: indoors, dry, and ventilated, away from heat and ozone sources. The specialist guide adds the only temperature range in the retrieved sources — 10–25°C — and extends the keep-away list to solvents, fuels, and ozone-producing equipment. A garage corner can meet that standard or miss it depending on what shares the space; the check is against the list above, not against whether the spot merely has room for four tires. Bagging supports the same goal from the other side: one tire per airtight bag limits the air and ozone exposure the environment rules are also trying to reduce.

Spring changeover: start from the marks

When the set comes out, the position marks made at removal — FL, FR, RL, RR — are the starting record for refitting, which is why step one of the procedure happens in the fall, not the spring. Unbag the tires, read the marks before any tire moves to a corner of the car, and check each tire over as it comes out of its bag: a storage season that began with foreign matter removed and a full dry is the season most likely to end without a surprise waiting inside the bag. The protocol’s last step, in other words, was written at its first one.
