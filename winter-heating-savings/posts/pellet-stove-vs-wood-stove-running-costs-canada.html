Pellet Stove vs Wood Stove Running Costs in Canada: The Verdict Flips With Your Local Fuel Price

Save daily · Published 2026-11-20 · Last updated 2026-11-20

General information only, not financial advice. Fuel prices were checked on 2026-11-20 from the Canadian sources named in the article, vary widely by region and season, and will differ at your local supplier. Run the same arithmetic with your own quotes before buying an appliance.

$41 against $47. Those are the costs per million BTU of usable heat for pellets and firewood at one Canadian retailer's current prices, and the six dollar gap is the whole story and also not the story at all. It is real, it is small, and at other published Canadian prices it disappears or reverses. Comparing a price per bag with a price per cord settles nothing, because the two units hold different amounts of energy and the two stoves deliver different shares of that energy to the room. Priced properly, per unit of usable heat, this decision turns on your local fuel quotes, the appliance already in your house, and how much of the work you want to do yourself.

The two fuels priced per unit of usable heat

The energy figures and the retail prices in this table come from the same Canadian source, a firewood and pellet retailer whose comparison was published on November 10, 2026 and whose product prices were checked again on 2026-11-20. A full cord of seasoned hardwood carries roughly 20 to 24 million BTU, and a modern wood stove delivers about 70 to 75 percent of it to the room, giving 15 to 17 million BTU of usable heat. A ton of pellets, sold as 50 bags of 40 lb, carries roughly 16 to 17 million BTU, and a pellet stove delivers about 80 to 85 percent of it, giving 13 to 14 million BTU usable.

Buying pellets bag by bag quietly erases the pellet advantage at this retailer. A single 40 lb bag lists at $14.99, checked 2026-11-20, so 50 individual bags total $749.50 against $549 for the same weight on a pallet. That is $200.50 paid for buying in small lots, before any heat reaches a room.

A worked season at 50 million BTU of usable heat

Take a house that asks its stove for 50 million BTU of usable heat across a season. That figure is a labelled example, chosen to make the arithmetic easy to redo with your own quotes, not a claim about your house. At the retail prices above, pellets need about 3.7 tons (50 divided by 13.5), which costs about $2,031. Firewood needs about 3.1 cords (50 divided by 16), which costs about $2,341. Pellets win that season by roughly $310.

Now price the same season from published Canadian ranges instead of one retailer. A 2026 Canadian wood fuel market report puts bagged residential pellets at CAD 250 to 400 per tonne. At the $325 midpoint of that range, the same 3.7 tons cost about $1,203. A separate 2026 Canadian technical comparison prices purchased fuel per million BTU directly: wood at $18 to $28 and pellets at $20 to $30. Run through the same 50 million BTU example, those ranges give $900 to $1,400 for wood and $1,000 to $1,500 for pellets. The ranges overlap almost completely. At one end of them wood is the cheaper fuel by $100 or more, at the other end pellets are, and in the middle they are the same money. Any article that names a universal winner is working from one supplier's price list and hoping you do not check another.

The costs outside the fuel line

Electricity. A pellet stove draws 100 to 200 W continuously while it runs, for the auger, fans, and controls, per the Canadian technical comparison above. At a labelled assumption of 10 running hours a day across 120 heating days, that is 120 to 240 kWh a season. Price it at the marginal rate on your own electricity bill. A wood stove draws nothing and keeps heating through a power cut. A pellet stove stops when the power stops, unless backup power is added to the budget.

Wet wood. Firewood at 30 percent moisture can lose a third of its usable heat to boiling off water, which turns a $47 per million BTU cord into roughly a $70 one. That single condition change is larger than the entire pellet versus wood gap at the prices in the table. Seasoned, dry storage is not a nicety in this comparison. It is the comparison.

The appliance itself. A pellet stove costs $2,500 to $5,000 installed at the retailer figures used here. Venting differs too: RONA's Canadian buying guide notes that pellet stoves can be vented horizontally or vertically, while traditional wood stoves require vertical venting and a chimney. The same guide warns that some municipalities restrict or ban wood and pellet burning devices, so local rules are a check to run before any purchase, not after.

Who should buy which, by what is already in the house

You already own a wood stove. Stay with firewood unless your local pellet quote is dramatically lower than your cord price. Saving even the full $310 from the worked example, every season, takes about 8 to 16 years to repay a $2,500 to $5,000 pellet stove purchase, and that is before pellet stove maintenance. A small per unit gap does not fund a second appliance.

You already own a pellet stove. Buy pellets by the ton in autumn, store the pallet dry, and do not price a wood stove against a fuel gap this size. Your savings lever is the purchase format, pallet against bag by bag, worth about $200 a ton at the prices checked here.

You cut or receive wood at low cash cost. Firewood wins outright. The purchased wood ranges above assume a bought cord; wood that costs labour and saw fuel instead of $749 a cord undercuts every pellet price in these tables.

Outages or unattended running decide it. If winter power cuts are routine where you live, the wood stove is the only one of the two still heating on the third evening. If the stove must hold a house at temperature while nobody is home to load it, the pellet hopper, refilled every day or two and capable of up to 20 hours of continuous automatic feeding per RONA's guide, is built for exactly that job.

Get two local quotes, one per fuel, divide each by the usable heat in the table, and the cheaper number for your street is your answer. It will not always match the answer one province over, and that is the honest result.
