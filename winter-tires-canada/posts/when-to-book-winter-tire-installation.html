When to Book Your Winter Tire Installation

Mid-October is the booking crunch. A region-by-region calendar for 2026, the early-bird deadlines worth knowing, and how to skip the line entirely.

← All guides

Every fall, the same thing happens: the first snowfall warning hits, everyone calls the tire shop on the same day, and the next available appointment is three weeks out. The drivers who booked in October are already done. Installation timing isn't really about the tires — it's about the calendar. Here's how to be in the first group.

Why mid-October is the crunch

The rush has simple mechanics: tire shops fill their schedules through the seasonal changeover wave, and booking late means long waitlists — or possible extra charges for squeezing you in. In Quebec the crunch has a legal edge: shops fill up through the second half of November ahead of the December 1 mandate, and the final week before the deadline is the worst for availability.

The good news: installing early costs you almost nothing in tire wear. Accelerated wear on winter tires happens in sustained warm weather; through October and November, with cooler days and cold nights, the extra wear is minimal while the safety benefit is real. The old rule stands: better early than too late. October snowfalls aren't hypothetical — the 2024 Thanksgiving storm dropped 15 cm in Charlevoix, and snow hit Mont Tremblant in October 2023.

The 2026 booking calendar by region

Quebec's regional guidance gives the clearest version of the national pattern — northern and higher-altitude areas first:

  • Mid-October: far north and high altitude — James Bay, Chibougamau, Fermont, Haute-Gaspésie. (In most of the rest of Canada, northern regions follow the same logic.)
  • Late October: northwest and central regions — Abitibi, Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean, Haute-Mauricie.
  • Early November: Laurentians, Lanaudière, Estrie, Montérégie.
  • Before mid-November: the Greater Montreal area.

Outside Quebec, the same gradient applies: book early to mid-October in most provinces. In Quebec, the legal deadline sharpens everything — winter tires become mandatory December 1, and shops fill up through the second half of November, with the final week before the deadline the worst for availability.

Our take

Book your appointment two to three weeks before you'd actually install. A late-October appointment booked in early October costs nothing extra and skips the entire November scramble. The people fighting for slots are always the ones who waited for the first snowfall.

Before your appointment: the prep checklist

  1. Know whether your tires are mounted on their own wheels. Shops ask this at booking because it changes the job (and the price): on-rim swaps are quick, loose tires need mounting and balancing.
  2. Check tread depth and age yourself. Quebec's guidance recommends at least 4.8 mm of tread for winter tires. If your set is near that, budget for replacement rather than discovering it at the shop.
  3. Confirm the 3PMSF symbol. Look for the mountain-snowflake symbol on each tire's sidewall — in Quebec it's the legal requirement from December 1 to March 15.
  4. Decide on storage. If you don't have a cool, dark, dry spot, ask the shop about seasonal storage when booking — see our storage guide for what proper storage looks like.
  5. Note any TPMS or vibration issues from last season so the shop can address them in the same visit.

After the swap: the torque re-check

Wheels can settle after installation, so the re-torque isn't optional. The standard guidance is to re-torque all wheel fasteners after the first 80–160 km of driving (50–100 miles) — initial tightening can compress the wheel material and reduce clamping load. When re-torquing, the wheels should be cool; loosen and re-tighten in sequence to your vehicle manufacturer's specification. If you don't own a torque wrench, have a shop do it — tighter is not necessarily better.

Mobile installation: skipping the line entirely

There's a way around the shop waitlist that barely appears in search results: mobile tire services, where a fully equipped van comes to your driveway or workplace. Options operating in Canada include GoMoTires in Calgary, Sparky X in the GTA and Durham (TIA-certified, with free mobile installation on complete winter packages for the first season), The Tire Valet on Vancouver's North Shore, and mobile-tire.ca in Toronto.

The bottom line

Book two to three weeks before you'd install, aim for early-to-mid October in most of Canada, and treat the appointment like the scarce resource it becomes in November. Or skip the line: a mobile service does the same swap in your driveway.
