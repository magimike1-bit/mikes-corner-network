Winter Tire Rebates in Canada, Fall 2026: How the Programs Work and How to Claim

The amounts cluster at $70 to $100 a set — what separates money collected from money advertised is the claim deadline, the channel exclusion, and the install condition. All three, compared.

← All guides

Every fall, the same pattern runs across Canada: tire manufacturers offer money back on a set of four, the amounts cluster in a narrow band, and a large share of buyers never collect — not because the programs are traps, but because the rebate is a second purchase decision with its own deadlines, exclusions and paperwork, made at exactly the moment the shop is busiest. This guide treats it that way. The fall 2026 programs below were checked on October 22, 2026 against a manufacturer rebate index current as of October 14, 2026 and against the manufacturers' own published terms where available. Amounts, windows and claim deadlines are stated as published; every program reserves the right to change, so the final check before buying is always the brand's own rebate page.

Fall 2026 programs side by side

The headline amounts look interchangeable — $100 here, $100 there — until the windows and claim deadlines are laid side by side. They are not interchangeable. One program closes its purchase window on December 6 and its claim window five days later; another runs to December 31 and accepts claims until the end of January. A driver who buys in mid-December has a full choice of programs; a driver who buys in late December is down to the long-window brands, however the advertising reads.

Two patterns in that table matter more than any single amount. First, the claim deadline is a separate date from the purchase deadline, and in the tightest programs — Pirelli's is the extreme, closing claims on December 11 for purchases ending December 6 — it leaves almost no slack for a lost invoice or a busy December. Second, several programs pay by model tier, not by brand: the same badge can be worth $100 on one winter line and $50 or $70 on another, so the model name on the invoice, not the brand on the window sticker, is what the cheque is calculated from.

The fine print that decides whether you get paid

Channel exclusions come first. Pirelli's official 2026 terms state plainly that the rebate is not available on tires purchased from Canadian Tire or Costco, and that it is not combinable with any other offer. The rebate index carries the same warning for Michelin — no Costco or Canadian Tire purchases — and for Uniroyal at Costco. A warehouse-club price that beats the tire shop by $60 and quietly voids a $100 rebate is not the cheaper purchase. Check the channel before comparing prices, not after.

Purchase sometimes means purchase and install. The Bridgestone and Firestone fall offers both require the tires to be purchased and installed during the promotion period. A set bought in the window but installed after it — easy to do when November installation queues back up — can fail the condition. When booking, ask the shop to confirm the installation date lands inside the program window, and keep the installation invoice as carefully as the purchase invoice.

The invoice is the claim. Pirelli's terms list what the invoice must show: invoice number, date of sale, the consumer's name, address and phone number, the tire model, the number of tires purchased, and the amount of the sale. The same terms state that order-confirmation or placed-order emails are not accepted as proof of purchase. That is the single most useful sentence in any rebate document, because it describes the most common failure: an online order email submitted where a proper invoice was required. Whatever brand is chosen, the documents to keep are the full invoice showing the model and quantity, and a copy of everything submitted — Pirelli advises retaining copies, and its submissions become its property and are not returned.

Who runs the program matters for questions. Honda Canada's offers page, which hosts the Kumho winter rebate (up to $60 on a set of four select tires, October 1 to December 31, 2026), states that the offers are the exclusive responsibility of the manufacturer and that questions must go to the manufacturer. The same division applies almost everywhere: the retailer sells the tires, the manufacturer's rebate centre pays the claim, and Yokohama's program states the point directly — claims are submitted to Yokohama, not through the retailer.

How to claim: the procedure

Nokian's published steps are the cleanest summary of the shape every program shares — purchase, submit, receive — so the procedure below follows that shape and adds the checks the other brands' terms make necessary:

  1. Before buying, confirm the exact model is on the eligible list and the channel is not excluded. Model tiers decide the amount; Costco and Canadian Tire purchases void at least two major programs. This check takes two minutes at the counter and cannot be fixed afterwards.
  2. Buy a set of four qualifying tires inside the purchase window. Nokian's terms require four of the same qualifying tire; every program in the table is built on a set of four. If installation is part of the condition, book it inside the window too.
  3. Get a complete invoice. Check it shows the invoice number, sale date, your name and contact details, the tire model, the quantity (four) and the sale amount. If any element is missing, have the shop reissue it the same day.
  4. Submit before the claim deadline, by the channel the program specifies. Online where offered (Hankook's program is claim-online; Bridgestone's deadline is stated for online claims), or by mail postmarked by the deadline where mail is accepted. Note the deadline on a calendar the day of purchase — Pirelli's falls on December 11, 2026, weeks before the January deadlines elsewhere.
  5. Keep copies of everything. Photograph the invoice and the submitted form. Submissions are not returned, and a claim questioned weeks later is answered from copies or not at all.
  6. Wait the published delivery window. Pirelli issues a digital card to the buyer's email within four weeks, while a physical card can take six to ten weeks after submission. A card that has not arrived inside the stated window is a reason to contact the rebate centre; one inside it is simply in process.

What success looks like: a prepaid card for the tier amount arrives within the program's stated delivery window, and the file at home holds the invoice and claim copies that would prove the purchase if the claim were ever questioned.

What the rebate is actually worth

Honest arithmetic keeps the rebate in its place. On a set of four winter tires, the fall 2026 programs return $40 to $110 depending on brand and model, with most winter lines at $70 to $100 — checked October 22, 2026. That is a genuine discount on a purchase the safety case already justifies, and it can decide between two otherwise-equal tires. It does not turn an overpriced set into a bargain: a $100 rebate on a set priced $150 above a competitor's leaves the buyer $50 behind. The order of operations that works is the order in this guide — choose the tire, check the channel and the model tier, then let the rebate adjust the final comparison. Drivers who reverse that order, shopping the rebate table first, reliably end up with the tire the promotion department preferred, bought from a channel they did not choose, claimed against a deadline they nearly missed.

Our take

The fall rebate season rewards one habit above all others: reading the claim deadline on the day of purchase. The amounts are similar enough across brands that the deadline, the channel exclusion and the install condition — not the headline number — are what separate money collected from money advertised.

FAQ

Can a rebate be claimed on tires bought at Costco or Canadian Tire? For several major programs, no: Pirelli's 2026 terms exclude both retailers outright, and the rebate index notes the same exclusion for Michelin. Always check the specific program before assuming a warehouse purchase qualifies.

Is the rebate taken off at the till? No. These are manufacturer mail-in or online-claim programs: the full price is paid at purchase, and a prepaid card follows after an approved claim, on the delivery timelines the terms state.

What if the tires are bought in the window but installed after it? For programs with a purchase-and-install condition — Bridgestone's fall offer states one — a late installation can disqualify the claim. Book installation inside the window and keep both invoices.

Can a rebate be combined with a store sale or another offer? Pirelli's terms say its rebate is not combinable with any other offer, and other programs carry their own conditions. Assume stacking is not allowed unless the program's terms say otherwise in writing.
