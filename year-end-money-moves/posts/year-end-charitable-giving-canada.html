Year-End Charitable Giving in Canada: Cash vs Securities

Donations must be made by December 31 to count for this year — but "made" means three different things depending on how you give. Pick the channel that fits your December. General information only — your accountant's advice wins.

The decision table: cash vs securities in-kind

Cash donationSecurities in-kind
Donation receiptFull amount givenFull fair market value
Capital gains taxYou pay it when you sellEliminated (0% inclusion on the gain)
Processing timeMinutes (online) to days (mail)Days to weeks (brokerage transfer)
Works forAny amount, any charityAppreciated securities; charities set up to receive them
December start-byMid-to-late DecemberEarly-to-mid December

The in-kind advantage is simple: donating appreciated publicly traded securities directly to a qualified donee lets you eliminate the capital gain on the appreciation while still receiving a donation receipt for the full fair market value. Sell the shares first and donate the cash, and you pay tax on the gain. Donate the shares themselves, and the inclusion rate on that gain is zero. The bigger the unrealized gain, the bigger the difference — which is why this strategy rewards starting early enough in December for the transfer to actually clear.

The securities transfer procedure

  1. Confirm the charity can receive securities. Not all charities are positioned to accept in-kind gifts. Ask before you start anything — this single check prevents the most common failure mode.
  2. Confirm the charity is a qualified donee and the security qualifies. The capital-gains elimination applies to securities listed on a designated stock exchange given to a qualified donee — typically a registered charity. A mistaken assumption on either point can produce an unexpected capital gain or a receipt that won't survive a CRA review.
  3. Contact your brokerage for its transfer process. You'll typically complete a transfer authorization naming the charity's brokerage account. Your broker and the charity's broker handle the movement; there is no sale, and no cash changes hands.
  4. Start early in December. The transfer must complete with enough time to clear before December 31. Brokerage processing, the charity's own processing, and the December holiday calendar all eat days. Waiting until the last week turns a certain 2026 deduction into a 2027 one.
  5. Get the receipt and file it with your tax package. The receipt is for the fair market value on the transfer date. Keep the transfer confirmations too — they document the in-kind nature of the gift.

Why this order: the charity's capability check comes before anything else because it determines whether the strategy exists at all; the qualified-donee check comes before the transfer because it determines whether the tax benefit exists; and the early start exists because December transfers have a failure mode that cash donations don't — a transfer that clears on January 2 is a 2027 donation no matter when you signed the form.

The timing traps: three channels, three finish lines

  • Online donations are dated when the payment is processed, not when you click donate. A gift made on the evening of December 31 that processes on January 1 counts for 2027. Donate online by December 29 at the latest.
  • Mailed cheques are generally treated as made on the postmark date. The last week of December is a poor time to rely on the post — mail a cheque early or don't mail one at all.
  • Securities transfers count when the transfer clears, not when you authorize it. This is the longest lead time of the three — start in early-to-mid December.

The paperwork that makes the receipt count

Most Canadians can claim the donation tax credit on up to 75% of their income in a year (100% in the year of death and the year before it). Unused credits can be carried forward and claimed in any of the five following years — so a large December gift in a low-income year isn't wasted, it's banked. One caution for large gifts: if you're donating big while also realizing significant capital gains or claiming large credits in the same year, you may fall under the Alternative Minimum Tax, a parallel calculation that ensures a minimum tax is paid. That's an accountant conversation, not a reason to skip the gift — but have it before December 31, not after.

What success looks like by Dec 31

You hold a donation receipt dated in 2026 for every gift you intended to make this year; any securities transfer shows a cleared transfer date in December; and nothing is sitting in a brokerage queue or a mailbox on New Year's Eve. Your accountant gets the receipts in January alongside everything else.
